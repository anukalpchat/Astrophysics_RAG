Abstract
We present the DECam Mapping the Ancient Galaxy in CaHK (MAGIC) survey, a 54-night NOIRLab
Survey Program to image≳5,000 deg 2 of the southern hemisphere using a metallicity-sensitive narrow-band
filter covering the CaiiH&K lines centered at 3955 A
◦
. This filter is installed on the Dark Energy Camera
(DECam), mounted on the 4-m NSF V ´ıctor M. Blanco Telescope. The survey reaches typical 10𝜎depths
of mag CaHK ≈22.5, 3–4 mag deeper than comparable surveys in the southern hemisphere. By combining
photometry from this CaiiH&K filter with existing DECam𝑔, 𝑟, 𝑖broadband photometry from the DECam
Local Volume Exploration (DELVE) survey, MAGIC is deriving photometric metallicities for red giant branch
stars down to the magnitude limit of usable proper motions fromGaiadata release 3 (DR3). MAGIC has
already imaged∼3,000 deg 2, supplemented by other affiliated observing programs that have used this filter
to image star clusters, dwarf galaxies, and stellar streams. We overview MAGIC’s survey strategy, describe
data processing through the derivation of metallicities and photometric distances, and summarize early science
results that have been published with this dataset. In addition, we present several new results, including the
confirmation of a distant (>5𝑟 ℎ) member of the Reticulum II ultra-faint dwarf galaxy, on-sky density maps of
low-metallicity stars into the distant Milky Way halo (∼150 kpc) recovering 13/14 ultra-faint dwarf galaxies in
the current footprint, and a validation of our initial targeting of extremely metal-poor stars. Collectively, these
results demonstrate that the MAGIC dataset enables cutting-edge studies of the faint, low-metallicity regime of
the Milky Way and its substructures.

1 INTRODUCTION
The local universe is an active discovery space, facilitating
studies of the Milky Way’s assembly (e.g., Ivezi´c et al. 2012;
Belokurov 2013; Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016; Helmi
2020; Deason & Belokurov 2024), early galaxy formation
(e.g., Brown et al. 2014; Simon 2019), the small-scale distri-
bution of dark matter (e.g., Bullock & Boylan-Kolchin 2017;
Bonaca & Price-Whelan 2025), and the production of elements
across cosmic time (e.g., Nomoto et al. 2013; Frebel & Norris
2015; Kobayashi et al. 2020). For instance, wide-field photo-
metric surveys such as the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS;
York et al. 2000) enabled the discovery of ultra-faint dwarf
galaxies (UFDs; Willman et al. 2005; Zucker et al. 2006),
which are the most dark-matter-dominated and faintest known
galaxies (mass-to-light ratios M/L≳100 M ⊙/L⊙;𝐿≲10 5 L⊙;
Simon & Geha 2007; Martin et al. 2008; Mu˜noz et al. 2018; Si-
mon 2019). SDSS and other such surveys (e.g., Pan-STARRS,
DES, DECaLS, DELVE,Gaia; Chambers et al. 2016; Abbott
et al. 2018; Dey et al. 2019; Drlica-Wagner et al. 2021; Gaia
Collaboration et al. 2016, 2023a) have additionally revealed a
large population of stellar streams around the Milky Way (e.g.,
Belokurov et al. 2006; Grillmair & Dionatos 2006; Shipp et al.

2018; Li et al. 2022), which trace its gravitational potential and
are sensitive to perturbations from starless dark matter halos
(e.g., Erkal et al. 2016). TheGaiaspace mission (Gaia Collab-
oration et al. 2016, 2023a) has now measured proper motions
for over a billion stars, providing evidence for the last major
merger of the Milky Way (known as Gaia-Sausage/Enceladus;
Belokurov et al. 2018; Haywood et al. 2018; Helmi et al. 2018)
and motions on the plane of the sky for nearly every known
Milky Way satellite galaxy (McConnachie & Venn 2020; Li
et al. 2021a; Pace et al. 2022). Complementing these stud-
ies, spectroscopic surveys (e.g., SEGUE, LAMOST, GALAH,
APOGEE, S5, DESI; Yanny et al. 2009; Zhao et al. 2012;
De Silva et al. 2015; Majewski et al. 2017; Li et al. 2019;
DESI Collaboration et al. 2022) have collectively provided
information on the chemical composition of millions of stars.
Stars in the Milky Way halo and its faint substructures
(e.g., dwarf galaxies and stellar streams) are generally metal-
poor1. Low-metallicity stars, where “metallicity” is defined
as the abundance of elements heavier than helium, function

of stars as a function of metallicity can trace element produc-

metal-poor stars (EMPs; [Fe/H]<−3.0), or ultra metal-poor
stars (UMPs; [Fe/H]<−4.0), are hypothesized to be enriched
by so few events that they provide insight on the nucleosyn-

2014; Ji et al. 2015; Placco et al. 2016; Ishigaki et al. 2018;
Hartwig et al. 2018; Vanni et al. 2023).
This has motivated searches for the lowest metallicity
stars spanning several decades (e.g., Bond 1970, 1980; Bidel-
man & MacConnell 1973; Beers et al. 1985; Christlieb 2003;
Christlieb et al. 2008; Keller et al. 2007; Schlaufman & Casey
2014; Starkenburg et al. 2017), driven both by finding low-
mass, metal-free first stars (aka. “Population III”) and by in-
ferring the properties (e.g., initial masses, explosion energies)
of the first generation of metal-producing supernovae through
their nucleosynthetic yields (Tominaga et al. 2007; Heger &
Woosley 2010; Placco et al. 2015; Sk´ ulad´ottir et al. 2024).

obtained low-resolution prism/grism spectroscopy, typically

Recent untargeted searches for low-metallicity stars have
leveraged photometric metallicities (e.g., Keller et al. 2007;
Starkenburg et al. 2017; Whitten et al. 2019, 2021; Galarza
et al. 2022; Fan et al. 2023), building on the long-recognized
fact that blanketing at bluer wavelengths by metal lines, and
particularly absorption from the prominent CaiiH&K lines
can lead to a mapping between blue intermediate- and narrow-
band filters and stellar metallicity (e.g., Schuster & Nissen
1989; Anthony-Twarog et al. 1991). Such work has shown
that this photometry can provide quantitative measurements
of the stellar metallicity down to [Fe/H]≈ −3.0 (e.g., Ivezi ´c
et al. 2008; An et al. 2013; Chiti et al. 2021a; Huang et al.
2022; Martin et al. 2024; Barbosa et al. 2025) and enables the
identification of the lowest metallicity stars (i.e., with [Fe/H]
<−4.0; Keller et al. 2014; Starkenburg et al. 2018; Nordlander
et al. 2019; Lardo et al. 2021; Placco et al. 2021b, 2025; Chiti
et al. 2024, 2026; Limberg et al. 2025a). Collectively, these
efforts have discovered∼50 known stars in the [Fe/H]<−4.0
regime (Suda et al. 2008; Abohalima & Frebel 2018; Sestito
et al. 2019; Bonifacio et al. 2025), directly showing these stars
tend to be exceptionally carbon-enhanced (e.g., Placco et al.
2014; Arentsen et al. 2022) leading to a range of theories on
how the first stars may have enriched primordial dark matter
halos (e.g., Iwamoto et al. 2005; Meynet et al. 2006; Cooke &
Madau 2014; Hartwig & Yoshida 2019; Vanni et al. 2023).
A key benefit of photometric metallicity surveys is that they
simultaneously provide metallicities for all stars that are de-
tected with sufficient signal-to-noise (S/N) in the field-of-view
of the imager, in contrast to fiber-fed/multi-slit spectroscopic
surveys which have a spatial selection function or suffer from
incompleteness. This characteristic makes photometric metal-
licity surveys a critical complement to wide-field broad-band
and astrometric imaging surveys (e.g., SDSS, DES,Gaia). For
instance, recent work has shown that a combination of photo-
metric metallicities, proper motion, and color-magnitude di-
agram (CMD) selection can provide insight on the formation
and dynamical state of UFDs by detecting extended features
in their peripheries (Chiti et al. 2021c; Longeard et al. 2022a,
2023; Ou et al. 2024; Pan et al. 2025; Chiti et al. 2026).
Similarly, wide-field photometric metallicities have been used
to confirm the existence of faint stellar streams in the Milky
Way halo through their coherent metallicities (Martin et al.
2022a), including one with a metallicity below the apparent
floor for globular clusters (GCs; Martin et al. 2022b), in addi-
tion to faint structures in the vicinity of GCs (e.g., Kuzma et al.
2025). Large datasets of metallicities within dwarf galaxies
can also be used to trace their star formation histories and
evolution (e.g., Tolstoy et al. 2004; Kirby et al. 2011; Fu et al.
2023, 2024; Barbosa et al. 2025; Hong et al. 2025); simi-
larly, metallicity distribution measurements across the Milky
Way halo can uncover its assembly history (e.g., Deason et al.
2023), constrain the properties of Population III star enrich-
ment (e.g., Hartwig et al. 2022a), and study the formation of
the inner galaxy (e.g., Rix et al. 2022). Such datasets can
also enable comparative studies of the enrichment of satellites
associated with the LMC (e.g., Patel et al. 2020; Pace et al.
2022), which may have formed in a distinct early environment
from Milky Way satellite galaxies.
These datasets also enable the discovery of rare objects
(e.g., low-metallicity stars) across environments (i.e., dwarf
galaxies, streams) with well-described photometric selection
functions as targets for current and next-generation multi-
plexed surveys (4MOST, Subaru-PFS, DESI, WEA VE, Spec-
S5; de Jong et al. 2012; Tamura et al. 2016; DESI Collabora-
tion et al. 2022; Jin et al. 2024; Besuner et al. 2025). These
facets motivate a wide-field, photometric survey that provides
metallicities into the extremely low metallicity regime (i.e.,
[Fe/H]≲−3.0) reaching depths (𝑔≈20.5) complementing

2.1 Scientific aims
(1) Discovery and follow-up of second-generation stars:
Stars with [Fe/H]<−4.0 (and plausibly some with [Fe/H]
<−3.0) have likely been enriched by a single generation of
supernovae, meaning that they preserve direct signatures from
Population III stars (Frebel & Norris 2015). However, ex-
isting data is sparse due to the rarity of the most metal-poor
stars; to date, only∼50 stars are known with [Fe/H]<−4.0
(e.g., Sestito et al. 2019). Nonetheless, we see hints of crit-
ical insights from this small sample; for instance, nearly all
stars in this regime (∼80%) are enhanced in carbon, and very
few are deficient in [Mg/Ca] (Suda et al. 2008; Placco et al.
2014; Arentsen et al. 2022). This can affect the shape of the
initial mass function (via cooling mechanisms) of the second
and subsequent generations of stars, and the mass distribution
of Population III stars themselves (e.g., Chiaki et al. 2017).
While observations of the MDF of the Milky Way halo have
conflicting trends at the extremely metal-poor regime (e.g.,
Sch¨orck et al. 2009; Yong et al. 2013; Da Costa et al. 2019;

Youakim et al. 2020; Chiti et al. 2021b), Roederer et al. (2019)
presented a calculation assuming the MDF follows the power
law presented in Sch ¨orck et al. (2009), indicating a rate of 1
star with [Fe/H]<−4.0 per∼100 deg 2 down to a brightness of
𝑉=18. Nominally, this calculation indicates that the MAGIC
footprint may enclose∼50 stars above that brightness limit
with [Fe/H]<−4.0, suitable for detailed chemical abundance
studies with high-resolution spectroscopy. Based on current
compilations (e.g., Sestito et al. 2019),∼10 known UMP stars
lie in the MAGIC survey footprint.
The MAGIC footprint will also provide meaningful in-
sights on how the above properties vary across early environ-
ments, including in the MCs and other satellites of the Milky
Way. Only a handful of stars in the LMC with [Fe/H]<−2.5
have recently had detailed chemical abundances published (Oh
et al. 2024a; Chiti et al. 2024; Ji et al. 2025). Given that the
LMC fell into the Milky Way only∼2 Gyr ago (e.g., Besla et al.
2007; Kallivayalil et al. 2013; Garavito-Camargo et al. 2019;
Conroy et al. 2021; although see Vasiliev 2024), its lowest
metallicity stars carry the signatures of the first supernova in
a formerly different region of the universe (Chiti et al. 2024).
This makes it an ideal test for variations in the properties of the
first stars across environments, and the universality of early en-
richment. Initial results from MAGIC have already provided
a detailed chemical abundance analysis of a star with [Fe/H]
<−4.0 in the distant Milky Way halo (Placco et al. 2025), and
have presented the detection and analysis of the first known
star in this regime (with [Fe/H]<−4.6) in an ultra-faint dwarf
galaxy (Chiti et al. 2026).
(2) The MDF of the Milky Way and its satellites:Recent
models of the Milky Way MDF show that the low-metallicity
tail ([Fe/H]≲−3.0) is sensitive to parameters such as the initial
mass function of Population III stars, early star-formation ef-
ficiency, and early feedback prescriptions (Tarumi et al. 2020;
Komiya et al. 2020; Hartwig et al. 2022b). The DECam
MAGIC survey will allow us to derive metallicities forO(103)
stars with [Fe/H]<−3.0. These measurements will form
model constraints on the initial mass function of the first stars
(e.g., whether the maximum possible mass of Population III
stars includes or excludes pair instability supernovae; Tarumi
et al. 2020), the star formation efficiency of Population III
star-forming minihalos (de Bennassuti et al. 2017), and the
stochasticity of feedback from Population III stars (Hartwig
et al. 2022b). The MAGIC dataset will also strongly constrain
the overall MDF, which is sensitive to the assembly history of
the Milky Way (Deason et al. 2023).
Apart from the detailed MDFs extending to [Fe/H]≲−3.0,
the new photometric metallicities will also allow homoge-
neous analyses of metallicity gradients and other complex
chemo-dynamics (e.g., asymmetric gradients, signatures of
mergers) inside dwarf galaxies within the footprint of the sur-

(3) Detecting low-metallicity substructures in the Milky
Way and MCs:The Milky Way’s low-metallicity substruc-
tures (e.g., accreted dwarf galaxies, stellar streams) and the
complex substructures in the MCs link to several fundamental
questions, including the nature of dark matter and the assem-
bly history of our Galaxy. The dozens of metal-poor stellar
streams in the Milky Way halo provide a test forΛCDM, as
they are sensitive to perturbations from its predicted popula-
tion of low-mass (Mvir <10 8 M⊙) dark matter halos (Johnston
et al. 2002; Ibata et al. 2002; Erkal et al. 2016) and also trace
the mass distribution of the Galaxy (Johnston et al. 1999;
Ibata et al. 2001; Koposov et al. 2023). Substructure in the
periphery of the MCs probes the dynamical history of these
satellite galaxies, which in turn influence the Milky Way halo
through their induced dark matter wake (Garavito-Camargo
et al. 2021; Dillamore et al. 2026). The discovery of such
streams and substructure is still ongoing, accelerated byGaia
astrometry (Malhan & Ibata 2018; Martin et al. 2022a; Ibata
et al. 2024).
Stellar streams and substructures are nearly all at low-
metallicities (⟨[Fe/H]⟩<−1.0)and are obscured by metal-
rich Milky Way foreground stars, meaning that the CaHK
filter presents a unique opportunity for significant foreground
removal in substructure searches to access this low surface
brightness regime. For instance, the Pristine survey has con-
firmed the existence of many stellar streams through the coher-
ent metallicities of their stars, in addition to detecting several
more candidate streams (Martin et al. 2022a). MAGIC will
enable such studies over a contiguous footprint in the southern
hemisphere with deeper narrow-band photometry (see Sec-
tion 2.3), in a particularly promising region for faint substruc-
ture searches due to a high density of known dwarf galaxies
and several stellar streams. This complements existing DES
stream searches at low surface brightnesses that span part
of our footprint (Shipp et al. 2018). Early results from the
program have already detected the presence of faint (∼35–
36 mag arcsec−2) tidal tails around the Crater II dwarf galaxy
(Atzberger et al. 2026).
The MAGIC survey also has the potential to provide the
largest and deepest metallicity-resolved map of the outer halo
to∼200 kpc. Metallicities are the dominant uncertainty in
determining isochrone distances to outer halo giants (Conroy
et al. 2021), and including metallicity information from the sur-
vey can reduce the distance uncertainty from∼50% to∼10%
at 100 kpc and∼30% at 200 kpc. This will enable precise
measurements of the outer halo’s stellar density profile, and
given our survey’s wide coverage, clues about its shape and
dynamical response to perturbations from LMC infall (Conroy
et al. 2021; Garavito-Camargo et al. 2021).
(4) Probing extended features around faint Milky Way
satellites, star clusters, and stellar streams:The Milky
Way’s UFDs are analogs of some of the earliest systems that
formed in the universe (Simon 2019), being largely composed
of very metal-poor stars (⟨[Fe/H]⟩≲−2.0) and are the most
dark-matter dominated systems known (M/L>100 M ⊙/L⊙;
e.g., Simon & Geha 2007; Martin et al. 2008; Geha et al. 2026;
Geha 2026). The outskirts of UFDs are increasingly recog-
nized as promising areas to probe signatures of tidal disruption
(Li et al. 2018a), the possible extents of their dark matter halos
(Chiti et al. 2021c; Deason et al. 2022; Jensen et al. 2024), or
evidence of early galactic mergers (Tarumi et al. 2021; Querci
et al. 2025). This discovery space was previously obscured
due to the faintness of dwarf outskirts (∼33 mag arcsec−2; Rey
et al. 2019), but recent work has demonstrated that a combina-
tion ofGaiaproper motions and photometric metallicities can
uncover stars in these low surface brightness outskirts (e.g,
Chiti et al. 2021c; Longeard et al. 2022b, 2023; Pan et al.
2025) for targeted spectroscopic follow-up (e.g., Chiti et al.
2023; Waller et al. 2023; Sestito et al. 2023; Ou et al. 2024).
These approaches can also readily be extended to the Milky
Way’s GCs to uncover faint features in their outskirts (e.g.,
Kuzma et al. 2025). These regions have generated interest due
to searches for signatures of the tidal disruption of GCs (e.g.,
Rockosi et al. 2002; Sollima 2020; Bonaca et al. 2020; Zhang
et al. 2022; Chiti et al. 2025), associations with stellar streams
(e.g., Ibata et al. 2024), or the presence of diffuse outskirts that
may reflect their progenitor (Bellazzini et al. 2003; Carballo-
Bello et al. 2014; Kundu et al. 2022). The same sensitivity to
faint, low-metallicity features can be used to trace morpholog-
ical features in stellar streams. These include cocoons, gaps,
spurs, and off-track overdensities (e.g., Malhan et al. 2019;
Bonaca et al. 2019, 2020) that can trace heating by dark matter
subhalo impacts, dynamical interactions with nearby systems,
or other components of the Milky Way (e.g., Ibata et al. 2002;
Johnston et al. 2002; Erkal et al. 2016; Pearson et al. 2017;
Erkal et al. 2019; Koposov et al. 2019; Li et al. 2021b; Ni-
bauer et al. 2024). Photometric metallicities can be used to
both detect these features by removing foreground metal-rich
stars, and also confirm features by matching their metallicities
to those of a host stream.

The DECam MAGIC survey utilizes the∼3 deg 2 field-of-

large and high quality astronomical filters. The CaHK filter

2017). The as-built filter has a central wavelength (CWL)

3 DERIVING PHOTOMETRIC METALLICITIES,
DISTANCES & VALIDATION
In this section, we describe our methods for deriving pho-
tometric metallicities from the source catalogs described in
Section 2.3. Briefly, we derive metallicities by comparing our
observed CaHK photometry, along with broadband DECam𝑔
and𝑖photometry, to a grid of forward-modeled synthetic pho-
tometry that predicts the magnitudes that stars ought to have
across a range of stellar parameters (𝑇eff, log𝑔, [Fe/H]). This
grid allows a mapping between observed colors and metallic-
ity, especially in color-color spaces with metallicity-sensitive
magnitudes (i.e., CaHK; see Figure 4). Our particular imple-
mentation of this approach largely follows Chiti et al. (2020,
2021a), which applied this technique on SkyMapper photom-
etry (Keller et al. 2007; Onken et al. 2019). Furthermore,
the analysis described here has already been implemented and
tested in Barbosa et al. (2025), which provided metallicities
for∼3800 stars in the Sculptor dSph, and used in target se-
lection for various published spectroscopic follow-up studies
from MAGIC imaging (Placco et al. 2025; Chiti et al. 2026).
We describe the process of generating the grid of syn-
thetic photometry and deriving photometric metallicities in
Section 3.1 and Section 3.2. When deriving metallicities from
this approach, we have to assume a surface gravity (log𝑔) for
each star. We discuss how log𝑔is inferred, and outline how
these are mapped to photometric distances in Section 3.3. In
Section 3.4, we discuss how we flag common failure modes
and contaminants in the photometric metallicity catalog. We
validate our methods in Section 3.5.

3.1 Grid of synthetic photometry
In order to generate our grid of synthetic photometry,
we first create a grid of flux-calibrated spectra using the
Turbospectrum radiative transfer code 13 (Alvarez & Plez
1998; Plez 2012), along with MARCS model atmospheres 14
(Gustafsson et al. 2008), and a linelist from the Vienna Atomic
Line Database15 (V ALD; Piskunov et al. 1995; Ryabchikova
et al. 2015). Specifically, we use standard composition
MARCS models, which assume [𝛼/Fe]=0.4 when [Fe/H]
<−1.0 and linearly declines to [𝛼/Fe] = 0 at [Fe/H] = 0. We
adopt spherical MARCS models when log𝑔≤3.5 dex, and
otherwise use plane-parallel models to generate 4490 synthetic
spectra spanning log𝑔=−0.5 to log𝑔=+5.0,𝑇 eff =2500 K
to𝑇 eff =8000 K, [Fe/H] =−5.0 to [Fe/H] =+1.0. We note
that these spectra were generated under the assumption of 1D
local thermodynamic equilibrium, with a microturbulence set
to 2 km s−1 for all spectra. Since the purpose of this grid is
to forward-model photometry and not perform detailed mod-
eling of line profiles, we opt for a fixed microturbulence to
streamline analysis.
From this grid of synthetic spectra, we derive predicted
photometry in the CaHK filter and DECam𝑔, 𝑟, 𝑖filters using
the formalism described in Casagrande & VandenBerg (2014),
following the implementation in Chiti et al. (2020). We down-
load the system throughput for the DECam𝑔, 𝑟,and𝑖band
photometry from the NOIRLab website. For the CaHK filter,
we assume the tophat function for the filter used in the Pris-
tine survey (Starkenburg et al. 2017), which closely approxi-
mates the as-built DECam filter profile (Section 2.2) and is the
bandpass to which our photometric zeropoints are calibrated
in Section 2.3. We apply an empirical offset of +0.12 mag

3.2 Deriving photometric metallicities
We derive photometric metallicities using the aforemen-
tioned grid of synthetic photometry, largely following Chiti
et al. (2020, 2021a). Aspects of our methodology are also
briefly described in Barbosa et al. (2025), which presents a
pilot MAGIC study of the Sculptor dSph. As noted, Figure 4
shows metallicity-sensitive color-color plots that are populated

start by using thescipy.interpolate.griddatapython

the location of stars in CaHK−𝑔−0.9× (𝑔−𝑖)vs.(𝑔−𝑖)
color-color space to metallicity. These interpolation functions
are generated separately for each log𝑔, due to the visible shifts
in color-color space as log𝑔varies (see Figure 4).

licity in this framework is an estimate of the log𝑔for each
star. Given that the focus of MAGIC is to investigate the an-

approximate log𝑔by matching the observed(𝑔−𝑖) 0 color
of each star to theoretical 12 Gyr isochrones from the Dart-

branch stars and the necessity of time-domain information for

much more complex.
For each star, we compute metallicities for each of the
MS and RGB assumptions across all four isochrones, yielding
eight initial metallicity estimates. We then average the RGB-
based metallicities and the MS-based metallicities separately.
To select the most appropriate isochrone for the final metal-
licity determination, we identify which reference isochrone
has a metallicity closest to the averaged value for each evo-
lutionary state. The metallicity derived using the log𝑔from
that isochrone is adopted as the final metallicity for the MS
and RGB cases. In current processing, we exclude log𝑔val-
ues from the [Fe/H] =−1.0 isochrone and instead adopt those
from the [Fe/H] =−1.5 isochrone for the metal-rich end, as
the inclusion of the former introduces a metallicity system-
atic relative to APOGEE (see Section 3.5). This iterative
approach accounts for the coupled dependence of metallic-
ity and log𝑔, i.e. that the isochrone-derived log𝑔depends
on assumed metallicity, and the synthetic-grid metallicity de-
pends on assumed log𝑔. The outcome of this procedure is
two metallicity estimates per star: one assuming the star is
on the RGB ([Fe/H] RGB), and one assuming it is on the MS
([Fe/H]MS). We describe how we discriminate between these
two scenarios in the next subsection.
Uncertainties on the photometric metallicities are derived
by propagating the photometric uncertainties in CaHK,𝑔, and
𝑖-band magnitudes. We recompute the metallicity after vary-
ing each input magnitude by its 1𝜎uncertainty in the photo-
metric catalog, and then derive a total random uncertainty by
summing in quadrature the three resulting shifts in [Fe/H]. We
then add a systematic uncertainty floor of 0.16 dex in quadra-
ture to account for e.g., imperfections in the synthetic grid,
following Chiti et al. (2020). This metallicity uncertainty
floor also produces errors that are consistent with spectro-
scopic studies of red giant stars in the Sculptor dSph (Barbosa
et al. 2025).

3.3 Deriving photometric distances
The metallicity derivation described in the previous sub-
section yields two estimates per star ([Fe/H] RGB, [Fe/H]MS).
For a subset of these stars, we can determine their evolution-
ary state using the fact that the isochrone-matching procedure
implicitly provides a distance estimate under each assumption
(i.e.,𝑑 RGB,𝑑 MS). Specifically, matching the(𝑔−𝑖) 0 color of
a star to an isochrone yields an inferred absolute magnitude
for the star, from which a distance modulus can be derived.
Since RGB stars are intrinsically more luminous than MS stars,
the inferred𝑑 RGB is typically far larger than𝑑 MS for a star at
a given apparent magnitude. Accordingly, the distances of
these stars that are inferred from all-skyGaiaDR3 parallax
measurements (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023a) can be used to
discriminate between the RGB and MS scenarios for a subset
of sources when𝑔≲19.5.
In Figure 5, we plot a 2D histogram of apparent DECam𝑔
magnitude vs.GaiaDR3 parallax for sources in our catalog,
where each pixel is colored by the median significance of the
parallax measurement (i.e.,parallax/parallax error).
The cyan dashed line indicates the contour that traces the
pixels at S/N = 3, showing the typical distance out to which
GaiaDR3 detects 3𝜎parallaxes as a function of appar-
ent magnitude𝑔. For stars with resolved parallaxes (i.e.,
parallax/parallax error>3), we convert theGaiaDR3
parallax to a distance (𝑑 Gaia,kpc = 1/parallax) and compare
this to the photometric distances inferred under each assump-
tion. The star is classified as MS or RGB based on the photo-
metric distance that is most consistent with the parallax-based
distance.
If theGaiaDR3 parallax is unresolved (i.e., S/N<3), we
can still discriminate distant RGB stars from MS stars via two
methods. First, if𝑑 MS is to the left of the curve in Figure 5 and
the parallax is unresolved, then the star is classified as a distant
RGB star since a parallax ought to have been detected if it was
on the MS. Second, if the inferred tangential velocity of the
star based on the RGB distance andGaiaDR3 proper motion
is>600 km s −1, then the star is classified as an MS star, due
to this being larger than the escape velocity of the Milky Way,
even in the solar neighborhood (Koppelman & Helmi 2021).
In all other cases, the star is classified as having an ambiguous
evolutionary state.

Extragalactic contamination– Unresolved galaxies and
active galactic nuclei (AGN) can contaminate our catalog and
will have spurious metallicities, since the grid for inferring
metallicities is derived from synthetic stellar photometry. We
first exclude likely galaxies from having metallicities in the
catalog by requiringextended class g<2 in DELVE DR2
(Drlica-Wagner et al. 2022) before any metallicity derivation.
This flag is a purely morphological selection from broadband
DECam𝑔-band images, and ought to reliably flag extended
sources in the magnitude ranges for which we derive metallic-
ities (𝑔≲21). We supplement this with a broadband color-
color selection in(𝑔−𝑟) 0 vs.(𝑟−𝑖) 0 space, where RGB
and MS stars trace a tight relation and galaxies can occupy a
broader locus from the stellar sequence (see Figure 6). The
blue cutoff in this selection is motivated by the blue end of
the horizontal branch in old, metal-poor Milky Way satellites
(Cerny et al. 2023).
Variable sources– Time-varying sources will have spu-
rious photometric metallicities in the MAGIC catalog, as
the CaHK imaging will almost certainly be sampled at dis-
crepant phases from the broadband𝑔, 𝑖photometry. We flag
known variable sources in our catalog via cross-match with
theGaiaDR3 variable star catalog (Eyer et al. 2023; Rimol-
dini et al. 2023). Before any spectroscopic follow-up, we also
cross-match candidate lists with the SIMBAD astronomical
database (Wenger et al. 2000), and exclude sources flagged as
variable sources (e.g., eclipsing binaries, Cepheids, RR Lyrae,
AGN, quasars). Additionally, the broadband color-color selec-
tion can exclude variable sources since the broadband𝑔, 𝑟, 𝑖
imaging in DELVE DR2 are typically obtained over multi-
ple epochs. Any significant variability between these epochs
causes stars to have inferred broadband colors that lie outside
the locus in Figure 6, providing an independent check against
variable sources. We note that upcoming time-domain data
from the Vera Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space
and Time (LSST; Ivezi´c et al. 2019) will likely minimize this
failure mode.
Dwarf/giant misclassification– As described in Sec-
tion 3.3, our classification of stars as being on the MS or
RGB relies onGaiaDR3 parallax constraints, which have
limited utility when𝑔≳19.5. Figure 7 illustrates the im-
pact of dwarf/giant misclassification on the inferred metallicity
as a function of color and metallicity. The misclassification
of sources can significantly impact the metallicity estimate,
especially for cool stars (e.g.,(𝑔−𝑖) 0 >0.9) and at low-
metallicities. In the MAGIC catalog, we flag the adopted
classification for each source (RGB, MS, Ambiguous), and
propagate [Fe/H]RGB,MS,𝑑 RGB,MS to the final [Fe/H] or𝑑if
theGaiaparallax permits a classification. Stars without a
clear classification are reported with two metallicity and dis-
tance estimates, corresponding to an RGB or MS status. We
note for completeness that stars not in these evolutionary states
(e.g., horizontal branch stars, blue stragglers) may contami-
nate a subset of the bluest stellar sources in the dataset. The
most extreme of these fail the broadband color-color flag (i.e.,
Figure 6), and are likely to be flagged due to having unphysical
photometric metallicities, as our grid of synthetic photometry
is not optimized for these stars.
High-reddening regions– Regions of high reddening are
known to potentially lead to spurious photometric metallicity
estimates. This is due to several reasons, including the pres-
ence of young stars with CaiiH&K emission features and the
fact that the foreground line-of-sight reddening for each source
is required for accurate de-reddening, as opposed to the treat-
ment in Section 2.3. The net effect of the latter is to artificially
decrease the photometric metallicity estimate of sources in
high-reddening regions. Consequently, we conservatively ex-
clude regions of high reddening (𝐸(𝐵−𝑉) SFD98 >0.2 in
Schlegel et al. 1998) in our analyses at this time, and prior-
itize low-metallicity sources in low reddening regions when
performing spectroscopic follow-up. In addition, young or
active stellar sources with emission features over the CaHK
band will likely contaminate any photometric metallicities in

Photometric quality flags– In addition to the above, we

No extended sources (via DELVE DR2𝑔-band morphology;

4 EARLY SCIENCE RESULTS
In this section, we overview results from the MAGIC sur-
vey that are already in the literature (Barbosa et al. 2025;
Placco et al. 2025; Chiti et al. 2026; Atzberger et al. 2026; Do
et al. 2026), demonstrating the feasibility of our scientific aims
(Section 4.1). In addition, we present several initial results that
touch on future prospects (Section 4.2– 4.4).
4.1 Summary of public results
At the time of this writing, a total of seven publications
have been submitted or published using data collected through
MAGIC or MAGIC-affiliated P.I. programs processed with the
MAGIC pipeline (see Section 2.2). Five of these publications
used these data in a primary capacity, and we summarize
those works below. The two additional publications (Pace
et al. 2025; Tan et al. 2025) used MAGIC data in a limited
capacity to cross-verify the membership of stars in UFDs.
Barbosa et al. (2025) presented the largest sample of
members for the Sculptor dSph with measured metallici-
ties (𝑁≈3800). This sample allowed an external cross-
validation of MAGIC photometric metallicity estimates with
the∼1000 available spectroscopic metallicities (Tolstoy et al.
2023) within the system. Our sample improved the determi-
nation of the MDF and metallicity gradient of Sculptor, due to
the depth and spatially unbiased coverage of the survey around
the dwarf galaxy. We measured a broken metallicity profile,
with a steep gradient within∼1𝑟 ℎ (∼11.17 ′; Mu ˜noz et al.
2018) of−3.26±0.18 dex deg −1, and−0.55±0.26 dex deg −1
beyond the break radius. Moreover, we detected six new ex-
tremely metal-poor candidates, demonstrating the utility of the
MAGIC survey to target the most metal-poor stars in Milky
Way satellites for future programs.
Placco et al. (2025) presented high-resolution (𝑅 >
20,000) spectroscopic observations for six metal-poor stars
with [Fe/H]<−3.0 (including one carbon-enhanced star
with [Fe/H]=−4.12), selected from MAGIC. We con-
firmed the accuracy of the photometric metallicities (median
Δ[Fe/H]=−0.05) and determined chemical abundances for
up to 16 elements per star, from carbon to barium. A kine-
matic/dynamical analysis indicated that all program stars be-
long to the distant Milky Way halo population (≳30 kpc),
including three stars with high-energy orbits that might have
been associated with the MCs, one consistent with being a
member of the Sagittarius stream (Ibata et al. 1994; Majewski
et al. 2003), and another two showing kinematics consistent
with the Gaia-Sausage/Enceladus dwarf galaxy merger. Our
detection of a new [Fe/H]<−4.0 star at a large distance
(≈35 kpc) in the Milky Way halo demonstrated the capacity
of the survey to recover the lowest metallicity stars in new
regimes.
Chiti et al. (2026) presented the discovery and initial chem-
ical characterization of the star with the lowest iron abundance
(a non-detection at [Fe/H]<−4.63) known in any UFD. The
star, designated PicII-503, is located in the outskirts of the Pic-
tor II UFD and was targeted through its extremely low metal-
licity in the MAGIC catalog ([Fe/H]RGB ≈ −3.8). Notably, the
only two elements with detected abundances in the star based
on𝑅≈10,000 VLT/X-Shooter (Vernet et al. 2011) spec-
troscopy were calcium ([Ca/H] =−5.2 +/−0.31/0.5) and car-
bon ([C/H] =−1.44±0.38). The extreme paucity of heavy ele-
ments (e.g., [Fe/H]<−4.63, [Ca/H]=−5.2, [Mg/H]<−4.42)
in PicII-503 shows it to clearly preserve enrichment by Popula-
tion III supernovae within a UFD, demonstrating that extreme
carbon enhancement is a pathway by which the first stars can
enrich primordial, small-scale structures. We note that the
Pictor II UFD is a satellite of the LMC (Pace et al. 2025).
Atzberger et al. (2026) used MAGIC metallicities to iden-
tify RGB stars in the Crater II dSph’s stellar stream. The
paper presents DECam CaHK photometry of 162 candidate
stars across 128 deg2, measuring the first significant metallic-
ity gradient of−0.34±0.17 dex deg −1 from 124 candidates in
the main body and validating𝑁-body tidal disruption model
predictions for a near and far aligned stream component using
37 candidates in the tidal tails. The star counts indicate a
scaled surface brightness of∼36 mag arcsec −2 in the stream
and a stream width of approximately 0.8 ◦ (∼1.63 kpc) that is
consistent with both cored and cuspy dark matter halo mod-
els. This advances studies of Crater II’s unusual properties,
showing how the MAGIC survey will enable unprecedented
sensitivity to faint, diffuse tidal features in metal-poor sys-

with those in spectroscopic studies of Crater II (Ji et al. 2021;
Limberg et al. 2025b).

further spectroscopic follow up.
4.2 An r-process enhanced star in the outskirts of Retic-
ulum II
Reticulum II is a UFD that is likely bound to the LMC
(e.g., Pace et al. 2022), and hosts stars that show a dramatic en-
hancement in elements produced by the rapid neutron-capture
process (i.e.,𝑟-process; Ji et al. 2016a; Roederer et al. 2016;
Ji et al. 2023a). The𝑟-process enhancement in stars in Retic-
ulum II matches the highest level known among stars in the
Milky Way halo ([Eu/Fe]>+1.0), known as𝑟-II stars (e.g.,
Beers & Christlieb 2005; Holmbeck et al. 2020). Conse-
quently, Reticulum II preserves enrichment from a single pro-
lific r-process production event from the early universe (Ji et al.
2016a; Brauer et al. 2021). This peculiar signature of Retic-
ulum II stars principally allows one to tag likely members of
Reticulum II in its far outskirts, since this level of𝑟-process
enhancement is uncommon among stars in the Milky Way
halo (8 % of stars with [Fe/H]<−2.0 in the halo are𝑟-II stars;
Ezzeddine et al. 2020; Holmbeck et al. 2020).
As discussed in Section 2.1, an open question is whether
UFDs host extended populations of stars that may indicate
their ongoing tidal disruption, or trace their underlying dark
matter halos. We performed a search for stars in the outskirts
of Reticulum II using MAGIC photometry (illustrated in Fig-
ure 11), following standard search sequences for metal-poor
stars in the outskirts of UFDs (e.g., Chiti et al. 2021c; Longeard
et al. 2023; Ou et al. 2024). First, we selected stars with
broadband DELVE DR2 photometry consistent with a 12 Gyr,
[Fe/H]=−2.5 Dartmouth isochrone (Dotter et al. 2008) placed
at the distance modulus of Reticulum II (𝑚−𝑀=17.5;
Mutlu-Pakdil et al. 2018). Then, we selected stars with
GaiaDR3 proper motions consistent with that of the sys-
temic motion of the system (𝜇𝛼 cos𝛿=2.377±0.24 mas yr −1,
𝜇 𝛿 =−1.379±0.25 mas yr −1; Pace et al. 2022) at the 3𝜎
level, based on the proper motion uncertainties on individual
stars. Finally, we selected stars that passed various metallicity
thresholds in the MAGIC catalog, assuming RGB values (see
top left of Figure 11). Only three stars appear in the outskirts
(>5𝑟 ℎ) of Reticulum II out to∼2 ◦ that pass the above selec-
tion with MAGIC [Fe/H]RGB <−2.5. This cut represents the
most stringent (i.e., purest) threshold for candidate selection;
relaxing to [Fe/H] RGB <−2.0 and [Fe/H] RGB <−1.5 yield
additional candidates (Figure 11).
We obtained spectroscopy of nine stars in the outskirts of
Reticulum II at a range of metallicities withGaiaDR3 proper
motions consistent with membership using the MagE spec-
trograph (Marshall et al. 2008) on the 6.5 m Magellan/Baade
Telescope at Las Campanas Observatory on 2023 December
6–8, using the 0.′′7 slit (yielding𝑅≈6000), with two can-
didates observed on 2023 October 14. We derived velocities
for these stars by cross-correlating the observed stars with
a template MagE spectrum of HD122563, exactly following
the procedure in Chiti et al. (2026). All but three of the ob-
served candidates are excluded as candidate members based
on their heliocentric radial velocities, given the velocities of
Reticulum II’s member stars (≈63 km s−1; Simon et al. 2015;
Walker et al. 2015). We note that most of our MagE targets
were observed for completeness in the near-vicinity of Retic-
ulum II, and had metallicities near the metal-rich end of our
candidate selection (i.e.,−2.5<[Fe/H] RGB ≲−1.5). Initial
targeting was also based on preliminary metallicities from the
November 2023 version of the MAGIC catalog, which did
not have the full suite of quality-checks (e.g., stellar locus
selection) discussed in Section 3.4. In Table 1, we list the
coordinates, brightnesses, MagE exposure times, and derived
heliocentric radial velocities for stars in Figure 11.
We derive spectroscopic metallicities for stars in Table 1
using the strength of the CaiiK line, following the metallicity
calibration described in Beers et al. (1999). This calibration
involves mapping a measure of the strength of the CaiiK
line known as the KP index, along with the𝐵−𝑉color, to
a metallicity. Briefly, the KP index measures the pseudo-
equivalent width of the CaiiK line by directly integrating the
flux across the feature, using a linear interpolation through
continuum bands to estimate the background flux. Our imple-
mentation of this method exactly follows Chiti et al. (2018b).
The𝐵−𝑉color was derived by matching DELVE DR2(𝑔−𝑖) 0
colors to theoretically predicted𝐵−𝑉colors using 12 Gyr,
[Fe/H] =−1.5,−2.0,−2.5 Dartmouth isochrones, based on
the MAGIC photometric metallicity of the star (Dotter et al.
2008). As shown in Table 1, the resulting KP index-based
metallicities are consistent with photometric metallicities in

We present a detection of a high [Ba/Fe] = 1.24±0.28,

members versus Milky Way halo stars at the same metallicity
([Ba/Fe]≈1.0 vs. [Ba/Fe]≲0.0; see Figure 3 in Ji et al.

tracer element europium at the [Eu/Fe]≈1.95 level, inde-

this star is likely not from𝑠-process production from a binary
companion (e.g., Ryan et al. 2005), and rather originates from
an𝑟-process signature. This𝑟-process enhancement provides
chemical evidence that RetII-409 is associated with Reticulum
II, and shows that the galaxy hosts a spatially extended popu-
lation. RetII-409 lies at∼8𝑟 ℎ and is along the elongation of
Reticulum II, which aligns with its predicted tidal debris track
if the UFD were losing mass due to tidal forces from the Milky
Way accounting for the LMC (D. Erkal et al., priv. comm.).
The same debris track also predicts a several km s −1 drift
upward in the systemic radial velocity of Reticulum II mem-
bers in the location of RetII-409, consistent with the observed
higher radial velocity than the central members. We identify
another candidate with MAGIC [Fe/H]RGB <−2.5 along this
same elongation (see Figure 11), but the star was marginally
too faint (𝑔=20.49) for rapid spectroscopy with MagE. The
adopted stellar parameters and element abundances of RetII-
409 are shown in Table 2. Of the other velocity-consistent
candidates, RetII-406 is too metal-rich ([Fe/H] =−1.62±0.36)
for its [Eu/H] to act as a discriminant relative to Milky Way
halo stars (see Figure 2 in Ji et al. 2016a), and RetII-502
has a spectrum with insufficient S/N for chemical abundance
analysis. Accordingly, we list their membership status as just
RV-consistent.
A demonstration that Reticulum II is influenced by Milky
Way tidal forces is not necessarily surprising, given that it
is likely at orbital pericenter (Li et al. 2021a; Hammer et al.
2021; Pace et al. 2022). However, as a likely satellite of the
LMC, Reticulum II has not been subject to the tidal field of the
Milky Way for long and its pericenter is sufficiently far from
the center of the Milky Way (∼37 kpc) that it has been argued
to have not lost significant mass from tidal stripping from our
Galaxy (e.g., Pace et al. 2022). Our observation establishes
that photometric metallicities can be used to uncover stars in
the faint outskirts of Reticulum II (∼1 RGB star from𝑟≈0.5 ◦
to𝑟≈1 ◦) to test these scenarios via targeted spectroscopy.
The additional detection of stars in the outskirts of Reticu-
lum II ought to be able to disentangle the mechanism by which
its faint outskirts formed. Since most UFDs do not show𝑟-
process enhancement (Ji et al. 2019), theories proposing the
formation of UFD stellar halos from merging UFDs would
predict that the stars in the outskirts of Reticulum II ought not
be𝑟-process enhanced. Alternatively, if stars in the outskirts
of Reticulum II are𝑟-process enhanced, this would indicate
in-situ formation of stars and subsequent displacement to the
outskirts via other mechanisms (e.g., tidal disruption, binary
interactions). The identification and spectroscopic characteri-
zation of larger samples of stars in the outskirts of Reticulum II
are needed to test these hypotheses. Such samples will require
deeper proper motions thanGaiaDR3 for candidate selection,
and would also be facilitated by deeper metallicity-sensitive
photometry of the region.

4.4 Initial EMP star follow-up
As discussed in Section 2.1, a key scientific area for
MAGIC is the identification and follow-up of the lowest
metallicity stars across the Milky Way, MCs, and satellite
dwarf galaxies. Systematic spectroscopic campaigns target-
ing MAGIC metal-poor candidates are underway across mul-
tiple facilities, including Gemini/GMOS and Gemini/GHOST
(Stringfellow et al., in prep., Skeffington et al., in prep) and
Magellan/MagE and Magellan/MIKE (e.g., Placco et al. 2025;
Chiti et al. 2026, Prabhu et al., in prep). In this subsection,
we present results from our very initial follow-up of candidate
low-metallicity stars based on MAGIC metallicities from the
medium-resolution MagE spectrograph on the 6.5 m Magel-
lan/Baade Telescope obtained on 2023 December 6-8, with
the same observing setup that was used for the Reticulum II
result in Section 4.2.
A total of 44 sources were observed. These sources were
selected based on: (1) Their low MAGIC [Fe/H] in the version
of the catalog at the time of targeting (November 2023); (2)
their location in the vicinity of the Reticulum II, Carina, and
Sextans dwarf galaxies. In Figure 15, we plot the MagE spec-
troscopic [Fe/H] of these stars (derived following Section 4.2)
versus their current MAGIC [Fe/H], and include RetII-501
and RetII-502 in this plot (see Table 1). Stars that fail the
quality cuts described in Section 3.4 are shown as gray data-
points in this plot, almost exclusively due to their photometric
[Fe/H]MAGIC <−4.0, and were targeted to explore the pop-
ulation of stars in this regime of the MAGIC catalog. One
source in the Sextans field is not plotted, as its MagE spec-
trum indicated it to be a non-stellar source. We note that
the stars with the two lowest MagE-based metallicities in Fig-
ure 15 are recoveries of two known low-metallicity members
of the Sculptor dSph (Scl07-50 in Tafelmeyer et al. 2010, and
Scl031 11 in Starkenburg et al. 2013). Stars that were ob-
served with Magellan/MIKE in Placco et al. (2025) are shown
as magenta stars in Figure 15.
Overall, we find a reasonable recovery range of low-
metallicity stars when limiting our sample to the quality cuts in
Section 3.4 (notably, [Fe/H]MAGIC >−4.0). Of the 28 stars that
pass the quality cuts and are selected to have MAGIC [Fe/H]
<−2.5, 25 (∼89%) indeed have MagE spectroscopic [Fe/H]
<−2.5. Similarly, 13/22 stars (∼59%) that pass quality cuts
with MAGIC [Fe/H]<−3.0 have MagE spectroscopic [Fe/H]
<−3.0. When including stars with [Fe/H] MAGIC <−4.0 and
removing other quality flags, these fractions become 31/36
(∼86%) for [Fe/H]<−2.5, and 14/30 (∼47%) for [Fe/H]
<−3.0. Future work will constrain these fractions with larger
samples of low-metallicity stars, and explore the cause of the
population of outliers at [Fe/H] MAGIC <−4.0 in our catalog.
Potential explanations include second-order CaHK magnitude
zeropoint corrections (e.g., that can be addressed with Uber-
Cal; Padmanabhan et al. 2008) or subtle imperfections in the
synthetic photometry, which may disproportionately impact
metallicity estimates in the most metal-poor regime.
5 CONCLUSION & PLANNED DATA RELEASES
We have presented an overview of the DECam Mapping the
Ancient Galaxy in CaHK (MAGIC) survey, a 54-night NOIR-
Lab Survey Program imaging≳5,000 deg 2 of the southern
hemisphere using a narrow-band filter covering the CaiiH&K
lines on the DECam/Blanco 4 m Telescope. We have shown
that by combining DECam CaHK photometry with broad-
band𝑔, 𝑟, 𝑖photometry from DELVE DR2 and astrometry
fromGaiaDR3, we can derive photometric metallicities for
stars across our data set, enabling studies of dwarf galaxies
(e.g., Barbosa et al. 2025; Chiti et al. 2026), low-metallicity
stars (e.g., Placco et al. 2025; Chiti et al. 2026), faint sub-
structures (e.g., Atzberger et al. 2026), stellar streams (Do
et al. 2026), and the Milky Way halo (see Section 4.3). In
Section 3.5, we present an initial validation of our metallici-
ties relative to those presented in APOGEE DR17 (Abdurro’uf
et al. 2022),GaiaXP spectrophotometric data (Andrae et al.
2023), Pristine DR1 (Martin et al. 2024), and several other
spectroscopic works (see Figure 10 caption). These com-
parisons show nominal scatters of∼0.3 dex down to [Fe/H]
≈ −3.0, with slightly improved precision when [Fe/H]<−2.0
(𝜎= 0.24 dex) and potential systematic effects relative to cer-
tain catalogs when [Fe/H]≳−1.0. Future work will improve
this performance through investigating second-order photo-
metric zero-point offsets across the dataset (e.g., Section 4.4),
and the performance at higher metallicities relative to spectro-
scopic metallicity measurements.
We summarize our primary scientific goals in Section 2.1,
which include the discovery of the lowest metallicity stars,
constraining the metallicity distribution of the Milky Way
and its satellites, detecting faint substructures in the Milky
Way halo, and probing the faint peripheries of dwarf galax-
ies. In Section 4, we show that early science results demon-
strate that the MAGIC dataset already enables key results
in each area. For instance, Barbosa et al. (2025) leveraged
MAGIC metallicities to construct the largest metallicity sam-
ple for the Sculptor dSph (∼3800 stars), revealing qualita-
tively new insights on its metallicity distribution and gradi-
ent. Placco et al. (2025) derived detailed chemical abun-
dances of six distant halo stars with [Fe/H]<−3.0 through
high-resolution spectroscopy, including a carbon-enhanced

The first public data release of the MAGIC catalog is antic-
ipated to occur in Fall 2026, and will include all data collected
within the first year of the survey (∼800 deg 2, through July
2024). The second data release will occur one year after
the survey’s completion (likely Fall 2028), and will include
the full footprint. These data releases will be hosted on the
NOIRLab’s Astro Data Lab (Fitzpatrick et al. 2014; Nikutta
et al. 2020), and will include CaHK photometry along with
value-added products such as photometric metallicities, pho-
tometric distances, and associated quality flags for a subset
of vetted stellar sources. The associated CaHK catalog for
the early MAGIC results on Sculptor, Crater II, and the Jet
stream are publicly available on Zenodo (Barbosa et al. 2025;
Atzberger et al. 2026; Do et al. 2026) 20. We also intend to
publish outcomes from ongoing spectroscopic campaigns with
Gemini/GMOS, Gemini/GHOST, Magellan/MagE, and Mag-
ellan/MIKE that are characterizing the low-metallicity tail of
the catalog, following on previously published results (Placco
et al. 2025; Chiti et al. 2026), and ongoing efforts in prepara-
tion (Stringfellow et al. in prep., Skeffington et al. in prep.,
Prabhu et al. in prep.).
We anticipate additional scientific utility from the MAGIC
dataset as it completes its proposed∼5,000 deg 2 coverage.
The MAGIC data releases will strongly complement deep
𝑢, 𝑔, 𝑟, 𝑖, 𝑧, 𝑌photometry from Rubin LSST (Ivezi ´c et al.
2019), deeper proper motions fromGaiaDR4 and on-
wards (Brown 2024), and ongoing and forthcoming mul-
tiplexed spectroscopic surveys (i.e., 4MOST, Subaru-PFS,
DESI, WEA VE, and Spec-S5; de Jong et al. 2012; Tamura
et al. 2016; DESI Collaboration et al. 2022; Jin et al. 2024;
Besuner et al. 2025). The discovery potential in the local
Universe remains rich, and the MAGIC survey provides a cru-
cial step in enabling the wide-field characterization of ancient,
faint stellar populations across the Milky Way and its satellite
galaxies.
