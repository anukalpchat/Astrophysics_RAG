Abstract
Galaxy rotation curves exhibit systematic deviations from the Newtonian ex-
pectation inferred from visible matter alone. Existing phenomenological descrip-
tions capture many aspects of these deviations, but a common residual structure
across massive disks and dwarf irregular galaxies remains unclear. We investi-
gate whether rotation-curve residuals organize into a simple empirical form across
the SPARC and LITTLE THINGS samples. We analyze 175 SPARC galaxies
and 22 LITTLE THINGS dwarf irregular galaxies in velocity-squared space after
subtracting a leading Newtonian-like term. We fit a generalized residual family,
v2 −A/r=B+Cr q+1, and examine which radial scaling is selected by the data.
The galaxy population systematically favors the limitq≃0, corresponding to an
approximately linear residual relation,v 2 −A/r=B+Cr. SPARC galaxies gener-
ally occupy a high-Bbranch, whereas LITTLE THINGS dwarf galaxies show sup-
pressed residual intercepts, including several systems consistent withB=0. For
the SPARC sample, the high-Bbranch approximately followsB∝M 0.72
bar . Rotation-
curve residuals are not featureless scatter beyond the leading Newtonian-like con-
tribution, but instead show a simple population-dependent empirical organization
across massive and dwarf galaxy systems.
1 Introduction
Galaxy rotation curves have long indicated systematic deviations from the Newtonian
expectation inferred from visible matter alone (Rubin et al. 1980; McGaugh et al.
2016).The approximately flat outer rotation curves observed in spiral galaxies mo-
tivated the introduction of dark matter halos (Navarro et al. 1996, 1997; Begeman
et al. 1991; de Blok et al. 2001; Bullock & Boylan-Kolchin 2017) as well as various
modified-gravity frameworks (Milgrom 1983; Sanders & McGaugh 2002; H.-N. Lin
& Chang 2013) designed to reproduce the observed dynamics without invoking non-
baryonic matter. Among these approaches, MOdified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND)-

like phenomenology (Milgrom 1983; Sanders & McGaugh 2002) has been particularly
successful in describing many massive disk galaxies and empirical scaling relations in

However, a universal empirical description applicable across different galaxy popu-
lations remains elusive. In particular, gas-rich dwarf irregular galaxies continue to rep-
resent a difficult regime for phenomenological models developed primarily for massive
spiral systems. Although MOND-like approaches successfully reproduce many rota-
tion curves, dwarf galaxies exhibit substantial diversity in their inner rotation structures
(Oman et al. 2015), and tensions remain in some MOND descriptions of dwarf-galaxy
cores (Sanchez Almeida et al. 2022). These results suggest that the residual dynamical
structure may not be fully captured by a single asymptotic prescription alone.
Rather than introducing a specific modified-gravity model from the outset, it is use-
ful to examine the residual structure itself after subtraction of the leading Newtonian-
like contribution. In velocity-squared space, we define the residual component as
v2 − A
r .
If galaxy rotation curves contain an underlying empirical organization beyond the
leading Newtonian-like contribution, such structure may become more directly visible
in the residual component.

2.2 Linear residual relations in SPARC and LITTLE THINGS
Theq=0 limit implies that the residual velocity-squared component should follow
an approximately linear relation in radius. Figure 2 shows representative examples of
the residual structure for both the 175 SPARC galaxies and the 22 LITTLE THINGS
dwarf galaxies. Each panel shows the residual component together with the best-fit
linear relation.
The panels are ordered from larger to smaller|B|. SPARC galaxies generally occupy
a high-Bbranch with clear positive residual offsets, whereas LITTLE THINGS dwarf
galaxies systematically show suppressed residual intercepts. Several dwarf systems are
consistent withB=0, indicating that the residual structure can become dominated by
the linear radial component in the low-mass regime.
Despite the large difference in residual-intercept scale between the two populations,
the residual component remains approximately linear in radius across both SPARC
and LITTLE THINGS galaxies. This result suggests that the residual velocity-squared
structure is not random scatter but instead follows a common empirical organization
over a broad range of galaxy masses.

3 Discussion
The present analysis differs from conventional rotation-curve modeling in several im-
portant aspects. In standard Newtonian mass modeling, the baryonic contribution is
typically inferred directly from luminosity-based mass decomposition. However, such
estimates can contain substantial systematic uncertainties, particularly in low-surface-
brightness and dwarf irregular systems. In the present work, the leading Newtonian-
like term was therefore treated phenomenologically through the free parameterA, al-
lowing the residual structure itself to be examined more directly from the observational
data. Importantly, the same Newtonian-like functional form was applied uniformly
across all galaxies, while only the fitted amplitude was allowed to vary between sys-
tems.
The present approach also differs conceptually from MOND-like frameworks. In
MOND phenomenology, modified acceleration is usually introduced through a non-
linear interpolation between Newtonian and low-acceleration regimes. In addition,
various discussions have been developed regarding the inner core structure of dwarf
galaxies within MOND-related frameworks. By contrast, the present analysis does not
introduce any explicit assumption regarding core structure, halo profile, or interpola-
tion function, but instead extracts the residual velocity-squared structure directly from
the observed rotation curves.
5

A qualitatively similar linear radial contribution has previously appeared in phe-
nomenological modified-gravity models containing additional constant-acceleration terms
(H.-N. Lin & Chang 2013). However, previous studies generally treated the baryonic
mass distribution directly from luminosity-based estimates, whereas the present analy-
sis allows the leading Newtonian-like component to vary phenomenologically through
the fitted parameterA. Despite this difference, the resultingC-term scale is broadly
consistent with previous constant-acceleration phenomenology.
The emergence of a linear residual structure is nontrivial, since neither the New-
tonian contribution nor generic asymptotic modified-gravity behavior requires such a
relation a priori. The present results therefore provide an empirical extension of ear-
lier phenomenological approaches by directly extracting the residual structure from the
SPARC and LITTLE THINGS galaxy populations.
In particular, the emergence of a suppressed-Bbranch in dwarf galaxies suggests
that the residual velocity-squared structure contains additional population-level orga-
nization beyond a single asymptotic scaling relation. Despite the large diversity previ-
ously reported in dwarf-galaxy rotation curves, the residual component systematically
approaches an approximately linear relation in radius across both SPARC and LITTLE
THINGS galaxies. This suggests that a substantial fraction of the observed dwarf-
galaxy diversity can be organized within a common empirical residual structure.
4 Conclusions
We investigated the residual structure of galaxy rotation curves using the SPARC sam-
ple of 175 galaxies and the LITTLE THINGS sample of 22 dwarf irregular galaxies.
Rather than introducing a specific modified-gravity prescription a priori, we analyzed
the residual velocity-squared component after subtraction of a leading Newtonian-like
term.
A generalized residual family was considered to explore the radial scaling behavior
of the residual component. The galaxy population systematically selected the limit
q≃0, indicating that the residual structure approaches an approximately linear relation
in radius in velocity-squared space,
v2 − A
r =B+Cr.(5)
This linear residual organization was observed across both SPARC and LITTLE
THINGS galaxies over a broad range of galaxy masses and morphologies. While
SPARC galaxies generally occupy a high-Bbranch, dwarf irregular galaxies systemat-
ically show suppressed residual intercepts, with several systems consistent withB=0.
The results suggest that galaxy rotation-curve residuals are not featureless scatter
beyond the leading Newtonian-like contribution, but instead follow a simple empirical
structure. The emergence of a common linear residual relation across both massive and
dwarf galaxies indicates the presence of a previously unrecognized population-level
organization in galaxy dynamics.
