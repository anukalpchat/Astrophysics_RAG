Abstract
Galaxy rotation curves have long indicated that the observed motions of stars
and gas cannot be fully explained by visible matter alone. Here we investigate the
residual component that remains after subtraction of the baryonic contribution.
Using galaxies from the SPARC and LITTLE THINGS surveys, we find that
the residuals are not randomly distributed but instead follow a common linear
pattern across a diverse galaxy population. The residual structure separates into
a mass-coupled component and a nearly mass-independent component. These
empirical regularities provide new constraints on theories of galaxy dynamics.
Galaxy rotation curves, which trace the orbital velocities of stars and gas as a function
of distance from the galactic centre, provide one of the most direct probes of the
distribution of matter in galaxies. Observations over the past decades have consistently
shown that the observed rotational velocities exceed those expected from the visible
baryonic components (stars and gas) alone [1].In many galaxies, particularly at large
radii, the discrepancy becomes comparable to or larger than the baryonic contribution
itself.
The physical origin of this residual component remains one of the central ques-
tions in astrophysics. Within the standard cosmological framework, it is commonly
attributed to dark matter halos surrounding galaxies [2, 3]. Alternative explana-
tions based on modifications of gravitational dynamics have also been extensively
explored [4, 5]. Despite their different physical interpretations, both approaches seek
to explain the same observational phenomenon: the residual component that remains
after accounting for baryonic matter.

Here we investigate the residual component of galaxy rotation curves using the
SPARC sample [6] and the LITTLE THINGS sample [7, 8]. Rather than interpret-
ing the residuals within a specific theoretical framework, we ask a simpler empirical
question: do galaxy rotation-curve residuals exhibit an underlying organizational
structure?
We find that the residuals are not randomly distributed. Instead, they exhibit a
remarkably simple and organized structure across a diverse population of galaxies
spanning a wide range of masses, morphologies, and dynamical properties. Population-
level analysis shows that the data favour a common linear residual pattern over
alternative models. Furthermore, the residual structure separates into two distinct
components: one that scales systematically with baryonic mass and another that
remains nearly independent of galaxy mass. These results reveal an unexpected
degree of organization in galaxy rotation-curve residuals and provide a new empirical
perspective on the relationship between baryonic matter and galaxy dynamics.

The emergence of the same residual structure across such diverse systems suggests

Figure 2 demonstrates that the linear residual structure is favoured across the full
SPARC and LITTLE THINGS samples. To quantify the agreement between mod-
els and observations, we use the reducedχ 2, a statistical measure of goodness-of-fit
for which values near unity generally indicate consistency with the data, whereas
substantially larger values indicate poor agreement.
Baryon-only models yield median reducedχ 2 values of approximately 55 in the
SPARC sample (Fig. 2a), indicating that the observed rotation curves cannot be
reproduced by the baryonic contribution alone. Including the residual component dra-
matically improves the agreement with the observations, reducing the median reduced
χ2 to approximately 0.4.
We next test whether the residual structure is genuinely linear by fitting the gen-
eralized modelR(r) =B+Cr q+1 while allowingqto vary between 0 and 1. For the
SPARC sample, the best overall agreement is obtained nearq= 0 (Fig. 2b), indicat-
ing that the data preferentially select a linear residual form. In the LITTLE THINGS
sample, the dependence of fit quality onqis weaker. Nevertheless, representative sys-
tems such as UGC 8508 (Fig. 1h) recover an approximately linear residual relation
whenq= 0 is adopted, consistent with the common residual structure observed across
the broader galaxy population.
Removing the linear residual term leads to a marked deterioration in fit qual-
ity (Fig. 2c), demonstrating that the improvement cannot be attributed simply to
additional free parameters. Consistently, the distribution of best-fit values is strongly
concentrated nearq≃0 (Fig. 2d), indicating that the linear residual structure emerges
directly from the observations rather than being imposed by the model.

The principal result of this work is that galaxy rotation-curve residuals are not ran-
domly distributed after subtraction of the baryonic contribution. Instead, the residuals
collapse onto a simple linear relation,
R(r) =B+Cr,(7)
across galaxies spanning a wide range of masses, morphologies, and rotation-curve
shapes. The emergence of the same residual law in systems with markedly different
rotation-curve morphologies suggests the presence of an underlying organizational
structure within galaxy dynamics. The preference for a linear residual form emerges
not only in individual galaxies but also at the population level in both the SPARC
and LITTLE THINGS samples. The appearance of the same functional form across

such diverse systems indicates that the residual component contains information that
is not captured by baryonic mass models alone.
A second notable result is the separation of the residual structure into two distinct
components. The coefficientBexhibits a clear dependence on baryonic mass and
separates into positive, negative, and near-zero branches, whereas the coefficientC
remains nearly independent of galaxy mass over the full sample. Empirically, the two
coefficients therefore display qualitatively different behaviour. This suggests that the
residual structure may not be described by a single scaling variable, but instead reflects
at least two distinct contributions with different population-level properties.

In contrast, the coefficientCexhibits little measurable dependence on bary-
onic mass. Within the uncertainties of the present sample, its scaling is statistically
consistent with zero. The coexistence of a mass-coupled component and a nearly mass-
independent component therefore represents a previously unrecognized aspect of the
residual structure. Regardless of physical interpretation, any successful description of
galaxy dynamics must account not only for the existence of the linear residual relation
itself but also for the markedly different population behaviour of its two coefficients.
The present analysis is intentionally empirical and does not assume a particular
theoretical framework. Rather than focusing on the observed rotation curves them-
selves, it isolates the residual structure that remains after subtraction of the baryonic
contribution. Consequently, the present work does not address whether the observed
residual relation is independent of previously reported galaxy scaling relations or rep-
resents an alternative projection of them. Nevertheless, the existence of a common
linear residual form provides a compact empirical description of galaxy rotation-curve
residuals and identifies new observational regularities that require explanation.
Future work will determine whether the linear residual structure extends beyond
galaxy rotation curves and whether the mass-coupled and nearly mass-independent
components identified here also appear in other gravitational observables. Establishing
such connections may clarify whether the residual relation represents a phenomenologi-
cal description of known galaxy scaling laws or points toward a previously unrecognized
level of organization in galaxy dynamics.

Methods
The SPARC sample consists of 175 late-type galaxies with high-quality rotation curves
and baryonic mass models derived from Spitzer photometry and HI observations [6].
The LITTLE THINGS sample consists of nearby dwarf irregular galaxies with resolved
HI rotation curves [7, 8]. Twenty-two galaxies satisfying the adopted data-quality and
radial-coverage criteria were included in the analysis.
For SPARC galaxies, the baryonic contribution was constructed using the fiducial
stellar mass-to-light ratios adopted by the SPARC survey, namely 0.5 and 0.7 for
stellar disks and bulges, respectively [6]. The model was written as
