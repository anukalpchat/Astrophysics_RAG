ABSTRACT
We present photometric and spectroscopic studies of a new WZ Sagittae (Sge)-type dwarf nova (DN)
KSP-OT-202104a discovered by the Korea Microlensing Telescope Network Supernova Program. The
source exhibits outburst amplitudes of∼8 mag with a duration of∼28.5 days in theV-band. It is
a type D DN among WZ Sge-types, and we estimate the superhump period to beP sh ≈71.7 minutes
(= 0.04978 days). Its spectrum shows blue continuum as often found in optically-thick accretion disks
of DNe during outbursts with hydrogen absorption lines from Hβto Hζ. Since the orbital period in
WZ Sge-type DNe is typically very close to the superhump period, we consider that this target would
belong to the small sample of DNe below the period minimum and may be evolving toward AM Canum
Venaticorum (AM CVn) stars. This system therefore adds an example of a short-period dwarf nova
with a low mass-transfer rate to the known sample.

1.INTRODUCTION
Dwarf novae (DNe) are binary stellar systems com-
posed of a white dwarf (WD) primary and a lower-mass
secondary companion, forming an important population
of cataclysmic variables (CVs). The Roche lobe-filling
secondary transfers mass to the primary through the
inner Lagrangian point to form an accretion disk that
leads to thermal instability-induced outbursts with am-
plitudes mostly in the range of 2–10 mag and durations
of days to weeks (J. K. Cannizzo & S. J. Kenyon 1987;
B. Warner 1995; Y. Osaki 1996; J. Patterson et al. 1996;
A. J. Drake et al. 2014). DNe have been classified into
three groups based on their outburst shapes: U Gem
(Geminorum)-type with repetition of similar outbursts,
SU UMa (Ursae Majoris)-type showing superoutbursts
and normal outbursts, and Z Cam (Camelopardalis)-

type displaying standstills with intermediate brightness
between the outburst peak and quiescent phase bright-
ness (B. Warner 1995; M. Otulakowska-Hypka et al.
2016). SU UMa-type DNe are further classified into
three sub-classes by mass transfer rate to ER UMa-,
SU UMa-, and WZ Sge-types in the decreasing order of
mass transfer rate (M. Otulakowska-Hypka et al. 2016).
ER UMa-type DNe have relatively short time intervals
between the outbursts (Y. Lee et al. 2024), while infre-
quent outbursts (∼once a decade) of WZ Sge-type DNe
show large outburst brightness changes of∼6–10 mag
(D. O’Donoghue et al. 1991; J. Patterson et al. 1993; Y.
Osaki 1995; T. Kato et al. 2001; T. Kato 2015; T. Kato
et al. 2017; Y. Tampo et al. 2023, 2025; Y. Tampo 2025;
Y. Tampo et al. 2026).
SU UMa-type DNe show periodic magnitude oscilla-
tions of up to∼0.2 mag during superoutbursts, which
are called “superhumps” considered to be from the pre-
cessing, tidally distorted accretion disk (R. Whitehurst
1988; M. Hirose & Y. Osaki 1990; T. Kato et al. 2009a;
T. Kato 2015). The periods of the superhumps (P sh)

As the mass transfer from the low mass secondary to
the primary in a DN continues, the angular momen-
tum, orbital period, and orbital separation decrease to
reach the “period minimum” of about 76 minutes (B.
Paczynski & R. Sienkiewicz 1981; U. Kolb & I. Baraffe
1999; J. R. Thorstensen et al. 2002; B. T. G¨ ansicke et al.
2009; C. Knigge et al. 2011; M. McAllister et al. 2019; D.
Mu˜ noz-Giraldo et al. 2024; V. Krushevska et al. 2024).
(Note that a broad range for the period minimum of
65−80 minutes have been adopted in literature.) The
mass loss and developed degeneracy in the secondary in-
duce it to become a BD-like object with mass <
∼ 0.07
M⊙ and to terminate its contraction (V. V. Neustroev
et al. 2017; T. Kato et al. 2017). The orbital period after
this begins to increase to conserve angular momentum,
and the DN become “period bouncers” as evolving back
towards a longer orbital period (A. R. King 1988; A. F.
Pala et al. 2018; D. Mu˜ noz-Giraldo et al. 2024; T. L.
Killestein et al. 2025). Consequently, DNe are believed
to evolve initially with a main sequence secondary as its
orbital period decreases and later to evolve with a de-
generate secondary as the orbital period increases (M.
Uemura et al. 2002)
Of particularly interest are those DNe with periods
smaller than the period minium. Only handful of these
objects have been identified (e.g., M. J. Green et al.
2020; Y. Lee et al. 2022), and their evolutionary paths
remain uncertain. Although at least part of them are
thought to evolve eventually to AM Canum Venatico-
rum (AM CVn) stars (ultracompact binaries with a WD
primary and a He star or He WD secondary having the
most shortest orbital period of<70 minutes; P. Podsi-
adlowski et al. 2003; G. Nelemans 2005; J. E. Solheim
2010; T. Kato & N. Kojiguchi 2021; A. Aungwerojwit
et al. 2025; N. Kojiguchi et al. 2026), whether or not
some of them evolve back to longer periods is poorly
understood. Also, whether their evolutions depend on
their DN types remains unknown. In our previous stud-
ies (Y. Lee et al. 2022, 2024), we report discoveries
of SU UMa-like (KSP-OT-201701a) and ER UMa-type
(KSP-OT-201712a) DNe with large mass transfers be-
low the period minimum that show transitional nature
with small He abundance. Here, we present the discov-
ery and photometric and spectroscopic studies of a new
WZ Sge-type DN KSP-OT-202104a under the period
minium discovered in the Korea Microlensing Telescope
Network (KMTNet) Supernova Program (D.-S. Moon
et al. 2016).
This paper is organized as follows. We describe our
observations and data reduction in§2 followed by pho-
tometric analysis of LCs, period analysis, color evolu-
tion, and spectroscopic results in§3. In§4, we discuss
the DNe with periods shorter than the period minimum
and their evolution. We summarize our results in§5.
2.OBSERVATIONS AND DISCOVERY
KSP-OT-202104a was discovered on 2021 April 9
(UT) by the KMTNet (S.-L. Kim et al. 2016) Super-
nova Program (KSP; D.-S. Moon et al. 2016) using
three wide-field 1.6-m telescopes in Chile, South Africa,
and Australia. KSP focuses on discovering early optical
transients such as infant supernovae (e.g., N. Afsariard-
chi et al. 2019; D.-S. Moon et al. 2021; Y. Q. Ni et al.
2022, 2023b,a, 2025; N. Jiang et al. 2025; E. Chang et al.
2026) at a high cadence in the JohnsonBV I-bands.
The program also provides opportunities of serendip-
itously discovering other types of transients such as
DNe (e.g., S. Brown et al. 2018; Y. Lee et al. 2019,
2022, 2024). The discovery of KSP-OT-202104a was
made by the Australian telescope of KMTNet at mag-

(123.58390◦, –26.04367 ◦) (J2000) 9, and it was subse-
quently detected by the other two telescopes. The source
was not detected on images obtained just previously by

Many WZ Sge-type DNe show a rapid decline in
brightness which is caused by the viscosity decrease in a
cold disk (Y. Osaki et al. 2001; T. Kato 2015) and show
rebrightening, or “echo outbursts”, later than one day
after the rapid decline (Y. Osaki & F. Meyer 2002; J.
Patterson et al. 2002). A. Imada et al. (2006) classified
WZ Sge-type DNe into five types based on rebrighten-
ing: (1) ‘A’ with a long-duration rebrightening; (2) ‘B’
with multiple discrete rebrightenings; (3) ‘C’ with a sin-
gle rebrightening; (4) ‘D’ with no rebrightening;, and (5)
‘E’ with double superoutbursts (T. Kato et al. 2009a; M.
Kimura et al. 2016). They are thought to represent dif-
ferent evolutionary stages of WZ Sge-type DNe as C→
D→A→B→E (T. Kato 2015). KSP-OT-202104a
appears to be type D given the absence of rebrightening
in its LC (see Figure 1).

3.2.Period Analysis
To obtain the superhump period of KSP-OT-202104a,
we use the phase dispersion minimization (PDM: R. F.
Stellingwerf 1978) method, which searches for optimum
periods from non-sinusoidal pulsation data (A. F. Lin-
nell Nemec & J. M. Nemec 1985; A. Schwarzenberg-
Czerny 1997; Y. Tampo et al. 2026). Considering the
sporadic observations of KSP-OT-202104a inadequate
for time-series analysis, we also conduct the Lomb-
Scargle (LS) analysis (N. R. Lomb 1976; J. D. Scargle
1982)12. To apply these two methods, 10th-degree poly-
nomial fits are subtracted from each of theB-,V-, and
I-band light curves of the source during the plateau part
(MJD = 59315–59337), and the resulting residual mag-
nitudes from the three bands are combined to improve
statistical reliability.
We obtain the periods from the PDM and LS methods
using the midterm period data of the plateau part emu-
lating it as stage B among the three evolutionary stages
of A, B, and C (T. Kato et al. 2009a; A. Tarasenkov
et al. 2025) with 1,000 bootstrap resampling. Since we
are not able to find clear boundaries of stage B from
our data, we select an optimum range in the middle of
the plateau part that is as narrow as possible while still
including sufficient data.

3.3.Color Evolution
Figure 6 shows the evolution of the (B−V), (V−I),

4.DISCUSSION
In general, the superhump period is∼1% longer than
the orbital period (‘positive superhumps’) in WZ Sge-
type DNe (Y. Osaki 2003; T. Kato et al. 2009b; A.
Tarasenkov et al. 2025); however, in rare cases, super-
hump period has been reported to be∼1% shorter than
the orbital period (‘negative superhumps’; J. Smak 2009;
M. A. Wood et al. 2009, 2011; Y. Osaki & T. Kato 2013b;
V. P. Kozhevnikov 2015; Q.-B. Sun et al. 2024a,b; E. P.
Pavlenko et al. 2025; D. Vallet et al. 2026).
Figure 8 shows the distribution of the orbital periods
and mass ratios of several WZ Sge-type DNe from T.
Kato (2015) and other DNe with short periods compared
with theoretical evolutionary tracks from C. Knigge
et al. (2011): orange dashed and green solid curves for
the standard and optimal tracks, respectively. Here, the
‘optimal’ evolutionary tracks are the improved version
of the standard model modified to match to the data,
especially below the period gap (C. Knigge et al. 2011).
As delineated by the evolutionary tracks, the orbital pe-
riods decrease down to the period minimum of∼76
minutes as a result of angular momentum loss of the
secondaries caused by continuous mass transfer. The
mass ratio also decreases along these processes. Near the
period minimum, the increasing electron degeneracy in
the secondary together with its decreasing density lead
to the transition from a main-sequence star to a brown
dwarf (U. Kolb & I. Baraffe 1999). During this transi-
tion, the orbital period starts to increase, known as the
“period bounce” (U. Kolb & I. Baraffe 1999; A. F. Pala
et al. 2020). As shown in Figure 8, the DNe EG Cnc (M.
Kimura et al. 2021), GOTO0650 (T. L. Killestein et al.
2025), and QZ Lib (A. F. Pala et al. 2018) are candi-
date “period bouncers” that recently passed the period
bounce (T. Kato et al. 2013; J. Patterson 1998; A. F.
Pala et al. 2020; V. V. Neustroev & I. M¨ antynen 2023).
In Figure 8, since the orbital period and the mass ra-
tio are not known for KSP-OT-202104a, we instead used
the superhump period (usually∼1% longer than the or-
bital period) and the mean and standard deviation val-
ues of the mass ratios known for the WZ Sge-type DNe
from T. Kato (2015). CVs with orbital periods shorter
than the period minimum, possibly including the DN
like KSP-OT-202104a, are believed to be composed of
a WD and a small-mass secondary star (M. J. Green
et al. 2020; Y. Lee et al. 2022). It is generally believed
that these small-mass secondaries have evolved to high-
density, He-rich stars via significant mass transfers by
which their atmospheres are stripped away (e.g., M. J.
Green et al. 2020, 2025). The most common samples
for these He-rich secondaries are He CVs and AM CVn
stars exhibiting He-rich spectra with H abundance being
much smaller in the latter than the former (M. J. Green
et al. 2020). AM CVn stars are considered to be com-
posed of a WD primary and a secondary star, which is
(1) a less massive WD, (2) a low-mass semi-degenerate
He-burning star, or (3) an evolved main-sequence star
with its H-envelope stripped away (J. E. Solheim 2010;
E. Breedt et al. 2012; M. J. Green et al. 2020).
While we denoted the period minimum to be around
76 minutes (C. Knigge 2006; T. Kato et al. 2015) in
Figure 8, this value is not accurately known yet. Obser-
vations usually report∼80−82 min values for it (B. T.
G¨ ansicke et al. 2009), while it is theoretically predicted
to be around 65−70 min (U. Kolb & I. Baraffe 1999;
S. B. Howell et al. 2001; C. Knigge et al. 2011). Fur-
ther efforts are needed to resolve this ‘period minimum
problem’, or to clarify whether the period minimum in-
trinsically spans a range (T. Kato et al. 2015).

KSP-OT-202104a) with orbital periods shorter than the
period minimum (76 minutes) that we have collected

5.SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION
In this work, we present the discovery and photomet-
ric and spectroscopic studies of KSP-OT-202104a, which
is a new WZ Sge-type DN, possibly under the period
minimum from KSP during its superoutburst. We sum-
marize our results as below.
•The peakV-band magnitude of the superout-
burst is∼16.01 mag which is about 8 magnitudes
brighter than its quiescent brightness. The dura-
tion of the superoutburst is∼28.5 days featured
with a plateau without rebrightening, identifying
it to be a type D WZ Sge-type DN. We obtain
its superhump period to beP sh ≈71.7 minutes
(= 0.04978 days).
•KSP-OT-202104a might be a source with orbital
period shorter than the “period minimum” of∼
76 min, considering the fact that the superhump
and orbital periods have a very similar value (∼
1% difference). The small number of objects in
this period region are transitional ultracompact
accreting binaries (M. J. Green et al. 2020) that
may be in the evolution between longer period
DNe (with main-sequence companion stars) and
AM CVn stars with very short orbital periods. If
the orbital period of KSP-OT-202104a is proved
to be shorter than the period minimum, this DN
with a small mass transfer may also be evolving
toward AM CVn.
•Our spectrum of the source clearly shows blue con-
tinuum from the optically thick accretion disk dur-
ing the superoutburst with hydrogen absorption
lines from Hβto Hζ, while signal at the wave-
length near Hαis too weak to identify the nature.
