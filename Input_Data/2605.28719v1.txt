ABSTRACT
Stellar obliquity (λ) and orbital eccentricity (e) trace the dynamical histories of close-in giant plan-
ets, but the current observational picture is assembled from heterogeneous analyses that have obscured
population-level trends. In this work, we homogeneously refit systems with Rossiter-McLaughlin (RM)
measurements by performing a joint global fit to spectral energy distributions, transit light curves, mid-
transit times, out-of-transit and in-transit radial velocities, yielding self-consistent posterior distribu-
tions for the physical and orbital parameters of both stars and planets across 255 systems1. Restricting
to 145 single-star systems with reliable planet-mass measurements, we uncover pronounced structure
in thee−λplane that depends on planet mass: (i) sub-Saturns (M p ≤∼0.3M J) can be both eccentric
and misaligned; (ii) Jupiters (∼0.3M J < M p ≤∼3M J) are misaligned only on circular orbits; and
(iii) super Jupiters and brown dwarfs (M p >∼3M J) are aligned across the full eccentricity range. A
two-dimensional Kolmogorov-Smirnov test shows that the joint (e, λ) distributions differ significantly
among these three mass regimes. These trends demonstrate thatλdepends jointly on eccentricity and
planet mass, implying that obliquity alone is not a unique tracer of evolutionary history and under-
scoring the need for a unified framework for the origins of spin-orbit misalignment. The full catalog
from this work is publicly available at www.stellarobliquity.com.

A growing body of theory shows that this one-to-one
mapping between mechanisms and observables is rarely
preserved. Eccentricities can be excited without signif-
icantly altering spin-orbit alignment through co-planar
secular resonances (G. Li et al. 2014; C. Petrovich 2015).
Conversely, stellar obliquities can be tilted while leaving
eccentricities essentially unchanged. Proposed sources
of such misalignments include primordial star-disk tilt-
ing driven by chaotic accretion (M. R. Bate et al. 2010;
I. Thies et al. 2011; D. B. Fielding et al. 2015; M. R.
Bate 2018), magnetic warping (F. Foucart & D. Lai
2011; D. Lai et al. 2011; M. M. Romanova et al. 2013,
2021), or internal gravity wave-driven stellar tumbling
(T. M. Rogers et al. 2012), as well as disk torquing by
stellar companions (K. Batygin 2012; T. Matsakos &
A. K¨ onigl 2017; Y. Su & D. Lai 2025). Tidal evolu-
tion adds further complexity because the characteristic
damping timescales foreandψdiffer in both magnitude
and functional dependence (D. Lai 2012; J. N. Winn
et al. 2010a; S. Albrecht et al. 2012b; G. Li & J. N.
Winn 2016; J. J. Zanazzi et al. 2024). As a result, tides
may efficiently erase eccentricity while leaving the stel-
lar spin misaligned, or conversely realign the star while
preserving a remnant of the planet’s dynamical past.
As a means toward unraveling this complexity, pre-
vious studies have primarily linked stellar obliquity to
stellarproperties, with the most notable trends appear-
ing across theT eff boundary (K. C. Schlaufman 2010;
J. N. Winn et al. 2010a; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2026). While
not immediately expected, there is no a priori reason
thatplanetaryproperties cannot be equally important.
Accordingly, we treat these as hitherto under-explored
degrees of freedom and search for empirical structure in
thee-ψplane, recognizing thateandψneed not rise and
fall together but can retain specific,correlatedimprints
of distinct dynamical pathways.
A range of statistical techniques can be used to infer
eccentricity from the photo-eccentric effect (R. I. Daw-
son & J. A. Johnson 2012; D. M. Kipping 2013), es-
timate mutual inclinations from multiplicity statistics
(J. J. Lissauer et al. 2011; S. Tremaine & S. Dong
2012), transit-duration distributions (D. C. Fabrycky
et al. 2014), and transit-timing and transit-duration
variations (TTVs/TDVs, W. Zhu et al. 2018; S. C. Mill-
holland et al. 2021). Simultaneously, line-of-sight stellar
obliquity (i⋆) can be constrained fromvsini ⋆ statistics
(K. C. Schlaufman 2010; J. N. Winn et al. 2017; E. M.
Louden et al. 2021, 2024; M. Morgan et al. 2024; L. I.
Biddle et al. 2025) or from the amplitude distribution
of spot modulation in light curves (e.g., T. Mazeh et al.
2015). Nevertheless, the statistical power of these en-
semble methods rest chiefly on large samples of detected
small planets (J.-W. Xie et al. 2016; V. Van Eylen et al.
2019; W. Zhu et al. 2018; S. C. Millholland et al. 2021;
G. J. Gilbert et al. 2025). Consequently, they have his-
torically offered limited population-level constraints on
the orbital architecture of giant planets because of their
low occurrence rate, although recent studies have be-
gun to assemble larger samples of giant planets (J. Dong
et al. 2021b; J. M. Jackson et al. 2023; T. R. Fairnington
et al. 2026).
Decisive constraints for giant planets, therefore princi-
pally arise from direct, system-by-system measurements,
i.e., out-of-transit radial velocities for eccentricity and
in-transit spectroscopy (the Rossiter-McLaughlin effect,
RM effect; R. A. Rossiter 1924; D. B. McLaughlin 1924)
for the sky-projected spin-orbit angle which serves as an
established observational proxy for the true obliquityψ
(S. Albrecht et al. 2012b; J. Dong & D. Foreman-Mackey
2023; J. C. Siegel et al. 2023; E. Knudstrup et al. 2024;
V. Bourrier et al. 2023). Even such direct measure-
ments, however, mix results from heterogeneous analy-
ses with inconsistently modeled uncertainties, obscuring
population-level inferences.

suffer from the Lucy-Sweeney bias (since the zero phase
lies exactly ate= 0, observational uncertainties tend
to bias the best-fit value upward; L. B. Lucy & M. A.

bootstrapping methods (prior to the widespread adop-

tively, these issues have fueled debate over whether any
e–λrelation is real or merely an artifact of biases and
systematics (M. Rice et al. 2022a; E. Knudstrup et al.
2024), despite intriguing results suggesting that eccen-
tric Jupiters tend to be aligned (J. I. Espinoza-Retamal
et al. 2023a) (see Figure 2 and Section 4 for our mass-
stratified view).
To address these issues, we perform a homogeneous
global analysis of 255 systems with RM measurements,
jointly modeling spectral energy distributions, transit
light curves and mid-transit times, and both out-of-
transit radial velocities and in-transit RMs, with a con-
sistent, modern treatment ofeandλ(see Section 3).
This homogeneous refit yields self-consistent posteriors
fore,λ, and the full set of orbital and physical pa-
rameters and reveals pronounced structure in thee−λ
plane for single-star hosts with robust planet-mass mea-
surements: Sub-Saturns can be both eccentric and mis-
aligned; Jupiters are misaligned only on circular orbits;
and super Jupiters and brown dwarfs are aligned across
the full eccentricity range (See Section 4).
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes
the sample construction and data collection. Section 3
presents the global modeling. Section 4 presents the key
results, and Section 5 offers the discussion.

complementary techniques, including transit-based, spa-

variants, starspot-crossing and gravity-darkened tran-
sits, as well as inclination-based methods such as aster-
oseismology or by inferringi ⋆ from (R⋆, Prot, vsini ⋆).
In practice, 339 out of 468 obliquity measurements 3
for individual planet-hosting stars are RM-based. We
therefore perform homogeneous global modeling for each
system to uniformly derive the physical and orbital pa-

2.2.Data Collection
2.2.1.Transits and Mid-transit Times
Ground-based transits
We searched for ground-based photometry from the
Strasbourg Astronomical Data Center (CDS) 4 and the
Exoplanet Follow-up Observing Program (ExoFOP) 5 .
To efficiently compile photometry hosted at CDS, we
first queried the NASA Exoplanet Archive using the
TIC IDs of the systems in our sample to identify as-
sociated references. We then used the bibcodes of these
references to check whether the corresponding publica-
tions provide photometric data archived at CDS. For
ExoFOP, we downloaded all available photometry for
the systems in our sample and removed data sets that
have not yet been published. For the ExoFOP data, we
adopted the default detrending configuration specified
in the .plotcfg files provided on ExoFOP. The ground-
based transits used in this work are listed in Table 2.

parameters and to propagate parameter uncertainties
coherently, we usedallesfast 10, which builds upon

MIST + SED fit
To derive the stellar parameters, including effective
temperature (T eff), metallicity ([Fe/H]), surface grav-
ity ( logg), mass (M ∗), and radius (R ∗), we per-
formed a spectral energy distribution (SED) analysis by
jointly fitting broadband photometry with the MESA
Isochrones & Stellar Tracks (MIST) evolutionary mod-
els (J. Choi et al. 2016; A. Dotter 2016).
The broadband photometry for each star was compiled
from various catalogs, including 2MASSJ,H, andK
bands (R. M. Cutri et al. 2003), WISEW1,W2, and
W3 bands (R. M. Cutri et al. 2021), and GaiaG,G BP,
andG RP bands ( Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023). As
suggested by J. D. Eastman et al. (2019), reasonable sys-
tematic error floors were applied: 0.02 magnitudes for
GaiaG,G BP,G RP, 2MASSJ,H, andKbands, and
0.03 magnitudes for WISEW1,W2, andW3 bands.
Gaussian priors on [Fe/H] and parallax were applied,

2023). To be conservative, a minimum error of 0.08 dex
was adopted for [Fe/H]. The V-band extinction and its
corresponding uncertainty for each system were derived
from the 3D dust map by G. M. Green et al. (2019) using
thedustmaps 11 (G. Green 2018) package. If the 3D map
was unavailable, an upper limit on the V-band extinc-

(2011)12. The priors used in this work are shown in Ta-

Photometry + Transit Times + RV + RM Modeling
We jointly modeled the transit light curves, transit
mid-times, radial velocities, and Rossiter-McLaughlin
observations. The combined fit provides self-consistent
constraints on the transit profiles and ephemerides, or-
bital RV, as well as stellar spin and planetary orbit ge-
ometry.
For each transiting planet, we adopted uniform pri-
ors for the orbital period (P), reference transit mid-
time (Tc), cosine of the orbital inclination (cosi), planet-
to-star radius ratio (R P/ R∗), and RV semi-amplitude
(K). To mitigate the well-known eccentricity bias to-
ward large values (L. B. Lucy & M. A. Sweeney 1971),
we fitted the parameters √ecosω ∗ and √esinω ∗ in-
stead of eccentricity and argument of periastron sepa-
rately (A. H. M. J. Triaud et al. 2011; J. Eastman et al.

4.THREE POPULATIONS ON THEe−λSPACE
Our refit analysis provides uniform, self-consistent
posteriors and enables a systematic investigation of de-
pendencies among system parameters, particularly the
two most informative indicators of dynamical history
among the currently best-characterized exoplanetary
census, orbital eccentricity (e) and stellar obliquity (λ).
Analysis Sample Construction
Starting from the homogeneous modeling sample de-
scribed in Section 2, we apply the following additional
selection criteria to construct the analysis sample used
in this section: (1) to avoid planetary-type misclassifi-
cation, we remove 6 systems with poorly constrained
planetary masses, defined byM P −3σ MP <0, (2) Stel-
lar multiplicity has long been theorized as a potential
driver of spin-orbit misalignments (M. Holman et al.
1997; Y. Wu & N. Murray 2003; D. Fabrycky & S.
Tremaine 2007; S. Naoz et al. 2012). Recent obliquity
studies confirm that warm Jupiters tend to be aligned
in single-star systems (S. Wang et al. 2021; M. Rice
et al. 2022b; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2024; J. I. Espinoza-
Retamal et al. 2023b) yet are frequently (and sometimes
famously, e.g., HD 80606; C. Moutou et al. 2009; F.
Pont et al. 2009; J. N. Winn et al. 2009b; G. H´ ebrard
et al. 2010; TIC 241249530; A. F. Gupta et al. 2024)
misaligned in binaries; super Jupiters show a similar
single-versus-binary contrast (J. Rusznak et al. 2025).
Moreover, hot Jupiters orbiting stars cooler than the
Kraft break (∼6500 K) are rarely misaligned in single-
star systems but are frequently misaligned in binary and
multi-star systems (X.-Y. Wang et al. 2026). Motivated
by this, 90 binary and multiple star systems were re-

and Multiple Star Systems Catalog (R. Schwarz et al.
2016), the Catalog of Exoplanets in Visual Binaries (C.

nary catalog (K. El-Badry et al. 2021), and the NASA

Of these systems, 10 (4 sub-Saturns, 2 Jupiters, and
4 super Jupiters) either have obliquities derived from

products; therefore, we adopt the literature values of the
projected spin-orbit angle. The statistical significance of
our results is insensitive to this choice; excluding these
systems yields consistent conclusions.
Unambiguous three different populations
A key advance in recent stellar-obliquity studies is the
demonstration by J. Rusznak et al. (2025) that sub-
Saturns, Jupiters, and super Jupiters/brown dwarfs oc-
cupy distinct host-starT eff −λregimes: sub-Saturns can
be misaligned around cool stars, hot Jupiters tend to be
misaligned only around hot stars, while super Jupiters
and brown dwarfs remain aligned regardless of the host
star’s effective temperature (Teff). Using our refined sys-
tem parameters, we find strong evidence that these pop-

Statistical Test
We perform a two-dimensional Kolmogorov–Smirnov
(K–S) test (J. A. Peacock 1983; G. Fasano & A. Frances-
chini 1987; W. H. Press 2007), comparing the joint (e, λ)
distributions of i) sub-Saturns vs. Jupiters, ii) Jupiters
vs. super Jupiters/brown dwarfs and, iii) sub-Saturns
vs. super Jupiters/brown dwarfs, implemented with the
public codendtest 14. For each pairwise comparison, we
propagate measurement uncertainties ineandλwith a
Monte Carlo resampling scheme to obtain a distribution
14 https://github.com/syrte/ndtest
ofp-values. We model the (generally asymmetric) un-
certainties ineandλfor each system with a skewed
Gaussian probability density function, providing an an-
alytical representation of the measurement distribution.
We then generate 10,000 synthetic realizations by inde-
pendently drawing (e, λ) for every system from these dis-
tributions and computing the 2D K–Sp-value each time.
The fractions of realizations withp <0.05 are 100%
for sub-Saturns versus Jupiters, 100% for Jupiters ver-
sus super Jupiters and brown dwarfs, and 99% for sub-
Saturns versus super Jupiters and brown dwarfs, sug-
gesting statistically significant differences in the three
comparisons.
We caution, however, thatT eff is not uniformly sam-
pled across the three mass regimes. This is particularly
important for sub-Saturns: thee–λpattern inferred for
this population is based almost exclusively on cool-star
systems. This paucity likely reflects both the likely low
occurrence rate of sub-Saturns around hot stars (J.-Y.
Yang et al. 2020) and the difficulty of measuring their
stellar obliquity signals (see K. Hixenbaugh et al. 2023;
E. Dugan et al. 2025, for details). Therefore, the com-
parison between misaligned sub-Saturns and misaligned
Jupiters, which occur predominantly around hot-star
hosts, is not yet a controlled comparison within the same
Teff regime. Nevertheless, the difference inT eff alone
does not provide an obvious explanation for why mis-
aligned sub-Saturns would retain measurable eccentric-
ities whereas misaligned Jupiters around hot-star hosts
would not.
5.DISCUSSION
For many years, stellar obliquity has been treated as
a master diagnostic for migration histories: measureλ
well enough and the rest will follow (as reviewed by J. N.
Winn & D. C. Fabrycky 2015; A. H. M. J. Triaud 2018;
S. H. Albrecht et al. 2022). Numerous studies (See,
e.g., D. C. Fabrycky & J. N. Winn 2009; E. Knudstrup
et al. 2024) have drawn inferences along these lines, typ-
ically linking largeλto violent high-emigration, and
more modest, aligned configurations to disk-driven mi-
gration. In this work, we revisit that premise. We ho-
mogeneously refit an existing catalog of exoplanet sys-
tems with Rossiter-McLaughlin effect measurements to
obtain robust estimates for the full set of orbital and
physical parameters. We then analyzed the resultingλ
distribution with explicit attention to its dependence on
both stellar and planetary properties, in particular plan-
etary eccentricityeand planetary massm. The central
outcome is thatλdepends strongly onboth. This joint
dependence is surprising and places considerable pres-

9
sure on existing theoretical interpretations that rely on
λalone.
The first major tension is with high-emigration sce-
narios. A broad class of such mechanisms (see R. I.
Dawson & J. A. Johnson 2018, for a review of theo-
ries for the high-e origins of hot Jupiters), beginning
with the Kozai picture15, predicts substantial spin-orbit
misalignments with relatively well-defined characteristic
distributions (C. Beaug´ e & D. Nesvorn´ y 2012; C. Petro-
vich & S. Tremaine 2016) and has often treated residual
eccentricity as a signpost of their operation. Our re-
sults point in the opposite direction for Jovian-class and
more massive companions: within these regimes,eis
anti-correlatedwithλ. Taken at face value, this pat-
tern is difficult to reconcile with high-emigration as a
dominant pathway.
We further remark that although coplanar high-emi-
gration (G. Li et al. 2014; C. Petrovich 2015) successfully
predicts the existence of aligned Jupiters on substan-
tially eccentric orbits, it is, at face value, unclear why
this mechanism would seem to operate much more read-
ily in the super-Jupiter and brown-dwarf regimes than in
the Jovian regime. That said, even if high-emigration is
responsible for the high obliquities, additional physics is
required to explain why some misaligned sub-Saturns
retain non-zero eccentricities, whereas all misaligned
hot Jupiters have circularized, despite sub-Saturns be-
ing expected to dissipate tidal energy more efficiently
(A. C. M. Correia et al. 2020; K. Batygin 2025).
A distinct class of proposals invokes primordial exci-
tation of obliquity. Variants include magnetic or grav-
itational star-disk torques (F. Foucart & D. Lai 2011;
D. Lai et al. 2011; M. M. Romanova et al. 2013, 2021;
K. Batygin 2012; Y. Su & D. Lai 2025), chaotic accre-
tion (M. R. Bate et al. 2010; I. Thies et al. 2011; D. B.
Fielding et al. 2015; M. R. Bate 2018), or internal wave-
induced stellar tumbling (T. M. Rogers et al. 2012) that
tilt the stellar spin without requiring largee. In their
simplest forms, these mechanisms operate at the star-
disk level and, to leading order, are not expected to
depend sensitively on the mass of a single planet or on
its orbital state (e.g., orbital eccentricity). Our trends
therefore suggest that any successful primordial process
must either introduce planet-mass-dependent disk cou-
pling or admit later dynamical modulation that mod-
ifiesewithout substantially reorienting the star. It is
tempting to speculate that unmodeled planet-disk inter-
actions could provide such a mass handle; notably, the
15 In our context, specifically theplanet-planetvariant (C. Petro-
vich & S. Tremaine 2016; S. Naoz 2016), since we restrict at-
tention to systems without confirmed stellar companions.
transitions we identify coincide with familiar changes in
disk-planet torque regimes, such as the neighborhood
of the thermal mass and, at higherm, the approach to
deep gap opening. However, quantitative models that
connect these regime changes to the observedλ(e, m)
structure have not yet been developed in sufficient de-
tail for rigorous comparison.
It is also plausible that multiple processes contribute
in combination. What our analysis clarifies is that the
mixture isnotuniform across mass, and thatλcannot
be used in isolation as a reliable tracer of migration his-
tory. The relative roles and operating fractions of the
underlying mechanisms remain to be established (see J.
Esposito et al. 2026 for a recent related effort). Cu-
mulatively, the trends reported here underscore the ab-
sence of a unified theoretical framework for the origins of
spin-orbit misalignment, and they imply that strong in-
ferences about the migratory histories of close-in planets
drawn solely fromλare premature.

A.SPECIAL CASES AND MODELING
CONSIDERATIONS
Bimodality in stellar mass and age posteriors
SED+MIST modeling can yield bimodal posteriors in
stellar mass and age when a star lies near an evolution-
ary transition point, such as the main-sequence turnoff
or the subgiant branch. Similar bimodal solutions have
been reported for TOI-569 (T. W. Carmichael et al.
2020), TOI-3894 and TOI-5301 (J. Schulte et al. 2024),
and TOI-4138 (J. Schulte et al. 2025). In our sam-
ple, 14 systems exhibit such bimodal behavior: HAT-
P-33, HAT-P-41, HAT-P-50, HAT-P-67, K2-234, K2-
261, KELT-6, KELT-11, TOI-1842, TOI-2025, TOI-
2145, TrES-4, WASP-13, and WASP-38. We identify
bimodality using the Gaussian kernel-density estimate
of the derivedM ⋆ posterior. Specifically, we locate the
two highest local maxima in the KDE and use the inter-
vening local minimum as the boundary between the two
modes. A system is flagged as truly bimodal if (1) the
lower-mass mode contains at least 10% of the total pos-
terior probability and (2) the two modes are separated
by at least 2σ. Both the lower- and higher-mass solu-
tions are listed in Table 1. For the statistical analysis,
we adopt the mode with the higher posterior probability.
Imperfections in modeling fast rotators:
HAT-P-2 and HAT-P-67
For HAT-P-2 and HAT-P-67, the T. Hirano et al.
(2011c) RM model does not capture all features of the
observed RM signal, leaving significant structured resid-
uals. Both systems host bright, rapidly rotating stars
(HAT-P-2:vsini ∗ = 22,km s −1,G= 8.6; HAT-P-67:
vsini ∗ = 36km s−1,G= 10). Similar residual structure
was reported for HAT-P-2 by S. Albrecht et al. (2012b),
who speculated that it may arise from variations in limb
darkening with the depth of stellar absorption lines.
This mechanism may also explain the structured resid-
uals observed for HAT-P-67. Notably, applying the em-
pirical RM model to HAT-P-2 (J. N. Winn et al. 2007c),
and the numerical RM model to HAT-P-67 (D. Sicilia
et al. 2024), does not produce such residuals, indicating
that additional physical effects may need to be incorpo-
rated into the analytical T. Hirano et al. (2011c) RM
model for rapidly rotating stars. Nevertheless, the de-
rivedλvalues are consistent with those reported by S.
Albrecht et al. (2012b) and D. Sicilia et al. (2024).
Infrared excess: HAT-P-1
The exoplanetary system HAT-P-1 has a stellar com-
panion (G= 9.6) at a separation of 11. ′′3, which should
not contaminate the WISE photometry given the WISE
angular resolution of∼6. ′′5. Nevertheless, in our SED
fits, the WISEW1,W2, andW3 fluxes are consistently
higher than the best-fit model. We therefore inflated the
uncertainties of these bands to 0.1 mag to absorb any
residual contamination from the nearby companion and
other possible systematics.
Possible corrupted SED: KELT-24
The recent analysis by M. R. Giovinazzi et al. (2024)
shows that the SED of KELT-24 may be corrupted.
Therefore, following M. R. Giovinazzi et al. (2024),
we did not include the SED in our global modeling
and instead imposed a spectroscopic prior onT eff of
N(6499,98) K.
Degeneracy for high-grazing systems:
WASP-174 and TOI-5027
For systems exhibiting high impact parameters, the
transit geometry becomes intrinsically degenerate, mak-
ing it difficult to precisely constrain the orbital inclina-
tion (or equivalently the impact parameter) andR p/R∗.
In our sample, both WASP-174 and TOI-5027 have val-
ues ofbclose to unity.
To regularize the solution and avoid poorly
constrained grazing configurations, we impose
additional Gaussian priors onR p/R∗, adopting
N(0.1098,0.0030) for WASP-174 (L. Mancini et al.
2020) andN(0.106,0.005) for TOI-5027 (M. Tala Pinto
et al. 2025).
Orbital decay: WASP-12
Due to the significant orbital decay (K. C. Patra et al.
2017), the ephemeris for WASP-12 derived fromTESS
data is not suitable for predicting the RM mid-transit
time. Therefore, we instead used ground-based tran-
sit observations from G. Maciejewski et al. (2018b) ob-
tained both before and after the RM measurement (S.
Albrecht et al. 2012b).
