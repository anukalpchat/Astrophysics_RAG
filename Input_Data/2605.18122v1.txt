Abstract
LHAASO J0248+6021, a possibleγ-ray pulsar halo associated with PSR J0248+6021 (J0248), exhibits a highly curved spectrum
as revealed by LHAASO and Fermi-LAT measurements. We propose a direct interpretation of this large curvature: the energy-loss
bump in the parent electron spectrum has not yet significantly departed from the high-energy cutoff. This requires either that the
ambient magnetic field strengthBaround J0248 be lower than the typical value in the interstellar medium, or that the electron
injection age be significantly shorter than the pulsar characteristic age. For the much older Geminga pulsar, the expected energy-
loss bump in itsγ-ray halo spectrum has shifted below 100 GeV, in excellent agreement with Fermi-LAT measurements. Thus,
the broadband spectra of young and old pulsar halos find a unified interpretation in the picture of a time-dependent energy-loss
bump. Meanwhile, the spectral measurements of LHAASO J0248+6021 only constrain the combination ofBand electron injection
age. The uncertainty inBleads to an order-of-magnitude variation in the fitted diffusion coefficient. Future X-ray observations are
expected to break the degeneracies.
Keywords:Pulsar halos, PSR J0248+6021, Geminga, Energy losses
1. Introduction
When a bow-shock pulsar wind nebula (PWN) traverses the
interstellar medium (ISM), it may release relativistic electrons
and positrons that diffuse through the ambient turbulent mag-
netic field. Through inverse Compton scattering (ICS) with
background photons, these electrons 1 produce aγ-ray halo
around the pulsar (Abeysekara et al., 2017), known as a pul-
sar halo (or a TeV halo, Linden et al., 2017). Since the mor-
phology of theγ-ray halo is a direct projection of the spatial
distribution of the parent electrons, such halos serve as ideal
probes of cosmic-ray transport on scales of∼10 pc (Fang,
2022; Liu, 2022; López-Coto et al., 2022; Amato and Recchia,
2024). Stacking analyses suggest that pulsar halos may be com-
mon around middle-aged pulsars (Albert et al., 2025). To date,
more than ten confirmed or candidate pulsar halos have been
reported (Alfaro et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2022; Albert et al.,
2023; Cao et al., 2025; Wach and Mitchell, 2025; De Sarkar,
2026)2.
LHAASO J0248+6021 is a possible pulsar halo discovered
by the LHAASO experiment (Cao et al., 2025). Its centroid
shows good agreement with that of PSR J0248+6021 (here-
after J0248). As the energy increases, its morphology contracts
toward the pulsar, which disfavors the hypothesis that it origi-
nates from hadronic interactions between cosmic-ray nuclei and
a nearby molecular cloud (Cao et al., 2025). Furthermore, its

J0248 has a characteristic age ofτ c =62.4 kyr (Manchester

the prototype pulsar halo, Geminga (τ c =342 kyr). Therefore,
LHAASO J0248+6021 offers an opportunity to study a young

measurements from LHAASO and Fermi-LAT, itsγ-ray spec-

shows that the spectrum can be explained by a time-integrated
electron spectrum following an exponential cutoffpower law,
and argues that the fitted power-law indexp∼1.0 is consistent
with expectations for the electron injection spectrum.
However, due to radiative energy losses, the time-integrated
electron spectrum can differ significantly from the originally
injected spectrum. Consequently, the fitted parameters of the
time-integrated electron spectrum, such as the power-law index
mentioned above, cannot be directly interpreted as properties
of the injection spectrum. We will show that, as the system
evolves, an energy-loss bump may appear in theγ-ray spec-
trum. Accounting for this effect, explaining the observed large
spectral curvature requires not only a hard power-law compo-
nent in the injection spectrum, but also imposes stringent con-
straints on parameter combinations such as the electron injec-
tion age and the ambient magnetic field strength.

4.1. Spectrum fitting
We quantify the uncertainty inb 0 by considering a range of
magnetic field strengths. Although estimates of the temperature
and energy density of the infrared photons also carry uncer-
tainties, we regardb 0,syc as the dominant source of uncertainty,
since the magnetic field strength around J0248 remains uncon-
strained andb 0,syc is highly sensitive to it.
The current electron injection age of J0248 isτ c −˜τ0, where
˜τ0 is a free parameter. As indicated by Eq. (5), the energy-
loss rate and the injection age are degenerate in fitting theγ-
ray spectrum. Therefore, rather than treatingBand ˜τ 0 as free
parameters simultaneously, we holdBat different values and fit
for ˜τ0. The other free parameters areE e,c andη.
The spectral fitting results for LHAASO and Fermi-LAT are
presented by the first three curves in the left panel of Fig. 3. In
comparison with Fig. 1, the energy-loss bump is hidden, and the
pronounced spectral curvature exhibited by the data is well re-
produced. The right panel shows the constraints on the electron
injection age obtained from the fitting under specific magnetic
field strengths. AsBincreases from 1µG to 5µG, the electron
injection age decreases from≈50 kyr to≈15 kyr. This im-
plies that if the magnetic field strength is relatively large, the
effective injection time would be significantly smaller than the
characteristic age of J0248.
The synchrotron loss rate forB=5µG is 25 times higher
than that forB=1µG. To maintain the sameγ-ray flux, a
larger number of electrons must be injected. Correspondingly,
the best-fit energy conversion efficiency is 12% forB=5µG
versus 1.1% forB=1µG. Yet, both efficiencies fall within rea-
sonable ranges. Additionally, the cutoffenergy of the injection
spectrum remains stable at 120−130 TeV across the different
fitting results.
The reducedχ 2 statistics of all best-fit models are close to
unity, yet the best-fit curves lie slightly above the 95% UL of
Fermi-LAT. According to our fitting rule detailed in Section 2,
when the model exceeds an UL, theχ2 contribution is a constant
value of 4. Owing to the high precision of the LHAASO data,
the fitting procedure prioritizes agreement with the LHAASO
results, completely sacrificing the fit to the Fermi-LAT UL. This
indicates that the LHAASO data alone are sufficient to constrain
theB−˜τ 0 combination.
We also show a result in which the model is forced to satisfy
the Fermi-LAT UL, indicated by the blue dotted line in the left
panel of Fig. 3. The spectrum becomes narrower, correspond-
ing to a largerE e,cr. Consequently, forB=3µG, the required
˜τ0 increases from 34 kyr to 44 kyr.

electron cloud (Theureau et al., 2011). The only suitable en-
vironment along the line of sight is the giant HII region W5.
J0248 is located near the open cluster IC 1848 within W5, for
which the Gaia DR3 parallax measurement gives a distance of
2004±9 pc (Della Croce et al., 2025). Even considering the
physical scale of W5 itself, which is several tens of pc, this
introduces only a 1−2% uncertainty in the distance estimate
to J0248. Furthermore, from proper motion measurements of
J0248, the transverse velocity corresponding to a distance of
2 kpc is 500 km s −1 (Theureau et al., 2011), which is also rea-
sonable. This implies that we may be able to provide an accu-
rate estimate of the diffusion coefficient around J0248 once the
magnetic field is effectively constrained.

4.3. Expected synchrotron emission
Theγ-ray spectral characteristics of LHAASO J0248+6021
only provide constraints on theB−˜τ 0 combination. Measure-
ments of synchrotron emission in the X-ray band from the halo
region could break this degeneracy. However, no diffuse X-ray
measurements are currently available in this region. The X-ray
UL fromChandrais based on point-source searches toward the
pulsar itself (Mignani et al., 2016).

within a 1 ◦ ×1 ◦ field of view (Yuan et al., 2025), making it
well-suited for measuring the X-ray counterparts of pulsar ha-
los. Figure 5 shows the synchrotron emission3 predicted by the

5. An old pulsar halo system
As the characteristic age of Geminga is approximately six
times that of J0248, the expected spectrum of the Geminga halo
differs from that of LHAASO J0248+6021, resembling the pink
curve in the right panel of Fig. 2. In this case, the energy-loss
bump in theγ-ray spectrum has shifted below∼100 GeV, such
that it is clearly separated from the high-energy cutoff. Taking
into account the proper motion of Geminga and using a large
region of interest (ROI) of 70 ◦ ×70 ◦, Di Mauro et al. (2019)
detected a signal that may correspond to the Geminga halo in
the 10−100 GeV range using the Fermi-LAT data. This result
was confirmed by Meng et al. (2026)4. Compared with the the-
oretical expectation, this sub-GeV signal likely corresponds to
the energy-loss bump in theγ-ray spectrum.
We fit the broadbandγ-ray spectrum of the Geminga halo as
measured by Fermi-LAT (Meng et al., 2026) and HAWC (Al-
bert et al., 2024). As in Section 4, we fixp=1 and treatE e,c,
η, and ˜τ0 as free parameters. Unlike the case of J0248, mul-
tiple studies have searched for diffuse X-ray emission around
Geminga, providing ULs on flux and, consequently, on the
magnetic field strength (Liu et al., 2019; Khokhriakova et al.,
2024; Wu et al., 2024; Manconi et al., 2024; Krivonos et al.,
2026). Based on the result of the degree-wide search from
eROSITA (Khokhriakova et al., 2024), we assumeB=1.4µG.

arises from the accumulation of electrons injected at the earliest
times. From Eq. (4), a smaller ˜τ0 corresponds to a larger num-
ber of electrons injected at small ˜t, resulting in a more promi-
nent energy-loss bump. It can also be seen that the energy at
which the energy-loss bump appears almost remains unaffected

τc −˜τ0 is dominated byτ c.
By substitutingP=0.237 s,τ c =342 kyr, and the fitted

Interestingly, this UL is very close to the small initial period of
the Crab pulsar,P 0 =0.019 s.

6. Conclusion and discussion
The parent electrons of a pulsar halo accumulate through
continuous injection. Radiative energy losses shift the early-
injected electrons to lower energies, forming a time-dependent
energy-loss bump in the spectrum. This work proposes a direct
interpretation of the highly curvedγ-ray spectrum of LHAASO
J0248+6021: its energy-loss bump has not yet significantly de-
parted from the high-energy cutoffstructure. This implies that
either the ambient magnetic field strength, which affects the
energy-loss rate, is lower than the typical ISM value (3µG), or
the electron injection age is significantly smaller than the char-
acteristic age of its central pulsar J0248. For Geminga, whose
characteristic age is about six times that of J0248, the energy-
loss bump in its expected pulsar halo spectrum has shifted be-
low 100 GeV, showing remarkable consistency with Fermi-
LAT measurements.
Fitting the LHAASO and Fermi-LAT spectra of LHAASO
J0248+6021 constrains the combination of magnetic field
strengthBand electron injection age, while the degeneracy be-
tween them remains. The uncertainty inBallows the diffusion
coefficient from the profile fit to vary by an order of magni-
tude. We expect EP X-ray observations to determineB, thereby
providing definitive estimates of the diffusion coefficient and
electron injection age.
The broadbandγ-ray spectral characteristics of the Geminga
halo justify a hard power-law component in the injection spec-
trum (p∼1.0). In addition, based on the height of the energy-
loss bump measured by Fermi-LAT, we infer that the initial spin
period of Geminga may be shorter than 0.021 s, comparable to
that of the Crab pulsar.
The quantitative results of this work may be subject to the
following assumptions: (1) We attribute the main uncertainty
in the electron energy-loss rate to the magnetic field strength,
ignoring uncertainties in the dust radiation field. (2) We assume
that the pulsar spin-down is purely due to magnetic dipole ra-
diation, i.e., a braking index of 3. (3) We assume that the elec-
tron injection follows exactly the time evolution of the pulsar
spin-down power, i.e., a constant energy conversion efficiency.
These factors currently lack clear constraints but do not quali-
tatively affect our conclusions. Therefore, we leave further dis-
cussion to future work.
