ABSTRACT
The discovery of a population of massive, ancient quiescent galaxies within the first 2 Gyr of the Universe’s
history has led to significant tensions with models of galaxy formation. However, these analyses are often
based on slit spectroscopy, which typically captures only the center-most region of these galaxies and, crucially,
assumes these cores are representative of the entire galaxy. To illustrate the varying stellar populations present

throughout these galaxies, we present an analysis of color gradients in fourz>3 log(M ⋆/M⊙)>11 quiescent
galaxies which previous works have argued are in tension with models. Using medium-band photometry from
MINERV A JWST observations, we measure resolved photometry in a series of elliptical annuli out to 0. ′′7
(∼4R e). We find negative color gradients in three galaxies, and for the most extreme color gradient (∆(U−
V)/∆R=−0.126±0.030 mag kpc −1), we find the stellar mass is 0.1 dex lower when compared to photometry
measured within NIRSpec slits. In the limiting case where these color gradients are entirely driven by age, we
find lessened tensions with extreme value statistics models out toz∼9.5, though different stellar population
modeling choices also contribute significantly. Ultimately, these findings highlight the need for integral field
unit spectroscopy. Spatially-resolved spectra can provide the evidence needed to break the age-dust-metallicity
degeneracy, and reliably separate the effects of the observed color gradients from the effects of different physical
modeling assumptions on the formation histories of these galaxies.

1.INTRODUCTION
Over the past decade, deep ground-based imaging and
spectroscopy have revealed a population of extremely mas-
sive (log(M ⋆/M⊙)>11) quiescent galaxies atz>3 (e.g.,
Z. C. Marsan et al. 2015; K. Glazebrook et al. 2017; C.
Schreiber et al. 2018; B. Forrest et al. 2020; J. Antwi-Danso
et al. 2025). Spectroscopic observations from the James
Webb Space Telescope (JWST) found that a subsample of
these galaxies are very old, having formed the bulk of their
stellar mass during the epoch of reionization (e.g., A. C. Car-
nall et al. 2023a,b, 2024; S. Belli et al. 2024; F. D’Eugenio
et al. 2024; K. Glazebrook et al. 2024; S. M. Urbano Staw-
inski et al. 2024; M. Xiao et al. 2024; A. de Graaff et al.
2025; I. McConachie et al. 2025; T. Nanayakkara et al. 2025;
M. L. Hamadouche et al. 2026; S. D. Stevenson et al. 2026; Y .
Zhang et al. 2026). The large stellar mass of these galaxies,
paired with their ancient formation ages, has placed stress
on cosmological models. In particular, several of these mas-
sive quiescent galaxies exceed the expected stellar mass of
the most-massive galaxies predicted by current models in
the first∼1−2 Gyr of the Universe even after assuming
100% star-formation efficiency (A. C. Carnall et al. 2024; K.
Glazebrook et al. 2024; H. G. Chittenden et al. 2026). Sev-
eral mechanisms have been proposed to explain these ten-
sions, including a lack of understanding of early stellar pop-
ulations or galaxy formation, different initial mass functions
(IMFs), environmental bias (e.g., C. K. Jespersen et al. 2025;
C. Turner et al. 2025), and challenges toΛCDM cosmology
and the cold dark matter paradigm as a whole (e.g., B. Liu &
V . Bromm 2022; U. Maio & M. Viel 2023; H. Padmanabhan

One concern is that a key assumption in these analyses
has been that the spectrum observed by slits is representative
of the whole galaxy. Spectroscopy from NIRSpec Micro-
Shutter Assembly (MSA) shutters only cover a 0.′′46×0. ′′20
area on-sky, whereas the average half-light diameter of a
3<z<4 quiescent galaxy at log(M ⋆/M⊙)∼11 is roughly
0.′′3 (e.g., L. Chen et al. 2026). As such, MSA spectra of
high-redshift galaxies only capture half the total light of the
galaxy, and thus may be mostly dominated by light from the
galaxy’s center. Therefore, if we assume this inner 50% of
the galaxy’s light is representative of the entire galaxy we
could be overestimating the stellar mass and age. Further-
more, inexact slit positioning (sources are rarely centered on
the MSA slit) and bar shadows add further spatial complex-
ity, which may lead to additional biases in the total galaxy
values.
Lower-redshift results suggest that indeed these MSA ob-
servations may be biased by color gradients. Atz<3, mas-
sive quiescent galaxies have strong negative color gradients
(i.e., redder centers and bluer outskirts), especially in older
stellar systems (e.g., Y . Guo et al. 2011; D. Szomoru et al.
2013; K. A. Suess et al. 2019a, 2020; T. B. Miller et al.
2023; C. M. Cheng et al. 2024; M. Clausen et al. 2025; M.
Martorano et al. 2026). Studies have found that in massive
quiescent galaxies, color gradients are primarily driven by
metallicity at 0≲z≲1 (e.g., C. Tortora et al. 2010; J. E.
Greene et al. 2012, 2015; C. M. Cheng et al. 2024) or a mix
of age and metallicity atz∼2 (e.g., Y . Guo et al. 2011;
K. A. Suess et al. 2021; C. M. Cheng et al. 2025). More-
over, analyses of lensed galaxies atz= 2 with ground-based
MOSFIRE and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) grism spec-
troscopy have revealed largely flat age and metallicity gradi-
ents atz∼2 (M. Jafariyazani et al. 2020; M. Akhshik et al.
2023), while JWST/NIRISS slitless spectroscopy found evi-

dence for flat and positive metallicity gradients atz∼3 (A.
Acharyya et al. 2025; M. Ju et al. 2025). In post-starburst
galaxies (PSBs), several studies find flat age gradients (e.g.,
D. J. Setton et al. 2020; K. A. Suess et al. 2020, 2021),
while others find age gradients present even in PSBs (e.g.,
F. D’Eugenio et al. 2020; C. M. Cheng et al. 2024; H.-H. Le-
ung et al. 2026). Notably, most studies of physical parameter
gradients in quiescent galaxies atz>1 are impacted heavily
by systematics such as the spectral/spatial resolution, which
emission/absorption lines are present, and contributions from
diffuse ionized gas (e.g. K. Zhang et al. 2017; H. Poetrodjojo
et al. 2019; M. Ju et al. 2025). Age gradients may even be ex-
pected in early massive quiescent galaxies due to inside out
formation and quenching (e.g., J. V . Kepner 1999; A. Zolo-
tov et al. 2015; E. J. Nelson et al. 2016; S. Tacchella et al.
2016; L. Lin et al. 2019; A. Pillepich et al. 2019; J. S. Spilker
et al. 2019; S. E. Cutler et al. 2023) or rejuvenated star for-
mation in outer regions due to continued gas accretion(e.g.,
S. Fortuné et al. 2025; R.-S. Remus & L. C. Kimmig 2025;
J. A. A. Trussler et al. 2025). It is thus plausible that massive
quiescent galaxies atz>3 also exhibit strong negative color
and age gradients, and subsequently younger, bluer outskirts
which may ease the aforementioned tensions caused by their
modeled formation timescales.
Directly measuring these color gradients and obtaining
more accurate stellar masses and formation ages requires
spectroscopy from the NIRSpec Integral Field Unit (IFU),
deep, multi-object or long-slit spectroscopy with slits aligned
along the galaxy’s semi-major axis (e.g., A. van der Wel et al.
2016; M. Kriek et al. 2024; M. Slob et al. 2024), or NIR-
Cam or NIRISS grism spectroscopy, all of which can account
for differing stellar populations within galaxies. However,
IFU observations in particular are expensive, require targeted
observations for each source individually, and high-redshift
sources may be too compact to justify IFU spectroscopy.
Though JWST NIRCam imaging has enabled spatially re-
solved spectral energy distribution (SED) modeling, photom-
etry from broadband imaging alone may be insufficient to ob-
tain robust stellar population modeling. In particular, the star-
formation histories (SFHs) of galaxies modeled with broad-
band photometry alone are dominated by model priors (B.
Wang et al. 2024a), making it difficult to constrain forma-
tion age, especially on the short timescales of thez>3 uni-
verse. However, medium-band photometry, in tandem with
broadbands, is capable of robustly modeling stellar popula-
tions (e.g., J. Antwi-Danso et al. 2025). Here, we use the
“Mediumband Imaging with NIRCam to Explore ReV olu-
tionary Astrophysics” (MINERV A) JWST cycle 4 treasury
survey (ID: 7814, PI: Muzzin, CoPIs: Marchesini, Suess; A.
Muzzin et al. 2025), as it extensively covers the UDS field,
where our target galaxies are located, and provides a mini-
mum of 16 photometric filters (8 medium-bands) when com-
bined with existing JWST surveys.
In this Letter, we augment existing HST and JWST data
with medium-band photometry from MINERV A to per-
form spatially-resolved SED modeling of four ultra-massive
(log(M⊙/M⋆)>11 quiescent galaxies atz>3 in the UDS
field from A. C. Carnall et al. (2024), hereafter referred to
as C24. The Letter is structured as follows. In Section
2, we detail the MINERV A observations and data products
used, as well as the four target galaxies. Section 3 de-
scribes the spatially-resolved photometry and three SED fit-
ting prescriptions used to model the photometry. Color and
age gradients are shown in Section 4, while Section 5 dis-
cusses the impact of these results on our understanding of
thez>3 massive quiescent galaxy population. The work is
summarized in Section 6. We assume a G. Chabrier (2003)
IMF and a WMAP9 cosmology (G. Hinshaw et al. 2013):
H0 = 69.32 km s −1 Mpc−1,Ω M = 0.2865, andΩ Λ = 0.7135.
We also assumeMV,⊙ =+4.81 ABmag, taken from the CAL-
SPEC database27. All magnitudes are in the AB system (J. B.
Oke 1974).

2.2.Target Sources
Our analysis focuses on the four ultra-massive
(log(M⋆/M⊙)∼11) quiescent galaxies in UDS analyzed
with medium resolution spectroscopy in C24. Two of the
galaxies (ZF-UDS-6496 and ZF-UDS-7329) were originally
observed with Keck/MOSFIRE (C. Schreiber et al. 2018)
and subsequently followed up with and extensively stud-
ied prior to C24 using NIRSpec MSA prism spectroscopy
(K. Glazebrook et al. 2024; T. Nanayakkara et al. 2024).
These two galaxies also have the lowest redshifts in the
sample (zspec = 3.9884 and 3.1943, respectively). ZF-UDS-
6496 (hereafter MINERV A-1084946) was found to have
a robust PSB spectral shape. Conversely, K. Glazebrook
et al. (2024) (and subsequently C24) find ZF-UDS-7329
(MINERV A-1092611) to be extremely old, with formation
redshifts consistent withz form ∼11. PRIMER-EXCELS-
109760 and 117560 (MINERV A-1189865 and 1208449)
are both observed atz spec = 4.62, with the two galaxies
possibly being part of az∼4.6 overdensity. In particu-
lar, MINERV A-1189865 is consistent with significant star
formation atz>12. Table 1 shows the four galaxies we
perform follow up analysis on, including the MINERV A ID,
right ascension, declination, and flux radius as well as the
ID, spectroscopic redshift, and metallicity from C24. The
MINERV A IDs are used primarily hereafter.
3.ANALYSIS
3.1.Resolved Photometry
We measure resolved photometry on the PSF-matched
imaging in elliptical annuli using PHOTUTILSfor all avail-
able filters, including from ancillary fields. This often re-
sults in certain sources having additional coverage compared
to others (e.g., MINERV A-1092611 is the only source with
F480M photometry). The outer semi-major axis of each an-
nulus ranges from 0.′′1 to 0.′′7 with a width of 0.′′1, though for
the smallest semi-major axis we consider all the flux within

3.2. EAzY: Rest-frame Colors
We utilize the photometric fitting code EAzY (G. B. Bram-
mer et al. 2008) to measure the rest-frameUV Jcolors in
each annulus. While fitting, we increase the uncertainty on
observed filters furthest from the rest-frame wavelength of
interest (i.e., weighted interpolation) so that the rest-frame
colors are better constrained by observed photometry, as op-
posed to using the observed photometry to correct the rest-
frame colors after fitting (e.g., G. Rudnick et al. 2003; O.
Ilbert et al. 2005; R. J. Williams et al. 2009; G. Noirot
et al. 2022). Annular photometry is modeled using the
SFHZ_BLUE_AGNtemplates with the redshift fixed at the
spectroscopic redshifts shown in Table 1 (z spec). For com-
parison, we also run EAzY on the super aperture and fixed
0.′′70 color aperture photometry (both with redshifts fixed to
zspec). The optional magnitude andβ-slope priors are dis-
abled in our fits and a 5% systematic error is applied to the
photometry.
We use EAzY for rest-frame colors as we find that it can
more closely mimic the observed photometry. This is be-
cause EAzY uses a linear combination of galaxy models to
match the SED, which allows for more flexibility (though
note some non-standard stellar models are not present in the
EAzY templates, e.g., S. Lu et al. 2026), and, more impor-
tantly, determines rest-frame colors with a weighted interpo-
lation. On the other hand, since PROSPECTORmodels are
physical, we use them in Section 3.3 for general stellar popu-
lation modeling and inferring non-parametric SFHs, making
the use of the two different SED modeling codes complimen-
tary.
3.3. PROSPECTOR: Stellar Population Modeling
We model the resolved and total photometry with the
Bayesian SED-fitting code PROSPECTOR(B. D. Johnson
et al. 2021), applying the default libraries: Flexible Stel-
lar Population Synthesis models (FSPS; C. Conroy et al.
2009, 2010; C. Conroy & J. E. Gunn 2010), MIST stellar
isochrones (J. Choi et al. 2016; A. Dotter 2016), and the dust
attenuation model from M. Kriek & C. Conroy (2013). We
elect to use the synthetic C3K (C. Conroy et al. 2019) stellar
templates due to their high rest-frame wavelength coverage
(0.01≤λ≤2.0µm) and template stellar metallicity range
(−2.12≤log(Z/Z ⊙)≤0.5). SFHs are also modeled non-
parametrically using the “continuity” prior with 11 lookback
time bins: 1 yr to 30 Myr, 30 Myr to 100 Myr, and 9 bins
linearly spaced from 100 Myr tot univ(z= 20). Notably, these
age bins impose the condition of negligible star formation
atz>20 following C. Turner et al. (2025). This condition
prevents PROSPECTORfrom “hiding” stellar mass at very
early times and artificially inflating the mass-weighted age
of the galaxy. The effect of thez= 20 limit on the inferred
SFHs is discussed in Appendix B. To account for any sys-
tematic uncertainties in both the observed photometry and
also in our PROSPECTORmodels, we again add a 5% sys-
tematic error floor to all photometry before running the fit-
ting (B. Wang et al. 2024b). Sampling is performed using
dynamic nested sampling viaDYNESTY(J. S. Speagle 2020).
A full list of priors and prior ranges used can be found in
Table A1. Neither the prism spectroscopy from K. Glaze-
brook et al. (2024) or the medium-resolution spectroscopy
from C24 are included in our fitting.
The major confounding factor in our SED modeling is the
age-dust-metallicity degeneracy, as all three of these param-
eters can contribute to reddened colors. Since the dataset
used in this Letter is purely photometric in nature and spans
only the rest-frame ultraviolet (UV) to near-infrared (NIR),
i.e., 0.1≲λ rest ≲1.1µm, it is insufficient to reliably sepa-
rate out our galaxies’ ages, metallicities and dust attenuation
parameters (e.g., E. F. Bell & R. S. de Jong 2001; B. D. John-
son et al. 2021; S. Tacchella et al. 2022; A. Nersesian et al.
2024, 2025). We therefore chose to adopt the extreme “limit-
ing” case of modeling the observed color gradients as a gra-
dient in mass-weighted age by imposing narrow priors on the

4.1.Observed Color Gradients
Figure 1 shows the resolved SEDs (normalized to unity
in F444W) of the ultra-massive quiescent galaxies from
C24, alongside (F090W+F115W+F150W)-F277W-F444W

and medium-band-only RGB cutouts of the galaxies. Qual-
itatively, the SEDs are clearly different, with the large scat-
ter in the rest-optical (0.4≲λ rest ≲0.6µm) illustrating the
varying spectral shapes. The effect is stronger in MINERV A-
1084946 and MINERV A-1092611 due to their lower red-
shifts, which allow for higherS/Nphotometry at shorter
wavelengths out to larger radii.
In MINERV A-1084946, we observe bluer ultraviolet
slopes atR⩾0. ′′4 relative to smaller radii, though atR⩾0. ′′6
this is mostly driven by low-S/Nphotometry (<5) atλ rest <
0.3 in these annuli. The SEDs atR⩾0. ′′4 also demonstrate a
steeper slope between 0.4≲λ rest ≲0.6µmcompared to those
in central regions. Meanwhile, in the outer annuli (R⩾0. ′′4)
of MINERV A-1092611 the SED sharply decreases from 2 to
3µm, while it is much flatter in the galactic center. These
bluer rest-optical SEDs may indicate an age gradient (though
a strong dust gradient is also a potential explanation, e.g.,
J. C. Siegel et al. 2025). In MINERV A-1092611, owing to
the combination of F140M, F150W, and F162M photome-
try in MINERV A, we are also able to distinguish a stronger
Balmer break (λobs = 1.53µm) at larger radii, compared to a
more pronounced 4000Å (λobs = 1.68µm) break in the inner
radii. This transition from a prominent Balmer break in the
outer annuli to a 4000Å break in the galactic center suggests
a younger stellar population in the galactic outskirts.
The top row of Figure 2 shows observed color gradi-
ents for each galaxy. We choose the broadband filters (to
maximizeS/N) that best approximate a rest-frameU−V
color, though we opt to use F140M−F250M for MINERV A-
1092611 as its lower redshift enables highS/Nobservations
with medium-bands. MINERV A-1084946 and 1189865 have

the center (R≲3 kpc, 0. ′′5), while 1189865 is steeper at
R≳2 kpc (0. ′′3). If we only consider the colors in these
regimes, the gradients become significantly more promi-
nent (−0.192±0.010 and−0.275±0.066 mag kpc −1, re-
spectively). Conversely, we find that MINERV A-1208449
has a flat gradient (−0.022±0.071 mag kpc −1). Likewise,
MINERV A-1092611 is consistent with a flat color gradi-
ent (−0.054±0.065mag kpc −1), though if we only con-
sider the central∼2.5 kpc (0. ′′4) whereS/Nis higher,
we find a mild, statistically-significant negative color gra-

MINERV A-1092611 (both centrally and globally) compared

Section 4.2 is likely due to its unfortunate redshift (z spec =

PSF smoothing (see Section 3.1).
4.2.Rest-Frame Colors
In the middle panel of Figure 2, we show the rest-
frameU−Vcolor gradients derived from EAzY. For all
galaxies except MINERV A-1208449, we find apparent neg-
ative color gradients in at least some region of the galaxy.
Quantitatively, MINERV A-1084946 has the strongest color
gradient (−0.131±0.018mag kpc −1), though MINERV A-
1189865 also has a>3σnegative gradient (−0.123±
0.039 mag kpc −1). Interestingly, MINERV A-1092611 has a
>3σsignificant rest-frameU−Vcolor gradient (−0.084±
0.017mag kpc−1), while its observed F140M-F250M color
gradient was less significant, especially across all annuli.
This is likely due to EAzY leveraging multiple filters to mea-
sure the rest-frame fluxes.
The bottom panel of Figure 2 shows the evolution of
theUV Jcolors of each galaxy with annular distance. At
larger radii (indicated by larger points) galaxies tend to
have dramatically bluer rest-frame colors, even when com-
pared to the super aperture total photometry (black-outlined
points). Notably, the outer annuli colors of all galaxies
except MINERV A-1208449 fall below the K. E. Whitaker
et al. (2011) 2<z<3.5 quiescent galaxy selection (dot-
ted black line, left), though these are still classified as quies-
cent when using high-redshift-specificUV Jselections (e.g.,
W. M. Baker et al. 2025). MINERV A-1208449 becomes
∼0.2 mag bluer in bothU−VandV−Jcolor, while
MINERV A-1084946 and 1189865 show even larger (>0.4
mag) decreases in bothU−V(though these colors may
be model dependent due to high photometric uncertainty at
λrest <0.4µm) andV−Jcolors. In particular, the color evo-
lution of MINERV A-1084946 and 1189865 also follow the
slope of the K. E. Whitaker et al. (2011) diagonalUV Jcut
(dashed line in Fig. 2, bottom left). This relation is known to
roughly trace the aging of quiescent galaxies due to the tran-
sition from Balmer-break- to 4000Å-break-dominated spec-
tra (K. E. Whitaker et al. 2012, 2013; S. Belli et al. 2019),
implying that these two galaxies’ color gradients are due to
age (though they may also be driven by dust as shown by
theA V vector in Fig. 2). Lastly, MINERV A-1092611 has
the most distinct color gradient from the other three galaxies,
with a sharp (>0.4 mag) drop inU−V, but relatively little
change inV−J. We note that theV−Jcolors are extrapolated
for all galaxies except MINERV A-1092611 due to a lack of
rest-frameJband coverage, though we do find rough agree-
ment between the annularV−Jcolors for these galaxies and
the global fits which have rest-Jcoverage thanks to MIRI.
The negative rest-frame color gradients seen in three of the
galaxies suggest that stellar populations in the outskirts of
these ultra-massive quiescent galaxies are younger. Recently,

more restricted colors than EAzY (see Section 3.3). The ad-
dition of medium bands also allows for more robust determi-
nation of rest-frame fluxes, especially in theV-band where
Balmer and 4000Å breaks are the dominant spectral feature.
The outerUV Jcolors of MINERV A-1084946 and
1208449 (Figure 2, red and yellow points, respectively) ex-
tend into the PSB region of theUV Jdiagram (e.g., S. Belli
et al. 2019; M. Park et al. 2023). Specifically, Flexible Stellar
Population Synthesis (FSPS; C. Conroy et al. 2009, 2010; C.
Conroy & J. E. Gunn 2010) models suggest that the resolved
colors of MINERV A-1084946 and 1208449 occupy a simi-
lar parameter space to an exponentially declining SFH model
with ane-folding time of 200 Myr (gray lines in Fig. 2). This
agrees with the findings of C24, who measure PSB-like SFHs
for both these galaxies. MINERV A-1189865 follows a sim-
ilar trajectory to the FSPS models but is significantly bluer
overall. This is also reflected in the PROSPECTORresults
(Figure 3), which show a young (<500 Myr) population at
almost all radii and a higher sSFR than all other galaxies in
the sample. Conversely, the color gradient of MINERV A-
1092611 follows an older, slow quenching color evolution,
in line with the findings of K. Glazebrook et al. (2024).
Crucially, colors measured from total photometry (solid or
dashed lines and open or black-outlined points in Fig. 2)
are strongly biased by light from the galactic center. If pho-
tometry is measured in rectangular apertures approximating
the central NIRSpec/G235M slitet of C24 (“slit photometry”;
open points), we find observed and rest-frame colors in line
with the 0.′′2 annuli. Larger 0. ′′7 diameter apertures (such
as those used in the K. Glazebrook et al. 2024 photometry)
also have colors roughly equivalent to smaller diameter an-
nuli (e.g., 0.′′3) and redder than the photometry at larger radii.
This suggests that studies utilizing small color apertures or
slit photometry (e.g., T. Nanayakkara et al. 2025; Y . Zhang
et al. 2026) are often biased significantly by the central pop-
ulation of the galaxy, which could lead to biases in stellar
population modeling (see also C. Pacifici et al. 2015). Alter-
natively, the super photometry (black-outlined points), which
uses 1.′′4 diameter apertures for all galaxies herein, is com-
parable to the 0.′′4 annular photometry. Thus, in the absence
of resolved photometry, choosing an aperture size closest to
the isophotal area is key to measuring colors representative
of the entire galaxy, especially in galaxies with strong color
gradients.
4.3.Stellar Population Modeling
Figure 3 shows the distribution of PROSPECTOR-measured
mass-weighted age, sSFR, and strengths of the de-reddened
(dust-corrected)D4000 (G. Bruzual A. 1983; B. M. Pog-
gianti & G. Barbaro 1997) and Balmer breaks (D B =
fν(4160−4290Å)/f ν(3500−3630Å), G. Roberts-Borsani
et al. 2024) as a function of aperture size for our fidu-
cial model, while Figure 4 shows the median PROSPEC-
TOR-modeled SFHs for each individual annulus. In order
to explore the effect of a maximal age gradient, metallic-
ity and dust gradients are flat by construction (see Section
3.3), though are shown in Appendix A. In general, under this
assumption, relatively strong color gradients in two of the
galaxies lead to age and sSFR gradients in our SPS mod-
eling. We also find consistently steep declines in the de-
reddenedD4000 andD B strengths with increasing aperture
size in those same galaxies. Figure 3 also shows the change
in rest-frameVband mass-to-light ratio (M ⋆/LV ) with in-
creasing radial distance. The mass-to-light ratio follows the
same trend as the mass-weighted age, with the centers of
MINERV A-1084946 and 1092611 containing far more mass

code, dust/metallicity treatment, SFH prior) can significantly

photometry is handled.
5.DISCUSSION
We find evidence of negative color gradients in two of
the four UDS ultra-massive quiescent galaxies from C24.
In the test-case of flat dust and metallicity gradients, we
also find negative (MINERV A-1084946 and 1092611), flat
(MINERV A-1208449 and 1189865 atR⩾0. ′′3), and mildly
positive (MINERV A-1189865 atR⩽0. ′′3) age gradients via
PROSPECTORSED modeling. Irrespective of the stellar pop-
ulation modeling, the existence of strong color gradients in
high-redshift ultra-massive quiescent galaxies implies that
single-slit spectroscopic observations do not always capture
the full extent of the spatial variations found within these
galaxies. Thus, scaling the mass and other physical prop-
erties from the single slit, which covers only half the light of
the galaxy if perfectly centered, to the total flux of the galaxy
can result in biased stellar masses and global ages and exac-
erbate tensions with existing models of galaxy evolution.
Similarly, in the case of maximal age gradients, we find
resolved SFHs that suggest older or even entirely different
stellar populations when compared to the C24 SFHs derived
from slit-based spectroscopy, though the differences in stel-
lar population modeling also play a significant role. In the
following Section, we highlight how analyzing these ultra-
massive quiescent galaxies in a resolved context with our
fiducial model can help ease tensions with models – further
emphasizing the need for resolved IFU spectroscopy.

C24 consider this tension with an extreme value statistics
(EVS) approach (I. Harrison & P. Coles 2011; C. C. Lovell
et al. 2022). The EVS framing predicts the stellar mass
of the most-massive expected galaxy within the volume of
the PRIMER (and MINERV A) UDS observations. With this
analysis, C24 find that MINERV A-1092611 and 1189865
both exceed the 3σupper limit of the EVS model, predomi-
nantly atz>8. Notably, C24 ignore potential impacts from
mergers. While C24 disregard major mergers due to the
short time intervals over which these galaxies formed, minor
mergers could also contribute to the overall stellar mass and
build the strong color gradients we observe. At lower red-
shifts (z<3), color gradients are likely (re-)established by
the “inside-out” growth of quiescent galaxies: minor merg-
ers deposit younger/metal-poor stars at large radii, leading to
bluer colors in the outskirts (R. Bezanson et al. 2009; T. Naab
et al. 2009; K. A. Suess et al. 2019a). However, it is unclear
if there is sufficient time atz>3 for minor mergers to cre-
ate a color gradient in the<1 Gyr post-quenching. Stud-
ies place timescales for minor mergers atτ e ⩾1 Gyr (e.g.,
A. B. Newman et al. 2012), with the fastest occurring on
τe ∼1 Gyr (K. A. Suess et al. 2019b). For that reason, we
also consider the growth of these galaxies in a merger-free
context, though we note that merger rates may be faster in
more overdense environments and at higher redshifts (e.g.,
V . Rodriguez-Gomez et al. 2015; Q. Duan et al. 2025; D.
Puskás et al. 2025), and thus may contribute significantly to
the formation of massive quiescent galaxies atz>3 (R. K.
Cochrane 2025).
In Figure 5, we consider the C24 extremely-massive quies-
cent galaxies in the EVS framework using our resolved SED
modeling approach. For comparison, we use the same fidu-
cial EVS model as C24: a truncated lognormal distribution
of baryon conversion efficiency (f ⋆) with meanµ=e −2 and
standard deviationσ= 1 (i.e., 0.050<f ⋆ <0.368). We then
compute the stellar mass assembly histories for each annu-
lus individually using the best-fit SFHs and summed into a
mass assembly history for the total galaxy. While the total
stellar masses remain large relative to the most-massive ex-
pected galaxy, we find less tension overall with the fiducial
EVS model. In particular, the median mass assembly histo-
ries only exceeds the 3σupper limit atz≳11 in MINERV A-
1084946 and MINERV A-1092611 andz≳10 in MINERV A-
1208449. MINERV A-1189865 is also consistent with the
fiducial EVS model within 2σ. Crucially, the best-fit assem-
bly histories never exceed the 3σlimit of an EVS with fixed
f⋆ = 1 (i.e., all baryons are converted into stars) atz<12 (i.e.,
tlookback <0.95−1.65 Gyr). Beyond these redshifts, our non-
parametric SFHs are less certain, in part due to larger redshift
bins and fewer observational constraints, which is shown by
the Bayes’ factor test in Appendix B. Thus, while we do still
find that the mass assembly histories of MINERV A-1092611
and 1208449 exceed thef ⋆ = 1 EVS model atz>12, any ten-
sions at these redshifts are likely entirely driven by the choice
of prior for the SFH.
The mass assembly histories inferred from C24 are shown

imply much earlier formation and higher overall stellar

ages (e.g., A. C. Carnall et al. 2018; J. Leja et al. 2019a,b).

However, for MINERV A-1092611 and 1189865 these limits

of both resolved photometry and SED modeling choices.

Our results imply that both strong color gradients in
ultra-massive quiescent galaxies, particularly in MINERV A-
1084946, and different modeling prescriptions could allevi-
ate tensions withΛ-CDM. While significant contributions
from dust or metallicity gradients could lessen age gradients
and increase tension with early formation times, this study
effectively serves as a test case for maximal age gradients.
Moreover, when dust attenuation is left as a free parame-
ter in PROSPECTORSED fits (i.e., only metallicity is con-
strained to the C24 values), we still find lessened tension with
models compared to the mass assembly histories from C24.
This is due to another significant contributing factor in the
stellar mass assembly histories: the use of a non-parametric
SFH prior that prevents mass from being formed atz>20.
While this prescription only prevents star formation in the
first∼180 Myr of the Universe, any significant star forma-
tion at these redshifts causes extreme tension with the EVS
models in Figure 5, as the most massive halo would only
containM ⋆ ∼10 8 M⊙ atz= 20. The effect of thez= 20 SFH
prior is detailed further in Appendix B.
5.2.MINERVA-1189865: A possible PSB at z= 4.62?
MINERV A-1189865 differs significantly from the other
ultra-massive quiescent galaxies in this sample. Resolved
PROSPECTORfits find mass-weighted ages consistently≲
500 Myr, with the innermost annuli having ages<400 Myr.
Notably, the SFH in the central bin is indicative of a post-
starburst galaxy, with 90% of the stellar mass forming in the
∼430 Myr betweenz= 8 and 5.4 before quenching over
the next∼230 Myr, as shown by the dark blue line in the
bottom left panel of Figure 5. The sSFR gradients support
a similar PSB nature, as the log(sSFR/yr −1)∼−9.4 mea-
sured in the central 0.′′1 occupies a “transitional” region, be-
ing roughly 0.5 dex below the P. Popesso et al. (2023) SFMS
and indicating a very recent quenching event (e.g., S. Tac-
chella et al. 2019). Similarly, the de-reddenedD4000 and
DB strengths of theR≤0. ′′4 annuli in MINERV A-1189865
are firmly in the PSB range (D B >2.0 andD4000<1.4,
e.g., B. M. Poggianti & G. Barbaro 1997; G. Roberts-Borsani
et al. 2024; C. L. Steinhardt et al. 2024; S. M. Wilkins et al.
2024), further suggesting the central regions of this galaxy
have recently and rapidly quenched. The C24 stellar popu-
lation modeling findt form = 0.80±0.05 Gyr for MINERV A-
1189865, over twice as old as our best-fit age of the central
R= 0.′′1 aperture (0.29±0.07 Gyr). The older global for-
mation age inferred by C24 is likely due to the older ages
in theR= 0. ′′2 and 0.′′3 annuli, which contribute∼50% of
the total mass of MINERV A-1189865 and thus dominate the
photometry atR<0. ′′3, as well as the higher dust attenuation
(AV = 0.84±0.09 in C24 compared to 0.74±0.03 herein). If
the annular photometry for MINERV A-1189865 is modeled
with a uninformative prior onAV , we find a more comparable
global SFH to C24, though the central aperture still retains a
PSB-like SFH irrespective of the dust prior, further illustrat-
ing the importance of a resolved analysis.
Significantly, the SFHs and mass-weighted stellar ages of
outer annuli (0.′′3⩾R) in MINERV A-1189865 indicate an
older stellar population than the center. This positive age gra-
dient in the central 0.′′3 may be a result of a recent secondary
starburst in the galactic center (e.g., A. Dekel & A. Burk-
ert 2014; A. Zolotov et al. 2015; S. Tacchella et al. 2018;
E. J. Nelson et al. 2019). The lack of high levels of star
formation prior to the recent starburst (see the blue line in
the bottom left panel of Figure 5) may be primarily due to

“outshining” (C. Papovich et al. 2001), in which rest-optical
emission – MINERV A photometry only covers out to rest-
frame∼0.85µmatz= 4.62 – is dominated by light from
younger stars, thus making it challenging to disentangle pre-
vious episodes of star-formation from the most recent burst
in the absence of rest-frame NIR photometry. Several recent
studies utilizing JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy have also
found similar flat/slightly-positive age gradients and young
galactic centers in log(M ⋆/M⊙)>11 PSBs atz>3 (F.
D’Eugenio et al. 2024; A. Edward et al. in prep.). These
results highlight that massive quiescent galaxies can have a
variety of gradients in their colors and physical parameters,
even atz>3. Regardless of whether the observed gradi-
ents are positive, negative, or flat, fixed aperture photome-
try and/or slit spectroscopy are insufficient to determine the
nature of the whole galaxy, therefore producing biased star-
formation and assembly histories.
6.SUMMARY
In this work, we examine the colors and physical properties
of 4 extremely-massive,z>3 quiescent galaxies presented in
C24 and K. Glazebrook et al. (2024) within a resolved frame-
work. Utilizing high-quality mosaics (L. Robbins et al. in
prep.; N. S. Martis et al. in prep.) and photometric catalogs
(S. Cutler et al. in prep.; Y . Asada et al. in prep.) of archival
broadband and MINERV A medium-band data, we measure
the observed SEDs of these 4 galaxies in a series of 0.′′1 ellip-
tical annuli. The resolved photometry is modeled with EAzY
and PROSPECTORto measure gradients in rest-frameUV J
colors and stellar population parameters. In our stellar pop-
ulation modeling, the dust attenuation and stellar metallicity
are constrained using global measurements (including MIRI
photometry) and medium-resolution spectroscopy from C24,
respectively, in order to maximize any potential age gradi-
ents. Our main results are listed below:
(i) Ultra-massive quiescent galaxies atz>3 can have
strong gradients in both observed and rest-frame color.
In the extreme case of MINERV A-1084946, we find an
observed F150W−F277W color gradient of−0.218±
0.075 mag kpc −1 and a rest-frameU−Vcolor gradi-
ent of−0.126±0.030 mag kpc −1. These redder centers
can lead to overestimated stellar masses and ages when
photometry is measured in small apertures or slits and
scaled to the total flux of the galaxy.
(ii) Stellar population modeling from PROSPECTORsug-
gests that there could be significant negative and pos-
itive age gradients in MINERV A-1084946 and the
central 2 kpc of MINERV A-1189865, respectively,
with more mild gradients in the other two galaxies
(MINERV A-1092611 and 1208449). However, in re-
ality, dust and metallicity gradients are also possible
which would temper these maximized age gradients.
Follow-up IFU spectroscopy iscriticalto reveal which
physical parameters drive the observed color gradients.
(iii) Combining the mass assembly histories of each spa-
tial bin, we find less tension with the EVS models
of C. C. Lovell et al. (2022), especially atz<9.
This is driven both by color gradients (especially in
MINERV A-1084946) and differing stellar population
modeling prescriptions. At higher redshifts, SFHs are
much more uncertain, especially in the absence of re-
solved rest-NIR data (C. Papovich et al. 2023).
These results suggest that massive quiescent galaxies at
high redshifts should be analyzed in a resolved lens, despite
their relatively compact sizes (R e ∼1−1.5 kpc). Specifi-
cally, future “universe-breaking” candidate galaxies should
be examined for color, as well as age and/or metallicity gra-
dients, before considering violations of extreme-value statis-
tics. Likewise, careful consideration of the stellar popula-
tion modeling and priors is crucial. Compared to the ellip-
tical morphologies dominating massive quiescent galaxies at
lower redshifts, there is evidence that some of these galaxies
have both disky and elliptical substructures (e.g., C. Turner
et al. 2025; L. Kawinwanichakij et al. 2026), which sug-
gest at least two distinct stellar populations within the galaxy,
even in the absence of mergers. It is therefore possible that
atz>3, the stellar masses and ages of massive quiescent
galaxies can be overestimated when using global photom-
etry in tandem with single-slit spectroscopy, particularly in
cases where the slit is not aligned to the semi-major axis of a
galaxy. Moving forward, deeper photometry at bluer wave-
lengths (λ rest <4000Å) is crucial in improving constraints
on rest-frame colors and ages, especially in the faint galactic
outskirts. Ultimately, while our photometric analysis pro-
vides evidence of these effects, IFU spectroscopy will be
needed to fully break the age-dust-metallicity degeneracy in
a resolved framework.

B.EFFECTS OF THEZ= 20 STAR FORMATION LIMIT
To assess the impact of imposing no star formation before
z= 20 in our PROSPECTORmodeling, we fit the slit-based
photometry with the same modeling assumptions as we use
for our fiducial model with the strong prior on dust atten-
uation removed. Then, we re-ran the fitting with the sin-
gle modification of removing thez max = 20 limit on star for-
mation used in our fiducial model. This change resulted in
typically higher stellar mass assembly histories atz≳8 for
all four of our objects. To test the impact of this modifica-
tion on the goodness-of-fit, we estimated the Bayes’ factor
between the two models as the ratio of the maximum natu-
ral log-evidences (and their uncertainties) as determined by
DYNESTYwithin the PROSPECTORfitting procedure:

We find an average factor ofBF∼1.50±0.15. This indi-

thez max,SF = 20, suggesting that the data do not prefer ei-

C.SUPER+MIRI PROSPECTORMODELING

(where available) in F770W, F1280W, F1500W and F1800W
are used in tandem with the super (D=1. ′′4) color-aperture
photometry to measure a global value ofA V. We use the

median values of both posteriors agree well within 1σ. See
Appendix A for more information on the specific priors we
use in our fiducial model.
