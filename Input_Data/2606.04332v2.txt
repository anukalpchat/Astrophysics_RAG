Radiation-induced electron spin polarization in ultrarelativistic kinetic turbulence
Peng Liu, 1 Karen Z. Hatsagortsyan, 2 Christoph H. Keitel, 2 and Zheng Gong 1,∗
1Institute of Theoretical Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China
2Max-Planck-Institut f¨ ur Kernphysik, Saupfercheckweg 1, 69117 Heidelberg, Germany
(Dated: June 5, 2026)
Electron spin polarization in radiative plasmas with ultrarelativistic kinetic turbulence under
highly magnetized conditions is investigated using particle-in-cell simulations. We observe that a
significant spin polarization can be sustained when the leptons undergo energetic photon emission
accompanied by spin flips during the nonequilibrium turbulent evolution. By analyzing the time
evolution of spatially dependent spin polarization, we identify an electromagnetic (EM) regime of
kinetic turbulence, distinct from the well-known density-dominated regime characterized by vortex
currents and magnetic islands. While in the latter regime the spin polarization exists only transiently,
in the EM regime significant anisotropic net polarization emerges and persists in non-dissipative
scenarios. The correlation between spin signals and turbulence features is leveraged to introduce
the characteristic parameter delimiting the EM regime via the ratio of electric and magnetic energy
densities and to gain insight into complex plasma turbulence. This study demonstrates the versatility
of a spin-resolved study of the plasma turbulence in extreme environments, such as black holes and
magnetar magnetospheres.
Plasma kinetic turbulence (PKT) is a chaotic, multi-
scale motion of a plasma where the physics can no
longer be described by fluid dynamics. Here, the en-
ergy injected at macroscopic scales cascades down to
kinetic scales through nonlinear wave-particle interac-
tions [1–8], leading to dissipation and nonthermal par-
ticle acceleration [9–20]. In nonrelativistic plasmas,
PKT governs anomalous transport and confinement in
magnetic fusion [21–23], drives Fermi-like stochastic
cosmic-ray acceleration in laser-produced counterstream-
ing plumes [24, 25], modulates ion cyclotron damping
in Earth’s magnetosheath [26], and regulates solar-wind
heating in the heliosphere [27–29]. In relativistic out-
flows from active galactic nuclei [30–32] and gamma-ray
bursts [33–35], PKT mediates the conversion of ordered
bulk energy into chaotic particle motion, shaping the ob-
served nonthermal spectra [36, 37]. In pulsar wind neb-
ulae, turbulence downstream of the termination shock
enables efficient particle acceleration and powers bright
synchrotron and inverse-Compton emission [38, 39], with
the Crab Nebula providing a canonical example of an ul-
trarelativistic, magnetized turbulence radiating from ra-
dio toγ-rays [40–42].
In highly magnetized gamma-ray bursts [33–35] and
pulsar [43–45] or magnetar magnetospheres [46–49], PKT
enters into the radiative-cooling-dominated regime [50–
54], where rapid energy loss fundamentally alters the
turbulent cascade and particle energization. Similarly,
in collisionless accretion flows near compact objects [55–
57], magnetorotational instability develops into radiative
PKT, driving energetic variability around supermassive
black holes [58–61]. When such turbulence reaches the
quantum electrodynamical conditions, with the quantum
strong-field parameterχ e ≳0.1 [62–64], radiative cool-
ing proceeds through discrete high-energy photon emis-
sion accompanied by a photon recoil, and lepton spin-
flip transitions [65–73]. These processes can induce sub-
stantial net spin polarization (SP) correlated with as-
trophysical relevant scenarios, including abnormal fila-
mentation in ultrarelativistic beam transportation [74],
polarized lepton pair cascades in pulsar polar caps [75],
and anomalousγ-ray polarization in magnetic reconnec-
tion [76]. Whether ultrarelativistic radiative PKT can in-
trinsically generate SP, and to what extent the resulting
spin signatures encode information about the underlying
collective turbulence dynamics in highly magnetized en-
vironments, such as pulsar [43–45] or magnetar magneto-
spheres [47–49], and compact black-hole coronae [58–60]
are still open questions.
In this Letter, we investigate radiative SP effects in
electron-positron plasma during strongly magnetized ki-
netic turbulence. Using PIC simulations, we show that
leptons are rapidly accelerated by inductive electric fields
during the initial nonequilibrium state. Then intense
radiative spin-flips occur for leptons as the turbulence
evolves, and consequently result in a substantial net SP.
By tracking the spatiotemporal evolution of SP, we iden-
tify an electromagnetic (EM) regime of PKT, charac-
terized by near equipartition between electric and mag-
netic energy. This regime is fundamentally different from
well-studied density-dominated (DD) turbulence, which
is marked by vortex-like magnetic islands and compressed
current-sheet flow. In the DD regime, electrons exhibit
a slow and nearly isotropic depolarization. In contrast,
within the EM regime, SP evolves anisotropically and
can either decay or undergo modest amplification, de-
pending on the turbulent dynamics. Thus, the radiative
SP, which emerges naturally in ultrarelativistic magne-
tized PKT, introduce an additional dynamical degree of
freedom that ‘probes’ otherwise inaccessible turbulence
regimes in extreme plasmas.

In conclusion, we demonstrate a dynamical spin-
polarized state in ultrarelativistic PKT. The accumu-
lation of spin polarization and its anisotropic depolar-
ization are shown to correlate with characteristic fea-
tures of PKT in an EM regime, distinct from the pre-
viously studied DD turbulence [11–15]. These findings
indicate that spin-polarized turbulence could intrinsically
exist in magnetorotational instabilities [82] or reconnec-
tion [83] near hyper-accreting disks on stellar-mass black
holes [84–86], gamma-ray burst engines [87], and magne-
tar magnetospheres [88] with violently twisted magnetic
fields≳10 10G. The modification of photon polarization
by transient spin-polarized leptons in PKT might offer
an alternative interpretation of the nontrivial polariza-
tion angles ofγ-ray emission from the Crab pulsar [89] or
the binary system Cygnus X-1 [90], as well as of prompt

5
emission mechanisms in gamma-ray bursts [91]. Besides,
the EM regime identified here represents a key element
of highly-magnetized kinetic turbulence potentially pre-
vailing in softγ-ray repeaters [92, 93] and energetic pul-
sars [94, 95] with magnetizationσ≳10 4. With the ad-
vent of powerful beam facilities, spin-polarized pair tur-
bulence may be accessible in the near future [96–101],
promoting our understanding of PKT features across dis-
tinct regimes and scenarios.
