Abstract
The recent detection of an atmosphere surrounding the trans-Neptunian object (TNO) 2002 XV 93 from stel-
lar occultation measurements has challenged the longstanding view that only the largest TNOs can sustain an
atmosphere. Atmospheric refraction modeling of the occultation light curves indicated a surface pressure of
100–200 nbar, despite 2002 XV93’s relatively small size (∼510 km in diameter) and weak surface gravity. To-
gether with the detection of methane fluorescence on Makemake, this result suggests that tenuous atmospheres
may be more common among TNOs than previously thought. We report JWST/NIRSpec observations acquired
before and after the 2024 stellar occultation measurements, obtained with the PRISM and medium-resolution
gratings at resolving powers of∼100 and∼1000, respectively. We detect no statistically significant emission
features attributed to methane or carbon monoxide gas. By comparing the higher spectral resolution data with
synthetic fluorescence models, we report upper limits for the methane and carbon monoxide surface partial pres-
sures of (3−10)×10 −6 and (50−300)×10 −6 nbar, respectively, substantially below the atmospheric pressure
inferred from the occultation measurements. Additionally, we report no evidence of an extended source of either
methane gas or refractory material. Our results indicate that the atmospheric interpretation of the occultation
measurements may require either a composition dominated by volatile species other than methane and carbon
monoxide, with nitrogen and argon as possible candidates, or a methane-dominated atmosphere confined near
the surface with a steeply decreasing vertical density profile.

1. Introduction
The trans-Neptunian object (612533) 2002 XV93 is a mid-
sized plutino that lies in the 3:2 mean motion resonance
with Neptune. A radiometric analysis of thermal infrared
measurements from the Spitzer Space Telescope and the
Herschel Space Observatory indicated a mean diameter of
∼550 km and a dark surface with an optical geometric albedo
of 4% (Mommert et al. 2012). Infrared spectroscopy of
2002 XV93 from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)
uncovered the presence of water (H 2O) and carbon dioxide
(CO2) ices on the surface (Pinilla-Alonso et al. 2025). Re-
markably, time-series measurements of a stellar occultation
event on UT 2024 January 10 displayed a distinctive photo-
metric signature at ingress and egress that is consistent with
a thin atmosphere surrounding 2002 XV 93. Analysis of the
occultation chords indicated a sky-projected disk diameter
of∼510 km, while atmospheric refraction modeling of the
ingress and egress light curves yielded a surface pressure of
100–200 nbar (Arimatsu et al. 2026).
Until recently, only the largest trans-Neptunian objects
(TNOs) were known to host atmospheres. The atmospheres
on Pluto and Triton have surface pressures on the order of
10µbar and molecular compositions dominated by nitro-
gen (N2), with minor contributions from methane (CH4) and
carbon monoxide (CO). For detailed reviews of TNO atmo-
spheres, see, for example, Stern & Trafton (2008), Young
et al. (2020), and Young et al. (2021); see also the recent
JWST atmospheric study of Pluto by Lellouch et al. (2025).
Spectroscopy of the dwarf planet Makemake obtained by
JWST in 2023 revealed characteristic emission features of
gaseous CH4 (Protopapa et al. 2025). Comparisons with flu-
orescence models suggested that the observed spectral signa-
ture may arise from either localized outgassing or a tenuous
bound atmosphere; in the atmospheric scenario, the inferred
CH4 surface partial pressure is of order∼10 pbar, six or-
ders of magnitude below the corresponding levels on Pluto
and Triton. Meanwhile, stellar occultation measurements of
other large TNOs—including Eris, Haumea, and Quaoar—
have so far not yielded definitive detections of global at-
mospheres, with surface pressure upper limits spanning 1–
100 nbar (Sicardy et al. 2011; Ortiz et al. 2017; Proudfoot
et al. 2025).

atmosphere of 2002 XV 93 is∼100 times thinner than Pluto

lies well above the∼1 pbar threshold below which the at-

the age of the Solar System (e.g., Schaller & Brown 2007;
Stern & Trafton 2008; Young et al. 2020; Protopapa et al.
2025). Arimatsu et al. (2026) calculated a characteristic es-

plying rapid volatile loss.
In this context, the atmospheric detection on 2002 XV 93
may indicate a transient phenomenon, possibly associated
with a recent impact event or ongoing cryovolcanism (Ari-
matsu et al. 2026). The likely transient nature of its atmo-
sphere, combined with the object’s substantially lower sur-
face gravity compared to the dwarf planets (0.1 m s −2 vs.
0.4 m s −2 for Makemake and 0.61 m s −2 for Pluto) places
2002 XV93 in a hitherto unexplored regime for TNO atmo-
spheric studies, providing a valuable testing ground for our
broader understanding of cold, tenuous atmospheres.
In this Letter, we present an analysis of JWST spectro-
scopic observations of 2002 XV 93 obtained in 2022 and
2024. Motivated by the recent occultation result, we derive
quantitative constraints on the presence and abundances of
the key volatile species CH 4 and CO through comparisons
with synthetic fluorescence models. Our results will serve as
an important reference point for future efforts aimed at un-
derstanding the physical nature, composition, and temporal
evolution of 2002 XV93’s atmosphere.
2. Observations and Data Reduction
2002 XV93 was observed with the integral field unit (IFU)
on JWST’s Near Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec) during
two separate epochs. The first observation was obtained us-
ing the double-pass PRISM disperser on UT 2022 November
3 as part of Cycle 1 Program #2418 (PI: N. Pinilla-Alonso;
Pinilla-Alonso et al. 2025), producing a continuous spectrum
from 0.6 to 5.3µm at a spectral resolution ofR∼30–300.
The second observation occurred on UT 2024 October 8 as
part of Cycle 3 Program #4627 (PI: M. Brown) and utilized
the medium-resolution G395M grating with the F290LP fil-
ter, which provides coverage of the 2.9–5.1µm region at
R∼1000. Both observations employed a 4-point dither pat-
tern to mitigate the effects of cosmic rays and improve the
spatial sampling of the target’s point-spread function (PSF).
Table 1 lists the observational details of the two JWST visits,
including the heliocentric distancer h, observer distance∆,
and phase angleαof 2002 XV 93 at the midpoint of the ob-
servations. For comparison, the viewing geometry of the UT
2024 January 10 occultation measurements is also included.
The publicly accessible data from the JWST observations
were downloaded from the Mikulski Archive for Space Tele-
scopes (MAST) and analyzed using thejwstspectool
(Wong 2025).1 The stacks of uncalibrated up-the-ramp de-
tector readouts were passed through the first two stages of the
official JWST calibration pipeline (Version 2.0.1; Bushouse
et al. 2026) to produce dark-corrected, flat-fielded, flux-
calibrated, and spatially-rectified IFU data cubes for each
dithered exposure. The 1/fdetector readnoise systematic
was removed from the intermediate countrate images us-
ing the optionalclean_flicker_noisestep. To ex-
amine the target’s PSF profile in detail, we also generated
dither-combined data cubes using Stage 3 of the calibration
pipeline, with theoutlier_detectionstep skipped to
prevent spurious flagging of pixels near the target centroid
during the resampling process.
We carried out custom spectral extraction on the individual
dithered exposures by applying the specialized empirical PSF
fitting method contained injwstspec. Each wavelength
slice was fit with a PSF template derived from the data cube
using a moving-median filter with a width of 21 wavelength
elements. The spectrum was then measured from the best-fit
PSF templates within a 6-pixel-diameter (0
′′
.6) circular aper-
ture centered on the target’s calculated centroid position. The
background flux level was set as the median pixel value out-
side of a 21×21 pixel box surrounding the target. The in-
dividual dither spectra were cleaned of outliers using a 21-
point-wide 5σmoving median filter before being averaged
together to produce the dither-combined irradiance spectrum.

To correct for wavelength-dependent aperture flux losses,
calibration curves were derived from JWST observations
of the solar-type standard stars SNAP-2 (PRISM; Program
#1128) and GSPC P330-E (G395M; Program #6606) that uti-
lized the same dither and readout patterns. Those data were
processed in an identical manner to the 2002 XV 93 observa-
tions, and the spectra were extracted using the same aper-
ture size and background region. We then computed the ratio
between the measured stellar spectra and the corresponding
CALSPEC models (Bohlin et al. 2014), smoothed the ratio
arrays using a third-order Savitzky–Golay filter, and multi-
plied the resultant vectors to the 2002 XV 93 spectra. To re-
move regions of low throughput and poor data quality, the
PRISM spectrum was trimmed to exclude wavelengths be-
low 0.9µm. The final irradiance spectra of 2002 XV 93 from
the two JWST observations are shown in panel a of Figure 1.
3. Modeling and Discussion
The low-resolution PRISM spectrum of 2002 XV 93 dis-
plays major absorption bands of H 2O ice at 1.5, 2.0, 3.0,
and 4.5µm, as well as a prominent Fresnel reflectance peak
at 3.1µm. The absorption band at∼4.25µm is attributed
to the fundamentalν 3 asymmetric stretching mode of solid
CO2. The medium-resolution G395M spectrum reveals the
morphology of this CO 2 feature in greater detail, showing
enhanced reflectance shoulders above the local continuum
that bracket a strongly asymmetric absorption centered near
4.27µm. At 4.38µm, theν 3 band of the 13CO2 isotopo-
logue is also discernible. Overall, the spectral profile of
2002 XV93 is consistent with the broader class of TNOs that
exhibit prominent H2O-ice features—one of the three major
compositional types that have been identified from ensemble

4
analyses of JWST TNO spectra (Pinilla-Alonso et al. 2025;
Holler et al. 2025; Wong et al. 2025a).
At the low temperatures of TNO surfaces (≲50 K), only
the most volatile molecules—N 2, CH 4, and CO—and their
photochemical products can persist in the gas phase and sus-
tain a thin atmosphere. We probed the G395M spectrum of
2002 XV 93 for the spectral signatures of gaseous volatiles,
focusing on CH 4 and CO. Under the non-local thermody-
namic equilibrium (non-LTE) conditions expected in rarefied
atmospheres, CH 4 and CO are predicted to fluoresce fol-
lowing excitation by solar radiation, producing characteristic
emission features superimposed on the continuum that would
be resolvable at the spectral resolution of the G395M grating.
Although N2 may be the dominant species in the atmosphere,
its near-infrared features are limited to collision-induced ab-
sorption in the fundamental 4.3µm band and its overtone
at 2.15µm(e.g., Lafferty et al. 1996), resulting in negligible
opacities in sub-millibar atmospheres. Moreover, the N2 fun-
damental band coincides with the CO2-ice absorption feature
on the surface (see Figure 1, panel a). It would therefore
be challenging to disentangle any possible weak N2 emission
lines from the complex underlying solid-state spectral pro-
file.
The strongest CH 4 fluorescence band in the near-infrared
is centered at 3.31µm and corresponds to theν 3 asymmetric
stretching mode. To search for the spectral signature of atmo-
spheric CH4, we modeled the 3.25–3.40µm continuum with
a cubic polynomial function and subtracted the best-fit trend
from the irradiance spectrum. The continuum-removed spec-
trum in the CH 4 fluorescence region is shown in panel b of
Figure 1. The computed root-mean-square (RMS) metric of
the residual array—0.073µJy—quantifies the intrinsic scat-
ter of the data across this wavelength range. The residuals
exhibit significant correlated noise, and we therefore adopted
the RMS value as an empirical estimate of the 1σdetection
limit for atmospheric emission features. In the case of CO,
the fundamentalv= 1 stretching band is located near 4.7µm.
We removed the local continuum using a cubic polynomial fit
across the 4.55–4.80µm region. The resulting residual spec-
trum is shown in panel c of Figure 1 and has an RMS value
of 0.096µJy.
To derive constraints on the abundance of CH 4 and CO
in 2002 XV 93’s atmosphere, we compared the measured
continuum-subtracted irradiance to synthetic atmospheric
model spectra, following a similar semi-empirical approach
to the one used to interpret the CH 4 ν3 fluorescence band
detected in Makemake’s JWST/NIRSpec G395M spectrum
(Protopapa et al. 2025). We considered isothermal atmo-
spheres at different kinetic temperatures (T kin) and incorpo-
rated prescribed vibrational temperature (T vib) profiles into
an otherwise LTE radiative transfer framework using the for-
malism of Edwards et al. (1993). We adopted a representa-
tive surface pressure of 160 nbar, consistent with the range
of best-fit pressures inferred from the atmospheric refraction
analysis of the 2024 stellar occultation event (Arimatsu et al.
2026), and adjusted the CH 4 and CO mixing ratios, which
were assumed to be vertically uniform.
While Makemake’s atmosphere may be in hydrostatic
equilibrium, with pressure decreasing exponentially with al-
titudez, this is almost certainly not the case for 2002 XV 93,
given the object’s small size (R∼255 km) and correspond-
ingly weak surface gravity. As pointed out by Arimatsu et al.
(2026), the Jeans parameter of 2002 XV 93’s atmosphere is
close to unity. It follows that the hydrodynamic state of
the atmosphere is expected to approach the free molecu-
lar flow regime, with the density decreasing asr −2, where
r=R+z(e.g., V olkov et al. 2011). We therefore adopted
ther −2 density profile and considered three different gasT kin
values—30, 70, and 100 K. The first temperature represents
an endmember cold atmosphere that is unaffected by radia-
tive processes, while the other two are typical of Pluto’s at-
mospheric temperatures in the upper atmosphere and near the
stratopause. Ther −2 density profile entails a large spatial ex-
tent for 2002 XV93’s atmosphere, and we tailored the model
outputs to the aperture used for extracting the JWST spec-
trum. At∆= 37.86 au, a 0
′′
.6-diameter aperture corresponds
to∼16,500 km. When producing disk-integrated synthetic
spectra, we calculated the limb-tangent radiances for alti-
tudes up to 50,000 km and spatially convolved the results
using a two-dimensional Gaussian with a full width at half
maximum equal to the aperture size.
To estimate theT vib profiles, we followed the guidance
from the fully self-consistent non-LTE models developed for
Triton (Wong et al. 2025b, 2026), taking into account the
difference in heliocentric distance between Triton (29.9 au)
and 2002 XV 93 (37.9 au). We only included the primary
CH4 ν3 and CO(1–0) bands, as the combination and hot
bands (e.g., CH 4 ν3 +ν 4 −ν 4 and CO(2–1)) are expected to
be much weaker. Under fluorescence equilibrium—a rea-
sonable assumption in the upper part of the atmosphere—
the expectedT vib for the CH4 ν3 and CO(1–0) levels are 5.5
and 4.5 K colder at 2002 XV 93 compared to Triton, respec-
tively. Thus, for the CO(1–0) band, we based theT vib pro-
file on the roughly isothermal value of 172 K calculated for
Triton (Wong et al. 2025b, 2026) and adopted an altitude-
independentT vib of 167.5 K. For CH 4, the non-LTE models
of Triton’s atmosphere indicate thatTvib transitions from fluo-
rescence equilibrium in the upper atmosphere to a monotoni-
cally decreasing trend in the denser lower atmosphere, where
Tvib becomes influenced byT kin and the departure from LTE
is pressure-dependent. The prescribed CH4 and COT vib pro-
files are shown in Figure 2.

Our modeling has constrained the abundance of CH 4 and
CO in 2002 XV 93’s atmosphere to at most trace amounts.
It follows that the bulk gas composition must be dominated
by other species that do not show strong fluorescence signa-
tures in the observed wavelength range, such as N 2 or a no-
ble gas (e.g., argon). However, invoking such hypervolatile
compounds exacerbates the issue of the extremely short at-
mospheric depletion timescale from Jeans escape. An al-
ternative explanation is that 2002 XV 93 could actually host
a CH 4-dominated atmosphere, but the gas is restricted to a
near-surface layer, with the density falling off steeply with
altitude due to insolation-driven CH 4 photolysis. This sce-
nario has been invoked to explain the lack of fluorescent
CH4 emission on Triton, even though CH 4 is known to be
present in Triton’s atmosphere and is detected in absorption
at near-infrared wavelengths (Lellouch et al. 2010; Wong
et al. 2025b, 2026). In this case, some replenishment mecha-
nism would be necessary to maintain a sufficiently high con-
centration of CH4 gas to account for the surface pressure in-
ferred from the stellar occultation measurement.
The hydrodynamical regime of gaseous volatiles in
2002 XV93’s atmosphere raises the possibility of significant
outflow, which may entrain solid grains. This scenario is
analogous to cometary comae, where sublimation from the
surface or sub-surface expels dust and/or ice, leading to spa-
tially extended emission around the central nucleus. In the
case of 2002 XV 93, diffuse solid-state material would con-
tribute to the observed occultation signature and could there-
fore lower the density of the gaseous atmosphere necessary
to reproduce the ingress and egress light curves. To search
for evidence of extended emission, we examined the shape
of 2002 XV 93’s PSF in the calibrated dither-combined im-
ages. We computed the azimuthally averaged radial profiles
in two wavelength ranges: (1) 3.25–3.40µm, which spans the
region of CH4 fluorescence, and (2) 3.5–3.7µm, which sam-
ples the nearby continuum and is sensitive to ejected grains.
These profiles were compared to analogously derived radial
profiles of the solar-type standard star GSPC P330-E. As il-
lustrated in Figure 3, the spatial morphology of 2002 XV93’s
PSF is indistinguishable from that of a point source at all sep-
arations, indicating the nondetection of both a CH4 coma and
extended emission from solid-state material. It is important
to note, however, that due to the large heliocentric distance of
2002 XV93 and the corresponding coarse spatial resolution of
the NIRSpec IFU data cubes (∼2,750 km per pixel), we can-
not entirely exclude the possibility of extended structures that
are confined close to the surface.
