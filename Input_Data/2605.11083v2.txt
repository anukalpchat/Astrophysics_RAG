that regulate the growth of structure, such as the present-day matter

Beyond the bias-weighted pressure, it is also useful to consider
the quantityd𝑦/d𝑧, to which we refer to as the thermal history. At a
given redshift, the bias-weighted pressure is proportional tod𝑦/d𝑧,

studies. We anticipate that the bias will also depend on cosmology
and baryon physics and therefore any mismatch between assumed
and actual bias will propagate into systematic uncertainties in the
inferred thermal energy history.

2023; Kugel et al. 2023). FLAMINGO combines Gpc-scale vol-

back prescriptions, all calibrated against observed galaxy and clus-

spectra across 18 simulation variants spanning different values of

The main objectives of the present study are: (i) to quantify the

4 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
We have used the FLAMINGO suite of cosmological hydrodynam-
ical simulations to study the bias-weighted mean electron pressure
⟨𝑏h𝑃e⟩,thethermalhistoryd𝑦/d𝑧,andtheeffective𝑦-weightedhalo
bias𝑏 𝑦 across 18 simulation variants spanning different cosmolo-
gies and baryonic feedback implementations. Cross-correlating tSZ
intensitymapswithgalaxyorquasarsurveysyieldsadirectmeasure-
ment of⟨𝑏 h𝑃e⟩as a function of redshift, where𝑏h is the linear halo
bias, quantifying how the massive haloes that dominate the thermal
pressure cluster relative to the underlying matter field, and𝑃e is the
mean electron pressure. With a model for the𝑦-weighted halo bias
𝑏 𝑦, one can further derive the thermal historyd𝑦/d𝑧, which charac-
terises the redshift evolution of the Compton𝑦parameter. Our main
findings are:
• ⟨𝑏 h𝑃e⟩is well converged with resolution and box size in
FLAMINGO, with differences below 5 per cent between the in-
termediate (m9) and high (m8) resolution runs (top left panel of
Fig. 2).
• ⟨𝑏 h𝑃e⟩depends steeply on cosmology, scaling as𝑆 𝜖(𝑧)
8 with
an effective exponent𝜖(𝑧) ≈3over the redshift range0.1≤𝑧≤1
(see Fig. 3). The Planck cosmology (𝑆8 =0.833) systematically
overpredicts the observational data, while the LS8 cosmology (𝑆8 =
0.766) provides the best fit (top right panel of Fig. 2).
•Baryonic feedback variations are subdominant at low redshift
but become increasingly important at higher redshifts (bottom pan-
els of Fig. 2). Stronger feedbackincreases⟨𝑏h𝑃e⟩by redistributing
thermal energy to larger scales (see Fig. 10 and Fig. 11) — opposite
to the effect of feedback in cosmic shear analyses.
•We find that the FLAMINGO model with a low𝑆8 cosmol-
ogy and strong feedback (i.e., LS8−fgas−8𝜎) provides the best fit
totheobservational measurementsof⟨𝑏 h𝑃e⟩,consistentwith recent
studies examining the tSZ power spectrum (McCarthy et al. 2025;
Raghunathan et al. 2026), tSZ–cosmic shear cross-spectrum (Mc-
Carthy et al. 2023, 2025), and kSZ effect stacking measurements of
massive galaxies (McCarthy et al. 2025; Siegel et al. 2026).
•Weintroduceacompactphenomenologicalparameterisationof
⟨𝑏h𝑃e⟩as a joint function of𝑆8 and the baryon fraction of group-
mass haloes (Eq. (13)), which reproduces all FLAMINGO variants
(excluding NoCooling) over0.05≤𝑧≤1with a mean error of
1.7±1.3per cent (worst case≈7per cent; Fig. A1). An MCMC
inference exercise using this model, marginalising over the model-
parameter uncertainties, yields𝑆8 =0.72 +0.03

Leaving aside these observational challenges, our results demon-
strate that thermal pressure statistics provide a powerful and com-

on cosmological parameters from an entirely independent channel.
