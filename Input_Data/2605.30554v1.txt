By varying the symmetry energy slope ( L), I investigate how the exotic content within the interiors
of neutron stars changes and how it aﬀects both macroscopic a nd microscopic quantities. Using two
diﬀerent parametrizations (L3 ωρ and BigApple), and three diﬀerent possibilities about the n eutron
star core (nucleons+hyperons, nucleons+deltas, nucleons +hyperons+deltas), I show that, for the
models analyzed in this work, changing the slope barely chan ges the amount of hyperons, but it can
strongly suppress the ∆ resonances for large values of L. I also show that, in general, the presence
of exotic content will be more evident for lower values of L than for large ones. Diﬀerences and
similarities between the two parametrizations are also ana lyzed.
I. INTRODUCTION
The Pauli exclusion principle states that two or more
fermions cannot simultaneously occupy the same quan-
tum state within a system that obeys the laws of quan-
tum mechanics [
1]. Neutron stars, conversely, are cold
objects (T = 0 K is a valid approximation) formed in
principle by neutrons, protons, and leptons in a chem-
ical equilibrium conﬁguration. As the density increases
towards the star’s core, the energy of the nucleons also in-
creases. At suﬃciently high densities, the increase in the
nucleon chemical potentials, driven by Pauli blocking, to-
gether with the constraints of chemical equilibrium, ren-
ders it energetically favorable to replace part of the nucle-
onic population by more exotic, non-nucleonic baryonic
degrees of freedom. The more usual exotic baryons that
can be present at the neutron stars’ core are the hyper-
ons [
2]. The study of hyperons in neutron stars is an old
subject [ 3, 4] but is still an active ﬁeld nowadays [ 5–7].
Indeed, two QHD-based papers [ 8, 9] suggest that hyper-
ons are inevitable, and changes in parameterizations or
even the inclusion of additional repulsive ﬁelds (such as
theφ meson) cannot suppress the hyperon threshold. In
the same sense, the inevitability of hyperons is not ex-
clusive of QHD-based models; ab initio approaches also
indicate that hyperons are always present, as can be seen
in Refs. [
10, 11].
Beyond hyperons, other exotic non-nucleonic degrees
of freedom that can be present at the neutron star core
are the ∆ resonances. Although the ∆ particles are more
massive than the Λ 0 hyperon, they present a much more
attractive potential depth. Indeed, modern results point
out that the ∆ potential depths are stronger than the
nucleon itself, U∆ ≈ -90 to -100 MeV [
12]. In recent
years, several authors have explored the eﬀect of such a
high potential depth [
13–16], the main conclusion being
that not only are the ∆ resonances present, but the ∆ −
is the ﬁrst exotic degree of freedom to appear.
Concerning nuclear properties, the authors of two dif-
ferent review articles (ref. [ 17, 18]), were able to constrain
ﬁve nuclear quantities at saturation density: saturation
density itself (n0), eﬀective nucleon mass ( M ∗
N/M N ) the
binding energy per baryon ( B/A ), (in) compressibility
(K), and symmetry energy ( S0). A sixth quantity, the
symmetry energy slope, or simply the slope ( L), is still
a matter of debate. In the early 2010s, most studies
pointed to a relatively low value for L. For instance, in
refs. [
19–21] upper limits of 54.6, 61.9, and 66 MeV, re-
spectively, were suggested. However, the situation has
changed in the last couple of years, and new experiments
have pointed to a signiﬁcantly higher upper limit. For
example, in a study on the spectra of pions in interme-
diate energy collisions, an upper limit of 117 MeV was
obtained [
22], while in one of the PREX II analyses [ 23]
an upper limit of 143 MeV was suggested. All these con-
ﬂicting results have been well summarized in a recent
paper [
24]: the CREX group points to a slope in the
range 0 < L < 51 MeV, while PREX II results point
to 76 MeV < L < 165 MeV. The CREX and PREX II
results do not overlap. It is a huge problem that must be
solved.
In addition to the constraints related to nuclear physics
at the saturation density, some constraints come from
neutron star observations, which are related to the be-
haviour of the nuclear matter at densities far above the
saturation point. For example, the mass and radius of
the PSR J0740+6620 lies at M⊙ = 2. 08 ± 0. 07 and 11.41
km < R < 13.70 km respectively [
25]. Concerning the
canonical 1.4 M⊙ star, two NICER teams have pointed
to a limit of 13 . 85 km [ 26] and 14. 26 km [ 27]. These re-
sults were reﬁned in ref. [ 28] to 11.80 km <R 1. 4< 13. 10
km. A weaker and more conservative constraint com-
ing from state-of-the-art theoretical results at low and
high baryon density points to an upper limit of R1. 4 <
13.6 km [
29]. Still on the canonical star, another impor-
tant quantity and constraint is the so-called dimension-
less tidal deformability parameter Λ. The gravitational
wave observations by LIGO/VIRGO in the GW170817
event put constraints on the dimensionless tidal param-
eter of the canonical star Λ 1. 4 < 800 [
30]. This result
was then reﬁned in ref. [ 31], to 70 < Λ 1. 4. < 580. A
more conservative constraint was suggested in ref. [ 32],
pointing out the range 133 < Λ 1. 4< 686 from a Bayesian
analysis.
In this work, I study the inﬂuence of the symmetry
energy slope on the exotic content of neutron stars. To

study the model-dependency of the results, I use two dif-
ferent parameterizations of the quantum hadrodynamics

43, 44]. I select a soft EOS, the L3 ωρ [45], and

stars with masses M ≳ 2. 0M⊙ , even in the presence of

stiﬀer; otherwise, the predictions would be too similar.
Consequently, the BigApple parametrization is in tension
with some constraints coming from astronomical observa-
tion, especially the dimensionless tidal parameter of the
canonical star. It is, nevertheless, used here mainly as an
illustrative stiﬀ-limit case to explore model dependence.
Both chosen parameterizations satisfy all ﬁve constraints
from nuclear matter at saturation density discussed in
Refs. [
17, 18], while exhibiting distinct behavior at high
densities and, consequently, diﬀerent neutron-star prop-
erties.

III. MICROSCOPIC RESULTS - P AR TICLE
POPULATION
I now study how diﬀerent values of the slope aﬀect the
population of exotic particles on neutron stars. I used
three diﬀerent possibilities for the neutron star core: hy-
peronic matter, consisting of hyperons (Y) and nucleons
(N); ∆ matter, with nucleons and ∆ resonances (D); and
∆ admixed hyperonic matter, consisting of NYD matter.
Also, instead of plotting the total population of particles
for ﬁxed values of L, here I show the amount of the de-
sired particle for diﬀerent values of L. This allows us to
see the diﬀerences in one particle population even when
the inﬂuence of the slope is small.
Furthermore, the slope not only aﬀects the population
of exotic particles. The proton fraction is also strongly
dependent on the slope at low and high densities. The
proton fraction for six diﬀerent parameterizations with

4
diﬀerent values of L can be seen in Fig. 1 of ref. [ 41] and
will remain unchanged until the onset of the ﬁrst exotic
particle.
A. Hyperonic matter
I start with the NY matter. Due to the high repul-
sive potential, combined with a strong coupling to the
ω meson, the Σ triplet is not present, independently of
the parametrization as well as the slope. The hyperons
present are the Λ 0 and the Ξ − , and the results are shown
in Fig.
1. From a qualitative point of view, increasing the
slope does not signiﬁcantly change the onset of Λ 0, but
it does suppress this particle at higher densities. This
result is exacerbated for the L = 92 MeV in the L3 ωρ ,
whose onset is pushed to higher densities. The threshold
of the Ξ − , on the other hand, is pulled to lower densities
as we increase the slope. From a quantitative point of
view, it can be seen that there is little diﬀerence between
L = 44 and L = 60 MeV. The diﬀerences are relevant for
L = 92 MeV, especially for L3 ωρ , because this parame-
terization has Λ ωρ = 0. As discussed in refs. [
39, 41], the
Λ ωρ reduces the ρ ﬁeld at high densities. Furthermore,
for L = 92 MeV, within L3 ωρ , the Λ 0 particle is sup-
pressed at low densities and the onset of the Ξ − becomes
the ﬁrst exotic particle to appear. This was ﬁrst noted
in ref. [
48] and it is due to both, Λ ωρ = 0, as well the
presence of the mixed term gΣΛ ρ .
Finally, it can also be pointed out that in the BigApple
parameterization, the appearance of exotic particles oc-
curs at lower densities and their population reaches
higher values, compared to L3 ωρ . For example, in the
BigAppale parameterization, for L = 44 MeV, the thresh-
old Λ 0 occurs around 0.36 fm − 3 and the population of Λ 0
reaches a fraction YΛ = 0.22 at 1.0 fm − 3. On the other
hand, L3ωρ parametrization with the same slope predicts
the onset of Λ 0 around 0.43 fm − 3, reaching YΛ = 0.19
at 1.0 fm − 3. The only exception is for Ξ − with L = 92
MeV. It appears earlier and reaches high values in L3 ωρ
due to Λ ωρ = 0, and the presence of the mixed term
gΣΛ ρ . In Tab.
III, I show the density where each hyperon
reaches the density of 0.01 fm − 3.
B. ∆ matter
A second possibility is the onset of ∆ resonances, with-
out the appearance of the hyperons, which I call here ND
matter. In ref. [
15], the authors study the onset of ∆ res-
onances in dense matter for a ﬁxed value of the slope,
but changing the value of α V . They show that usually,
∆ − and ∆ 0 are present. The only exception is for α V =
0.50, which is the value used in the present work. There-
fore, this is an opportunity to investigate whether, by
changing the slope or changing the parameterization, ∆ 0
can also appear within α V = 0.50. The results show that
∆ 0’s are never present for α V = 0.50, making ∆ − the
sole exotic particle for ND matter. The ∆ − population
is shown in Fig.
2.
We can see that, analogously to the Λ 0 particle, in-
creasing the slope pushes the onset of the ∆ − to higher
densities. However, ∆ − has a diﬀerent behavior. While
Λ 0 shows a fraction that increases monotonically with
density, ∆ − shows a peak and starts to decrease at higher
densities. In the case of ND matter, the presence of the
peak happens because g∆∆ ω > gN N ω. There is an ex-
ception again for L = 92 MeV, where the ∆ − fraction is
strongly unfavored in the L3 ωρ due to Λ ωρ = 0.
From a quantitative point of view, it can be seen that
the onset of ∆ − occurs at even lower densities than Λ 0.
For example, with L = 44 MeV, the ∆ − reach a fraction
of 0.01 at 0.27 fm − 3 for BigApple, and 0.32 fm − 3 for
L3ωρ . As will be shown in the next section, the onset of
exotic particles at such low densities indicates that even
the canonical 1.4M⊙ stars present non-nucleonic degrees
of freedom at their cores.
C. Hyperonic ∆ matter.
The last case I studied is the possibility of both hyper-
ons and ∆ resonances being present at massive neutron
star cores, the NYD matter. In this case, three exotic
particles are present: ∆ − , Λ 0, and the Ξ − . The ∆ 0 is
again absent for all values of L, as well as the Σ triplet.
The results are displayed in Fig.
3.
We notice that, in general, the ﬁrst exotic particle to
appear is the ∆ − , followed by the Λ 0 and ﬁnally the
Ξ − . However, the Λ 0 and the Ξ − onset are delayed to
higher densities. On the other hand, the peak and the
consequent decrease with the density presented in the
∆ − fraction are much more evident in the presence of
the hyperons once besides the eﬀect related to g∆∆ ω >
gN N ω, now the decline of ∆ − particles is enhanced due
to the competition faced with the much less repulsive
Ξ − . In the case of the BigApple parametrization, the
∆ − fraction goes to zero before 1 fm − 3. For the Λ 0’s,
we can see that the density of their onset occurs almost
independently of the value of L. At high density, lower
values of L present higher fractions of Λ 0.
Here again, L = 92 MeV for L3 ωρ presents an exacer-
bated behavior due to Λ ωρ = 0. The ∆ − is strongly sup-
pressed, and its fraction goes to zero for densities above
0.7 fm − 3. Besides Λ ωρ = 0, the other factor that makes
the quick decrease of the ∆ − is the competition due to
the onset of the Ξ − at densities only slightly superior to
that related to the ∆ − . The very small number of ∆ reso-
nances indicates that the NY and NYD stars will be very
similar. All relevant properties are present in Tab.

IV. MICROSCOPIC RESULTS - THE SQUARE
OF THE SPEED OF SOUND
I now discuss the quantities related to the equation of
state (EOS). Graphically, changing the slope has little
eﬀect on the EOSs. This is the reason that some studies
about the symmetry energy do not even present the EOS
(as for instance ref. [
34]). Others, on the other hand,
choose to display the EOS in log scale [ 39], and even
then, the diﬀerences are small.
Furthermore, the eﬀects of hyperons and ∆ resonances
are also well known in the literature, at least for ﬁxed
values of S0 and L [
9, 12–16]. The presence of hyperons
softens the EOSs, while the presence of ∆’s can soften
the EOS at moderate density, but if the coupling of the
∆ resonances with the ω meson is strong enough, the
EOS at high density is as stiﬀ as the pure nucleonic one.
Since the EOS has low sensitivity to changes in the slope,
I use here the square of the speed of sound:

The ﬁrst feature we can notice is that when an ex-
otic phase is absent, the L3 ωρ and the BigApple present
opposite behavior. Although the positive correlation be-
tweenδR and L is present in both, the values of δR are
always positive for the L3 ωρ and always negative for the
BigApple. This implies that as we increase the slope, the
1.4M⊙ and the 2.01 M⊙ stars become more and more
diﬀerentiable in the L3 ωρ , as the absolute value of δR
increases; but become more and more similar for the
BigApple as the absolute value of δR decreases.
When hyperons are present, massive neutron stars be-
come even more compact. For the L3 ωρ model, this
causes a value of δR being as large as 0.8 km in the case
of large slopes, while for the BigApple, the diﬀerence be-
comes negligible. ∆ resonances have a similar eﬀect to
hyperons, and ∆-admixed hyperonic neutron stars have
the larger positive correlation between δR and L. Fur-
thermore, the values of δR can be as high as 0.92 km.
These behaviors indicate that a precise measure of the
radii of massive and canonical stars can not only poten-
tially indicate the presence of hyperons, but also can help
us to choose which model has a better description of neu-
tron stars. For instance, a R2. 01 < 12 km can only be ob-
tained with the L3 ωρ with an exotic phase. In the same
sense, a δR < 0 may favor the BigApple parametriza-
tion, although the analysis of δR must be done altogether
with the other constraints as discussed in the text.

VI. CONCLUSIONS
In this work, I studied the eﬀects of the slope L in
the neutron stars’ exotic content. The main results are
summarized below.
• For the NY matter, increasing the slope does not
signiﬁcantly aﬀect the onset of Λ 0 hyperons, but
suppresses them at high densities. The eﬀects on
the Ξ − are the opposite. Increasing L increases
their population.
• For ND matter, increasing L also reduces the pres-
ence of ∆ − resonances at high densities. But unlike
the Λ 0 particle, which has a monotonically increas-
ing behavior, the fraction of ∆ − began to drop at
high densities. For the NYD matter, the drop of
the population of ∆ − resonances is even more ev-
ident. The exarcebed behaviour found for L = 92
MeV within the L3 ωρ is not exclusively due to the
large slope, but also because such a parametriza-
tion possesses Λ ωρ = 0.
• The slope aﬀects the square of the speed of sound,
v2
s . At low densities, lower values of slope have a
small value of v2
s , while large values of L present
a large value of v2
s . For nucleonic stars, there is a
peak in the curve for the BigApple parametrization,
but not for the L3ωρ , reﬂecting the quartic term for
the ω ﬁeld ξ.
• Qualitatively, the eﬀects of the exotic phase on v2
s
are the same for the L3 ωρ and the BigApple, al-
thoughv2
s presents a steep growth for the BigApple.
• The onset of hyperons causes a ﬁrst drop in v2
s fol-
lowed by a small second drop due to the onset of
the second hyperon.
• The presence of ∆ ′s causes a large drop followed by
a steep increase in v2
s . L = 92 MeV for the L3 ωρ
present a qualitate diﬀerent behaviour because it
also have Λ ωρ = 0, which allow large values for the
ρ ﬁeld.
• Increasing L also increases the stars’ radii, a well-
known result. Hyperons do not aﬀect the canonical
1. 40M⊙ , but a 2 . 01M⊙ star has its radius reduced
due to the onset of hyperons. The lower the slope,
the larger the reduction. The variations ∆ R2. 01 are
larger for the L3 ωρ .
• Unlike hyperons, ∆ resonances aﬀect both the
canonical 1.4M⊙ and a 2.01 M⊙ . In general, the
lower the slope, the larger the value of ∆ R1. 4 and
∆ R2. 01.
• ∆ ′s also aﬀects Λ 1. 4. More than that, the eﬀects are
even larger than the eﬀects in the radius, reaching
16% for the BigApple. Again, the eﬀects are larger
for lower values of L.
• In relation to the constraints related to the di-
mensionless tidal parameter, the L3 ωρ and the
BigApple exhibit opposite behaviour. While the
L3ωρ can fulﬁll even the strong constraint for al-
most all values of L, the BigApple cannot de-
scribe even the weak bound, unless ∆ particles are
present.
• A precise determination of both R1. 4 and R2. 01
will imply in a precise determination of δR. This
quantity when combined with additional con-
straint from astrophysical observation can poten-
tially not only indicate the presence of exotic mat-
ter in the core of neutron stars but also suggest
which parametrization is better suited to describe
these objects.
