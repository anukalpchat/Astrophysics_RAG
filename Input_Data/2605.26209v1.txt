ABSTRACT
Aims.JWST has measured an unprecedented abundance of galaxies above redshiftz≳4−5, whose formation and evolution are
still difficult to reconcile within traditional galaxy evolution models in aΛCDM framework. Here, we present a study on the star
formation histories of these high-redshift galaxies betweenz≃5−12 via a cutting-edge data-driven semi-empirical model that uses
the observed ultra-violet (UV) luminosity functions (LFs) as input to retrieve star formation rates (SFRs), naturally bypassing any
uncertain modelling of cooling, feedback and/or stochastic processes.
Methods.Galaxy stellar masses are progressively built in time by integrating their SFRs assigned along their progenitor haloes via
the SFR-halo accretion rate relation, derived from abundance matching between the input observed UV LFs with the dark matter
halo accretion rate distributions at each redshift. Our original method reverse-engineers empirical estimates of the high-zgalactic
SFRs directly from observations via abundance matching rather than fitting observed LFs with parametric star formation efficiencies
(SFEs). This makes the SFEs a full prediction of the model rather than a tuned input, serving as a natural baseline to test burstiness,
dust attenuation, or initial mass function variations.
Results.Our approach naturally reproduces the total stellar mass function, the large-scale clustering, and the star-forming main
sequence. We find that massive galaxies grew their stellar mass with a bursty star formation atz∼9−10, broadly in agreement with
the star formation histories inferred from spectral energy distribution fitting, with the SFE reaching high peaks of 0.8−0.9 atz>9
and lowering to standard values of 0.2−0.3 belowz≲9. We find that the presence of dust could enhance the predicted SFRs at
z≲8, better reproducing the observed SFRs of massive dusty galaxies, and increase the SFEs to values close to or even above unity at
z≳8. Finally, switching to top-heavy initial mass functions reduces the SFEs by a factor of 2−3, highlighting the need for a variable
initial mass function as an inevitable ingredient in the evolution of galaxies at high redshifts to avoid unphysical SFEs, especially in
the presence of dust.
Key words.Galaxies: abundances – Galaxies: evolution – Galaxies: star formation
1. Introduction
The formation and evolution of galaxies are still debated ques-
tions today. In a Lambda Cold Dark Matter (ΛCDM) Universe
galaxies grow in stellar mass via both in-situ and ex-situ pro-
cesses. The in-situ growth is regulated by the star formation rate,
i.e. the conversion of cold gas into stars, fuelled by the gas ac-
cretion of the host dark matter haloes. The ex-situ growth is
instead controlled by the rate of mergers with other galaxies,
which in aΛCDM Universe is the inevitable consequence of the
mergers between the host dark matter haloes. Many studies have
shown that at redshiftsz≲3 less massive galaxies (with stellar
massM ⋆ ≲10 11 M⊙ atz∼0) have formed their stellar mass
mainly via star formation, whereas more massive galaxies have
had more contribution from mergers (e.g., van Dokkum et al.
2010; Shankar et al. 2015; Buchan & Shankar 2016; Pillepich
et al. 2018a; Grylls et al. 2019, 2020; Fu et al. 2022, 2024; Eis-
ert et al. 2023).
At progressively higher redshifts the new observations from
the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have revolutionised
our view of galaxy formation and evolution. Firstly, JWST has
revealed a large number of star-forming, ultraviolet (UV)-bright
galaxies up toz∼17 and possibly beyond, with abundances ex-
ceeding pre-JWST observations by up to an order of magnitude
or more (e.g., Bouwens et al. 2023; Finkelstein et al. 2023, 2024;
Adams et al. 2024; McLeod et al. 2024; Robertson et al. 2024;
Pérez-González et al. 2025). Secondly, a large population of ac-
tive galactic nuclei (AGN) powered by very massive black holes

missed in previous observations and are challenging to reconcile
within current traditional models (e.g., Donnan et al. 2024; Mat-
sumoto et al. 2024; McGaugh et al. 2024; Weibel et al. 2024;
Shuntov et al. 2025a). For example, Glazebrook et al. (2024)
identified a massive quiescent galaxy, ZF-UDS-7329, at redshift
z=3.25 with inferred stellar massM ⋆ ∼10 11.3 M⊙, speculating
that it formed most of its stellar mass atz∼11 in a burst of star
formation. They discuss that this scenario is in tension with cur-
rent models of galaxy formation within theΛCDM framework,
pointing possibly towards a different stellar initial mass function
(IMF), or changes in the underlying stellar population and feed-
back modelling. Indeed, evidence for a radially varying IMF in
local massive galaxies is mounting (e.g., Cappellari et al. 2006;
Conroy & van Dokkum 2012; La Barbera et al. 2016; Bernardi
et al. 2019; Marsden et al. 2022; Lu et al. 2024).
More generally, the large number of UV-bright galaxies in-
ferred from JWST observations has been explained in a number
of different ways from the theoretical point of view. Some pop-
ular explanations rely on stochastic star formation to allow for
epochs of larger-than-average galaxy growth (e.g., Shen et al.
2023; Sun et al. 2023; Gelli et al. 2024), or reducing the con-
tent of dust (e.g., Ferrara 2024), lowering stellar metallicity or
assuming a top-heavy IMF to boost the UV emission for any
given episode of star formation (e.g., Inayoshi et al. 2022; Trinca
et al. 2024; Yung et al. 2024a), assuming density-modulated
star formation efficiencies (e.g., Somerville et al. 2025), and/or
a feedback-free scenario (e.g., Dekel et al. 2023). To explain
the formation of massive galaxies atz>5, in particular, some
groups invoked the extreme value statistics (Lovell et al. 2023;
Carnall et al. 2024; Jespersen et al. 2025; Enríquez-Vargas et al.
2026), to show that the existence of massive galaxies (with stel-
lar massM ⋆ >10 11 M⊙) observed between 3<z<5 may
be accommodated withinΛCDM but only assuming very high
star formation efficiency (SFE;ϵ⋆ =0.8−1). Alternatively, a
bottom-heavy IMF could also increase the stellar mass by boost-
ing the fraction of lower mass stars (e.g., Lapi et al. 2024; Yung
et al. 2024a; Mauerhofer et al. 2025; Fontanot et al. 2026). Al-
ternative possibilities for boosting the formation of galaxies at
early epochs may be found either in improving the modelling of
the halo accretion histories withinΛCDM (e.g., ellipsoidal col-
lapse) or in varying the underlying cosmological model, such
as allowing for dynamical dark energy models (e.g., Boylan-
Kolchin 2023; Menci et al. 2024; Sokoliuk 2025; Fakhry et al.
2026; Menci et al. 2026) or modified gravity models (e.g., Mc-
Gaugh et al. 2024).
In the context of aΛCDM Universe, some groups have at-
tempted to model the galaxy star formation histories and im-
plied UV luminosities via semi-empirical techniques. This ap-
proach bypasses the ab-initio modelling of the baryonic physics
behind star formation in galaxies, which is instead guided by ob-
servational data. For example, Kar et al. (2026) and Yung et al.
(2025), along the lines of Moster et al. (2018) and Behroozi et al.
(2019), assumed a parametric SFE of the typeϵ ⋆(Mh,z) charac-
terised by a number of free parameters defining its variation as
a function of host halo mass and redshift. They concluded that
the number density and spatial distribution of UV galaxies can
be reproduced withinΛCDM with relatively standard SFEs of
∼20−30%, but steadily increasing at higher redshifts and with-
out the need for very large scatters of up to∼1 dex or more in the
star formation rate-halo mass relation, as previously inferred by
other more comprehensive theoretical models (e,g, Dekel et al.
2023; Mason et al. 2023; Nikopoulos & Dayal 2024).
In this work, we make use, extending it to higher redshifts,
of the semi-empirical approach recently devised by our group
in two semi-empirical models,decode(Fu et al. 2025a,b) and
TopSem(Boco et al. 2023), which have been successful in repro-
ducing the stellar mass functions, star formation histories, and
bulge-to-total distributions of galaxies atz≲4−5. Our method
relies on extracting the star formation rate-halo accretion rate
(SFR-HAR) relation from abundance matching between the SFR
and HAR functions, where the former is directly inferred from
observations and the latter is theoretically predicted by N-body
simulations. There are several key advantages of using this data-
driven technique: 1) it does not require heavy parametrisations
of the SFE, which is instead a natural prediction of the abun-
dance matching procedure; 2) it allows to extract the star forma-
tion histories by tracking the SFRs along the host halo progenitor
histories; 3) the SFRs in input are directly inferred from obser-
vational data and as such they can be considered an effective
balance between any feedback and any ex-situ or in-situ process
(e.g., merger, disc instability, clumpy accretion) that may reduce
or enhance the star formation in the galaxy; and 4) the SFRs in
input do not require any modelling in terms of stochasticity or
feedback, and can in turn be effectively adopted to test the de-
gree of SFE, dust attenuation, or variations in IMF, as we will
carry out below.
The aim of this paper is twofold. On one hand, we will com-
pute the star formation histories of galaxies along their progeni-
tors to compare with direct observations. On the other hand, we
will integrate them to infer the implied stellar masses, in par-
ticular of massive galaxies, global stellar mass functions, and
clustering to pin down the SFEs and impact of including dust
attenuation or a variable IMF.
This paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we describe
the methodology followed in our model. In Section 3, we test
the SFR-HAR assumption at high redshifts by applying it to
the TNG simulation. In Section 4, we show our results on the
galaxy abundances, stellar mass-halo mass connection and star
formation histories. We also discuss the effect of choosing a dif-
ferent IMF and applying a dust correction to the SFRs. Finally,
in Section 5 and 6, we discuss our results and draw our con-
clusions. In this work, we adopt theΛCDM cosmology with
best fit parameters from Planck Collaboration et al. (2020) (i.e.,
(Ωm,Ω Λ,Ω b,h,n S, σ8)=(0.31,0.69,0.049,0.68,0.97,0.81))
and a Chabrier (2003) stellar IMF.
2. Methods
In this Section, we describe the implementation of our model
decode1 (Discrete statistical sEmi-empiriCal mODEl) and our
approach to grow and merge galaxies following the dark mat-
ter halo assembly histories. We build up galaxies following their
progenitors via input scaling relations between the galaxy SFR
and HAR. In the first two papers of the series (Fu et al. 2025a,b)
we have accurately tested the SFR-HAR abundance matching at
redshiftz≲5−6. In this paper, we will focus on the galaxy
star formation rates at high redshifts and how they compare to
the JWST spectral energy distribution (SED)-inferred star for-
mation histories at redshiftz≳5.

2.1. Dark matter halo merger tree
We base our analysis on a catalogue ofn=5×10 5 central dark
matter haloes with mass aboveM h >10 10.5 M⊙ at each redshift
of interest. The choice of the volume and cut in halo mass repre-
sents a good balance among computational efficiency, statistics
and mass resolution for the stellar mass range of interest. The cut
in halo mass in particular ensures a completeness in stellar mass
down toM ⋆ ≳3×10 9 M⊙. These haloes are randomly extracted
from the Tinker et al. (2008) halo mass function at any redshift
above the halo mass cut-off. We ignore the surviving satellite
subhaloes whose abundance at these high redshifts is less rele-
vant. The mass accretion history of each dark matter halo and
their merger trees are computed via the SatGen analytical code
from Jiang et al. (2021), based on the Parkinson et al. (2008)
algorithm fitted to the outputs of the MultiDark Planck N-body
simulation. Each of these parent dark matter haloes comes with a
mass accretion history along with the infall redshift of the satel-
lite subhaloes and their accretion history before infall. We have
also checked that these halo accretion tracks are on average well
consistent with those from high-redshift dark matter-only simu-
lations, such asgureft(Yung et al. 2024b). On top of this dark
matter framework, we self-consistently grow central galaxies by
assigning to them a SFR following the growth of the host haloes.
2.2. Abundance matching
The core of our model is based on the abundance matching be-
tween the galaxy SFR and host dark matter HAR. To compute
the SFR-HAR relation, we perform the abundance matching be-
tween the observed SFR function and the theoretical HAR func-
tion (we use Equation 37 in Aversa et al. 2015, see also Fu et al.
2022, Boco et al. 2023, Fu et al. 2024 and Fu et al. 2025a,b). In
the abundance matching we assume a scatter of 0.4 dex in SFR at
fixed HAR, as suggested by our analysis from the TNG simula-
tion. We checked that by varying the value of the scatter within a
reasonable range (0.3−0.5 dex) our results do not alter apprecia-
bly, as also discussed in Fu et al. (2025a) and Fu et al. (2025b),
with stellar mass assemblies altering by less than≲0.05 dex.
In our abundance matching, we input the theoretical HAR
function (from the results of the SatGen algorithm) and the ob-
served SFR function (gathered from multiple data sets). For the
former, we sample the distributions from our mock halo cata-
logue at each redshift, while for the latter, we combine the most
recently observed UV galaxy luminosity function (LF) at high
redshifts (see Appendix A for details). We convert UV lumi-
nosities to SFRs via the calibration factor from Kennicutt (1998)
which yields the following formula (e.g., Madau & Dickinson
2014)

Wilkins et al. 2023). We have checked that in the most extreme
case of lowest metallicity (i.e.,Z=0.1Z ⊙), the UV-SFR cal-

stellar population synthesis models (e.g., Bicker & Fritze-v. Al-

shift in SFR would be less than<0.1 dex, corresponding to a
∼0.1 dex shift in stellar mass. For the most massive galaxies,
withM ⋆ ≳10 11 M⊙, both observations and models suggest a
near-solar metallicity (e.g., Merlin et al. 2025), implying a neg-
ligible alteration in their stellar mass growth.
Hereafter, we will adopt the UV SFR as representative of
the total SFR, along with a Chabrier (2003) IMF, as a reference
model. We will also explore the consequences of considering the
dust correction in observed luminosities and adopting different
IMFs in Sections 4.7 and 4.8.

2.4. Galaxy mergers
We add the contribution from mergers to the total galaxy stellar
mass by following the merger histories of the host dark matter
subhaloes. In particular, we assign satellite galaxies with a stel-
lar mass at infall via the typical stellar mass corresponding to
the halo mass of the host subhalo on the stellar mass-halo mass
(M⋆−Mh) relation of the central counterpart, assuming that satel-
lite galaxies align with the scaling relations of central galaxies at
the time of infall. We assume that galaxies merge with a time
delay with respect to the host subhaloes following the merger
timescales described in Section 3.4 of Fu et al. (2022) corrected
by a factor of∼1.5 at redshiftz≳5. These timescales are fit-
ted to reproduce the number densities of surviving satellite sub-
haloes of the Millennium simulation up to redshiftz∼11−12.
For the purposes of this paper, we do not discuss in detail
the contribution from mergers, even though we include them
in the overall stellar mass assemblies of our galaxies for com-
pleteness. We have checked that mergers contribute to less than
∼5−10% of the total stellar mass growth in all our mock galax-
ies atz≳5−6, resulting in a minimal impact on the stellar mass
function. We note that this estimate is nicely consistent with the
recent observational work by Calabrò et al. (2026), who claimed
that mergers can contribute up to 5−10% at 5<z<14 in a
spectroscopic sample of 1233 JWST galaxies. We will dedicate
a separate work on the prediction of merger rates of galaxies at
high redshifts (Fu et al. in prep.).

3. Verifying the assumption of a monotonic average
relation between SFR and HAR at high redshift
Before applying our model to predict the star formation histories
of high-redshift massive galaxies, following F25 we test whether
the assumption of a monotonic relation between the SFR and the
HAR is still expected in cosmological galaxy evolution mod-
els even atz≳6. To this purpose, we make use of the TNG
(The Next Generation) simulation to test whether a state-of-the-
art cosmological hydrodynamic simulation is predicting a mono-
tonic relation between SFR and HAR atz>6 as it does at lower
redshifts. We stress that this analysis is not aimed at calibrating
our model on the TNG simulation which employs its own recipes
for the baryonic physics and is calibrated on low-redshift data it-
self, but only to test the reliability of the SFR-HAR correlation
hypothesis in a self-consistent model like the TNG. We note that
a whole body of theoretical work supports a close connection
between galactic SFR and host HAR (e.g., Dekel et al. 2009;
Bournaud et al. 2015; Dou et al. 2025; Waterval et al. 2025).
3.1. The TNG simulation
We make use of the cosmological TNG100 simulation, a com-
ponent of the IllustrisTNG project2 (TNG henceforth; e.g., Nel-

Springel et al. 2018). The TNG simulations are performed via

mation, stellar evolution and AGN feedback (e.g., Pillepich et al.
2018b; Weinberger et al. 2017). The TNG simulations include

good balance between volume size and mass resolution for the

4. Results
In this Section, we present our results on the galaxy stellar mass
growths at redshiftz≳5 by adopting as input the SFR-HAR
relation from the UV LF abundance matching. In particular, we
show the SFR function used as input in our abundance matching
and the output SFR-HAR relation (Section 4.1). Then, we show
the predicted stellar mass function (SMF) in Section 4.2, and the
correspondingM ⋆ −M h relation and the SFE in Section 4.3. We
will also show a case study on the star formation histories of
the newly detected high-redshift galaxies from JWST (Section
4.5). Finally, we discuss the role of dust obscuration and different
IMFs on the galaxy SFRs (Sections 4.7 and 4.8).
4.1. Star formation rate-halo accretion rate relation
We first show the SFR function used in our abundance match-
ing, computed starting from the UV LF, as described in Sec-
tion 2.2. Within this work we make use of UV data only, since
the observational constraints on the infrared (IR) LF are avail-
able only up toz<6 (e.g., Gruppioni et al. 2020; Wang et al.
2021; Traina et al. 2024, 2026). Figure 2 shows the SFR num-
ber densities at redshiftsz=6−11. In particular, we com-
bine the latest observational determinations in the UV band from
HST (Bouwens et al. 2015, 2021, 2022), UltraVISTA/COSMOS
and UKIDSS (Bowler et al. 2015), WUDS (Pelló et al. 2018),
PEARLS+JWST (Adams et al. 2024), JWST-PRIMER (Donnan

4.3. Stellar-to-halo connection
We now turn our attention to the connection between the galaxy
stellar mass and host dark matter halo mass. As discussed by
several groups, the shape and evolution of theM ⋆ −M h relation
are directly linked to those of the galaxy SMF (e.g., Rodríguez-
Puebla et al. 2017; Moster et al. 2018; Grylls et al. 2019; Fu et al.
2022, 2024).
Figure 6 displays theM ⋆ −M h relation at redshiftsz=6, 7
and 8 compared to the measurements from Spitzer/IRAC (Ste-
fanon et al. 2021) and COSMOS-Web (Shuntov et al. 2025a)
data. For completeness, we also include the outputs of the em-
pirical models EMERGE (Moster et al. 2018) and UniverseMa-
chine (Behroozi et al. 2019). The upper panels show the stel-
lar mass as a function of halo mass, and the lower panels re-
port the stellar-to-halo mass ratio and the SFE, defined asϵ ⋆ =
M⋆/(Mh fb), wheref b =0.16 is the cosmic baryon fraction
(Planck Collaboration et al. 2020). We find a strong correlation
between SFE and halo mass, with a SFE of∼70% the baryonic
value for the largest masses, significantly above common expec-
tations of∼10−20% as calibrated at lower redshifts (e.g., Hop-
kins et al. 2011; Schaye et al. 2015; Pillepich et al. 2018a; Davé
et al. 2019). We find that our predicted SFEs are well aligned
with independent estimates derived from abundance matching of
the SMFs and halo mass functions, with a slight tendency for our
results to increase by a factor of∼2−3 atz≥8. Our predictions
both in stellar mass and in SFE are also higher in normalisation
with respect to the results of previous semi-empirical estimates
from, e.g.,Moster et al. (2018) and Behroozi et al. (2019), due to
the fact that their models were calibrated to match the SMFs and
quenched fractions mostly up toz=4. Our current predictions
are particularly noteworthy as they point to very high baryon
conversion efficiencies, particularly in massive haloes atz≳7,
in ways independent of any specific theoretical model but simply
relying on observed UV data and halo abundances.

star formation in the central galaxy.

In this Section, we turn our attention to the star-forming main
sequence of galaxies as predicted by our model. The three pan-

4.7. The role of dust obscuration
As described above, our SFRs are empirically based on direct
observation of the UV luminosities, under the assumption that
the dust content at high redshift is low (e.g., Pettini et al. 1997;
Nath et al. 2023; Shchekinov & Nath 2025). However, the SFRs
may still be underestimated due to the effect of dust obscura-
tion that could damp the intrinsic UV luminosity arising from
a galaxy (e.g., Payyoor Vijayan et al. 2026), and mounting ev-
idence suggests that the dust may still be present even in very
high-redshift galaxies (e.g., Ferrara 2024; Somerville et al. 2025;
Rodighiero et al. 2026). Here, we test the effect of dust obscura-
tion by assuming different attenuation recipes from the literature.
In particular, we explore the attenuation laws from Charlot &
Fall (2000), Calzetti et al. (2000) and Salim et al. (2018), which
we describe in detail in Appendix C. In the same Appendix we
also show that the dust-corrected UV LF would match the IR
LF atz≲6, where IR measurements are available, providing
independent support to our dust-correction recipes.
We find that within the UV luminosity range explored here,
all the recipes quoted above produce similar results, yielding
SFRs higher by a factor of∼2−3 with respect to those pre-
dicted by our reference model. This shift would in turn en-
hance our predicted SFRs for the JWST massive candidates from
∼150−200M ⊙/yr to∼300−600M ⊙/yr, in closer agreement
with what measured by Xiao et al. (2024) at redshiftz∼5−6, al-
though still lower than their quoted value of∼700M ⊙/yr. When
including dust corrections, the galaxy SMF, shown in Figure
5, is shifted towards higher stellar masses, though still broadly
aligned with current data, except possibly atz∼6 and at larger
stellar masses. Also the star-forming main sequence, reported in
Figure 10, increases by 0.2−0.3 dex after dust correction. In-
triguingly, the dust correction would enhance the SFRs in Figure
8 at redshiftsz∼8−10 pushing to SFEs above unity, as we
further discuss below in Figure 12. These findings would imply
that 1) the dust content may still be low at these high redshifts
and high stellar masses and/or 2) that a variable IMF is an in-
evitable ingredient in the evolution of galaxies at high redshifts.
We explore the impact of a variable IMF in the next Section.
4.8. The dependence on the initial mass function
We now discuss the implications of varying the input IMF on the
stellar masses and SFRs of the galaxies analysed in this work.
The shape of the IMF and its dependence on the galaxy SFR
continue to be a matter of intense scrutiny and with the advent
of JWST data the topic has become even more relevant. Several
works have pointed out the dependence of the stellar population
and the derived total stellar mass on the assumed IMF, with the
new observed massive candidates pointing towards more exotic,

5. Discussion
In this work, we laid out a self-consistent and minimal data-
driven approach to assign empirical SFRs to haloes and build
the implied galaxy star formation histories. Instead of using ob-
served UV LFs as outputs to test the model, we used them as in-
put to infer SFRs mapped onto host dark matter haloes via abun-
dance matching techniques. With the least possible assumptions
and theoretical inputs, our methodology transparently allows to
1) infer the efficiencies of star formation in haloes as emerging
directly from the observed galaxy luminosities, and 2) to test the
consequences of adding dust obscuration and/or IMF variations
on empirical SFRs. In general, we find that large SFEs appear to
be unavoidable within the framework of aΛCDM Universe, in-
dependently of any stochasticity in the star formation histories or
dust obscuration, although the inclusion of a variable IMF could
reduce the SFEs by a factor of∼2. Here below we further dis-
cuss some implications of our results and how they compare to
other works.
We showed in Figures 8 and 9 that our method naturally pre-
dicts bursts of star formation characterised by extremely high
SFEs following the sustained halo accretion histories character-
ising host haloes at early epochs. This finding aligns with what
has been recently found in cosmological simulations by Dekel
et al. (2025), who reported feedback-free starbursts with mas-
sive galaxies at cosmic dawn. Similar results are also reported
by the work of Ferrara et al. (2025a), who found feedback-free
SFRs in the pre-supernova phase with SFEϵ ⋆ >0.4. We also
found that these galaxies are characterised by a number density
of∼1.8×10 −5 Mpc−3 at redshiftz∼5−6 forM ⋆ ≳10 11 M⊙,
which lies far below the hard upper limit imposed by the dark
matter halo mass function of∼10 −3 Mpc−3 at the same red-
shifts (as shown in Boylan-Kolchin 2023), showing no tension
between these objects andΛCDM.
Another important caveat of our modelling is the lack of IR
restframe data at redshiftz≳6. We have shown that assuming an
extinction law to account for the dust obscuration, may generate
some overestimation of the measured SMF and further increase
the already extreme SFEs above unity (dashed orange lines in
Figure 12), although it could help in boosting the star formation
histories of the very massive galaxies. We speculate that dust cor-
rection is a relevant factor in massive galaxies at the late phases
of cosmic dawn, but less impactful at higher redshift and lower
stellar masses. This hypothesis is also supported by the findings
of several other works, that support a dust-free star-formation
scenario abovez≳10 with a gradual transition period at redshift
z∼8−9 (e.g., Ferrara et al. 2022, 2023, 2025b; Algera et al.
2023; Cullen et al. 2024).
Finally, we studied how the galaxy SFE, for a given observed
luminosity, can vary by assuming different IMFs. In particular,
we found that a more exotic IMF, e.g., characterised by more
massive stars, is expected to improve the above tension between
the JWST high luminosity objects andΛCDM. This is due to the
fact that more massive stars emit generally a higher amount of
light per unit mass, producing higher luminosities at fixed galac-
tic stellar masses. Indeed, as a result of this, we found that a top-
heavy or bottom-light IMF yields SFEs a factor of∼10−50%
lower with respect to the extreme values of SFEsϵ ⋆ ∼1 sug-
gested by the fiducial model, in good agreement with what re-
ported in Ziegler et al. (2025) and Fontanot et al. (2026). We
found that our reference model with dust and/or the inclusion
of a non-canonical IMF, still yields a clustering strength at large
scales within±10−20%. Therefore, at the level of precision of
current data, galaxy clustering may not yet be able to efficiently
break degeneracies among different models, but it can still be
used as a consistency check for the validation of the mean corre-
lation between stellar mass and host halo mass.
All in all, our findings suggest that the observed UV LFs nec-
essarily translate into large SFEs when mapped against the halo
accretion histories in aΛCDM early Universe atz≳7, in line
with a variety of previous studies (e.g., Dekel et al. 2023; Fer-
rara et al. 2023; Lapi et al. 2024; Menci et al. 2024; Somerville
et al. 2025; Prada et al. 2026). In turn, such high SFEs, when
integrated in time, can generate massive galaxies close in stellar
mass to those observed by JWST. Even in the presence of a non-
canonical stellar IMF, the SFEs remain considerably high and
close to unity if dust corrections are included. Beyond-ΛCDM
cosmological models, making use of, e.g., dynamical dark en-
ergy, could release some of the tension, although they would still
require large SFEs (e.g., Menci et al. 2026; Comini et al. 2026).
6. Conclusions
In this work, we have presented a flexible yet powerful data-
driven method to estimate the star formation histories and ef-
ficiencies of the high-redshift galaxies detected by JWST. In-
stead of fitting the observed UV LFs through parametric SFEs,
we have used these data as input in our framework to extract
the implied SFRs, which we have mapped onto host dark matter
haloes via abundance matching techniques with the halo accre-
tion rates. Our SFRs are thus by design directly rooted in ob-
servations and as such can be considered an effective balance
among any cooling, feedback and accretion, without requiring
explicit modelling of any of these processes. By assigning SFRs
to galaxies via our abundance matching relations along the main
progenitors, we have built star formation histories, star formation
efficiencies, and integrated stellar masses. In our transparent and
minimalistic method based on abundance matching, SFEs sim-
ply emerge from the data rather than being a tuned input of the
model, thus serving as a natural baseline to test burstiness, dust
attenuation, or IMF variations.
Our main results can be summarised as follows:
–The assumption of a monotonic relation between the galaxy
SFR and dark matter halo accretion rate can robustly predict
and match the bulk of the observed galaxy stellar mass func-

–The star formation efficiency implied by our data-driven for-

efficiency atz>9 (Figure 12).
In conclusion, all our results point to large SFEs, close to
unity atz≳7, even when accounting for non-standard IMFs,
especially when correcting for dust extinction. Semi-empirical
models represent a powerful data-driven approach for addressing
key issues of galaxy evolution at high redshifts. Semi-empirical
models can indeed accurately predict several galactic properties,
such as the star formation histories, galaxy abundances and stel-
lar mass-halo mass relation in line with observations, with only a
minimal number of input observed parameters. With the upcom-
ing high-quality data from observatories, such as Euclid (e.g.,
Euclid Collaboration et al. 2025) and Rubin-LSST (e.g., Ivezi ´c
et al. 2019), the role played by data-driven models like ours will
become even more relevant to extract key constraints on galaxy
formation and evolution quantities in a self-consistent and flexi-
ble way (e.g., Lapi et al. 2026).
