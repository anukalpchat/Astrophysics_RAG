Title: Can COSI detect $γ$-ray lines from rare isotopes produced in the astrophysical intermediate neutron-capture process?
Authors: Falk Herwig, Pavel Denissenkov, Eric Burns
Published: 2026-05-27
Categories: astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.SR

Can COSI detectγ-ray lines from rare isotopes produced in the astrophysical
intermediate neutron-capture process?
Falk Herwig and Pavel Denissenkov
Astronomy Research Centre and Department of Physics and Astronomy
University of Victoria, Victoria, British Columbia V8W 2Y2, Canada
CaNPAN (Canadian Nuclear Physics for Astrophysics Network) Collaboration and
NuGrid Collaboration
Eric Burns
Department of Physics and Astronomy, Louisiana State University, Baton Rouge, LA 70803, USA
(Dated: May 28, 2026)
We investigate the nuclearγ-ray line emission from rare isotopes produced in the astrophysical in-
termediate neutron-capture process (iprocess) and assess the prospects of observing these emissions
withγ-ray telescopes. The astrophysical sites of theiprocess remain uncertain, but two candidates
with predicted rapid mass ejections at metallicities of stars in the solar neighborhood are post-
asymptotic giant branch (post-AGB) stars, such as Sakurai’s object (V4334 Sagittarii), and rapidly-
accreting white dwarfs (RAWDs). Detailed 1D and 3D simulations of these scenarios indicate that
the convective-reactive astrophysical fluid dynamics responsible fori-process nucleosynthesis can
lead to violent, non-radial outbursts that ultimately result in mass ejections ofi-process products.
We calculate the ejected yields of rare isotopes whose radioactive decays may produce detectable
γ-ray lines, particularly in the 0.5–2 MeV energy range. Our analysis focuses on isotopes such as
22Na, 89Sr, and 95Zr, which are expected to generate long-lasting emissions potentially observable
by the COSIγ-ray telescope. We estimate the formation rates of these sources and the likelihood
of detecting theirγ-ray emissions within 1000 parsecs of the Sun. We find that the probability of
observingi-process emission lines during COSI’s operational period is up to≈1%, but could rise to
11% for 89Sr if the event is observed within a few days. Due to the long lifetime and large produc-
tion of 22Na from proton-capture reactions its detection is more likely, with a probability of≈5%.
Future space missions could significantly enhance detection capabilities, potentially increasing the
observation probability to several tens of percent. Detection of long-lived neutron-rich isotopes such
as 137Cs would provide the first directγ-ray signature of intermediate neutron-density nucleosyn-
thesis, distinguishing the i process from classical s- and r-process pathways. These findings outline
a multi-messenger approach to studying dynamic stellar neutron-capture nucleosynthesis through
γ-ray observations.
INTRODUCTION
Multi-messenger astronomy is rapidly emerging as
a powerful tool for investigating time-domain astro-
physics and reactive flow processes [1]. The new
NASAγ-ray Compton Spectrometer and Imager (COSI)
space telescope, scheduled for launch in 2027, will have
a narrow-line sensitivity limit of approximately 3×
10−6 photons cm−2s−1 in the energy range of 0.5 to 2
MeV [2]. This sensitivity limit is estimated for point
sources at the 3σlevel over a two-year COSI survey ob-
servation period. While COSI’s overall energy coverage
spans approximately 0.2–5 MeV, its best narrow-line sen-
sitivity is in the 0.5–2 MeV band [2]. Below∼0.5 MeV
and above∼2 MeV, the sensitivity decreases due to in-
creased background and reduced effective area, making
detections of comparably weak lines less favorable. We
therefore focus on this interval, while noting that lines
outside it (e.g., the 2.6 MeV line of 208Tl [3]) are in prin-
ciple accessible. COSI operates in survey mode with full-
sky coverage approximately every day (≈13 h), so any
sufficiently brightγ-ray line transient lasting longer than
∼1 day will be within the survey field of view without
dedicated pointing [2].
COSI is expected to detectγ-ray lines from 56Co, 44Ti,
and 60Fe associated with known remnants of Type Ia and
core-collapse supernovae. It will improve upon previous
measurements by COMPTEL and INTEGRAL/SPI of
the 1.809 MeVγ-ray emission from 26Al, which is pro-
duced by massive stars and deposited into the Milky Way
disk [4–6]. COSI will observe the 0.511 MeV positron an-
nihilation line from the Galactic bulge and disk [7], and
it will also search forγ-ray lines from the decays of long-
lived isotopes 7Be and 22Na produced in nova explosions
[8, 9], provided that such an event occurs during its op-
erational period.
We propose additional targets of opportunity for COSI
observations:γ-ray lines from the decays of relatively
long-lived unstable isotopes produced in the intermediate
neutron capture process (iprocess) in post-asymptotic
giant branch (post-AGB) stars and rapidly-accreting
white dwarfs (RAWDs).
Theiprocess is a neutron-capture nucleosynthesis
regime intermediate in neutron density compared to the
much slowersprocess [10] and the much more rapid
rprocess [11]. It occurs in a star when He is ignited in

a thermo-nuclear runaway. If convective mixing induced

between those characteristic of thes- andr-process nu-

cluding its stellar simulation context, observational man-
ifestations and the associated nuclear physics, is given by
[16].
Direct evidence of theiprocess occurring in stars
comes from the observed enhancement of the first-peak
elements Rb, Sr, Y, and Zr by approximately two or-
ders of magnitude in the post-AGB star Sakurai’s object
(V4334 Sagittarii) [17, 18]. In this star, theiprocess
was triggered by a very late thermal pulse [VLTP, 19] of
its He shell [20, 21]. Another potential manifestation of
theiprocess is the observed enhancement of trans-Fe el-
emental abundances in H-deficient hot white dwarfs that
have undergone a He-core flash after leaving the red-giant
branch (RGB) due to extensive mass loss [22].
There is also ample indirect evidence ofiprocess
occurring in stars. One example is the distribution
of heavy-element abundances in C-enhanced metal-poor
(CEMP) stars that exhibit apparent mixtures ofs- and
r-process nucleosynthesis products, classified as CEMP-
r/sstars according to the scheme proposed in [23]. While
the heavy-element abundance patterns in most CEMP-
r/sstars are difficult to explain as simple mixtures ofs-
andr-process products [24], they can be well reproduced
by models ofi-process nucleosynthesis. This includes
both simplified one-zone models that assume a constant
neutron density [25–27] and more realistic multi-zone
models of low-mass AGB stars [28] and RAWDs [29, 30].
Another example is the discovery of anomalous isotopic
abundance ratios in certain presolar graphite grains [31],
as well as in SiC grains of type AB [32], and mainstream
type [33], which may have originated fromi-process nu-
cleosynthesis. Evidence for thei-process signature in the
Ba and La ratio has been found in open clusters [34],
suggesting that theiprocess can operate at high metal-
licities with neutron exposures sufficient to reach the sec-
ond peak. However, the mechanism by which this occurs
remains unclear.
Recent studies have shown that the radioactive isotope
208Tl can be produced in theiprocess at neutron densi-
ties of∼10 15 cm−3 [3]. Its 2.6 MeVγ-line emission from
a stellari-process source could be detectable if actinides
are synthesized alongside 208Tl, as suggested by [35].
In the following sections, we first present multi-
zone models ofi-process nucleosynthesis in a (solar-
metallicity) post-AGB very late thermal pulse (VLTP)
model and in RAWDs. This VLTP model is calibrated
to reproduce conditions inferred for Sakurai’s object [21],
but is used here as a generic post-AGB VLTP scenario.
These models are used to calculate the ejected yields of
unstable isotopes whose decays may produce narrow-line
γ-ray fluxes potentially detectable by COSI. We then es-
timate the formation rates of thesei-process sources and
the likelihood of COSI detecting theirγ-ray emission.
Finally, we summarize our findings in the conclusion.
MODELS OF I-PROCESS STELLAR SOURCES
AND CALCULA TION OF THEIRγ-RA Y LINE

Viablei-process sources ofγ-ray emission must not
only producei-process species abundantly but also eject
them on a timescale comparable to or shorter than the
species’ half-life. This ensures that the nuclearγ-ray lines
are not trapped within the optically thick stellar inte-
rior. Two sites that satisfy these criteria and have been
studied in detail are very late thermal pulses (VLTPs) in
low-mass post-AGB stars [19] and the recurrent He-shell
flashes in rapidly-accreting white dwarfs (RAWDs) [36].
Sakurai’s object erupted in 1994 and became a born-
again giant within∼2 years, and its mass ejections have
been observed [37]. In the born-again evolution scenario
[38, 39] a low- or intermediate-mass AGB star rapidly
loses most of its H-rich envelope as a planetary nebula
after multiple He-shell thermal pulses. Subsequently, the
He shell in the cooling CO white dwarf may undergo a
VLTP with H ingestion, triggeringi-process nucleosyn-
thesis [21] and expansion. H ingestion into the flash con-
vection can trigger a Global Oscillation of Shell H inges-
tion [GOSH, 40], producing dynamic ejections of He-shell
material as observed in H-deficient knots around some
late-thermal pulse candidates [41–44]. Stellar evolution
computations, supported by observations, suggest that
a VLTP may occur in approximately 20% of post-AGB
objects [45].
In these VLTP scenarios, the ejection is hydrody-
namic rather than quasi-static. The convective-reactive
instability can expel He-flash intershell material and thus
rapidly transport i-process products out of the optically
thick stellar interior. The observed H-deficient knots
around late-thermal pulse candidates provide direct ev-
idence that processed intershell material is ejected into
the circumstellar environment. A similar clumpy mor-
phology is seen in nova ejecta, supporting the picture
that radioactive nuclei reside in rapidly expanding, low-
column-density structures [46]. For the MeVγ-ray pho-
tons considered here, the expanding ejecta are therefore
expected to become optically thin on timescales short
compared to the relevant radioactive lifetimes, and sub-
sequent attenuation by circumstellar material is negligi-

RAWDs are considered the best candidates for the
single-degenerate channel leading to the explosion of
Chandrasekhar-mass Type Ia supernovae [e.g., 47, and
references therein]. A RAWD is a CO white dwarf in a
close binary system with a main-sequence, subgiant, or
red giant branch (RGB) companion. It accretes H-rich
material from its companion at a rate of approximately
10−7M⊙ yr−1, allowing the accreted H to burn steadily
on the white dwarf’s surface. This process leads to the
accumulation of a He shell, which eventually undergoes
a thermal flash, triggering theiprocess, envelope expan-
sion, and ejection of material, similar to the born-again
evolution pathway. The key difference is that RAWDs
may experience multiple He-shell flashes andi-process
nucleosynthesis events over time. It has been shown that
in low-mass RAWDs (M WD ≈0.7M ⊙) with an accreted
material metallicity of [Fe/H]≥ −1.55 [48] the reten-
tion efficiency is below 10% [29, 36]. This implies that
more than 90% of the accreted material is ejected into
the circumstellar medium after undergoingi-process nu-
cleosynthesis.
Detailed 1D stellar evolution and 3D hydrodynami-
cal simulations of H-ingestion scenarios show that the
convective-reactive astrophysical fluid dynamics respon-
sible fori-process nucleosynthesis often lead to violent,
non-spherical outbursts, ultimately resulting in a split of
the He convective zone, terminating thei-process, and
mass ejections [30, 36, 40, 49].
To estimate upper limits fori-process yields, the
masses of isotopes ejected by the post-AGB VLTP and
RAWD models into the circumstellar medium , we mul-
tiply their undecayed mass fractions by the He-shell
masses, as derived from the models of [50] and [29], re-
spectively. The mass fractions are averaged over the He
shell, and for comparison with observations, we select
their maximum values over integration timesteps, which
are typically reached by the end of the integrations.

This behavior is reflected in the predicted yields, which

model sequence (e.g., the ejected mass of 89Sr differs by
almost a factor of 40 between models A and D). Because
different isotopes respond differently to metallicity and
neutron exposure, simultaneous detection of multipleγ-
ray lines (such as 22Na, 89Sr, 95Zr, and 137Cs) would
provide direct constraints on the progenitor metallicity
and theiprocess conditions in RAWDs. A sufficiently
nearby event observed by COSI could therefore discrim-
inate between competing RAWD models.
The predicted 137Cs production in RAWD model D
reflects a larger neutron exposure underi-process neu-
tron densities. In this regime, neutron captures proceed
far from the classical s-process path along the valley of
stability but do not reach the extreme conditions of the
rprocess. A confirmedγ-ray detection would therefore
provide a direct, time-integrated probe of nucleosynthesis
in this intermediate neutron-density regime.
Figure 1 assumes a representative distance of 500 pc
to illustrate the detectability of a nearby future VLTP
event. At the actual distance of Sakurai’s object (≈3–
3.5 kpc), the fluxes would be reduced by nearly a factor

that the 1994 eruption occurred over three decades ago,
the decay of short-lived isotopes such as 22Na suppresses
any present-dayγ-ray signal by several orders of mag-
nitude. Our results therefore represent predictions for
a future nearby VLTP event rather than the historical

object, existingγ-ray facilities such as INTEGRAL/SPI
lacked the sensitivity to detect the expected line fluxes, so
the non-detection is consistent with instrumental limits
and motivates next-generation surveys.

During most of its lifetime, a rapidly-accreting white
dwarf (RAWD) steadily accretes H-rich material, which
burns on its surface at high temperature and luminosity,
potentially producing super-soft X-ray radiation. Sev-
eral super-soft X-ray sources, possibly associated with
such systems, have been identified in the Large Magel-
lanic Cloud [56, 57]. Binary population synthesis models
estimate the RAWD formation rate in the Milky Way
galaxy to be on the order of 10 −3 yr−1 [47, 58]. The
recurrence period for RAWD He-shell flashes is approx-
imately 5×10 4 years [29]. Given their low retention
efficiencies, at least for [Fe/H]≥ −1.55, RAWDs may
continue processing accreted H-rich material at a rate of
∼10 −7 M⊙ yr−1 for several million years. Therefore, we
estimate that the Milky Way hosts approximately 1000
RAWDs, with about 9 of them located within 1 kpc of
the Sun. This estimate assumes that the spiral arms
occupy 50% of the Galactic disk and that the disk has
a radius of 15 kpc. Consequently, the annual probabil-
ity of a RAWD ejectingi-process products within 1 kpc
is≈0.018%; over COSI’s two-year prime mission this
corresponds to a cumulative probability of≈0.036%, as-
suming linear scaling at low probabilities.

It is challenging to estimate the occurrence rate of
Sakurai’s object-like events. On the one hand, unlike
the multiple He-shell flashes in RAWDs, post-AGB stars
may undergo only a single VLTP, making such events
less frequent. On the other hand, approximately 20% of
white dwarf remnants from low- and intermediate-mass
AGB stars are expected to experience VLTPs, suggest-
ing a higher probability of their occurrence. The inter-
play of these factors complicates an accurate determina-
tion of the event rate. Three VLTP events have been
recorded within≈5 kpc over the past 100 yr: V605
Aql, HuBi 1 (IRAS 17514−1555), and Sakurai’s object
[45, 59]. Based on this empirical data, the annual proba-
bility of observing another similar event with COSI at a
distance of up to 1 kpc is estimated to be 0.12%, corre-
sponding to 0.24% over COSI’s two-year prime mission
(linear scaling). We can also estimate this probability
using a different approach. Taking a Galactic star forma-
tion rate of 2M⊙ yr−1 [e.g., 60], the Salpeter initial mass
function, and assuming that stars with initial masses be-
tween 1M⊙ and 8M⊙ evolve into post-AGB white dwarfs,
20% of which undergo a VLTP, we estimate an annual
probability for a VLTP event occurring within 1 kpc of
the Sun of 0.04%, corresponding to 0.08% over COSI’s
two-year prime mission.
For an extended mission beyond the two-year prime
phase, the cumulative detection probability would in-
crease approximately linearly with observing time, given
the low event rates considered here.

CONCLUSION
We have identified six rare isotopes produced in
i-process nucleosynthesis in our models of very-late ther-
mal pulses (VLTPs) in post-AGB stars and in rapidly-
accreting white dwarfs (RAWDs). Their radioactive de-
cays generateγ-ray lines that could be detectable by
COSI out to 5 kpc, provided that He-shell flashes in these
stars occur during its observational period. As empha-
sized in recent reviews [16], the i process represents an
emerging frontier in nuclear astrophysics requiring coor-
dinated advances in nuclear experiment, stellar hydrody-
namics, and observational diagnostics. MeVγ-ray spec-
troscopy provides a uniquely direct test of this interme-
diate neutron-density regime.
We have excluded low-mass AGB stars from [28] and
post-RGB stars from [22] from our analysis, asi-process
nucleosynthesis in the former was found to occur only at
[Fe/H]≤ −2.3, and neither of these stellar types is ex-
pected to promptly ejecti-process nucleosynthesis prod-
ucts. However, the presence of second-peaki-process
patterns in open clusters [34], which are high-metallicity
environments, suggests thati-process nucleosynthesis at
nearly solar metallicity could be responsible for the pro-
duction of heavieri-process elements, as proposed by [64].
Among these isotopes, 137Cs stands out as a poten-
tially significantγ-ray line emitter. The yields of 137Cs
from our models are presented in the final row of Ta-
ble I. The 0.662 MeVγline, which corresponds to the
decay of 137Cs, produced in the RAWD D model, re-
sults in a photon flux that, averaged over 2 years, ex-
ceeds the 2-yr COSI sensitivity at 0.662 MeV out to
dmax ≈850 pc, including the published 750 pc distance.
On a 1-year integration this same line is marginal at
750 pc (d max ≈720 pc; see Fig. 4).
Because the 0.662 MeV line from 137Cs remains de-
tectable for roughly two decades (Figure 2) following the
i-process event, its visibility window substantially ex-
ceeds COSI’s two-year prime mission. In this low-rate
regime, the cumulative detection probability therefore
scales approximately with the ratio of the visibility time
to the mission duration, enhancing the effective prob-
ability of observing a past RAWD D event within the
corresponding distance limit by more than an order of
magnitude relative to the two-year baseline estimate.
Our estimates indicate that the probabilities of a post-
AGB star undergoing a VLTP within 1 kpc or a solar-
metallicity RAWD experiencing a He-shell flash within
5 kpc over COSI’s two-year prime mission are both be-
low 1%. If such an event does occur, our results predict
theγ-ray line emissions that may be detectable. The
strongest and most long-lasting emissions are expected
from the radioactive decays of 22Na, 89Sr, and 95Zr, at
energies of 1.275 MeV, 0.909 MeV, and 0.757 MeV, re-
spectively. The predicted mass of 22Na ejected by our
RAWD model A even exceeds the largest mass produced
by nova models.
In the post-AGB VLTP and RAWD models, 22Na is
produced by H burning, similar to ONe nova models,
not by neutron-capture reactions. Therefore, a simul-
taneous observation ofγ-ray lines from decays of 22Na
and ani-process rare isotope would be direct evidence
ofiprocess in H-ingestion He-shell flash events and the
dynamic GOSH. With the relatively long half-life and
large yields of 22Na ejected by our post-AGB VLTP and
RAWD A models, the 1.275 MeVγ-ray line from 22Na
could potentially be detected by COSI for about 10 years
after thei-process in these stars ends. This increases the
estimated probability of detection by COSI for this line
by a factor of 5.
The combination of short half-lives and rapid ejection
makes our identifiedγ-ray lines a characteristic signature
of theiprocess engine. The equilibrium abundances of
short-lived nuclei such as 89Sr, 95Zr, 103Ru, and 123Sn
are negligible in the classical s-process operating in low-
mass AGB stars becauseβdecay outpaces neutron cap-
ture at the modest s-process neutron densities. Even if
these isotopes were produced, the years-long timescale
to bring He-shell material to the surface through con-
vective mixing and slow wind mass loss greatly exceeds
their months-long half-lives, so they decay before reach-
ing the interstellar medium. The post-AGB VLTP and
RAWD scenarios studied here are the only known stel-
lar sites that combinei-process neutron densities of or-
der 10 13–1015 cm−3 [thei-process engine, 16] with the
prompt, dynamically driven mass ejection of [the GOSH,
40]. This combination delivers the short-lived isotopes to
the interstellar medium before they decay. The consid-
ered neutron-rich unstable species could in principle be
the decay product ofn-process-nucleosynthesis in explo-
sive He-shell burning during a core-collapse supernova
[65], but a nearby supernova would be unambiguously

A non-detection over COSI’s two-year prime mission
carries diagnostic weight only if a nearby post-AGB
VLTP or RAWD He-shell flash demonstrably occurred
during the window, with independent observational con-

and noγ-ray lines were detected, the constraint would
bear on the astrophysics of the ejection (its efficiency,
timing, and degree of dynamical coupling to the underly-
ing nuclear engine) as much as on the predicted nuclear
yields themselves, which already vary by 1–2 orders of
magnitude across our model grid (Table I).
Stellar evolution models of RAWDs and post-AGB
VLTP stars predictingγ-ray line fluxes are still being de-
veloped and refined. As discussed in [16] the convective-
reactive nature of thei-process engine implies tight inte-
gration of nuclear energy generation and turbulent ad-
vection, an inherently three-dimensional problem that
cannot be fully captured by 1D stellar evolution mod-
els. The resulting uncertainties in the predictedi-process
yields are difficult to quantify, but they could potentially
impact the predictedγ-ray line fluxes in both directions.
For instance, if the GOSH is less violent than predicted
by current 3D hydrodynamical simulations, the mass
ejection and thus thei-process yields could be reduced.
Conversely, if the GOSH is more violent, it could lead
to more efficient mixing and higher neutron densities,
potentially increasing the production of certain isotopes.
There are also additional potential sites ofγ-ray emission
associated withi-process that we have not considered
here, such as super-AGB stars , for which dynamic mass
ejections have explicitely been speculated about [66].
