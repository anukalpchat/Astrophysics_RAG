ABSTRACT
The repeated tidal stripping of a star by a supermassive black hole, known as a repeating partial
tidal disruption event (rpTDE), can give rise to a transient that rebrightens months to years after the
first outburst. Among the rpTDE candidates so far observed, some exhibit dimmer peak luminosities
during each successive outburst, which is a trend that has not been reproduced from theoretical models
when the star survives more than one encounter with the black hole. Here we suggest that this trend
can be recovered if the partially disrupted star is initially (i.e., prior to its first mass-stripping event)
rapidly rotating, which is expected if the star was placed on its orbit through the Hills breakup
of a tidally locked and tight binary. We test this hypothesis with hydrodynamical simulations of
high-mass ( ⩾ 1M⊙) main sequence stars repeatedly partially disrupted by a 10 6M⊙ black hole, and
demonstrate that successively dimmer outbursts are indeed recovered for high (tens of percent breakup)
and prograde (i.e., aligned with the orbital angular momentum) stellar spins. Our results provide strong
indirect evidence for the operation of the Hills mechanism in seeding the stars in rpTDEs.

1. INTRODUCTION
A tidal disruption event (TDE) occurs when a star
is partially or completely destroyed by the tidal field
of a supermassive black hole (J. G. Hills 1975; M. J.
Rees 1988). Some of the tidally stripped debris remains
bound and ultimately accretes onto the black hole, pro-
ducing an observable flare. The distance at which the
tidal field of the black hole becomes comparable to the
self-gravity of the star is denoted as the tidal radius
rt = R⋆(M•/M⋆)1/3 (M⋆ and R⋆ are the stellar mass
and radius and M• is the black hole mass).
With the advent of wide-field time-domain surveys,
≳ 100 TDEs have been detected to date (e.g, I. Arcavi
et al. 2014; T. W. S. Holoien et al. 2014; S. Gezari et al.
2017; D. R. Pasham & S. van Velzen 2018; S. van Velzen
et al. 2021; A. V. Payne et al. 2021; T. Wevers et al. 2021;
Z. Lin et al. 2022; M. Nicholl et al. 2022; M. Guolo et al.

the observed orbital period. Alternatively, the system
could be formed in situ via the dynamical capture of a
star following the tidal breakup of a tight binary, i.e.,
Hills capture (J. G. Hills 1988; M. Cufari et al. 2022a;
W. Lu & E. Quataert 2023) (it is energetically infeasi-
ble to form a sufficiently tight orbit with a partial TDE
alone; M. Cufari et al. 2023).

Existing hydrodynamical simulations can produce a
dimmer second flare if the star survives only one en-
counter, i.e., if the majority of its mass is lost on the first
encounter and it is completely destroyed during its sec-
ond pericenter passage (see, e.g., Figure 7 of L. Makry-
gianni et al. 2025). The inability to generate progres-
sively dimming flares for more than two orbits presents
a notable tension between rpTDE models and observed
candidates. Here we suggest that the initial spin of the
star is the necessary physical ingredient (absent from ex-
isting hydrodynamical simulations of rpTDEs, e.g., A.
Bandopadhyay et al. 2024; C. Liu et al. 2025) that re-
produces this effect for more than two outbursts. Specif-
ically, while Hills capture can yield orbits that are con-
sistent with the ≲ 1 year orbital periods of observed
rpTDE systems, the required binary separations are suf-
ficiently tight that the binary and stellar binding ener-
gies are comparable. If the binary was tidally locked
prior to its breakup, the Hills-captured star is then ex-
pected to be spinning at a significant fraction of its peri-
center angular frequency Ωp =

2. HYDRODYNAMICAL SIMULATIONS
2.1. Simulation Setup
We used the Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics
(SPH) code phantom (D. J. Price et al. 2018) to model
the disruption of main sequence stars with structures de-
rived from mesa (B. Paxton et al. 2011), each of which is
sampled with 106 particles and set uniformly rotating at
an angular frequency Ω = λΩ⋆, where Ω ⋆ =
p
GM⋆/R3⋆
and −1 ≲ λ ≲ 1 is the dimensionless spin. We simu-
late the disruption of stars at zero age main-sequence
(ZAMS; when the core hydrogen fraction is Xc ∼ 0.7),
middle age main-sequence (MAMS; when Xc ∼ 0.2),
and terminal age main-sequence (TAMS; whenXc drops
below ∼ 0.001). Each simulation is initialized with the
star on a parabolic orbit about a 10 6M⊙ black hole at
a distance of 5 rt with pericenter distance rp = rt/β;
see E. C. A. Golightly et al. (2019a); C. J. Nixon et al.
(2021); A. Bandopadhyay et al. (2024) for additional
numerical details. Since the near-pericenter dynamics
appropriate to a highly eccentric orbit ( e ≳ 0.99, as is
relevant for the case of rpTDEs) are effectively equiva-
lent to those of a parabolic orbit, and the fallback rates
are likewise quite similar (M. Cufari et al. 2022b), these
results can be applied to observed systems with a wide
range of orbital periods. To model multiple encounters
we followed the procedure outlined in A. Bandopadhyay
et al. (2024), and we used the procedures in P. R. Miles
et al. (2020); C. J. Nixon et al. (2021); A. Bandopadhyay
et al. (2026) to calculate the fallback rates.

3. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
Some rpTDE candidates exhibit multiple flares with
successively dimmer peak luminosities, which is a trend
that is difficult to reproduce with hydrodynamical sim-
ulations over more than two encounters – if a star loses
most of its mass on the first tidal interaction with the
black hole, the second outburst can be less luminous
than the first, but at the expense of completely destroy-
ing it and preventing more than two accretion episodes
(e.g., Figure 7 from L. Makrygianni et al. 2025). While
some systems have only displayed two outbursts so far
(e.g., AT2022dbl, AT2021aeuk) and could be consistent
with the star being completely destroyed on the second
encounter, eRASSt-J045650 has thus far exhibited at
least 5 outbursts and AT2018fyk showed evidence for a
second cutoff in its emission (D. Pasham et al. 2024) (im-
plying the survival of the star on its second encounter;
T. Wevers et al. 2023), raising the question of how – or
if – such systems can be reproduced with the rpTDE
model.
Our results in Section 2 show that for relatively high-
mass stars ( M⋆ ≳ 1M⊙), if the star already pos-

As the tidal breakup of a tight binary system is thought
to be the means of generating in situ rpTDEs (M. Cufari
et al. 2022a), the existence of multiple and successively
dimmer outbursts provides strong evidence for the op-
eration of this mechanism.
We expect the initial stellar rotation to most efficiently
reduce the accretion rate for prograde stellar spins. Con-
versely, when the spin is retrograde with a magnitude
≲ Ωp, the relative motion between the star and the black
hole (in the co-rotating frame of the star) acts to impart
prograde angular momentum to the star. This results
in an increased amount of mass lost per encounter and
a shorter return time of the debris to the black hole
(E. C. A. Golightly et al. 2019a), the combined effect
from which is progressively brighter flares. However,
this trend likely does not continue for (retrograde) spins
above ∼ Ωp, as in this case the black hole makes more
than one complete rotation about the star (in the co-
rotating frame) near pericenter. Correspondingly the
net effect of the tidal torque is likely significantly re-
duced and the stellar spin remains effectively unmodi-
fied; we intend to explore this scenario in future work.
For the rpTDE candidate AT2018fyk, the peak lu-
minosity decreases by approximately an order of magni-
tude between the first and second outbursts (see Figure 1
of T. Wevers et al. 2023). To generate a factor of 10 de-
cline in the peak, our model suggests that the star would
likely have to be spinning at an angular velocity Ω sub-
stantially higher than Ω p. For β ≳ 0.6 − 0.8 orbits (for
lower values of β high-mass stars lose a negligible frac-
tion of their mass, yielding proportionately lower peak
luminosities), this would require Ω ≫ Ω⋆, calling into
question the stability (and existence) of such systems.
However, the inferred black hole mass in AT2018fyk is
M• ∼ 107.7±0.4M⊙, implying that this system possessed
a highly relativistic pericenter and that the binary was
very tight (T. Wevers et al. 2023; D. R. Pasham et al.
2024). In this regime, relativistic and chaotic three-body
effects could lead to a difference in the pericenter dis-
tance of the captured star between the first and second
encounter, plausibly generating a larger reduction in the
amount of mass stripped and reproducing the observed
decline in peak luminosity.
Finally, the Hills mechanism is necessary for generat-
ing the short orbital periods in observed rpTDE systems
(M. Cufari et al. 2022a), and for high-mass black holes
– as is inferred from the M − σ relation for some rpTDE
candidates (e.g., ASASSN-14ko, AT2018fyk, eRASSt-
J045650) – the required binary separations are very
tight. The tidally locked state of the star then naturally
leads to a stellar rotation frequency that is comparable
to Ω ⋆, and the high values of λ that generate the de-
clining trend in outburst strength with each successive
pericenter passage (Figures 1 – 4) are manifestly recov-
ered. The Hills mechanism can therefore place rapidly
spinning stars on an orbit around a supermassive black
hole that gives rise to progressively dimmer flares, such
as those exhibited by eRASSt-J045650. Alternatively,
while this leads to a self-consistency constraint on the
stellar spin for some observed rpTDE candidates with
orbital periods ∼ few × 100 days, the star may not be
tidally locked with its binary companion and could be
rotating at a super-orbital rate. This may alleviate a po-
tential tension that could arise between a wide binary
separation required to generate a longer recurrence time
(e.g., ∼ 1300 days for AT2018fyk), and a high value of
the stellar spin that would be simultaneously required
to generate a substantial drop in luminosity from one
outburst to the next.
