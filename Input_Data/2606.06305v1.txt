ABSTRACT
We report the detection of a short optical burst in TESS data o f IGR J17014-4306, the eclipsing intermediate polar with th e
longest known orbital period. The burst lasts 1.56 d and show s multiple peaks, reaching ( 9. 3 ± 0. 2) × 1033 erg s− 1, and releases
a total energy of ( 3. 25 ± 0. 01) × 1038 erg. The burst parameters are consistent with those of a micr onova eruption, currently
understood as a thermonuclear runaway in the magnetically- conﬁned accretion column. From its energy, we infer a burned
column mass of ∼ 1. 6 × 10− 11 M⊙ , which implies a recurrence time of ∼ 20 d. Our search for similar events in long-term Gaia,
ASAS-SN, and AA VSO light curves reveals 16 possible fast brightenings over ∼ 11 yr, suggesting that micronova events may be
frequent in IGR J17014-4306. Timing analysis of the TESS dat a shows that the white-dwarf spin period remains stable befo re
and after the burst. During the burst, however, the power spe ctrum becomes more complex and exhibits multiple peaks. The
classiﬁcation of IGR J17014-4306 as a micronova brings the t otal number of conﬁrmed systems to eight. Its extreme orbita l
period and eclipsing nature make it an ideal test-bed for further studies of magnetically conﬁned thermonuclear burnin g on white
dwarfs.

1 INTRODUCTION
Cataclysmic Variables (CV s) are binary stellar systems in w hich a
white dwarf accretes material from a low-mass, main-sequen ce or
slightly evolved companion through Roche lobe overﬂow (
Warner
1995). Most often, this process takes place through an accretion disc
that forms around the white dwarf. In systems where the white dwarf
possesses a suﬃciently strong magnetic ﬁeld, the ionized ac cretion
stream couples to the magnetic ﬁeld lines. This interaction channels
material directly onto the white-dwarf’s surface near its m agnetic
poles via accretion columns or curtains. These magnetic Cataclysmic
Variables (mCV s) are subdivided into two classes. Polars, character-
ized by high magnetic-ﬁeld strengths (B ∼ 7 − 230 MG), exhibit
synchronized white-dwarf spin and orbital periods due to ma gnetic
locking. In contrast, Intermediate Polars (IPs), with weak er ﬁelds
(B∼ 1 − 10 MG), are not synchronized; their distinct spin, beat, and
orbital periods may be detectable in optical and X-ray emissions. IPs
usually possess an accretion disk that is truncated at its in ner radius
by the white-dwarf’s magnetosphere.
Accretion disks in CV s can undergo recurrent thermal instabilities
known as Dwarf Nova (DN) outbursts, explained in terms of the
disk instability model (DIM,
Lasota 2001 ). DN outbursts increase
the system’s luminosity by 3 – 6 magnitudes in timescales as s hort

as few days, with recurrence intervals of weeks or months. In IPs,
however, these events are scarce and seem to be shorter than i n (full-
disc) non-magnetic CV s ( Hameury & Lasota 2017 ). Furthermore,
the short outbursts observed in IPs cannot be understood in t he DIM
scenario.
However, DN outbursts are not the only type of rapid bursts re gis-
tered in mCV s. Donor star ﬂares and the recently identiﬁed mi crono-
vae and magnetic-gating bursts share similar observational character-
istics, making their proper classiﬁcation diﬃcult. Micron ovae have
been proposed by Scaringi et al. (2022c) to explain the rapid succes-
sion of bursts observed in the IPs TV Col and EI UMa. They are
best-explained as thermonuclear runaways on a small portio n of the
white-dwarf surface, similar to the type-I X-ray bursts common in X-
ray binary neutron stars. In contrast to classical nova eruptions, which
are thermonuclear runaways spread over the entire white-dw arf sur-
face, micronovae are restricted to the base of the accretioncolumn by
magnetic conﬁnement of the accretion stream (
Scaringi et al. 2022b).
In the magnetic-gating model ( D’ Angelo & Spruit 2012 ;
Scaringi et al. 2017 ), accretion from the donor star is trapped at the
disc’s co-rotation radius by a centrifugal barrier created by the rotat-
ing magnetosphere of the white dwarf. When the increasing pressure
eventually allows the accumulated material to cross the bar rier, the
material is discharged onto the white dwarf, and a short burs t of
accretion energy is released. Currently, the identiﬁed mem bers of the
magnetic-gating group are MV Lyr ( Scaringi et al. 2017 ), TW Pic

IGR J17014-4306 was identiﬁed in hard X-rays as a possible
mCV by Masetti et al. (2013). Observations from XMM-Newton
(Bernardini et al. 2017 ) revealed total X-ray eclipses and partial
eclipses in the optical B band, alongside modulation s of 1859 s
corresponding to the white-dwarf spin. IGR J17014-4306 has an or-
bital period of 12.8 hours, making it the eclipsing IP with th e longest
known orbital period.
Shara et al. (2017) proposed that IGR J17014-4306 is the coun-
terpart of the historical Nova Sco 1437, a fast-declining cl assical
nova. The same study also identiﬁed three transient brighte nings in
the DASCH1 optical light curve, occurring in 1934, 1935, and 1942.
During these events, the system brightened from a quiescent mag-
nitude of ∼ 17 to approximately 12 in 1934 and to 14.5–15 in the
subsequent eruptions. These brightenings were interprete d as dwarf-
nova outbursts. The proposed connection to Nova Sco 1437 hassince
been challenged by Guerrero et al. (2025), who concluded that the
ionized mass of the nebula and its expansion velocity are inconsistent
with those expected for a nova remnant. Instead, they argue t hat the
nebula is likely an evolved planetary nebula, unrelated to the remnant
of Nova Sco 1437, and that IGR J17014-4306 is by chance projec ted
onto the nebula.
The IP nature of IGR J17014-4306 was corroborated by
Potter & Buckley (2018), who detected spin-modulated circular po-
larization. The stability of the polarization sign, despit e variations in
intensity, implies that only one magnetic pole of the white dwarf is ob-
served. A decrease in circular polarization during the eclipse is likely
caused by the occultation of the cyclotron emission region. More re-
cently, by analysing TESS and AA VSO eclipse timings, Zang et al.
(2025) claimed that the orbital period of IGR J17014-4306 is in-
creasing at a remarkably high rate, comparable to that obser ved in
compact binary supersoft X-ray sources (CBSS) and recurrent novae.
In this paper, we present the detection and analysis of a rapi d
burst observed in IGR J17014-4306 by TESS in June 2025. Section 2
describes the TESS data and calibration. Section3 presents the results
of the burst analysis, and Section 4 provides the discussion and
conclusions.

3.2 The burst classiﬁcation
Iłkiewicz et al. (2024) proposed empirical diagnostic diagrams based
on the integrated energies, peak luminosities, durations, and recur-
rence frequencies to diﬀerentiate the observational signa tures of the
various types of rapid optical bursts observed in CV s. Their results
demonstrate that dwarf-nova eruptions, micronovae, magne tically
gating bursts, and donor ﬂares occupy separate regions in th e param-
eter space, indicating that these phenomena originate from diﬀerent
physical processes.
Following the prescription from Tu et al. (2020) and
Iłkiewicz et al. (2024), the burst frequency in IGR J17014-4306
is 0.01 d − 1, calculated as the ratio of the number of bursts to
the continuous monitoring period. Table 1 lists the bursts peak
optical luminosity, total optical energy, duration, and fr equency
for IGR J17014-4306 and the known sample of micronovae, whil e
Fig. 2 presents these objects in the diagnostic diagrams alongsid e
the magnetic-gating, donor ﬂare, and dwarf novae systems fr om
Iłkiewicz et al. (2024). The diagrams show that the burst detected
in IGR J17014-4306 in June 2025 is consistent with a micronov a
eruption.
3.3 Previous bursts
Except for sector 93, none of the preceding TESS sectors (12, 39, or
66) displays any hint of bursts in IGR J17014-4306. To evalua te the
occurrence of other bursts, we created a long-term light cur ve using
Gaia, ASAS-SN, and AA VSO data (Fig.
3). The average brightness
in quiescence ﬂuctuates between ∼ 15. 5 and ∼ 16. 5 magnitudes.
There are many points consistent with bursts, many of which a re
not isolated, and the brightest one reaches around 12.5 mag. We
arbitrarily selected the data points brighter than 15 mag, r esulting
in 16 possible events (marked as dashed vertical lines in Fig . 3).
The burst at BJD 2457900 was also detected is the ASAS-SN data
by
Mendelsohn et al. (2026), who interpreted it as an evidence of a
short outburst but not a deﬁnitive micronova identiﬁcation . Due to
the data sampling, the Gaia, ASAS-SN, and AA VSO light curvesare
prone to miss bursts. This is exactly the case of the burst det ected in
TESS sector 93 (see Fig. 1): ASAS has a relatively good coverage
during this sector, but it was not able to detect the burst. Th e lower
panels of Fig. 3 detail four bursts, some of which are simultaneously
covered by ASAS-SN and AA VSO data. These selected bursts las t
less than 4 d, with recurrence times as short as 27 days, and th e burst
peaks are possibly missing.

4 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
The burst detected in IGR J17014-4306 during TESS Sector 93
reaches a peak optical luminosity of ( 9. 3 ± 0. 2) × 1033 erg s− 1, a
total radiated energy of ( 3. 25 ± 0. 01) × 1038 erg, and lasts 1 . 56 d.
When placed on the diagnostic diagrams of
Iłkiewicz et al. (2024, see
Fig. 2), it falls within the region occupied by known micronovae an d
clearly away from dwarf-nova outbursts, magnetically gate d bursts,
and donor ﬂares. This provides strong evidence that the even t in
IGR J17014-4306 is powered by a localized thermonuclear runaway.
Besides the bursts we detected in the long-term Gaia, ASAS-S N,
and AA VSO optical monitoring, Shara et al. (2017) identiﬁed three
transient brightenings (in 1934, 1935, and 1942) in historical DASCH
observations, which they interpreted as dwarf-nova outbursts. Given
the similarities of the amplitudes, these historical erupt ions are plau-
sible micronova events.
Assuming hydrogen burning through the CNO cycle with an en-
ergy release of ∼ 1016 erg g − 1 (e.g. Starrﬁeld 1971 ; Starrﬁeld et al.
1972), the observed burst energy of IGR J17014-4306 implies a
burned column mass of /u1D440col ≃ 1. 6 × 10− 11 M⊙ .

The micronova trigger mechanism proposed by Scaringi et al.
(2022b) favours systems with both high white-dwarf mass and high
mass-transfer rate, which accelerates the build-up of fuel at the base
of the magnetically conﬁned accretion column. For systems above the
period gap, angular momentum loss is dominated by magnetic b rak-
ing, leading to higher secular mass-transfer rates than bel ow the gap,
where gravitational radiation dominates. Consistent withthis picture,
IGR J17014-4306 is among the IPs with the longest orbital per iods,
holds the record for the longest orbital period among micron ova
systems, and harbours a massive white dwarf, /u1D440WD ≃ 0. 94 /u1D440⊙
(Souza et al. 2026 ).
The lack of conﬁrmed micronovae in polars is particularly no te-
worthy. The mass required to trigger a micronova scales withthe frac-
tional accretion area /u1D453, and polars are expected to have the smallest
/u1D453values among magnetic CV s (Scaringi et al. 2022c ), which would
favour ignition. On the other hand, polars lie predominantly below the
period gap (e.g. Ferrario et al. 2015 ), where white-dwarf masses are
typically lower and gravitational radiation drives low mas s-transfer
rates. Such low /dotacc/u1D440 would lengthen recurrence times, making mi-
cronovae in polars more diﬃcult to detect.
The white-dwarf spin behaviour oﬀers further insight into t he
event’s nature. During the burst, the power spectrum shows m ul-
tiple peaks, including a signal near but not exactly at the sp in
frequency. This resembles that reported by Irving et al. (2024) for
PBC J0801.2− 4625 and is interpreted as possible micronova oscil-
lations, analogous to Type I X-ray burst oscillations. Our a nalysis of
TESS Sector 93 shows that the spin period ( /u1D443spin ≃ 1859 s) remains
stable before and after the burst. This indicates that angul ar momen-
tum variation during a single micronova is negligible compa red to
secular torques on the white dwarf.
In summary, the TESS detection of a short optical burst in
IGR J17014-4306, combined with long-term optical monitori ng,
strongly supports its classiﬁcation as a recurrent microno va. To date,
eight micronovae are conﬁrmed, all of which are probably IPs , and
they appear to be homogeneously distributed over the orbita l period
range. The system thus provides a valuable addition to this still small
population and, owing to its long orbital period and eclipsi ng geom-
etry, oﬀers a promising laboratory for further tests of magn etically
conﬁned thermonuclear burning on white dwarfs.
