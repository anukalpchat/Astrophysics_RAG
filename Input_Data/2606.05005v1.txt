Recent DESI observations indicate a deviation from the ΛCDM model, showing a preference for
dynamical dark energy and thereby relaxing the upper limit on the neutrino mass within this frame-
work. This deviation can also be explained by the presence of an interaction between dark energy
and dark matter. In this work, we investigate the cosmological upper bounds on the total neu-
trino mass (P mν) across four different interacting dark energy (IDE) models. The present analysis
employs the latest DESI baryon acoustic oscillation, cosmic microwave background, and type Ia
supernova datasets. These results demonstrate that the upper bounds on P mν exhibit profound
sensitivity to the specific phenomenological formulation of the interaction term. While the IΛCDM2
model (Q∝Hρ c) substantially relaxes the stringent upper limit ( P mν <0.129 eV at 95% confi-
dence level), notably the IΛCDM3 model (Q∝H 0ρde), severely compresses the allowed parameter
space, yielding a highly restrictive bound of P mν <0.051 eV. Furthermore, rigorous goodness-of-
fit evaluations utilizing the Deviance Information Criterion and ∆χ 2
MAP indicate that the current
observational data statistically favor these mass-suppressing IDE models. This establishes an ex-
acerbated statistical tension between the observationally preferred IDE scenarios and the normal
hierarchy lower bound (∼0.06 eV) determined by terrestrial neutrino oscillation experiments.
I. INTRODUCTION
Recent observational data from the Dark Energy Spec-
troscopic Instrument (DESI) have brought new break-
throughs to cosmological research. In particular, the re-
cently released DESI baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO)
data, when analyzed in combination with cosmic mi-
crowave background (CMB) and type Ia supernova (SN)
data, exhibit a significant deviation from the standard
ΛCDM model [1, 2]. This deviation achieves substantial
statistical significance (approximately 2.8−4.2σ) within
the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) parametrization,
which implies that the equation of state (EoS) of dark en-
ergy may not be the cosmological constant (w=−1). In-
stead, it appears to exhibit a dynamical evolution, specif-
ically evolving fromw <−1 at early times tow >−1
at late times, which is referred to as quintom behav-
ior [3–7]. This phenomenon, revealed by the DESI data,
has rapidly sparked widespread interest and intense dis-
cussion within the cosmological community [8–72]. Al-
though early perspectives questioned whether this signal
might stem from systematic errors in the SN data [73, 74],
the rigorously calibrated DES-Dovekie SN sample sub-
sequently released by the Dark Energy Survey collabo-
ration retained 3.2σevidence supporting this deviation,
thus further consolidating the observational basis for the
existence of dynamical dark energy [75].
Additionally, neutrinos, as a unique relativistic com-

cosmic expansion history and the formation of large-scale
structures at late times [35, 76–134]. Consequently, cos-

While particle physicsβ-decay experiments provide di-
rect laboratory measurements of P mν (with the latest
KATRIN results yielding P mν <1.35 eV [135, 136]),
the joint analysis of the CMB and DESI Data Release 2
(DR2) data within the ΛCDM model sets a cosmologi-
cal upper bound on the total neutrino mass of P mν <
0.064 eV. This constraint not only far exceeds laboratory
precision but also approaches the lower bound given by
neutrino oscillation experiments and is in tension with
the inverted hierarchy. This highlights that current cos-
mological observations impose significantly stricter limits
onP mν than particle physics experiments [1].
Nevertheless, cosmological bounds on P mν exhibit
strong model dependence, as different dark energy sce-
narios can substantially alter these constraints [76, 91,
102, 107, 119, 137–139]. In a series of previous stud-
ies, we specifically focused on the cosmological degen-
eracy between P mν and the dark energy EoS param-
eterw[80, 88, 91]. Our research demonstrated that
the dynamical evolution of dark energy fromw <−1
tow >−1, as hinted by DESI observations, can ef-
fectively relax the upper bound on the total neutrino
mass [119]. Building on this framework, Duet al.[126]
demonstrated that incorporating weak gravitational lens-
ing data to simultaneously constrain P mν and the ef-
fective number of relativistic speciesN eff within the CPL

However, purely dynamical dark energy models pri-
marily alter the background expansion history of the uni-
verse, having a relatively limited direct impact on cos-
mic structure formation. Meanwhile, cosmological con-
straints on neutrino masses rely heavily on the CMB
power spectrum, meaning that ncosmological constraints
on neutrino mass are intrinsically much more sensitive
to the details of structure formation than to background
expansion alone. To further investigate this mechanism,
we turn to explore the interacting dark energy (IDE)
model [140–155]. Evaluated against the latest observa-
tional data, IDE models not only yield strong evidence
for a non-zero dark sector interaction at a statistical con-
fidence level of 3−5σ, but their parameter constraints
also significantly deviate from the standard cosmological
limits [150, 151, 154–157]. The most compelling feature
of the IDE framework is that, while it can phenomeno-
logically mimic the background expansion history of dy-
namical dark energy, its evolutionary dynamics at the
perturbation level (which govern structure formation) are
profoundly different. Therefore, reinvestigating neutrino
mass constraints under the IDE framework is theoreti-
cally well-motivated and highly necessary.
In this work, we comprehensively investigate the im-
pact of IDE models on neutrino mass measurements. We
measure the total neutrino mass within the framework
of IDE by examining four distinct forms of the inter-
action termQ. To constrain the models, we utilize a
robust combination of the latest cosmological observa-
tions, incorporating BAO from DESI, CMB data from
Planck and Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT), and
SN compilations including PantheonPlus, DESY5, and
DES-Dovekie.
This paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we briefly
introduce the IDE models, massive neutrinos, and the
observational datasets used in this work. In Sec. III, we
report the joint constraint results and provide relevant
discussions. Finally, we conclude this work in Sec. IV.

gate these nonphysical divergences, the extended Param-

provides a stable calculation of dark energy perturbations
across the entire parameter space [143, 166–168].

By incorporating P mν into the baseline interactive
framework, the extended models are hereafter denoted as
the IΛCDM+P mν models. In this analysis, we adopt a
hybrid scheme for the neutrino mass hierarchy based onP mν calculation. Specifically, when P mν <0.06 eV,
we perform the calculation assuming a degenerate hier-
archy (DH), where the three active neutrino eigenstates
are treated as equal (m 1 =m 2 =m 3). Conversely, whenP mν >0.06 eV, we calculate the physical quantities as-
suming a normal hierarchy (NH). We treat P mν as a
free parameter with a flat prior P mν ∈[0,5] eV.
To constrain the free parameters of the IDE model andP mν, we employ the public packageCobaya. The the-
oretical predictions are computed usingIDECAMB[168].
The MCMC chains are analyzed using the public pack-
ageGetDist[173]. The specific datasets utilized in this
work are as follows:
•CMB: We utilize the Planck CMB likelihoods for
TT and EE spectra in the multipole range 2< ℓ <
30 [174, 175]. For high multipoles, we employ the
NPIPECamSpeclikelihood for the TT spectrum
in the range 30≤ℓ≤2500, and for the TE and
EE spectra in the range 30< ℓ <2000 [176, 177].
Additionally, we include the CMB lensing likeli-
hood utilizing the latest high-precision reconstruc-
tion from NPIPE PR4 Planck data [178] and ACT
Data Release 6 [179–181].
•DESI: We employ the BAO measurements from
the DESI DR2 [1]. The data vector includes the
transverse comoving distanceD M/rd, the angle-
averaged distanceD V/rd, and the Hubble horizon
DH/rd. These measurements are derived from mul-
tiple tracers, including the bright galaxy sample, lu-
minous red galaxies, emission line galaxies, quasars,
and the Lyman-αforest.
•DESY5: The DESY5 sample comprises 1829 su-
pernovae. It consists of 1635 photometrically clas-
sified supernovae from the Dark Energy Survey 5-
year data in the redshift range 0.1< z <1.3, and
194 low-redshift supernovae from external samples
(CfA3, CfA4, CSP, and Foundation) in the range
0.025< z <0.1 [182–186].
•DES-Dovekie: We utilize the fully recalibrated
DESY5 sample, referred to as DES-Dovekie. This
dataset incorporates improved photometric cross-
calibration using Gaia and HST white dwarf stan-
dards to reduce systematic uncertainties in the
color-luminosity relation. The sample comprises
1623 DES supernovae and 197 low-z supernovae,
providing updated constraints on the distance mod-
ulus [75].
•PantheonPlus: The PantheonPlus sample com-
prises 1550 spectroscopically confirmed supernovae
collected from 18 different surveys, spanning the
redshift range 0.01< z <2.26 [187].

Since both Ω m and massive neutrinos affect the evolu-
tion of large-scale structures, the growth index (γ) is in-
troduced to characterize the growth rate of matter per-
turbations, with the standard ΛCDM model predicting
γ≃0.55. Ifγis treated as a free parameter, the upper
bound on P mν can be relaxed to 0.13−0.2 eV. This
mechanism can eliminate the conflict without changing
the fitted value of Ω m, but it requires the growth rate
of cosmic matter structures to be suppressed: to sat-
isfy the lower bound of terrestrial experiments (P mν ≥
0.06 eV), it is required thatγ >0.55. After introducing
this physical lower bound prior, current data exhibits a
statistical preference of 2.5σ−3.0σforγ >0.55.
To more clearly illustrate the correlations between
the neutrino mass and other parameters, we present
the two-dimensional contour plots of P mν versus key
parameters (β,H 0, Ω m, andσ 8) for each model us-
ing the CMB+DESI+DES-Dovekie data in Fig. 2. We
can clearly observe that the different forms of interac-
tion significantly alter the correlations between param-
eters. Among the four models, IΛCDM2+ P mν is sub-
ject to the strictest constraints. In the matter-dominated
epoch of the early universe, both the Hubble parame-
terHand the critical densityρ c are at extremely high
values, implying that the interaction source termHρ c
itself is large. However, the observational precision of
the CMB is extremely high, and any substantial un-
suppressed energy exchange would induce severe devi-
ations, disrupting the theoretical concordance with ob-
servational data. Consequently, to maintain the calcu-
lated interaction intensity within CMB constraints de-
spite the extremely large source termHρ c,βis forced
to be suppressed to a very small numerical range. In
contrast, the other three models are less constrained.
Specifically, the interaction terms in IΛCDM1+ P mν
and IΛCDM3+P mν depend on the dark energy density
ρde. Since dark energy only becomes dominant in the late
universe (z <1), the interaction term is minimal in the
early universe, thereby preserving a larger allowable pa-
rameter space. On the other hand, for IΛCDM4+ P mν
and IΛCDM2+P mν, although both involve the criti-
cal densityρ c, the IΛCDM4+ P mν formula uses the
constantH 0 instead of the dynamically evolvingH(z).

SinceH(z) was far greater thanH 0 in the past, the in-
teraction generated by the IΛCDM4+P mν model in the
early universe (at high redshift) is far smaller than that of
IΛCDM2+P mν. Therefore, the influence of this model
on early physical processes is relatively weak, ultimately
resulting in relatively loose parameter constraints.
We also observe a negative correlation betweenσ 8 andP mν.σ 8 directly reflects the degree of matter clus-
tering. However, as a high-velocity matter component,
neutrinos induce a free-streaming effect that smooths
out small-scale structures in the cosmic matter distri-
bution. The larger P mν, the more significant its sup-
pression of structural growth, manifesting as a suppres-
sion of the matter power spectrum at high wavenum-
bers (small scales). Since the scale corresponding toσ 8
falls precisely within the sensitive range of this effect, an
increase in P mν inevitably leads to a decrease in the
value ofσ 8. It is worth noting that for models where
Q∝ρ de (IΛCDM1+P mν and IΛCDM3+P mν), even
though the yielded P mν values are small, theσ 8 values
remain low, a phenomenon consistent with results from
other literature [17, 127, 154, 188].

IV. CONCLUSION
In this work, we investigate the cosmological con-
straints onP mν within the IDE framework, utilizing the
latest observational data from CMB, DESI DR2, and SN
(DESY5, PantheonPlus, and DES-Dovekie). We focus
on exploring four distinct forms of interaction models.
Furthermore, we perform a quantitative comparison of
the goodness-of-fit across different models by calculating
∆χ2
MAP and the DIC.
Our analysis indicates that introducing an interaction
significantly alters the constraints onP mν, with the spe-
cific effects depending strongly on the form of the inter-
action termQ. We find that the IΛCDM2+ P mν model
(Q∝Hρ c) yields the most relaxed constraints onP mν.
Compared to the standard ΛCDM model, this model re-
laxes the upper limit of P mν by approximately 64%–
98% across different data combinations, relaxing the con-
straint toP mν <0.129 eV. This is primarily attributed
to the positive values of the coupling constantβin this
model, which accommodates a larger P mν. Conversely,
for models where the interaction term is proportional to
the dark energy density, specifically IΛCDM1 + P mν
and IΛCDM3 + P mν, dark energy dominates only at
late times. Because these models involve the decay of
dark energy into dark matter, they are less constrained
in the early universe, leading to a further suppression ofP mν upper limit. Notably, in the IΛCDM3 + P mν
model (Q∝H 0ρde), the constraint obtained by com-
bining with DESY5 data is P mν <0.051 eV. This re-
sult suppresses P mν below the minimum mass bound
of the NH. In particular, the quantitative comparison
of model goodness-of-fit reveals that when DESY5 SN
data are included, the IΛCDM3 + P mν model exhibits
a relatively strong preference over the standard ΛCDM
model (∆DIC =−8.03). This result further underscores
the possible tension between current cosmological obser-
vations and the NH lower bound from particle physics
neutrino oscillation experiments.
In summary, our study highlights the pivotal impact of
IDE models onP mν measurements and further empha-
sizes the strong dependence of cosmological P mν con-
straints on the dark energy model. It is worth noting
that within the framework of the IDE models favored by
current observational data, the derived upper limit onP mν is lower than the lower bound required by par-
ticle physics experiments, revealing a tension between
cosmological observations and particle physics measure-
ments. In the future, with the release of full-shape power

8
spectrum data from the complete 5-year DESI observa-
tions, as well as the arrival of more precise observational
data from Euclid [189] and the Large Synoptic Survey
Telescope [190], we will be able to constrain P mν with
higher precision and further explore aP mν scenario that
can simultaneously reconcile high-precision cosmological
observations with particle physics experimental results.
