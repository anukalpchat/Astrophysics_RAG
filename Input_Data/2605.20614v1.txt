ABSTRACT
We present a detailed chemical-abundance and kinematic analysis of four extremely metal-poor
(EMP; [Fe/H] ≤ −3.0) stars identified from Gaia BP/RP data in our ongoing search for the most prim-
itive stars. This includes a primary target, Gaia DR3 2563539603865382656 (hereafter G256353), a
strongly r-process-enhanced star with [Eu/Fe] = +1.20 and [Ba/Eu] = −0.64. Our results are based on
high-resolution, high-signal-to-noise GHOST spectra from Gemini-South. For the full sample, we statis-
tically match the light-element abundances with those predicted from Population III supernova models.
The “best-fit” model suggests massive progenitors with stellar masses of M ⋆ ∼ 20-30 M⊙. In addition,
we determine orbital histories for all of the stars. We find that Gaia DR3 2887334237669844480 appears
to be kinematically associated with Atari, an accreted structure in the Galactic disk. This star has
low abundance ratios of strontium ([Sr/Fe] = −1.09) and barium ([Ba/Fe] = −0.37), which supports
an accretion origin. For G256353, we determine chemical abundances for 15 neutron-capture elements.
We compare the observed heavy-element pattern for G256353 with that of the Sun, HD 222925,
and two neutron star merger models. The r-process elements in G256353 align reasonably well with
HD 222925, the scaled-Solar pattern (except for the first peak), and a recent predicted pattern asso-
ciated with neutron star mergers. This consistency reinforces the universality of the main r-process
across diverse astrophysical environments.

1. INTRODUCTION

The rapid neutron-capture (hereafter r-) process
(Burbidge et al. 1957; Cameron 1957), is a critical nu-
cleosynthesis pathway that accounts for the production
of approximately half of the heavy elements (Z ≥ 30),
including the actinides, in the Sun (Beers & Christlieb
arXiv:2605.20614v1  [astro-ph.SR]  20 May 2026

2 Mardini et al.
2005; Frebel 2018). Identifying the astrophysical sites
where the r-process occurs has been a significant chal-
lenge in astrophysics (e.g., Qian 2003; Kasen et al. 2017;
Cowan et al. 2021; Arcones & Thielemann 2023; Bandy-
opadhyay & Beers 2025, and references therein). His-
torically, core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe) were con-
sidered the primary candidates due to their extreme en-
vironments and high neutron flux (e.g., Wanajo et al.
2002; Wanajo & Ishimaru 2005; Fr¨ ohlich et al. 2006;
Nomoto et al. 2006; Izutani et al. 2009; Farouqi et al.
2010). However, recent observations and simulations in-
dicate that supernovae may not consistently achieve the
neutron densities or eject sufficient neutron-rich mate-
rial for robust r-process element formation, highlighting
the need to consider alternative astrophysical sites (e.g.,
Nishimura et al. 2017; Fischer et al. 2020; Ghosh et al.
2022; Arcones & Thielemann 2023).
One of the most promising sites for r-process nucle-
osynthesis is neutron star (NS–NS) mergers (e.g., Lat-
timer & Schramm 1974; Eichler et al. 1989; Thiele-
mann et al. 2017), which has been confirmed through
gravitational wave observations, such as the remark-
able detection of the event GW170817 (Abbott et al.
2017a) and its subsequent kilonova AT2017gfo (Ab-
bott et al. 2017b; Drout et al. 2017; Shappee et al.
2017). These cataclysmic collisions between NS binaries
form environments with extremely high neutron den-
sities ( nn > 1020 cm−3), ideal for the r-process. The
ejected material from this merger has been observed
to produce heavy elements (Watson et al. 2019; Do-
moto et al. 2021). These observations support the idea
that NS–NS mergers play a significant role in cosmic
r-process production. However, the relatively low fre-
quency of NS–NS collisions, combined with the expected
long timescales required for NS binaries to merge (see
Sana et al. 2012, and references therein), raises ques-
tions about whether these events can fully account for
all of the r-process elements observed in the Universe
across different epochs (see Cˆ ot´ e et al. 2019, and refer-
ences therein).
In addition to NS–NS mergers and CCSNe, other po-
tential r-process sites include rare types of supernovae,
such as magneto-rotational supernovae (e.g., Symbalisty
et al. 1985; Reichert et al. 2021), where rapidly rotat-
ing, highly magnetized cores could provide the necessary
conditions for r-process nucleosynthesis. Another po-
tential environment for ther-process is in accretion disks
associated with NS–NS merger remnants or collapsars,
where neutron-rich outflows may provide suitable condi-
tions for heavy-element nucleosynthesis (see Siegel et al.
2019, 2022, and references therein). It has also been sug-
gested that, if the accretion disk around a neutron star
enters the core of a massive evolved star, in the frame-
work of a common-envelope jets supernova (CEJSN) r-
process scenario, it could penetrate the crust of the neu-
tron star, mix neutron-rich crust material into the disk,
and enrich the jets that the disk launches with neutron-
rich material (see Soker 2025, and references therein).
Despite these potential sites, each scenario faces limi-
tations, such as uncertainties in the rate of occurrence,
the amount of neutron-rich material ejected, and the
exact conditions required for the r-process (see Cowan
et al. 2021, and references therein). Thus, while signifi-
cant progress has been made, further observational and
theoretical work is necessary to fully understand the as-
trophysical origins of r-process elements.
In this context, the goal of the R-Process Alliance
(RPA) has been to increase the number of known r-
process-enhanced (RPE) stars in the Milky Way to bet-
ter understand r-process nucleosynthesis and the asso-
ciated astrophysical production site(s). Recent results
from the RPA include detailed chemical abundances of
both strongly enhanced r-II stars ([Eu/Fe] > +0.7) and
moderately enhanced r-I stars (+0.3 < [Eu/Fe] ≤ +0.7)
(Hansen et al. 2018; Sakari et al. 2018; Ezzeddine
et al. 2020; Holmbeck et al. 2020; Bandyopadhyay
et al. 2024). Building on these efforts, many individual
RPE stars have been studied in detail to constrain r-
process nucleosynthesis: J1521–3538, an r-III star, with
[Eu/Fe] > +2.0; Cain et al. 2020), RAVE J0949–1617
(an r + s star; Gull et al. 2018), RAVE J2038–0023 (U
detection; Placco et al. 2017), S-PLUS J1424–2542; an
(actinide-boost star; Placco et al. 2023), J0954+5246;
(U detection; Holmbeck et al. 2018), J2213–5137 (an r-
III star with a U detection; Roederer et al. 2024), and
a sample of stars that exhibit potential abundance sig-
natures of transuranic fission fragments (Roederer et al.
2023).
Here we present an additional effort to identify
metal-poor stars with r-process enhancements selected
from the Gaia BP/RP (also known as XP) survey
(Gaia Collaboration et al. 2022), and observed with
the GHOST spectrograph on the Gemini-South tele-
scope. As part of this first sample of high-resolution
spectra, we include observations of the bright EMP
r-II star Gaia DR3 2563539603865382656 (hereafter
G256353), originally identified by Li et al. (2022), with
Teff = 4784 K, [Fe/H] = −3.22 and [Eu/Fe] = +1.35. In
this paper, we determine abundances for 12 additional
elements, including eight neutron-capture elements, and
present the full chemo–dynamical analysis of this star,
as well as that of three newly discovered halo EMP stars
in our sample.

2. TARGET SELECTION AND OBSERVATIONS
The Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium
provided the GaiaXPy 1 package, which allows users to
estimate the expected flux of objects with BP/RP spec-
tra using pre-loaded transmission curves. Currently, the
package includes medium- and broad-band (e.g., u, i)
and narrow-band (e.g., SkyMapper Ca II K) photomet-
ric filters. In particular, the SkyMapper v filter, covering
the 3600–4100 ˚A range, and the Pristine filter, with an
even narrower 3900–4000 ˚A range, both encompass the
metallicity sensitive Ca II K line at ∼ 3933 ˚A, which
is a key feature for determining [Fe/H]. The combina-
tion of estimating fluxes from the broad- and narrow-
band filters for Gaia XP observations can then be effi-
ciently used to robustly identify metal-poor candidates
(see Martin et al. 2023; Mardini et al. 2024b; Chiti et al.
2024; Ou et al. 2025).
We used the filters mentioned above to derive photo-
metric [Fe/H] estimates for a sample of ∼ seven mil-
lion Gaia XP sources. These estimates were derived
by matching precomputed synthetic photometry grids
with the corresponding observed values, following the
methodology described in Chiti et al. (2021). This pro-
cedure was further refined to incorporate the Pristine
Ca II K filter and a machine-learning gradient-boosted
tree model. This combined approach yielded three in-
dependent [Fe/H] estimates for each candidate. In gen-
eral, our Gaia XP [Fe/H] estimates exhibit good agree-
ment, compared to high-resolution spectroscopic [Fe/H]
values, with differences within 0.30 dex, and extending
down to [Fe/H] ∼ − 4.0. This accuracy is comparable
to the typical uncertainties of medium–resolution spec-
troscopic results, which generally have uncertainties of
∼ 0.20–0.25 dex. A more detailed explanation of this
methodology, along with a catalog of metallicity esti-
mates, will be provided in a forthcoming paper (M. Mar-
dini et al. 2026, in preparation).
We selected four candidates with [Fe/H]SM SS ≤ −3.0,
[Fe/H]P ris ≤ −3.0, and [Fe/H]M L ≤ −3.0 from our cat-
alog as part of our effort to identify the most metal-poor

We then obtained high–resolution spectroscopic data
for these candidates. The spectra were obtained with
the newly commissioned Gemini High-resolution Opti-
cal SpecTrograph (GHOST; Ireland et al. 2014; Mc-
Connachie et al. 2022; Hayes et al. 2023; Kalari et al.
2024) at the Gemini-South telescope. The observa-
tions were conducted as part of the 2023B semester
GHOST shared-risk call for proposals (Program ID: GS-
2023B-FT-3012). The chosen instrument setup was the
standard resolution of R ∼ 47, 000 and target mode
IFU1:Target|IFU2:Sky, which provides improved sky
subtraction. All spectra were taken with a 2 × 4 binning
(spectral × spatial). The wavelength coverage is 3600
to 5400 ˚A for the blue camera and 5250 to 10600 ˚A for
the red camera.
The reduced spectra were provided by the US National
Gemini Office (US NGO; Placco et al. 2024). Data re-
duction was performed using v3.1 of the DRAGONS3 soft-
ware package (Labrie et al. 2023). The reduction steps
included bias and flat-field corrections, wavelength cali-
bration, barycentric correction, sky subtraction, extrac-
tion of individual orders, and variance-weighted stitch-
ing of the spectral orders. The final 1D spectra created
by DRAGONS were written in text format using standard
NOIRLab IRAF 4 routines. Finally, we combined the two
back-to-back individual exposures for each program star
using a simple average without applying any rejection
criteria.
We calculated the heliocentric radial velocity (RV)
by cross-correlating our GHOST final combined spec-
tra with a rest-frame Magellan/MIKE spectrum of
HD 122563, a red giant star with [Fe/H] ∼ −2.8 (Aoki
et al. 2025) as no RV standard star was observed as
part of our program. The cross-correlation was per-
formed around the Ca triplet region, which is a strong
feature commonly used in radial velocity measurements

3.1. Equivalent Widths
We used the linelist from Roederer et al. (2018) along
with atomic, hyperfine-structure, isotopic splitting (see

isolated lines, free from blends or other contamination,

For the equivalent width (EW) analysis, we employed
the Spectroscopy Made Hard ( SMHr) software6 (Casey
2014), which is specifically designed to facilitate the
measurement of absorption lines in stellar spectra. We
fitted the absorption features in our high-resolution
spectrum with Gaussian profiles to obtain the EW mea-
surements of the detectable lines listed in Table 2. Fi-
nally, each line was visually examined to ensure good
continuum placement and fitting of the line.
3.2. Atmospheric Parameters
To determine the atmospheric stellar parameters for
our target stars, we utilized the RPA methodology,
which combines multiple observational sources, includ-
ing magnitudes, distance, and iron-line abundances.

based on the precise G, BP , and RP magnitudes re-
ported in the Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3) catalog (Gaia
Collaboration et al. 2022). We applied the bolometric
correction methods described by Casagrande & Vanden-
Berg (2018a) and Casagrande & VandenBerg (2018b) to
obtain accurate intrinsic magnitudes. This step is es-
sential for ensuring that our temperature estimates re-
flect the true stellar luminosity. We then used the best
polynomial fit of the color–Teff relation from Mucciarelli

3.3.1. Light Elements
To determine accurate carbon abundances, we utilized
the molecular CH G–band head located at 4313 ˚A, as il-
lustrated in the upper panel of Figure 1. We also used
the CH features around 4217 ˚A to estimate the 12C/13C
ratios of the target stars, as described in Mardini et al.
(2019a). We then corrected the derived carbon abun-
dances based on the procedures described in Placco et al.
(2014b) to account for the evolutionary status of each
star. We find very low [C/Fe] ratios for three of our
stars, except for G297027, which exhibits a moderate
carbon enhancement of [C/Fe] = +0.45.
In addition to carbon, we derived the chemical abun-
dances of sodium, magnesium, aluminum, silicon, cal-
cium, scandium, titanium, vanadium, chromium, man-
ganese, cobalt, nickel, and zinc. These elements provide
insights into the nucleosynthetic processes that occurred
prior to the formation of our target stars. See Arcones
& Thielemann (2023) for more context on the nucle-
osynthesis processes in supernova explosions that are
responsible for the production of these light elements
in the early universe.

3.3.2. Neutron-capture Elements
Very few neutron-capture elements are detected in the
spectra for three of our target stars. This is primarily
because the spectral lines of these elements are gener-
ally very weak, making them challenging to detect and
analyze. We consistently detected two Sr II lines at

3.3.3. Associated Uncertainties
To ensure the precision and reliability of our results,
we undertook a detailed quantification of the system-
atic uncertainties that arise from variations in the atmo-
spheric parameters, particularly for the light elements.
We systematically changed each atmospheric parameter
with its adopted uncertainty (see Section 3.2), and recal-
culated the abundances for light elements. This allowed
us to identify how sensitive our derived abundances are
to variations in each parameter. Table 4 lists the abun-
dance variations when changing the atmospheric param-
eters. The σ values represent the standard error of the
mean value. The σtot values represent the quadratic sum
of the individual error estimates.
The data presented in Table 4 strongly support the
precision of our abundance measurements. The error
estimates for our abundance values are relatively small,
indicating that these uncertainties do not significantly
affect the overall results. The minimal variation in the
error estimates further reinforces the precision of our
analysis and the quality of the collected spectra.
We also note that, given the metal-poor nature of our
targets ([Fe/H] ≤ −3), departures from local thermody-
namic equilibrium (LTE) are expected to affect the for-
mation of several spectral lines, including those of iron.
In particular, Fe I lines are known to be susceptible to
NLTE over-ionization in metal-poor atmospheres. For
stars with [Fe/H] ∼ − 3, Ezzeddine et al. (2017) calcu-
lated < 0.25 dex corrections. Similar NLTE effects have
been found by Mashonkina et al. (2011), Bergemann
et al. (2012), and Lind et al. (2012). While our analysis
is performed under the LTE assumption, these effects
may introduce non-negligible uncertainties. A detailed
NLTE treatment is beyond the scope of this work and
might be explored in future studies.
4. ORBITAL HISTORIES OF TARGET STARS
Combining orbital information (e.g., velocity, ec-
centricity, and inclination) with the results from our
chemical-abundance analysis helps to discern the cosmic
origins of our stars. A full kinematic analysis thus allows
associating a given star with a distinct stellar population
(e.g., Helmi et al. 2018; Belokurov et al. 2018; Zepeda
et al. 2023; Mardini et al. 2022a; Andales et al. 2024;
Hong et al. 2024; Berczik et al. 2024) to learn if it was
formed in-situ within the Milky Way or it was accreted
from a satellite galaxy (see Helmi 2020, and references
therein).
We collected astrometric data for our target stars from
Gaia DR3 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2022), which in-
cludes proper motions in both directions ( µα cos δ, µδ)
and parallaxes ( ϖ). We adopted our own line-of-sight
velocities (see Table 1). We corrected ϖ as suggested by
Lindegren et al. (2021), ensuring the accuracy of our dis-
tance measurements (see Table 1). Subsequently, we de-
rived the heliocentric distances of our target stars by ap-
plying an exponentially decreasing space density prior,
as outlined in Mardini et al. (2022a). Combining these
parameters with positions (RA and Dec) allows us to

5.1. Origin of the Light Elements
We attempted to determine the primary characteris-
tics of the progenitor population responsible for enrich-
ing the gas clouds that formed our target stars with light
elements. To do this, we compare the observed abun-
dances with theoretical nucleosynthesis yields from Pop-
ulation III supernova models (Heger & Woosley 2010).
We employed fallback supernova models with stellar
masses ranging from 10 to 100 M ⊙ and explosion en-
ergies ranging from 0.3 × 1051 to 10.0 × 1051 erg. To
find the best-fit model that minimizes the differences
between our observed abundances and those of the the-
oretical yields, we utilized the χ2 matching algorithm
known as StarFit10.
We statistically compared each theoretical model with
10,000 realizations of the observed abundance pattern
for each star in our sample. These realizations were
generated using Gaussian distributions based on the ob-
served abundances and their corresponding uncertain-
ties. This approach enabled us to identify the progeni-
tor model that best reproduces the light-element abun-
dances observed in our target stars. The left panels of
Figure 4 show the predicted yields of the “best-fit” mod-
els compared to the observed abundances. The legends
show the key parameters of each “best-fit” model.

5.2. Origin of the Accreted Atari Disk Star G288733
Only G288733 exhibits disk-like kinematics, with a
circular orbit (e ∼ 0.2) that remains close to the Galac-
tic plane (within Z max ≈ 2 kpc). These parameters
clearly associate G288733 with the Galactic disk sys-
tem. Overall, the L z-E location of this star aligns with
the characteristics of the so-called Atari disk (Mardini
et al. 2022a), largely overlapping with the metal-weak
thick disk. This disk component is distinguished by its
low-metallicity stellar content and a notable velocity lag
when compared to the thick disk (and also the thin disk).
The Atari disk is believed to have formed through the ac-
cretion of a massive dwarf galaxy into the early Galactic
disk to enrich it with low-metallicity stars. The associa-
tion of G288733 with the Atari disk thus indicates that
it may be a relic of this ancient accretion event.
An accretion origin of this star is further supported by
its observed low strontium ([Sr/H] =−4.08) and barium
([Ba/H] = −3.36) abundances (see Andales et al. 2024
for more details). As shown in the “UFD-halo sepa-
ration” plot (see the last panel of Figure 2), G288733
occupies the same region as the ultra-faint dwarf (UFD)
galaxy stars in the [Sr/Ba] versus [Ba/Fe] plane (see also
panel C of Figure 6 of Mardini et al. 2024a). This dis-
tinctive signature furthermore supports the notion that
G288733 likely formed in an early dwarf galaxy that was
long ago accreted by the Milky Way, such as the Atari
progenitor.
5.3. Origin of the R-II Star G256353
The other astrophysically important star in our sam-
ple is G256353. It shows a significant enhancement in
neutron-capture elements, with [Eu/Fe] = +1.2, and ex-

Frebel 2018). G256353 was first identified as an RPE
star by Li et al. (2022) based on stellar parameters of
Teff = 4784 K, [Fe/H] = −3.22, and log g = 1.58. These
values are consistent with our derived parameters (see
Section 3.2). To demonstrate the reliability of our abun-
dance measurements, we compare our results with those
of Li et al. (2022) to quantify any systematic uncertain-
ties.

Beyond the abundance measurements, and based
on our kinematic analysis and stellar action approach
(see Mardini et al. 2022a for more details), we assign
G256353 to the Galactic halo population. The star
reaches a maximum vertical distance of Z max ∼ 6 kpc,
placing it within the inner halo, which is generally con-
sidered to extend up to Z max < 15 kpc (see Carollo
et al. 2012, and references therein). The inner halo is
known to host a considerable fraction of in-situ stars
(e.g., Bonaca et al. 2017), while the outer halo primar-
ily contains stars with accretion histories. Based solely
on the value of Z max and a prograde tangential velocity
of V ϕ = 60.5 km s −1, an accretion origin for G256353
cannot be determined at this time, but it cannot be ex-
cluded. Further comprehensive analysis, such as identi-
fying a potential dynamical connection between this star
and any satellite r-process galaxies, if possible, might
help to further clarify its origin.
Additional insight into the origins of G256353 in the
early universe and the nucleosynthesis processes operat-
ing in its progenitor(s) can be drawn from the observed
abundance pattern. Detailed simulations indicate that
canonical core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe) are gen-
erally inefficient at producing third-peak r-process el-
ements (e.g., Qian & Woosley 1996; Thompson et al.
2001; H¨ udepohl et al. 2010; van de Voort et al. 2015).
Hence, when considering an r-process-enhanced star, we
can assume that the astrophysical sites responsible for
the lighter elements (Z < 30) are likely decoupled from
those that generate the heavier neutron-capture species.
Accordingly, in the following discussion we consider the
possibility that the observed enhancement of neutron-
capture elements in G256353 originated not from a CC-
SNe, but from a neutron star merger.

abundances except for Sr, which is ∼ 0.2 dex higher in
G256353. The observed abundances of the second and
third r-process peak elements align closely with both the
HD 222925 and the scaled Solarr-process patterns. This
close agreement supports the known universality of the
main r-process, as demonstrated across numerous RPE
metal-poor stars that span a wide metallicity range (e.g.,
Hill et al. 2002; Sneden et al. 2003; Frebel et al. 2007;
Placco et al. 2020; Mardini et al. 2020; Hansen et al.
2025a). Such consistency implies that a robust nucle-
osynthetic mechanism is responsible for producing these
heavy elements that appears to be operating across di-
verse astrophysical environments.
Comparing the two different theoretical neutron star
merger patterns from Holmbeck & Andrews (2024) and
Fujibayashi et al. (2023) against our observed neutron-
capture element abundances reveals noticeable differ-
ences. The predicted pattern from the Holmbeck &
Andrews (2024) model, which incorporates both disk
winds and dynamical ejecta components, shows reason-
able agreement with our measured abundances across
both the second and third r-process peaks except for La,
which is under-predicted by ∼0.4 dex. Y and Zr, on the
other hand, are significantly over-predicted. The other
neutron star merger model reported by Fujibayashi et al.
(2023) exhibits a similar behavior. La, Ce, and Pr, as
well as Sr, are all under-produced (at a ∼0.3 dex level).
Given that the Holmbeck & Andrews (2024) model
matches the abundance pattern of G256353 reasonably
well, it suggests that it reasonably captures the nu-
cleosynthetic conditions of heavy-element production
within a neutron star merger that enriched the natal
gas cloud. The mismatch between the two key elements,
Sr and La, may result from uncertainties in the nuclear
physics inputs (e.g., nuclear reaction rates, fission yields,
entropy, gas rate of expansion, initial neutron richness
of the ejecta, etc.) used in the simulations (e.g., Zhu
et al. 2021; Brethauer et al. 2024; Mumpower et al.
2024). Therefore, measuring additional heavy-element
abundances in this star would be helpful in further dis-
cerning the origin(s) of these elements.

6. SUMMARY
This study presents a detailed chemodynamical anal-
ysis of an r-II metal-poor star G256353, characterized
by [Eu/Fe] = +1.20 and [Ba/Eu] = −0.64. We selected
G256353 from the Gaia BP/RP dataset (M. Mardini et
al. 2026, in preparation) alongside three newly discov-
ered EMP halo stars. High-resolution spectroscopy was
obtained for all targets using the GHOST spectrograph
on the Gemini-South telescope. The analysis of G256353
contributes to the ongoing efforts of the RPA to identify
and characterize RPE metal-poor stars.
The chemical abundances of 30 elements, including
15 heavy elements, were derived for G256353. We com-
pared the abundances of the light elements (Z ≤ 30)
with those predicted by Population III supernova mod-
els from Heger & Woosley (2010) to gain insight into
the origin of these elements. This comparison suggests
that a massive progenitor star (M ⋆ ∼30 M⊙) underwent
an energetic explosion before enriching the birth cloud
from which G256353 formed.
The abundance analysis of G256353 provides valuable
constraints on r-process nucleosynthesis in the early uni-
verse. Its heavy-element pattern shows strong agree-
ment with that of the benchmark star HD 222925. This
consistency also appears when compared with the Solar
r-process pattern for elements beyond the first peak, fur-
ther supporting the universality of the main r-process.
Comparisons with neutron star merger models re-
veal notable discrepancies across the different r-process
peaks. Although light neutron-capture elements (Sr,
Y, Zr) show varying levels of agreement between dif-

16 Mardini et al.
ferent models, elements from Ba to Pr exhibit substan-
tial over-abundances relative to the predictions from
Fujibayashi et al. (2023). These elements exhibit sig-
nificantly better agreement with the multi-component
model of Holmbeck & Andrews (2024). In particular,
the magnitude of these discrepancies is greatly reduced
for elements located beyond the second r-process peak.
These discrepancies likely originate from uncertainties
in nuclear physics inputs, underscoring the need for ad-
ditional abundance measurements. In particular, key
elements such as Nb, Pd, and Ag would provide critical
constraints on these neutron star merger models, ad-
vancing our understanding of heavy-element production
and Galactic chemical evolution.
In addition to G256353, we identified and analyzed
three other EMP stars from our observed sample.
G297027 and G470812 appear to be typical halo EMP
stars. The third star, G288733, shows a kinematic as-
sociation with Atari, a metal-poor accreted structure
in the Galactic disk system. The star exhibits a low
abundance of strontium ([Sr/Fe] = −1.09) and moder-
ate abundance of barium ([Ba/Fe] = −0.37), which is
consistent with those reported for UFD stars. This dis-
tinctive abundance pattern of neutron-capture elements
suggests an ex-situ origin for G288733, indicating that
the star was most likely accreted from a smaller external
system rather than forming within the Milky Way.
The data and methods applied here serve as a seed
work that will set the stage for a much larger sample of
∼ 200 EMP stars observed with the MIKE spectrograph
on the Magellan Telescope. The future analysis will pro-
vide homogeneous results, allowing us to delve deeper
into understanding the chemical trends across different
stellar populations and place crucial constraints on the
formation and evolution of the Milky Way.
