ABSTRACT
Balmer absorption lines are increasingly observed in the little red dots (LRDs) discovered by the
James Webb Space Telescope, potentially tracing dense circumnuclear gas around rapidly accreting
black holes. Motivated by this connection, we search for Balmer absorption using homogeneously
analyzed spectra of a representative parent sample of 14,584 low-redshift (z <0.35) type 1 active
galactic nuclei selected from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey. We identify seven sources with robust
Balmer absorption (occurrence∼0.05%) and model them with a partially covering absorber model,
accounting for the spectral resolution. By fitting Hα, Hβ, and Hγsimultaneously and tying their
optical-depth ratios to theoretical values, we constrain optical depth at the line center (τ 0) and the
covering factor (C f). All sources with robust modeling require optically thick Hαabsorption and
typically moderate covering factors (C f ≈0.2−0.6), while the LRD analog J1025 showsC f ≳0.8
consistent with recent measurements of high-redshift LRDs. The absorbers have modest velocity offsets
(∼150−850 km s −1) and narrow intrinsic widths (∼20−200 km s −1). Multi-epoch spectroscopy
of three sources reveals Balmer-absorption variability on both year and month timescales. Three
objects exhibit exceptionally weak FeIIemission, high Eddington ratio, and low gas-phase metallicity,
an atypically rare combination of properties that might elevate the incidence of Balmer-absorption
to∼10%. We argue that low-metallicity conditions may suppress disk winds and help retain dense
neutral gas along the line-of-sight in systems of high accretion rate.

1.INTRODUCTION
Deep James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) spec-
troscopy has revealed a rapidly growing population of
compact, optically red sources atz≳4, often referred
to as “little red dots” (LRDs). These objects show un-
usually red rest-frame UV/optical continua, frequently
accompanied by a pronounced turnover near the Balmer
limit in at least a subset of the population, as well as
broad emission lines with full width at half maximum
(FWHM)≳1000 km s −1 that are often treated as signa-
tures of accretion-powered activity (e.g., Kocevski et al.
2023; Labb´ e et al. 2023; Greene et al. 2024; Kokorev
et al. 2024; Matthee et al. 2024). Intriguingly, Balmer
absorption troughs that appear on top of the broad emis-
sion lines have now been reported in 10%–20% (Matthee

et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2024; Kocevski et al. 2025) or more
(Zhuang et al. 2026; Chen et al. 2026) of the JWST-
selected broad-line LRD/AGN samples. The superpo-
sition of absorption troughs over emission line profiles
differs significantly from the stellar absorption features
observed in evolved stellar populations, providing direct
evidence for dense, predominantly neutral gas along the
line-of-sight to the continuum and broad-line emission
regions. A key theoretical development motivated by
these discoveries is the idea that LRDs may host rapidly
accreting black holes (BHs) embedded in a dense, opti-
cally thick gas envelope (Inayoshi & Maiolino 2025; Kido
et al. 2025; Rusakov et al. 2026; Inayoshi & Ho 2025).
In such scenarios, the envelope can simultaneously red-
den the emergent continuum, imprint Balmer break-like
features and Balmer absorption, and—if coupled to pow-
erful outflows—help explain the weak variability and X-

Nonstellar Balmer absorption is exceptionally rare in
broad-line AGNs and has largely been identified through
serendipitous discoveries instead of systematic searches
(Hutchings et al. 2002; Aoki et al. 2006; Aoki 2010;
Hall 2007; Wang et al. 2008; Ji et al. 2012, 2013;
Wang & Xu 2015; Zhang et al. 2015, 2018; Schulze
et al. 2018; Burke et al. 2021). Physically, produc-
ing Balmer absorption requires a substantial population
of neutral hydrogen in then= 2 level. Two mecha-
nisms are commonly invoked. Pure collisional excita-
tion demands extremely high densities, approaching a
critical density ofn crit ≈10 11 cm−3 (e.g., Hall 2007),
which would imply very short recombination times (e.g.,
∼40 s forT= 10 4 K) and would seem to make long-
lived, readily detectable absorption unlikely. Alterna-
tively, Lyαtrapping can maintain an enhancedn= 2
population at more moderate densities, provided that
the column density is high; typical estimates require
NH(n= 2)≳10 17 cm−2 (e.g., Hall 2007; Wang & Xu
2015; Burke et al. 2021). Consistent with the need
for large column density, Balmer absorption is reported
more frequently in iron low-ionization broad absorption-
line quasars (FeLoBALQs; e.g., Aoki et al. 2006; Hall
2007; Aoki 2010; Ji et al. 2012; Zhang et al. 2015). Build-
ing on this connection, Leighly et al. (2025) compiled a
sample of 14 FeLoBALQs with Balmer absorption and
highlighted their potential similarity to LRDs, including
a sub-population with weak FeIIthat more closely re-
sembles the LRD phenomenology (Trefoloni et al. 2025).
At the same time, the emerging LRD context sug-
gests that Balmer absorption may not be confined to
classical FeLoBALQs and may instead trace a broader
set of physical conditions linked to rapid accretion and
circumnuclear gas structure. A particularly compelling
example is J1025+1402: originally reported as a low-z
Balmer-absorption AGN (Burke et al. 2021), it has since
been recognized as a local analog of LRDs (Lin et al.
2026a; Ji et al. 2025a). While its optical spectrum shows
little or no detectable FeIIpseudo-continuum, higher-
quality LBT/MODS and Magellan/FIRE spectroscopy
reveals abundant narrow FeIIemission and absorption
features (Lin et al. 2026a). Moreover, several Balmer-
absorption AGNs are not classified as FeLoBALQs (e.g.,
Hutchings et al. 2002; Wang & Xu 2015; Schulze et al.
2018), motivating a more unified view of the phe-
nomenon and its diversity. This raises three closely re-
lated questions. How rare are Balmer-absorption AGNs
within the overall low-ztype 1 population? How do
their absorber properties relate to global AGN prop-
erties, particularly accretion rate, as suggested by re-
cent LRD models (Liu et al. 2025; Rusakov et al. 2026)?
And what parameter space do they preferentially oc-
cupy? We address these questions by identifying a small
sample of low-ztype 1 AGNs with robust Balmer ab-
sorption and performing uniform spectral decomposition
and absorption-line modeling that enforces the atomic-
physics constraints among Hα, Hβ, and Hγ, then placing
the resulting measurements in the context of the parent
AGN population to quantify both the incidence and the
parameter-space preference of Balmer absorption.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 de-
scribes the parent sample, target selection, and spec-
troscopic and photometric data. Section 3 presents our
continuum and emission-line decomposition and the ab-
sorber model, including the treatment of instrumental
resolution. Section 4 summarizes the main measure-
ments, including absorber optical depths, covering fac-
tors, kinematics, and broad-band spectral energy dis-
tribution (SED) properties. In Section 5, we discuss
the number statistics and selection effects, the Balmer
decrements of the broad and narrow components, the
observed multi-epoch variability, and the physical impli-
cations for circumnuclear gas in high-accretion, poten-
tially low-metallicity systems. We summarize our main
conclusions in Section 6. This work adopts the follow-
ing parameters for a ΛCDM cosmology: Ω m = 0.308,
ΩΛ = 0.692, andH 0 = 67.8 km s −1 Mpc−1 (Planck
Collaboration et al. 2016).
2.SAMPLE AND DATA
The primary motivation of this work is to identify
low-redshift, type 1 AGNs exhibiting Balmer absorption
lines and to characterize their properties with respect to
the general AGN population. We therefore begin with
a representative low-zbroad-line AGN sample from Liu
et al. (2019), who provide a homogeneous parent catalog
for subsequent inspection and comparisons.
The Liu et al. (2019) sample is particularly well suited
for this purpose because it is based on careful spec-
tral decomposition in the Hαand Hβregions, which
are critical for measuring broad-line properties and for
inferring AGN physical parameters. Unlike other Sloan
Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) quasar catalogs (e.g., Wu
& Shen 2022), which are largely dominated by lumi-
nous, quasar-targeted objects, Liu et al. (2019) mined
the full SDSS spectroscopic database of both galaxies
and quasars atz <0.35 and selected AGNs by the pres-
ence of broad Balmer emission (especially Hα). This
strategy recovered a substantial population of lower lu-
minosity type 1 AGNs that can be missed or diluted in
quasar-style selections. As a result, the catalog spans
parameter ranges down to very low broad-line luminosi-

3.SPECTRAL DECOMPOSITION
We first summarize our spectral-fitting procedure be-
fore discussing individual sources. After correcting for
Galactic foreground extinction, we shift each spectrum
to the rest frame. We adopt the SDSS pipeline redshift
(Ahumada et al. 2020) as an initial guess and then refine
it using our spectral fits. All modeling is performed with
our custom packageGalSpec, 4 which provides multi-
Gaussian emission-line models and continuum compo-
nents compatible with theAstropymodeling framework
(Astropy Collaboration et al. 2022; Kuhn et al. 2024;
Santos et al. 2025). Our line models are parameterized
in velocity space (line width and centroid shift), which
facilitates tying kinematic parameters among different
transitions. A description of the adopted components
is provided in Appendix A. TheGalSpeccode also in-
cludes an MCMC fitting interface (see also Li, et al.
2026) based onemcee(Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013). In
practice, we first obtain a physically plausible solution
using a Levenberg–Marquardt least-squares fit and then
initialize the MCMC chains around the best-fit param-
eters to estimate uncertainties. We use uniform weights
for the least-squares stage, as the noise is approximately
constant across the fitted wavelength windows; for the
MCMC stage we use the SDSS/DESI inverse variance
spectra. While the best-fit emission-line parameters
from the two methods agree within the quoted uncer-
tainties, MCMC sampling provides a more robust way
to explore the weak emission and absorption line param-
eters and to quantify the associated model uncertainties.
Figure 2 shows an example fit to J0925; analogous fits
for the remaining sources are provided in the online fig-
ure set.

3.2.Emission-line Fitting
We focus on the wavelength range 4200–7000˚A for the
emission-line fitting (Figure 2a). It is necessary to build
a narrow-line model using isolated forbidden lines (e.g.,
the [OIII]λλ4959, 5007 or [SII]λλ6716, 6731 doublets) in
order to decompose the often severely blended narrow
and broad components of the Balmer lines (Ho et al.
1997). The [SII] doublet is usually preferred because
[OIII] often shows a stronger asymmetric, blueshifted
profile, likely associated with radial motions in the
narrow-line region. In practice, however, the weakness
of [SII] leaves little alternative but [OIII] as the only
viable empirical narrow-line template.
Both J0925 and J1126 show prominent [SII] emis-
sion. We model the [SII] doublet by fitting each line
with a double-Gaussian profile, allowing the relative am-
plitudes of the two Gaussian components to vary and
simultaneously fitting the local continuum over 6680–
6780 ˚A. In contrast, the [SII] lines are only marginally
detected in J1025, J1039, J1545, and J2220, and in
J1535 they are strongly blended with its exception-
ally broad Hαline. Therefore, for these five objects,
we use the [OIII] doublet, fit over 4900–5050 ˚A, to
construct the narrow-line template. Each [OIII] line
is modeled with either a multi-Gaussian profile (2–3
components) or a Gauss–Hermite function, with con-
stant relative amplitude of 2.98 (Osterbrock 1989).
We adopt either the full [OIII] profile (J1025, J1039,
and J2220) or only the core component centered at
0 km s −1 (J1535 and J1545) as the narrow-line tem-
plate. When modeling the (forbidden and permit-
ted) narrow emission lines, we tie the centroid velocity
shift across all narrow lines and allow only their am-
plitudes to vary. In addition to the [OIII] constraint,
we fix the peak-flux ratios of [NII]λ6584/[NII]λ6548
to 2.96 and [OI]λ6364/[OI]λ6300 to 3 (Osterbrock
1989). For J0925, J1126, J1535, and J1545, we in-
clude an additional blueshifted “wind” component for
both [OIII]λλ4959, 5007 and [OIII]λ4363, and we tie the
profile of the [OIII]λ4363 wind component to that of
[OIII]λλ4959, 5007.
Strictly speaking, narrow-line profiles depend on the
local physical conditions and can vary in the presence
of density stratification, since the gas density is often
centrally concentrated (Ho et al. 1996). For exam-
ple, the [SII]λλ6716, 6731 doublet may show different
profiles because the two lines arise from different up-
per levels. Likewise, [OIII]λ4363 can differ from the
[OIII]λλ4959, 5007 doublet, whereas the two [OIII] com-
ponents originate from the same upper level and there-
fore should share the same intrinsic profile. In prac-
tice, however, such differences are typically detectable
only in spectra with exceptionally high signal-to-noise
ratios (Filippenko & Halpern 1984; Filippenko 1985).
We therefore adopt the common and practical approx-
imation that all narrow forbidden lines share the same
profile and, when needed, all the wind components share
the same profile.
To decompose the spectra, we divide the full wave-
length coverage into three segments: 4200–5400 ˚A,
5400–6100 ˚A, and 6100–7000 ˚A. This piecewise approach
is necessary because the wavelength range is broad and
the continuum generally cannot be represented by a sin-
gle power law. We begin with the Hαregion (6100–
7000 ˚A), since Hαis typically the strongest line and
exhibits the most complex broad-line profile. We fit
the broad Hαcomponent with a sum of Gaussian mod-
els, starting with a single component and adding addi-
tional components until the fit no longer improves, as
judged by the residuals becoming flat. In most cases,
2–3 Gaussians suffice, whereas up to five components
are required to reproduce the exceptionally broad Hα
profile of J1535. After obtaining an acceptable fit in the
Hαregion, we extend the fit to include the Hβand Hγ
regions (4200–5400 ˚A). Owing to their lower signal-to-
noise ratios, the broad Hβand Hγlines generally re-
quire fewer Gaussian components than Hα. Whenever
possible, we tie the velocity shifts of the Hβand Hγ
components to those of the 2–3 highest-peak Hαcom-
ponents, while allowing their widths and amplitudes to
vary freely. Finally, we include the 5400–6100 ˚A seg-
ment for completeness. In addition to the broad Balmer
lines, we also model broad HeIλ5876 and HeIIλ4686
emission. We fit the continuum in each segment with a
power-law model truncated at the wavelength limits. As
discussed in Section 3.1, we add a blackbody component
to model the 4200–5400 ˚A continuum of J1025. Given
the limited wavelength range, we fix the temperature at
3800 K to mitigate degeneracy between the power-law
and blackbody components.

Because the Balmer absorption lines share the same
lower energy level (n= 2), their line-center optical
depths are fixed by atomic physics (Draine 2011), pro-
vided that they arise from a common line-of-sight ab-
sorber that screens the same background (continuum
plus broad-line) emission. For a given column density

3.4.MCMC Refinement and Uncertainty Estimation
We use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling
to refine the best-fit model and to estimate uncertain-
ties for the continuum, emission-line, and absorption-
line parameters. We initialize each MCMC run with the
best-fit parameters from the least-squares optimization.
Even when the least-squares fit does not require an FeII
component or some narrow-line components, we include
them in the MCMC model to obtain a consistent pos-
terior constraint. We restrict the FeIIvelocity shift to
±1500 km s−1 and its line width to within 50% of the Hα
FWHM. For the Balmer absorption, we sample the line-
center optical depth in logarithmic space with a uniform
prior over 0.01–100. Our mock tests (Section 3.5) show
that the fits become unreliable forτ 0 ≳100, motivat-
ing this upper bound. Because the absorption features
are intrinsically narrow, we adopt a uniform prior of
5–300 km s−1 for the velocity dispersion, and a uniform
prior of 0–1 for the covering factor. For all other param-
eters, we adopt sufficiently broad uniform priors so that
the posterior constraints are driven by the data instead
of by the prior bounds.
We employ the affine-invariant ensemble sampler im-
plemented inemcee(Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013) with

8
200 walkers, which exceeds 3 times the number of free
parameters in our most complex model (J1535) and pro-
vides adequate exploration of parameter space. We run
each chain for 100,000 steps and discard the first 50,000
steps as burn-in. We verify that the inferred parameters
are stable when the total chain length is increased by a
factor of 2.
For each derived quantity, we report the posterior me-
dian and standard deviation. For some parameters (e.g.,
the narrow Balmer line fluxes in J1545-0 andR Fe II in
J1025), the posterior is consistent with a non-detection
and should be interpreted as an upper limit. Conversely,
for the absorption-line parameters,τ 0,Hα orC f can be
consistent with a lower limit. We classify a measurement
as an upper (lower) limit when the posterior median lies
within 3σof the lower (upper) boundary of its prior
range. In such cases, we quote the 0.3% and 99.7% per-
centiles of the posterior as the corresponding 3σlower
and upper limits.
3.5.Robustness of the Absorption-line Fitting
We use Figure 3 to illustrate how the absorption-line
optical depth, velocity dispersion, and covering factor
shape the observed Hαprofiles. For each source, we
zoom in on the Hαregion and generate mock spectra
from the best-fit model described in Section 4.1. Before
fitting the mocks and assessing how well the input pa-
rameters are recovered, we first vary one parameter at
a time and display the resulting profiles as the colored
curves. To construct each mock spectrum, we (1) com-
bine the broad Balmer emission model with the contin-
uum, (2) convolve the intrinsic spectrum with a Gaus-
sian LSF appropriate for the instrumental resolution,
and (3) add the narrow-line model to form the full Hα
profile. The three SDSS epochs of J1535 have compara-
ble data quality and show no significant variability, and
we use only the first-epoch spectrum. Both epochs of
J1545 are presented because the SDSS and DESI spec-
tra have different resolutions and the absorption varies
significantly. We only include the first epoch of J2220 for
completeness while caution that its spectral decomposi-
tion may not be reliable, as discussed in Section 4.1.7.
As discussed above,τ 0 andC f are highly degenerate
when modeling a single absorption profile. We miti-
gate this degeneracy by fitting Hα, Hβ, and Hγsimul-
taneously and fixing their optical-depth ratios to the
theoretical values. With this in mind, Figure 3 is in-
tended as an intuitive guide to the sensitivity of the
data to each parameter. We find that the absorption in
J0925, J1039, and J1545-1 is consistent with the highest
optical-depth mocks (logτ 0 ≳2), which are difficult to
discriminate from one another. Varying the line width
further shows that the absorption troughs for the SDSS
spectra are largely washed out whenσ≲10 km s −1,
whereas the DESI spectrum retains a detectable signal
even forσ= 5 km s −1. Finally, the best-fit profiles gen-
erally favor moderate covering factors, except for J1025,
whose absorption is most consistent with theC f = 1
mock model.
To assess whether the absorption-line parameters can
be recovered robustly, we performed a suite of mock tests
based on the best-fit models of our targets. The results
for J0925 are shown in Figure 4, while those for the other
sources are provided in the online figure set. In brief,
we generate mock spectra using the best-fit Hα, Hβ, and
Hγmodels, including only the Balmer broad and narrow
emission components and the Balmer absorption com-
ponents. We then vary the input absorption parameters
on a grid:τ 0,Hα (10 values uniformly logarithmically
spaced from 1 to 10 5),σ(5, 10, 20, 40, and 80 km s −1),
andC f (10 values uniformly spaced from 0.1 to 1.0),
yielding 500 mock spectra in total. For each realization,
we initialize the MCMC walkers by perturbing the true
input parameters by 30%. We run 100,000 steps with
an ensemble size equal to 3 times of the number of free
parameters.
The mock tests lead to three main conclusions. First,
τ0,Hα is recovered reliably whenτ 0,Hα ≲100 but be-
comes poorly constrained at larger values because the
absorption-trough depth is then primarily controlled by
Cf instead of by further increases in optical depth. We
therefore adopt 100 as the upper bound of theτ 0,Hα
prior. Second, the absorption-line width is recovered
robustly only whenσ≳10 km s −1, as intrinsically nar-
rower troughs are strongly smoothed by instrumental
broadening; accordingly, when summarizing the mock
results we focus on realizations withσ >10 km s −1.
Third,C f is generally well constrained, although the
uncertainties increase asC f approaches zero, when the
absorption signal becomes weak. Overall, these tests
support the robustness of our absorption-line measure-
ments over the parameter ranges relevant to our data.
The ability to constrainτ 0,Hα up to∼100 is expected
because we fit three Balmer lines simultaneously: even
whenτ 0,Hα = 100, the Hγoptical depth remains only
moderate givenτ 0,Hα/τ0,Hγ ≈21, providing additional
leverage on the optical-depth regime.

4.1.1.J0925+6409
J0925 shows a relatively simple continuum and is
well described by an AGN power-law component plus a
moderate contribution from host galaxy starlight. The
best-fit stellar continuum is dominated by K- and M-
type templates. Because the [SII] doublet is reasonably
strong, we use its profile to construct the narrow-line
template. We fit each [SII] line with two Gaussian com-
ponents and apply the resulting template to the other
forbidden lines and the narrow component of Hα. The
full spectral decomposition, after subtracting the host
contribution, is shown in Figure 2.
The broad Hαand Hβprofiles are each well repro-
duced with two Gaussian components. Given the lim-
ited signal-to-noise ratio, the broad Hγline requires only
a single Gaussian component. We do not detect signifi-
cant FeIIemission in the least-squares fit; nevertheless,
we retain an FeIIcomponent in the MCMC sampling,
which yields a very weak contribution. Figure 5 shows
the broad Balmer profiles after subtracting the narrow-
line component. Notably, the Hγabsorption trough is
prominent in addition to those in Hαand Hβ. In the
fully covered, optically thin limit, the expected absorp-
tion EW ratios are EW Hα : EWHβ : EWHγ ≈9.8 : 1 :
0.3. In contrast, Figure 5 shows that the Hβand Hγab-
sorption troughs are comparable in depth to that of Hα.
This behavior requires a highly optically thick absorber
with partial covering. Consistent with this interpreta-
tion, our fit favors a very large Hαoptical depth, for
which we can only place a lower limit.

require it and we omit it. The spectrum exhibits excep-

Balmer lines), whereas the [SII] doublet is barely visible.

SDSS spectrum, an absorption counterpart at the same
velocity is not apparent in Hβ, likely because the Hβab-
sorption is intrinsically weaker and is strongly blended
with the narrow Hβemission component. Spectroscopy
of higher resolution and higher signal-to-noise ratio pre-
sented by Lin et al. (2026a) confirms that Hβabsorp-

Motivated by this, we include a single blueshifted ab-
sorber applied to Hα, Hβ, and Hγ, tying their line-center
optical depths to the theoretical ratios and enforcing
common kinematics (velocity shift and line width). Be-
cause broad Hγemission is only marginally detected,
the MCMC sampling yields only an upper limit on the
Hγabsorption EW (Table 4).
J1025 also exhibits a distinct Hβabsorption feature at
∆V≈+200 km s −1, while a corresponding Hαtrough is
not evident. Since any redshifted Hαabsorption could
in principle be masked by the narrow Hαemission, we
attempted to include a redshifted Hαabsorption com-
ponent and tie its optical depth to that of the redshifted
Hβabsorption. We were unable to obtain a physically
plausible fit under these constraints, implying that sim-
ple blending is unlikely to explain the apparent absence
of redshifted Hαabsorption. The origin of this devia-
tion from the expected Balmer absorption ratios remains
unclear. Similar inconsistencies between Balmer absorp-
tion strengths and velocities may also occur in JWST
spectra of other LRDs (Ji et al. 2025b; D’Eugenio et al.
2025; Chen et al. 2026). Ji et al. (2025a) emphasized
the mismatch in J1025 and interpreted it as evidence
for multiple absorbing components: the deep Hαab-
sorption (and part of Na D) traces slowly outflowing
gas, whereas the redshifted Hβabsorption suggests an
additional component of lower optical depth that may
be associated with inflowing material.
Given this complexity, we do not attempt a physical
interpretation of the redshifted Hβabsorber. We include
it phenomenologically as an additional absorption com-
ponent affecting only Hβ. Because this feature lies close
to the noise level, leaving all absorber parameters free
yields unstable solutions. We therefore fixC f = 0.8 and
σ= 30 km s −1 for this component; this choice does not
affect our main conclusions becauseC f is largely degen-
erate with optical depth and the feature is unresolved at
SDSS resolution. We show only the decomposed broad
Hαand Hβprofiles in Figure 6, as the broad Hγemission
is not securely detected. The blueshifted Hβabsorption,
whose optical depth is tied to that of Hα, is weak and
comparable to (or below) the noise. The resulting large
absorption-line EW ratio favors a high covering factor,
Cf ≈1, consistent with our best-fit value. We caution,
however, that the uncertain origin of the redshifted Hβ
component may introduce additional systematic uncer-
tainty in the inferred properties of the blueshifted ab-

4.1.4.J1126+4252
J1126 was previously identified as a Balmer-
absorption AGN by Wang & Xu (2015). They per-

galaxy starlight, an AGN power law, and the FeII
pseudo-continuum, and then fitting the emission and ab-
sorption features using multiple Gaussian components.
Wang & Xu (2015) reported that the inferred Balmer-
absorption EW ratios depend on how the spectrum is
normalized. When the absorption is normalized by the
continuum alone, the Hα/Hβabsorption EW ratio is
close to the optically thin expectation. In contrast, when
the absorption is normalized by the broad-line emission
component, the EW ratio becomes close to unity, imply-
ing that the absorber is highly optically thick and only
partially covers the BLR.
In this work, we model the absorption as a multiplica-
tive factor applied to the sum of the continuum and
broad-line emission, as discussed in Section 3.3. For

14
J1126, our continuum model is broadly consistent with

4.1.7.J2220+0109

first in 2001, and the latter two in 2017 separated by
only two months. Intriguingly, the first two spectra

the broad Balmer lines, while the optical continuum and
broad emission lines brighten rapidly between the last
two epochs. The Balmer line profiles also exhibit notice-
able changes. In addition, a secondary Balmer absorp-
tion component appears in the second-epoch spectrum
and becomes more prominent in the third epoch. We

16

only minor modifications to accommodate the observed
variability.
The narrow emission lines are all very weak, if de-
tected at all, except for the [OIII] doublet, which is

this narrow-line template may not accurately represent

the narrow Balmer components. In particular, the in-
ferred [SII] flux in the first epoch is inconsistent with
that in the other two epochs, even though narrow-line

modeled broad Hαand Hβwith three Gaussian compo-

4.2.AGN Emission-line and Continuum Properties
We compare our AGN emission-line and continuum
measurements with those reported for the parent sam-
ple in Liu et al. (2019), who performed a careful spec-

17
tral decomposition for the full sample and adopted tech-
niques similar to ours, including constructing a narrow-
line template and decomposing the Hαand Hβregions.
Overall, we find good agreement in the continuum lu-
minosities and broad-line fluxes. However, our mea-
sured broad Balmer FWHMs for J0925, J1025, J1039,
and J1545 tend to be≳30% lower. This difference is
likely because these Balmer lines are intrinsically nar-
row and/or strongly blended with the narrow-line com-
ponents, making the broad–narrow decomposition sen-
sitive to details of the modeling. In contrast, our broad
Balmer FWHM for J1535 is∼30% higher, possibly be-
cause we explicitly model the Balmer absorption, which
affects the recovered broad-line profile. Finally, the
narrow-line measurements and the inferredR Fe II values
are qualitatively consistent between our work and Liu
et al. (2019), although fitting the large sample suffers
more uncertainties.
4.2.1.Eigenvector 1 Sequence
Figure 15 compares the velocity width of broad Hβ
(FWHMHβ) withR Fe II and the EW of [OIII]λ5007 of
our Balmer-absorption-selected AGNs with those of the
parent sample. These quantities are widely used to
describe the AGN eigenvector 1 sequence (Boroson &
Green 1992; Shen & Ho 2014):R Fe II traces the rela-
tive strength of optical FeIIemission, FWHM Hβ char-
acterizes BLR kinematics (with additional sensitivity
to orientation), and [OIII] EW reflects the strength of
the narrow-line region. In the canonical interpretation,
higher accretion rates (or Eddington ratios) tend to be
associated with largerR Fe II, narrower broad Balmer
lines, and weaker [OIII] emission (Boroson & Green
1992; Shen & Ho 2014). In apparent tension with this
expectation, most of our targets show lowR Fe II, and
their [OIII] EWs lie toward the high end of the parent
distribution. Some of them also have relatively narrow
broad Balmer lines (FWHM Hβ ≲1000 km s −1; J0925,
J1025, and J1545). Taken at face value, these eigen-
vector 1 indicators would not place our objects among
the high-accretion rate population. However, Figure 16
shows that at least four of the seven sources (all except
J1126, J1535, J2220) have among the highest Edding-
ton ratios in the parent sample. Although J1126, J1535,
and J2220 haveM BH ≳10 8 M⊙, their Eddington ratios
are still high compared to parent objects with similar
line widths, suggesting that the disky broad-line region
has a high inclination angle to the sight line. Notably,
the three highest Eddington ratio sources are strong out-
liers from the overallR Fe II–λEdd trend (Figure 16c): the
subsample highlighted by the red dashed box occupies
only 0.4% of the parent sample among objects with a
measuredR Fe II. We defer further discussion until Sec-
tion 5.1.
4.2.2.Optical Emission-line Diagnostics
Our targets span a wide range of locations on the opti-
cal line-intensity diagnostic diagrams (Figure 17), indi-
cating a diversity in their narrow-line properties. J1126
and J1535 fall on the typical locus of Seyfert galaxies.
This is perhaps not surprising, as they are the most
“normal” objects in our sample in terms ofR Fe II, Ed-
dington ratio, the presence of strong forbidden lines, and
their very broad Balmer emission.
In contrast, the remaining five sources tend to show
very weak [NII], moderate [SII] and [OI], and a variety
of strengths of [OIII]. The most extreme case is J1545-0,
for which we can only derive an upper limit on [NII]. Be-
cause we can only measure upper limits for the narrow
Balmer lines in J1545-0, we do not plot it in Figure 17a,
as both [NII]λ6548 and Hαare upper limits. The DESI
spectrum of J1545-1 has much higher quality and can
better characterize the host galaxy, and the SDSS and
DESI measurements are largely consistent within the
uncertainties. These line ratios suggest that the host
galaxies of sources have low gas-phase metallicities. In
particular, [NII] is a sensitive metallicity indicator be-
cause nitrogen is a secondary element (Vila-Costas &
Edmunds 1993; Henry et al. 2000) and its abundance in-
creases more steeply with metallicity than those of oxy-
gen and sulfur (Nomoto et al. 2013). In this regard,
J1025 is the most extreme source: all low-ionization
lines, including [NII], [SII], and [OI], are weak, which,
together with strong [OIII], suggest a highly excited ion-
ized nebula, one with high ionization parameter and typ-
ically higher characteristic electron temperatures than
in lower-excitation systems (Groves et al. 2006).

4.4.Photometric Properties
Broad absorption-line quasars are, on average, redder
than regular quasars in the rest-frame UV/optical, com-

Richards et al. 2003). It is therefore of interest to com-
pare the broad-band SEDs of Balmer-absorption AGNs
to those of typical quasars and to LRDs. For LRDs in
particular, a central question is whether Balmer absorp-
tion is physically linked to the characteristic “V-shaped”
UV–optical SED, whose origin remains controversial (In-
ayoshi & Ho 2025). Liu et al. (2019) compiled UV, op-
tical, near-infrared, and mid-infrared photometric mea-
surements from the GALEX, SDSS, 2MASS, and WISE
catalogs.7 We use these data to examine the broad-band
SEDs of our Balmer-absorption AGNs and to compare
them with the color properties of LRDs (Figure 20). We
measure power-law slopes in the UV and optical bands
following the definition adopted in LRD selection stud-
ies (Kocevski et al. 2025). Specifically, we fit the 1250–
3645 ˚A range to derive the UV slope (β UV) and the
3300–8000 ˚A range to derive the optical slope (β opt).
The UV wavelength range is chosen to be 100 ˚A shorter
than that in Kocevski et al. (2025) so that the GALEX
far-UV band can be included for all sources.
Besides J1025, the low-zLRD analog identified by Lin
et al. (2026a), we find that J0925 also satisfies the LRD
color criteria, namelyβ opt >0 andβ UV <−0.37. We
note that the UV slope would likely be even steeper if
photometry at shorter wavelengths were available. Our

5.DISCUSSION
5.1.Number Statistics and Selection Effects
Balmer-absorption AGNs are rare in the overall low-
ztype 1 AGN population. We identify seven Balmer-
absorption AGNs in thez <0.35 parent sample of
14,584 sources from Liu et al. (2019). Taken at face
value, this corresponds to a miniscule occurrence rate
of∼0.05%. However, the incidence increases substan-
tially in a specific region of parameter space. Three of
our seven sources fall in the low-R Fe II and high-λ Edd
locus (Figure 16c), which is itself uncommon among
typical AGNs in the low-zUniverse. As discussed in
Section 4.2, only∼40 objects in the Liu et al. (2019)
sample with robust FeIImeasurement reside in this lo-
cus (e.g., logλ Edd >−0.45 andR Fe II <0.35). If we
restrict the parent population to this subsample, the
Balmer-absorption occurrence rate increases to∼10%,
comparable to the reported occurrence rate of LRDs
(Matthee et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2024; Kocevski et al.
2025). The extreme example is J1025, the low-zLRD
analog. The low-R Fe II property is also widely observed
in JWST AGNs and has been interpreted as a signature
of low metallicity (Trefoloni et al. 2025), suggesting that
Balmer absorption may be preferentially linked to this
broader population.
Even within this locus, the observed incidence can be
substantially suppressed by geometric and observational
selection effects. For example, J0925 and J1545 have
moderate covering factors,C f ≈0.4−0.6. This seem-
ingly implies only a∼40%−60% probability that the
absorber intersects our line-of-sight, even if such gas is
generically present. In addition, robust detection typi-
cally requires a modest velocity offset (≳100 km s −1) so
that the absorption is not strongly blended with narrow
Balmer emission; in the Hαregion, blending with the
adjacent [NII]λλ6548, 6584 doublet poses further com-
plications. Moreover, in all of the three sources, the
inferred absorption is quite narrow (σ≈20–50 km s −1),
so the troughs can be substantially diluted, if not en-
tirely missed, at modest spectral resolution. Collec-
tively, these effects plausibly can reduce an intrinsic in-
cidence of order tens of percent to an observed fraction
of∼10%, even if absorbing gas is common among AGNs
in this region of parameter space.
The recent analysis by Lin et al. (2024) offers a com-
paratively unbiased measurement of Balmer absorption
in high-zAGNs. Using JWST/NIRCam WFSS data
from ASPIRE (Wang et al. 2023), they identify 16
broad-line AGNs, three of which (19%) show Balmer ab-
sorption troughs. Although their measurement of con-
tinuum slope carries substantial uncertainties, two of
the three absorbers meet common LRD color criteria
(βUV <−0.37 andβ opt >0), while the third has a red-
der UV slope. More generally,≳60% of the AGNs in
their sample haveβopt >0. While the role of host galaxy

likely play a central role: absorption close to systemic
velocity is easier to conceal underlying narrow emis-
sion, and intrinsically narrow troughs are washed out
more readily at limited spectral resolution (D’Eugenio
et al. 2026). To illustrate this effect, we degrade our
SDSS/DESI spectra (R≳1800) to the typical resolu-

still shows a detectable trough because of its stronger
absorber (C f ≈0.6,τ 0,Hα ≳60). This experiment sug-
gests that current JWST detections may be biased to-
ward absorbers with large optical depths and/or broader
intrinsic profiles, and that higher-resolution JWST spec-
troscopy could reveal a substantially larger fraction of
LRDs with Balmer absorption. Consistent with this pic-
ture, Balmer absorption reported in high-zLRDs is of-
ten relatively broad (≳50 km s−1), rendering them more
easily detectable at moderate resolution (Chen et al.
2026). Interestingly, Lin et al. (2026b) recently reported
a sample of 27 low-zLRD analogs selected from DESI
spectra and found Balmer absorption in 67% of them.
This high detection fraction is consistent with the above
interpretation, suggesting that Balmer absorption may
be more common in LRD-like systems than currently
inferred from moderate-resolution JWST spectra.
5.2.Balmer Decrement
The Balmer decrement of narrow recombination lines
is commonly used to estimate dust attenuation in the
host galaxy interstellar medium, because the intrinsic
Hα/Hβratio is close to the Case B recombination value
and depends only weakly on gas temperature and den-
sity (e.g., Hummer & Storey 1987; Osterbrock 1989).
This approach is widely adopted for Hiiregions and
for AGN narrow-line regions when the narrow compo-
nents can be reliably measured. In contrast, LRDs often
show extreme Balmer decrements in their broad compo-
nents. Early interpretations attributed the large broad-
line Hα/Hβto heavy dust attenuation (e.g., Killi et al.
2024). However, it has long been known that broad-
line Balmer decrements in AGNs can deviate signifi-
cantly from the Case B prediction on account of in-
trinsic BLR physics, including high densities, radiative
transfer, and collisional effects (e.g., Dong et al. 2008;
Schnorr-M¨ uller et al. 2016; Gaskell 2017). Yan et al.
(2026) show that high Balmer decrements can be repro-
duced by dense, dust-free gas: atn≳10 8 cm−3, the
emergent Balmer line ratios converge to values closely
following the effect of dust reddening. Consistent with
this picture, Chen et al. (2026) measure emission and

the ionization state, the recombination timescale of HI
must be shorter than the observed baseline, ∆t≈16 yr.

even higher values are needed for collisional excitation
(Hall et al. 2002). At such densities, the recombination
timescale oft rec ≲0.1 yr is far shorter than the multi-

est variability in J1545, and essentially none in J1535,

highly transient absorber and instead suggests a dynam-

slowly. In this sense, our findings are qualitatively con-
sistent with the “enshrouding” scenarios proposed for
LRDs (Inayoshi & Maiolino 2025; Lin et al. 2026a; In-
ayoshi & Ho 2025).

5.4.Physical Implications
This work is motivated by the high fraction of LRDs
showing Balmer absorption lines. Recent theoretical
models propose that the BH accretion flow in LRDs is
enshrouded by a dense gas envelope, which can naturally
produce a red optical continuum, a prominent Balmer
break, and Balmer absorption lines (Inayoshi & Maiolino
2025; Kido et al. 2025). In these scenarios, powerful out-
flows expected in highly accreting systems may also help
to establish and maintain the envelope, and can con-
tribute to the weak variability and X-ray faintness re-
ported for LRDs (Kido et al. 2025). Motivated by these
ideas, we seek to test whether Balmer absorption at low
redshift is preferentially associated with high accretion
rates, and whether its measured properties support an
envelope-like geometry.
Our low-zBalmer-absorption AGNs span a range of
AGN properties. Four sources (J1039, J1126, J1535,
and J2220) resemble more typical broad-line AGNs and
have moderate Eddington ratios ofλ Edd ≈0.1. In
contrast, three sources (J0925, J1025, and J1545) re-
side in the low-R Fe II and high-λ Edd locus of the local
AGN population (Figure 16c). Intriguingly, they also
possess narrow-line intensity ratios consistent with low-
metallicity host galaxies (Figure 17). This combination
is unusual because in the overall parent AGN population
RFe II typically increases with Eddington ratio along the
eigenvector 1 sequence (Boroson & Green 1992; Shen &
Ho 2014). These three systems are closer in their global
properties to the high-zAGNs and LRDs uncovered by
JWST (Trefoloni et al. 2025). In particular, J1025 has
been identified as a local analog of LRDs (Lin et al.
2026a).
By fitting the Hα, Hβ, and Hγabsorption simultane-
ously, we find that the Balmer troughs are well repro-
duced by a single partially covering absorber in which
the line-center optical depths are tied to the atomic-
physics ratios. This supports a common line-of-sight
structure responsible for all Balmer absorption features.
Across the sample, excluding J2220, for which the fits
are less robust, we measureτ 0,Hα ≳4.5, and in the most
optically thick cases we obtain lower limits ofτ0,Hα >25.
The inferred covering factors spanC f ≈0.2−0.6 for
most sources, whereas the LRD analog J1025 requires
a substantially higher value ofC f ≳0.8. We find no
significant trend betweenτ 0,Hα and Eddington ratio. In
contrast,C f shows a tentative increase with Eddington
ratio among the non-LRD sources and, more strikingly,
a strong inverse correlation withR Fe II, including J1025
(Figure 19). One plausible interpretation is that the
lowR Fe II in the high-λEdd locus reflects low metallicity,
instead of weak accretion. In a low-metallicity, high-
accretion rate system, sustaining the mass supply may
require a larger reservoir of dense neutral gas, which
naturally raises the probability of intersecting absorbing
material. Meanwhile, lower metallicity reduces the effi-
ciency of line driving, suppressing accretion-disk winds
and associated mass loss (Shlosman et al. 1985; Proga
et al. 2000). If disk winds are less effective at clearing or
fragmenting circumnuclear gas, the absorber can retain
a high covering factor, qualitatively accounting for the
observed inverse correlation betweenC f andR Fe II.
This interpretation does not imply that iron is ab-
sent. In particular, the detection of narrow FeIIemission
and absorption in J1025 (Lin et al. 2026a), J1039, and
J1126 demonstrates that iron exists in the gas. Balmer
absorption is also preferentially found in FeLoBALQs
(Leighly et al. 2025). The weak broad FeIIpseudo-
continuum may instead result from a combination of re-
duced enrichment and different ionization conditions in
the BLR. Photoionization calculations show that opti-
cal FeIIemission depends sensitively on metallicity and
on the thermodynamic and radiative state of the line-
emitting gas (e.g., Sigut & Pradhan 2003; Baldwin et al.
2004). For example, diminished metal-line opacity and
self-shielding at low metallicity can shift iron to higher
ionization stages and reduce the contribution of broad
optical FeIIemission (Gaskell et al. 2022).
6.SUMMARY
This work is motivated by the high incidence of
Balmer absorption lines reported in JWST observations
of LRDs. We search for low-redshift type 1 AGNs ex-
hibiting Balmer absorption and place them in context
relative to a homogeneous parent population. Starting
from thez <0.35 broad-line AGN catalog of Liu et al.
(2019), we visually identify Balmer-absorption candi-

26
dates whose narrow features are typically unresolved in
SDSS-quality spectra. We then perform a joint spectral
decomposition of the continuum and emission lines, and
model the Balmer absorption with a partially covering
absorber model. We consider the instrument broaden-
ing effect and fit Hα, Hβ, and Hγsimultaneously with
their optical-depth ratios fixed to theoretical values.
•Balmer-absorption AGNs are exceedingly rare in
the overall low-ztype 1 population: we identify
seven sources among 14,584 objects (∼0.05%).
The incidence increases substantially in the low-
RFe II and high-Eddington ratio locus, reaching
∼10% when restricting to this rare region of pa-
rameter space.
•Simultaneous fitting of the Hα, Hβ, and Hγab-
sorption lines shows that the absorption profiles
are well described by a single partially covering ab-
sorber with tied centroid and width, and with opti-
cal depths fixed to the theoretical ratios. This sup-
ports a common line-of-sight origin for the Balmer
absorption features.
•All but one source require optically thick Hαab-
sorption (τ Hα ≳4.5), with lower limitsτ Hα ≳25
in the most saturated cases. The inferred covering
factors are typically moderate (C f ≈0.2−0.6),
while the local LRD analog J1025 shows a high
covering factor (C f ≳0.8), consistent with high-
zLRDs studied in the companion study by Chen
et al. (2026).
•The absorption lines are intrinsically narrow
(σ≈20−200 km s −1) and often unresolved
in SDSS-quality spectra, requiring convolution
with the instrumental line-spread function. De-
tectability also depends on velocity offset and on
blending with narrow Balmer emission and the
[NII]λλ6548, 6584 doublet, leading to strong se-
lection effects at moderate resolution. In line with
this, degrading our SDSS/DESI spectra to JWST
prism-like resolution would cause some absorbers
to be missed, implying that current JWST detec-
tions may be biased toward broader and/or more
optically thick systems.
•Three sources (J0925, J1025, and J1545) oc-
cupy an unusual regime of high Eddington ratio
but lowR Fe II, and their location on the opti-
cal line-intensity diagnostic diagrams indicate low-
metallicity environments. These systems resemble
high-zAGNs and LRDs more closely than typical
low-zbroad-line AGNs. We find no significant cor-
relation betweenτ Hα and Eddington ratio, butC f
increases with Eddington ratio for the non-LRD
sources and shows a strong inverse correlation with
RFe II. We suggest that low metallicity suppresses
disk-wind clearing, while high accretion requires a
large nuclear gas reservoir; together these effects
increase the line-of-sight covering factor and hence
the likelihood of detecting Balmer absorption in
AGNs of low metallicity and high Eddington ra-
tio.
•For the three objects with available multi-epoch
spectroscopy, J1535 shows only modest continuum
variability and no significant absorption variabil-
ity over three SDSS epochs, while J1545 shows
strengthened absorption between the 2005 SDSS
and 2021 DESI spectra despite only minor changes
in the broad emission lines. J2220, in contrast,
exhibits much stronger variability: the continuum
and broad Balmer lines vary substantially on both
∼16 yr and∼2 month timescales, and a rapidly
varying redshifted Balmer absorber emerges in the
2017 spectra. The limited decade-scale variability
in J1535 and J1545, despite short recombination
times at the densities required for Balmer absorp-
tion, suggests that their absorbers reside in dy-
namically stable configurations whose global prop-
erties evolve slowly, whereas J2220 likely traces a
more rapidly evolving accretion/absorption state.
