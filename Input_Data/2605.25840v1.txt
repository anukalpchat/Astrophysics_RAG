ABSTRACT
Context.Accurate simulation of astronomical observations is a critical element for any modern analyses, be it to measure event rates, analyses
population properties, validate or train pipelines, account for selection effects, or correct biases.
Aims.We present a novel pure python package namedskysurveymade to enable the user to quickly simulate astrophysical transients as observed
by a survey. The package is structured to make the implementation of any complex population modeling fast and easy.
Methods.skysurveyrelies on three core objects: aTarget, that models how an astrophysical target exists in nature, aSurvey, that specifies
how the sky has been observed and, aDataSetthat combine these two to generate data as they would have been acquired. In addition, we present
a side stand-alone package namedmodeldagthat contains the core structure that simplifies the parameter modeling.
Results.We present in this paper howskysurveyis structured and we clearly illustrate how the code can straightforwardly be used to simulate
complex populations, such as Type Ia Supernovae with varying color-brightnessβterm. We also illustrate how the package can be made to replicate
the rate and redshift distribution of the ZTF SNe Ia DR2 dataset.
Conclusions.Theskysurveypackage, already used in recent scientific publications, is now ready for general usage and paves the way for future
use of simulations such as simulation based inference.
Key words.Simulation ; Cosmology ; Supernovae
1. Introduction
Simulations are the foundation of many scientific analyzes, as
they allow scientists to validate methods on cases where the
ground truth is known. This is particularly useful when deal-
ing with selection effects that impact the vast majority of astro-
nomical and cosmological studies ; be it because of instrumen-
tal limits, because the Milky Way, or, say, of the availability of
follow-up instruments. Simulations are then used to validate in-
ference pipelines or to probe what has not been observed given
what has. The latter aspect is key to derive rates (e.g. Pain et al.
2002; Leaman et al. 2011; Perley et al. 2020), to study proper-
ties of the underlying population (e.g. Li et al. 2011; Vincenzi et
al. 2019; Dimitriadis et al. 2025) or to correct the selection ef-
fect when deriving cosmological parameters (e.g., Betoule et al.
2014; Kessler & Scolnic 2017; Scolnic et al. 2018; Riess et al.
2019; Brout et al. 2022). The use of massive realistic simulations
has furthermore been shown to be able to train machine-learning
methods to infer model parameters, a technique now known as
simulation based inference (e.g. Cranmer et al. 2020; Karchev
et al. 2023; Ho et al. 2024; Boyd et al. 2026, and references
therein). Finally, simulations are essential to plan future instru-
ments and surveys (e.g. Goobar et al. 2002; Kessler et al. 2010;
Bernstein et al. 2012; Nissanke et al. 2013; Hounsell et al. 2018).
In extra-galactic transient sciences, theSNANApackage
(Kessler et al. 2009) has long been the central simulation soft-
ware. Among many other key successes,SNANAhas been the
reference software used to prepare the upcoming Rubin Large
Survey of Space and Time (LSST, Ivezi ´c et al. 2019) and the
cosmology program of the Roman Space Telescope (Rose et al.
2021, 2025). There,SNANAwas most notably the software suite

used to simulate realistic transients as part of the PLAsTiCC and
ELAsTiCC classification challenges (Kessler et al. 2019; Hložek
et al. 2023), which have been extensively used to develop and
explore classification algorithms. Outside its use for transients
modeling,SNANAhas been the central tool behind the last decade
of precision cosmological measurements from Type Ia Super-
novae (SNe Ia) (e.g. Scolnic et al. 2018; Brout et al. 2022; Vin-
cenzi et al. 2024; Popovic et al. 2025) via the Beams with Bias
Correction method (BBC, Scolnic & Kessler 2016; Kessler &
Scolnic 2017; Popovic et al. 2021).
ButSNANAis not without downsides, mostly stemming from
its long legacy.SNANAis a complex combination ofC,fortran
andpython, making installation famously complex and largely
limited to a handful of high performance computing servers. Fur-
ther compounding the approachability problem is the legacy of
its 10+years of development: the software contains hard-coded
numbers and structures that predate original analyses approach
to recently discovered issues. Notably, the SN-host correlations
(e.g., Sullivan et al. 2010; Rigault et al. 2013; Roman et al. 2018;
Kelsey et al. 2021; Rigault et al. 2020; Brout, & Scolnic 2021)
was not considered in the initial development ofSNANA. This led
recent analyses to iterative fudging and new implementations of
the SN-to-host matching to recover observed distributions for
the treatment of SN-to-host correlation across, e.g., Smith et al.
(2020), Brout, & Scolnic (2021), Popovic et al. (2021), Popovic
et al. (2023), and Wiseman et al. (2022). Because of this long
development cycle, these new features are piped through pre-
defined structures such as libraries of host galaxies (HOSTLIBs)
and hard-coded probability distribution functions (WGTMAPs).
SNANAuses.inputfiles, ideal for long-term storage and repli-
cability, but complex and spread across multiple fronts within
SNANA. The addition of thepippinwrapper (Hinton & Brout

a fast runningpip installable library that would enable any
user to model (complex) simulations of any kind of astrophys-
ical sources, and notably of any transients, as given by nature
and as observed by any kind of survey (e.g., ZTF or LSST). As
this paper was being written, theLightCurveLynxsurvey sim-
ulation package has been introduced through a first example on
simulated SN Ia populations (Dai et al. 2026), further illustrating
the need for alternative simulation codes toSNANA.
This paper presentsskysurveyversion 1.0. Ongoing or fu-
ture developments shall continue improving the code, both in
terms of speed and flexibility, while conserving the core aspect
of the package: an easy to use software made to enable the user
to simulate complex astrophysical targets and surveys. We in-
vite the user to refer to the online documentation that contains
many usage examples including those described in this paper.
The code is publicly available on GitHub and can be installed
using thepip install skysurveycommand.
Since its initial development, theskysurveypackage has
already been used to publish scientific analyses: to reproduce
an existing dataset to understand its characteristics (Amenouche
et al. 2025; Rigault et al. 2025), to simulate realistic Super-
novae data to validate an inference pipeline (Ginolin et al.
2025a,b), to study the origin of unexpected rate variations with
redshift (Gilles Lordet et al. 2026), to compare observed Super-
novae rate with expectations in early LSST data (Freeburn et al.
2025), to analyse the observations of strongly lensed supernovae
(Sagués Carracedo et al. 2024), to forecast measurements ofH 0
anisotropies with ZTF (Barjou-Delayre et al. 2026), etc. More
analyses are ongoing, such as a complex Type Ia Supernovae
modeling (Ginolin et al. in prep), a validation of dark energy
and large scale structure cosmological inference pipelines (Be-
toule et al. in prep, Kebadian et al. in prep.). Theskysurvey
package is also particularly well suited to train simulation based
inference pipeline (Trigui et al. in prep), to derive core-collapse
Supernovae rate and luminosity functions (Qin et al. in prep) or
to validate strongly lensed supernovae analyses (Osman Hjortlun
et al. in prep.).

2.modeldag: build any parameter model
At the core ofskysurvey’s parameter generation is the
modeldag1 package. Thispythonpackage enables a user to
build complex models from a simple dictionary structure, form-
ing a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). In this DAG, each node
represents a model parameter, and the edges represent dependen-
cies between them. Each parameter is associated to a function
that describes how this parameter is drawn. Thanks to the DAG
structure, the output of one parameter draw can be used as input
of the function that draws another. The strength ofmodeldagis
that this process is made straightforward by its simplepython
dictionary structure.
This is fully illustrated in Fig. 1 for a simple model inspired
by the SNe Ia brighter-slower brighter-bluer relations (e.g., Tripp
1998; Riess et al. 1996):m i =α×x 1,i +β×c i, withαand
βpopulation parameters andm i,x 1,i andc i individual SNe Ia
terms. As shown in the middle part of the left panel in Fig. 1,
Themodeldag(nested-) dictionary structure is the following.
The first levelkeys(x1,candm, Fig. 1) represent the param-
eters to be generated. The second level explains how to do so,
using only 2 entries: "func", which provide the information of
the function to be used to generate the parameter, and "kwargs",
which provide the function’s parameters. The core idea behind
modeldagis that one can use the symbol “@“ to use the result
of a parameter’s drawing as parameter input for another param-
eter, as illustrated in Fig. 1. In that example,x1 andcare drawn
from normal distribution, butmis generated by combining them
linearly ; notice thex1=’@x1’andc=’@c’inm’skwargs.
2.1. Any function to generate parameters
Any function can be used to generate parameters. As illustrated
in Fig. 1modeldaghandles functions that return parameter(s)
or that "transform" them (i.e. generate one from others). To han-
dle functions returning multiple parameters generated simultane-
ously, the second level dictionary accepts a third keyword along
sidefuncandkwargs: "as". This last keyword enables the user
to specify each returned parameter name. As illustrated in Fig. 2,
this is useful when several parameters have to be generated si-
multaneously, as when randomly drawing points uniformly dis-
tributed in a shell like sky-coordinates (RA, Dec). Alternatively,
ascould be used to rename a single parameter.
In addition,modeldagaccepts functions that return (n-d)
probability distribution functions (PDF). To be automatically
identified bymodeldag, these functions must return two arrays:
’xx’ and ’pdf’. Thenmodeldagwill sample the parameter by
randomly drawingxxvalues following the individual point prob-
ability given bypdf; see online documentation for examples.
Across the code, thesizeargument must be accepted by
functions that returned drawn parameters, likedraw_normalin
Fig. 1. Transformation functions do not need it assizewill be
taken from the length of input parameters (likex1 orc, Fig. 1).

3.skysurvey’s structure and logic
Theskysurveypackage aims to simulate the transient sky in a
forward way. Following that logic, the code enables (1) to gener-
ateTargetobjects as given by nature, i.e. noise- and selection-
free (see Section 4) ; (2) to createSurveyobjects containing ob-
serving information (which filter has been used when to observe
where under which conditions ; see Section 5) ; (3) to combine
both objects to generate realistic lightcurves. This combination
is trivially handled by theDataSetobject (see Section 6). This
is illustrated in Fig. 3.
In the following subsection, we detail how these three ob-
jects work. Theskysurveypackage has other objects that are
used internally and will be described when relevant within these
subsections.
4.Target
Askysurvey.Targetrepresents the astrophysical objects to
be simulated. The key attributes of this family of objects are:
templateandmodel.
Thetemplatecorresponds to the spectro-photometric
model from which a lightcurve or a spectrum could be gen-
erated. In its released version,skysurveyrelies onsncosmo
for handling templates (Barbary et al. 2016). This is handled
through theskysurvey.Templateobject that manages interac-
tions between otherskysurveyobjects andsncosmo.Models
(sosncosmo.Sources ; seesncosmo’s documentation).
As such, anysncosmo.Source(built-in or user-defined) can
be used inskysurvey.
Themodelattribute is amodeldagobject (see Section 2)
made to simulate target’s parameters. These parameters are, at
least, those needed by thetemplateand sky coordinates (RA
and DEC) needed to match the target with a survey and thereby
to simulate realistic observations of the target (see Section 6).

4.1. Simulating targets
Once theTargetis loaded, one can use thedraw()method
to generate a sample. This method internally calls thedraw()
method ofmodeldag(i.e. of themodelattribute) to generate a
pandas DataFrame where each row corresponds to an individual
target and the columns are the parameters specified in themodel.
This dataframe is stored as aTarget.dataattribute.
Up on calling of the.draw()method, the user can alter
the model or the model’s parameters, such as the redshift range
of the targets and their corresponding time-range (correspond-
ing tosncosmo’st0source parameters). This functionality fully
exploits the modularity of the dictionary-oriented structure of
modeldagat the core of theskysurveypackage.
Target redshifts are generated following theTarget.rate
attribute which could either be constant or a function of redshift
or of any parameter such as the host properties (see detail in
Section 4.2).
Finally, given the redshift and time-range you can
either specify the number of target you want (e.g.
draw(size=10_000)) or leave the code estimate the sam-
ple size given the volume of the Universe covered by the
redshift range, the target rate, the cosmology, and the time of
observation. In addition, sinceSurveycould be focusing on nar-
row region of the sky (e.g. few deep-fields), the user is invited to
provide theskyarea(skyarea = survey.get_skyarea())
as input of thedraw()method. Thisshapely(Multi)Polygon
object limits the area of the sky where targets will be drawn
(Gillies, et al. 2023 ; see Section 5). This is particularly useful
when simulating pencil-beam observations like deep-fields ; no
need to generate millions of never-observed targets.
4.2. rates and redshifts
The rate at which a transient occurs is a crucial input for any sim-
ulation, as it specifies how redshifts are drawn. Theskysurvey
package relies on volumetric rates in target/Gpc 3/yr which
could be specified in two ways: (1) as a constant (float) or (2)
as a function. The former assumes that the rate is the same re-
gardless of, say, direction or redshift. The latter enables a full
flexibility of the rate: it could depend of redshift (e.g. Frohmaier
et al. 2019), and/or host properties (e.g. Sullivan et al. 2006) etc.
In any case, the rate function is expected to return the target’s
volumetric rate in target/Gpc3/yr.
To draw redshifts, the code first defines redshift bins (flexible
size, but∆z=10 −4 by default), second computes the Universe’s
volume (Gpc 3) of this redshift shell given a cosmology, third
computes the volumetric target rate at the pivot redshift of the
shell. Fourth, the target redshifts are randomly drawn from these
redshift bins weighted by the number of targets per bin.
4.3. Collections of targets
Theskysurveypackage enables the user to buildTargetbased
on anysncosmotemplate (e.g. SALT-like, or SN templates) or,
even more generally, any user-definedsncosmo.Source.
However, for most of transients, such as Type II Supernovae,
there is no generic template (unlike SALT for SNe Ia) but rather
a variety of templates based on well-observed objects. For in-
stance,sncosmocontains 23 templates for Type II Supernovae
as defined by Vincenzi et al. (2019).
To simplify the user experience, a
MultiTemplateTSTransientobject has been implemented in
skysurveythat behaves just like aTSTransientbut handles
the multiple-template issue in the back-end. Based on that, we
have pre-built a variety of multiple-template targets, such as
Type II Supernovae (see a list in Section 4.4).
As such, theskysurvey.SNeIItarget object contains, as
atemplate, these 23 Vincenzi et al. (2019) templates. When
the user calls the.draw()method, the individual target’s tem-
plate is drawn alongside all other parameters defined by the ob-
ject’smodeldag(see Section 4). To draw these templates, the
code takes into account that the relative rate of each of these
templates may vary. To specify this, therateparameter of a
MultiTemplateTSTransientobject can either be a constant,
a function for any normalTarget, a list of constants, or a list of
functions respecting the numpy broadcasting rule (e.g. a list of 1
or 23 constants in that case). In the former case (or if the list has
one entry) the templates are equiprobable. This is illustrated in
Fig. 4.
In addition, theskysurveypackage also contain a
genericTargetCollectionobject that can be instantiated
by giving a list of anyTarget(or equivalent such as a
MultiTemplateTSTransient). The package is made such that
thisTargetCollectionworks anywhere aTargetobject
would ; most notably, such a target collection is accepted as input
ofDataSet.from_target_and_survey()(see Section 6).

4.6. Adding effects
skysurveyprovides a flexible mechanism for adding various
physical effects to the simulation. This is handled by theEffect
class, which can modify both the intrinsic properties of the tran-
sients and/or the way their lightcurves or spectra are generated.
In practice, askysurvey.Effecthas two core attributes:
1..effect, ansncosmo.PropagationEffect, which alters
how transient fluxes (mag) are generated, be it when getting a
lightcurve or a spectrum ; 2..model, amodeldagdictionary
that will be used to update the target’s model to generate the
.effectparameters if any.
An effect can be added to any target either during instan-
tiation, using theeffect=option of the.from_draw()class
method, or directly using the.set_effect()method. A target
can handle multiple effects simultaneously.

5.Survey
TheSurveyobjects inskysurveyrepresent a time-domain sur-
vey. A survey is defined by a spatial footprint of the camera in
the sky, and observing logs (or corresponding simulation), spec-
ifying what part of the sky has been observed when and under
what conditions. Under the hood,Surveyobjects are made both
highly modular while remaining extremely fast in matching ob-
serving logs with any sky location (hence any target) by a com-
bination ofshapely 3 andgeopandas 4 libraries and, for some,
by using the healpix decomposition 5.
There are two kind of surveys inskysurvey: the

5.1.1. ZTF: aGridSurvey

6.DataSet
TheDataSetobject is the final product of askysurveysimula-
tion. This object simulates the lightcurves by matching an input
target(or list of) with the survey logs. The lightcurves gen-
eration is detailed in Section 6.1 and keyDataSetmethods are

6.1. Generating lightcurves
The lightcurve generation is made at instantiation of the
DataSetusing the.from_targets_and_survey()class
method. This method, the backbone of theDataSetobject,
works as follows.
First, targets are associated with the survey’s logs using
the.radec_to_field()survey method, which takes a frac-
tion of second to provide thefieldid(i.e., the healpix pixel
id for generic survey) associated to each target. In case of a
GridSurvey, a target can have severalfieldids as fields can
overlap, and thisfieldidcould be a multi-layer index, espe-
cially if the surveyfootprintis decomposed by detectors or
amplifier (see Section 5). At this stage, we have a way to select
the entry logs corresponding to any given target and we know
which target has no entry (e.g. simulated outside the survey sky-
footprint).
Second, we loop over each target and we build its
sncosmo.Modelincluding its template parameter alongside

6.2. Using aDataSet.
Theskysurvey.DataSetobject has three main attributes:
.targets, a pointer to the input (multi-)target object;.survey,
a pointer to the input survey; and.data, the multi-index

Based on these, and mostly on.data, theDataSethas top level
functionalities to ease the user experience.
First, the.get_target_lightcurve()method enables the

have at leastndetections, including at leastmpre-max andp

8. Conclusion and Discussion
This paper presents theskysurveypackage. A pure python li-
brary that enables the user to easily simulate complex astrophys-
ical populations as they would be observed by a survey. This
package has been designed to be fast, intuitive, and highly mod-
ular in its ability to build any astrophysical target that could have
any parameter modeling structure. This later aspect is made pos-
sible thanks tomodeldag, a standalone package developed in
parallel toskysurveyand also presented in this article.
Themodeldagpython package provides a way to create a
direct acyclic diagram (DAG) between parameters using a triv-
ial python dictionary structure that only has three leafs:func
that defines how the parameters should be drawn,kwargsthat
provides the function arguments,asspecifies the name of the
returned parameter(s) – which is particularly useful when return-
ing multiple parameter simultaneously (e.g. coordinates). The
trick ofmodeldagis the introduction of the "@" keyword in
kwargs, such that "x=’@a’" is parsed by the code as "use the
output of theaparameter draw asxinput to draw this other pa-
rameter". This way,modeldagautomatically creates DAG be-
tween parameters while offering an entire flexibility to the user
to specify how parameters should be drawn as any function can
be used and any parameter connections are accepted.
Theskysurveypackage contains three main parts. The first
isTargetthat specifies how the astrophysical population ex-
ists in nature. TheTargetparameters are based onmodeldag
and the spectro-photometric (time series) template that provides
the target flux as a function of time, wavelength or filter are
based onsncosmo. As such, anysncosmotemplate or model
(built-in or user-defined) are accepted byskysurvey. By default
skysurveyhas many built-inTargetsuch as any Supernovae
type (SNeII, SNeIa, SNeIbc etc.) or kilonovae. The second part
isSurvey, that carries the information of how the sky has been
observed. A user can easily build any customed survey (see on-
line documentation) or use a list of predefined ones such asZTF
orLSST. the third part isDataSet, which builds the expected
observations ofTarget(or list of) by theSurvey. The user can
focus on complexifying the astrophysical population at will, they
may alter theSurvey– for instance to introduce calibration un-
certainties, but theDataSetwill still be able to handle them.
That is the strength ofskysurvey: the package is intuitive, user
oriented, and designed to be highly robust and easy to use. It is
finally solely based on well supported python libraries such as
numpy,pandasandsncosmo, ensuring stability and long term
user supports and maintenance.
