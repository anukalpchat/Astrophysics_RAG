ABSTRACT
Aims.We investigate the spatially-resolved ISM properties of the local (z=0.048), IR-luminous (L IR =10 11.87 L⊙), late-stage galaxy merger
IRAS F01364-1042, combining multi-wavelength IFU observations fromJWST/MIRI-MRS, ALMA and Keck/KCWI. The system exhibits excess
emission from mid-IR H 2 lines relative to dust, as identified in a previousSpitzer/IRSsurvey of local IR-luminous galaxies (LIRGs). The bright
H2 emission was speculated to arise from widespread shocks associated with a powerful starburst/AGN-driven outflow.
Methods.Using our multi-wavelength datasets, we assess and compare the resolved morphology and kinematics of multi-phase gas tracers on
sub-kpc scales. We construct emission line maps of several key tracers of the ionized (e.g., [Ne II] 12.8µm, [O III]λ5007), warm molecular (e.g.,
H2 0-0 S(3)), and cold molecular gas (e.g., CO (J=2−1)), and perform detailed decomposition of spectra extracted in resolved regions across the
areas of emission, with a focus on the central∼3 kpc of the system covered by all our datasets.
Results.We confirm the presence of a multi-phase galactic biconical outflow along the minor axis of a highly inclined rotating disk, based on
the multi-phase gas morphology and prominent line broadening (FWHM>500 km s −1) in the ionized gas tracers detected out to∼5 kpc from the
nucleus. The molecular gas tracers are detected out to∼2 kpc, and exhibit a distinct “X”-shaped morphology and different velocity fields from
the ionized gas, which we attribute to the higher concentration of molecular gas near the disk. We adopt a simple model to interpret the observed
multiphase gas kinematics and infer an outflow velocity of∼500 - 600 km s −1 ∼350 km s−1, and∼200 - 300 km s −1, in the ionized, warm and
cold molecular phase, respectively, with corresponding mass outflow rates of∼0.3−2.3,∼31, and∼38−240 M ⊙ yr−1. The cold molecular
phase dominates both the total mass outflow rate and the associated kinetic energy (∼2−8×10 42 erg s−1). ViaJWST/MIRI-MRSdetection of
the [Ne V] 14.3µm line, we identify, for the first time, a dust-obscured AGN in IRAS F01364-1042. The low inferred AGN bolometric luminosity
(1.2−1.8×10 43 erg s−1) suggests that the nuclear starburst alone, with a star formation rate of∼40−60 M ⊙ yr−1, can account for the energy
required to drive the outflow, though a more active AGN phase in the recent past may have also played a role.
Conclusions.Our work showcases the necessity of multi-wavelength observations in interpreting the gas dynamics in merger-driven dusty star-
bursts, and the capability ofJWST/MIRI-MRSin uncovering obscured low-luminosity AGN that may be common in these systems.
Key words.Galaxies: ISM – Galaxies: nuclei – Galaxies: starburst – Galaxies: individual: IRASF01364-1042
1. Introduction
Gas-rich galaxy mergers represent a crucial transformative phase
in the formation and evolution of massive galaxies. Simulations
have predicted that nuclear gas inflows due to strong gravita-
tional torque during a merger would trigger both a powerful
nuclear starburst and accretion onto the central supermassive
black hole (SMBH) (e.g., Di Matteo et al. 2005; Springel et al.
2005a; Hopkins et al. 2006). During this phase, rapid, simul-
taneous growth of the galaxy’s stellar content and the SMBH
takes place behind thick layers of dust and gas (e.g., Blecha et al.
2018), until the energy released by the accreting SMBH (i.e.,
Active Galactic Nuclei, or AGN) disperses the obscuring ma-
terials, revealing the bright and powerful central engine as a
quasar (Springel et al. 2005b; Hopkins et al. 2005). This clear-
ing process, so called “AGN feedback", in the form of winds
and outflows, is thought to be responsible for shutting down

galaxy star formation (SF) and further SMBH growth, which
eventually leads to the formation of massive quiescent galaxies
(e.g., Springel et al. 2005b; Khalatyan et al. 2008; Hopkins et al.
2013). Throughout this transformation, the SMBH and its
host galaxy would evolve in tandem (e.g., Silk & Rees 1998;
Kauffmann & Haehnelt 2000; Wyithe & Loeb 2003), which may
explain the observed cut-offat the bright end of the local galaxy
luminosity distribution (e.g., Bower et al. 2006), and the widely
observed tight relations between the masses of SMBHs and var-
ious properties of their host galaxies (i.e., stellar velocity disper-
sion, bulge mass, and luminosity; e.g., Magorrian et al. 1998;
Ferrarese & Merritt 2000; McConnell & Ma 2013).
Luminous and Ultra-luminous Infra-Red Galaxies (LIRGs:
LIR[8−1000µm] >10 11−12L⊙; ULIRGs:L IR[8−1000µm] >10 12L⊙) in
the local Universe (z<0.1) provide the best laboratories to study
in detail the fueling and feedback processes that are associated
with the above evolutionary scenario. Predominantly triggered
by interactions/mergers between gas-rich spirals (see review by
Sanders & Mirabel 1996), these systems are known to host ex-

tremely compact (r≲100 pc) nuclear starbursts that reach
above 1000M ⊙ yr−1 kpc−2 in star formation rate (SFR) surface
density (Σ SFR; e.g., Condon et al. 1991; Barcos-Muñoz et al.
2017; U et al. 2019; Larson et al. 2020; Song et al. 2022). In
many cases, these starbursts are accompanied by powerful, but
heavily-obscured AGN (N H >10 23 cm−2) that are detected
only in the radio, mid-IR and hard X-rays (e.g., Iwasawa et al.
2011; Stierwalt et al. 2013; Petric et al. 2011; Vardoulaki et al.
2015a; Inami et al. 2018; Torres-Albà et al. 2018; Ricci et al.
2021), and can even reach quasar luminosity (Boksenberg et al.
1977; Sanders et al. 1988; Rupke & Veilleux 2013). These ex-
treme nuclear activities also drive powerful outflows that have
been observed ubiquitously in local U/LIRGs across different
ISM phases and physical scales (e.g., Heckman et al. 1990;
Rupke et al. 2005b; Veilleux et al. 2005, 2013; Spoon et al.
2013; Cicone et al. 2014; Hill & Zakamska 2014; Cazzoli et al.
2016; Barcos-Muñoz et al. 2018; Pereira-Santaella et al. 2018;
U et al. 2019; Lutz et al. 2020; Fluetsch et al. 2021; Perna et al.
2021; Lamperti et al. 2022), manifesting signs of rapid galac-
tic scale transformation as predicted by theoretical works. How-
ever, many studies associate these outflows with the nuclear
starburst rather than AGN activity (e.g. Rupke et al. 2005a;
Lamperti et al. 2022), the role of which remains challenging to
quantify as their signatures are often attenuated by the heavy nu-
clear dust extinction.
The supreme spatial resolution and sensitivity of theJames
Webb Space Telescope(JWST; Gardner et al. 2023) have en-
abled a new, detailed view of the multi-phase ISM in local
U/LIRGs, providing low-extinction and spatially-resolved diag-
nostics on the properties of the interstellar dust, ionized, hot
(T∼1000−3000 K) and warm (100≲T≲1000 K) molec-
ular gas around their obscured nuclei on sub-kpc scales, as has
been successfully demonstrated with the Early Science Release
program 1328 (PI: L. Armus, A. Evans; e.g., Lai et al. 2022;
U et al. 2022; Lai et al. 2023; Armus et al. 2023; Rich et al.
2023; Bianchin et al. 2024; Buiten et al. 2024; Bohn et al. 2024).
To this end, we have obtained newJWST/MIRI-MRSobserva-
tions in Cycle-1 (1717; PI: V . U) to study the ISM proper-
ties in seven local U/LIRGs from the Great Observatories All-
sky LIRG Survey (GOALS; Armus et al. 2009) selected based
on their extraordinarily bright Near- and Mid-IR (NIR and
MIR) H 2 emission. These galaxies were first highlighted by
Stierwalt et al. (2013, 2014) based on their high H 2/PAH lumi-
nosity ratios measured withSpitzer/IRSon>kpc scales, which
were attributed to enhanced rotational H 2 emission from either
AGN heating or shocked warm molecular gas, rather than weak
PAH emission that traces SF. Observations with Keck/OSIRIS
similarly revealed enhanced NIR ro-vibrational H 2 relative to
Brγemission in the immediate vicinity of these galactic nu-
clei at spatial resolutions of≲100 pc (e.g. Medling et al. 2014;
U et al. 2019), pointing to nuclear activity as a key driver be-
hind the enhanced H2 emission. These newJWST/MIRI-MRSob-
servations, further complemented by ancillary multi-wavelength
IFU datasets from Keck, VLT and ALMA, allow comprehen-
sive multiphase diagnostics on the nuclear power source and
putative outflows in these bright H 2-emitters at matched, sub-
kpc scales. Several case studies utilizing these datasets have
been presented by U et al. (2022), Bianchin et al. (2024) and
Kader et al. (2026), who identified powerful multi-phase out-
flows driven by AGN photo-ionization and/or radio jets in the
Seyfert galaxies NGC 7469 and VV 340a.
In this work we focus on a starburst-dominated galaxy from
the sample, the late-stage galaxy merger and LIRG (L IR =
1011.87 L⊙; Armus et al. 2009), IRASF01364−1042 (hereafter as
IRASF01364), shown in Figure 1. As noted by Stierwalt et al.
(2014), IRASF01364 shows distinctly highL H2 /LPAH and
LH2 /LIR ratios among galaxies in the GOALS sample, making
it a promising target to search for shocked molecular outflows
at higher spatial resolution. While its compactness and high lu-
minosity surface density in the radio suggest an AGN presence
(Vardoulaki et al. 2015b; Barcos-Muñoz et al. 2017; Song et al.
2022), observations in the mid-IR (MIR), X-ray and optical all
indicate starburst activity as the dominant source of its bolo-
metric luminosity (Stierwalt et al. 2013; Veilleux et al. 1995;
Yuan et al. 2010; Iwasawa et al. 2011; Díaz-Santos et al. 2017).
Interpretation of these results is complicated by the relatively
low spatial/spectral resolution of the datasets, as well as high
dust content in the source, as evidenced by its high IR/UV ra-
tio (∼250; Howell et al. 2010) and deep 9.7µm silicate absorp-
tion feature (s9.7 =−1.27; Stierwalt et al. 2013). High-resolution
ground-based NIR IFU observations reveal a 200 pc-radius rotat-
ing nuclear gas disk in ro-vibrational H2 and Paαemission, with
the former showing turbulent motion beyond the nuclear disk
that potential traces a nuclear molecular outflow (Medling et al.
2014, 2015; U et al. 2019).
In this work we report the first confirmation and characteri-
zation of a kpc-scale multi-phase biconical outflow and a dust-
obscured AGN in IRASF01364, combining new sensitive IFU
observations fromJWST/MIRI-MRS, Keck/KCWI and ALMA.
The article is organized as follows: In Section 2 we present
the observations utilized in this work and the data reduction
procedures. We describe the analyses performed on the multi-
wavelength datasets in Section 3 and present the key results in
Section 4 where we identify the kinematic and morphological
signature of the outflow, as well as characterize the spatially-
resolved properties of the dust and multi-phase gas in the system.
We combine these results to infer the energetics of the newly-
confirmed outflow and its origin in Section 5, in the context of
similar galactic outflows reported in the literature. We summa-
rize the main findings in Section 6.
Throughout this article, we adopt cosmological parameters
H0 =70 km s −1Mpc−1,Ω vaccum =0.72 andΩ matter =0.28
based on the 5 yr Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe result
(Hinshaw et al. 2009) and the three-attractor model (Mould et al.
2000). IRASF01364 has a redshift ofz=0.04819 determined
from optical stellar absorption features (see Section 2.4), corre-
sponding to a luminosity distance ofDL =214 Mpc, with a scale
of 945 pc/′′.

2.2. JWST/MIRI-MRS
Mid-infrared IFU observations of IRASF01364 were conducted
with the JWST Mid-InfraRed Instrument (MIRI; Rieke et al.
2015) in Medium Resolution Spectroscopy (MRS) mode (here-
after asMRS; Wells et al. 2015) on August 23, 2023, as part of
Cycle-1 GO Program 1717 (PI: V . U). Exposures across the short
(A), medium (B), and long (C) sub-bands of each of the four
wavelength channels resulted in observations that cover the full
4.9 – 28.8µm range. The SLOWR1 readout pattern and standard
4-point dither pattern were adopted in each sub-band observation
of the science target, with an integration time of 800 s per expo-
sure. Dedicated background exposures were taken within each
of the three sub-bands using a 2-point dither pattern.
The uncalibrated frames were downloaded from MAST
and reduced using the JWST Science Calibration Pipeline
version 1.18.0 (Bushouse et al. 2023) and CRDS context of
jwst_1364.pmap. We utilized the official MIRI MRS Pipeline
Notebook (Law et al. 2025) 3, which includes steps to apply
detector-level corrections, outlier/cosmic-ray shower detection,
wavelength calibration, flux calibration, background subtraction,

2.4. Keck/KCWI
To complement theMRSobservation of the central re-
gion of IRASF01364, we further obtained new optical wide-
field IFU observations with the Keck Cosmic Wave Imager
(Morrissey et al. 2018) on the Keck II telescope (2023B_K454;
PI: R. McGurk). The observations were carried out on Septem-
ber 17, 2023 under clear sky conditions with seeing of 0′′.9 using
a two-point dither pattern with a dedicated off-target sky frame.
The BH1, BH3, RH1 and RH2 gratings were adopted to cover
wavelength ranges of 3500–4100, 4700–5600, 5900-6485 and
6592-7297 Å, respectively, with a high spectral resolution of
R∼3500 and∼4500, for RH and BH gratings, respectively.
Utilizing the large IFU slicer, we achieve a FoV of 33 ′′ ×20 ′′.4
per pointing with slit width-limited angular resolution (0′′.3 sam-

binned by a factor of 2×2. Basic reduction and wavelength solu-

3.1.1. Spectral decomposition & PAH fitting withCAFE
To conduct an initial assessment of the MIR spectral prop-
erties of IRASF01364, we utilize the spectral fitting tool
CAFE8(Marshall et al. 2007; Diaz-Santos et al. 2025). The pro-
gram performs extraction and stitching of spectra fromMRS
channel cubes, using the longest-wavelength cube as reference,
as well as decomposition of the stitched full spectrum into multi-
temperature continuum, emission lines, and PAH components.
Designed to allow flexible modeling of the complex and often
blended PAH profiles, the program also computes wavelength-
dependent attenuation (e.g. Lai et al. 2024) due to absorptions
from silicates, water ice and hydrogenated amorphous carbon
for estimating the intrinsic fluxes of individual PAH features.
As the MIR emission of IRASF01364 is dominated by the
nuclear starburst, we first focus on analyzing the nuclear spec-
trum across allMRSchannels extracted withCAFE. To iso-
late emission from the nuclear starburst, which spans∼0 ′′.4
in the 334 GHz continuum (see Figure 2), we adopt a coni-
cal aperture centered on the 334 GHz emission peak (i.e., RA=
24.72029 deg, DEC=−10.45324 deg) with a radius of 0 ′′.2 at
5.0µm and 1 ′′.0 at 25.0µm, to account for the increasingMRS
PSF with wavelength (see Section 2.2). As the chosen aperture
size is twice the PSF FWHM at any given wavelength, no aper-
ture correction was performed. The aperture sizes and the ex-
tracted spectrum, along with the results of spectral decomposi-
tion withCAFEare shown in Figure 2. We later refer to this spec-
trum as the “nuclear” spectrum. The observed and attenuation-
corrected fluxes of the PAH features obtained fromCAFEare
listed in Table B.1.
In Figure 2, we also show, in cyan, another spectrum ex-
tracted with a cylindrical aperture with a fixed radius ofr=1 ′′.5.
This is chosen to capture the total MIR emission from the cen-
tral 3 kpc, which is the largest area covered by allMRSchannels.
This spectrum closely matches that obtained withSpitzer/IRS
with a slit size of∼10 ′′ ×4 ′′ (Stierwalt et al. 2013, 2014), shown
in grey, confirming that the bulk of the mid-IR emission from
the system is concentrated in the central few kpc. We later refer
to the area defined by this cylindrical aperture as the “central"
region or region C, for which we also extract profiles of the sub-
mm CO lines from ALMA datasets (Section 3.2) and the optical
emission lines from Keck/KCWI cubes (Section 3.3) to perform
a direct comparison between multi-phase gas tracers observed
within the central∼3 kpc of the galaxy. The PAH fluxes ob-
tained withCAFEfrom this central region are also reported in
Table B.1.
3.1.2. Multi-component fitting of emission lines
AsCAFEis designed to model the full MIR spectrum and PAH
features rather than individual emission lines (Marshall et al.
2007), we further perform more detailed multi-component Gaus-
sian fitting withlmfit 9 (Newville et al. 2025) of each emis-
sion line to more robustly characterize their flux and kinemat-
ics. For each line, we first model and subtract the local contin-
uum in adjacent line-free regions between 0.02 to 0.06µm away
from the expected central wavelength, using a third-degree Leg-
endre polynomial. Several atomic lines exhibit double-peaked
8 https://github.com/GOALS-survey/CAFE
9 https://lmfit.github.io/lmfit-py
profiles and broad line wings, indicating the presence of mul-
tiple kinematic components. Hence, we model the continuum-
subtracted line profile three times, with one, two and three Gaus-
sian components, respectively. Each component is restricted to
have a central velocity within [-500, 500] km s−1 of the line cen-
ter, and a linewidth larger than the instrumental linewidth at the
observed wavelength (see Section 2.2). We adopt the default
Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm for the least-square minimiza-
tion, accounting for the flux uncertainties associated with the
spectrum by providing its reciprocal as weights to the residual.
We then choose among the three different models the one with
the lowest value of the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) as
the best-fit that sufficiently characterizes the line profile with-
out overfitting. We show the fitting results for Huδ, H2 0-0 S(2)
and [Ne III] 15.6µm in Appendix C as examples to illustrate the
model selection process.

3.3. KCWI
To properly recover the flux distribution and kinematics of the
emission lines detected in each KCWI cube over the large FoV
(e.g., Figure 3), we perform simultaneous modeling of the emis-
sion line profile and the underlying stellar continuum at each
spaxel withppxf. This is done in two stages: First, V oronoi bin-
ning is performed on each cube so that each bin reaches SNR
of 60 for the brightest line detected within the wavelength range
covered by the cube. We then fit the stellar spectrum at each bin
after masking the emission lines. Afterwards, we repeat the fit-

where the former is set by rescaling the best-fit stellar template
derived for the corresponding V oronoi bin in the first stage, and
the latter is modeled by a single Gaussian. At this stage, we set
the initial guess for the gas velocities to the stellar velocity de-
rived at each spaxel, and tie the kinematics of emission lines
together to further reduce degeneracy, given that some lines are
blended (e.g., [O II], [S II] doublets). In Figure 8 we show the
stellar velocity field derived from the BH1L cube where the stel-
lar features are most prominent. In Figure 9, we show the emis-
sion line maps for [O III]λ5007, which best showcase the large-
scale kinematics of the extended ionized gas emission detected
out to∼10 kpc from the nucleus.

4. Results
4.1. A Multi-phase Biconical Outflow
Despite the different gas phases and spatial scales probed by
our multi-wavelength datasets, the emission line maps separately
presented in Figure 5, 7 and 9 all exhibit consistent, prominent
linewidth enhancement along the SE-NW direction, which is
the kinematic minor axis of the stellar rotation shown in Figure
8. We model the observed stellar velocity field using 3DBarolo
(Di Teodoro & Fraternali 2015), and overlay the best-fit results
in Figure 8, which yield disk inclination angle ofi∼60 ◦ and
position angle PA∼220 ◦. This indicates a highly-inclined stellar
disk with a minor axis along SE-NW direction, which may also
produce broadened linewidths in the gas tracers due to the large
velocity gradient along the line-of-sight. However, the spectral
analyses presented in Figure 6 and 9 reveal that the linewidth
enhancement arises from the superposition of at least two dis-
tinct kinematic components at and beyond the nucleus. In par-
ticular, Figure 9 shows that, in regions SE and NW, both the
[O III]λ5007 and Hβlines exhibit a prominent, broad component
with FWHM of≳700 km s −1 (see Table D.4), indicating highly
turbulent ionized gas motion at∼5 kpc away from the nucleus
and beyond the bulk of the stellar content. This, combined with
the biconical shape of the linewidth-enhanced regions from the
multi-wavelength emission line maps, signifies the presence of a
multi-phase biconical outflow along the SE-NW direction.
In the left panel of Figure 10, we show the line profiles of
several gas tracers extracted from region C, which is covered
by all our datasets. Similar to what is seen in the nuclearMRS
spectrum (Figure 4), broad line wings indicative of outflowing
gas are present in most ionized gas tracers but not the molecu-
lar ones, and the high-ionization [Ne V] 14µm line shows a dis-
tinct, red-shifted profile. These differences imply that they do not
necessarily trace the same kinematic structure(s), despite being
measured over the same spatial scale. We leave a more in-depth
discussion of the origins of the observed line profiles in the cen-
tral region to Section 5, where we further infer the energetics of
the multi-phase gas involved in the biconical outflow.
In the central panel of Figure 10 we visualize the morphol-
ogy of the multi-phase gas derived from the multi-wavelength
datasets. The extended [O III]λ5007 emission unambiguously
traces the ionized bicone along the SE-NW direction beyond the
bulk of the stellar emission probed byHST. The warm and cold
molecular gas traced by H 2 and CO both exhibit “X”-shaped
morphology and outline the inner edges of the ionized bicone.
Given that ALMA observations have a much larger FoV than
those fromMRS, the close spatial correspondence between H 2
and CO indicates that molecular gas is mostly concentrated at
the base of the bicone, similar to what was found in the outflows
of nearby starbursts (e.g., Leroy et al. 2015; Beirão et al. 2015;
Bolatto et al. 2021). The brighter and more extended molecular
emission in the NW indicates that it occupies the near-side. This
is supported by the line profiles presented in Figure 6 and 9,
where the ionized gas tracers consistently present blue-shifted
broad wings in region 8 and NW, and red-shifted ones in region
2 and SE. We provide a sketch of the inferred outflow configu-
ration in the right panel of Figure 10, where we also highlight
the fact that dust is present in the bicone based on the promi-
nent obscuration along the NW cone observed in theHSTdata
shown in Figure 1. In the following Sections we present key
results drawn from our analyses in Section 3 to characterize
the spatially-resolved and integrated multi-phase ISM proper-
ties across IRASF01364 in the context of this newly discovered
galactic outflow.
4.2. Dust Extinction
The non-uniform and extended distribution of dust and heavy
nuclear obscuration are among the most defining features of the
optical morphology of IRASF01364, as shown in Figure 1. We
quantify the spatially-resolved variation in visual extinction us-
ing the Hαand Hβline flux measured with the KCWI dataset
(Table D.5) across the regions defined in Section 3.3 and shown
in Figure 9. Following Fluetsch et al. (2021), we adopt the ex-
tinction law for actively star-forming galaxies from Calzetti et al.
(2000) (i.e.,R V =4.05), and assume the intrinsic Hα/Hβratio of
2.86 for Case B recombination with temperature ofT=10 4 K
(Hummer & Storey 1987).
The resulting values are highest in region SW, covering a
dusty tidal tail of the merger, withA V ∼3.0. Regions SE and C
have consistentA V ∼2.1−2.4, while regions NW and exN have
significantly lower values, withA V ∼0.75−0.8. Region exS has
a high derivedA V of 2.67±0.40, but the faint and highly blended
Hαand [N II] doublet emission here may have biased the mea-
sured line ratio. These values further lend support to the picture
presented Figure 10 where the NW cone lies on the near-side,
and the SE cone on the far-side. The latter is much fainter due to
heavier obscuration by the disk and/or other foreground materi-
als associated with the tidal tail (Fluetsch et al. 2021). The high
extinction in the SW region likely comes from a combination of
pre-existing dusty gas in the tidal tail and partial obscuration by
dust along the edges of the bicone.

This result is consistent with the nuclear dust extinction derived
by U et al. (2019) at 2.2µm using Brγ/Brδline ratios, which

impact the derived optical depths. Hence, we adopt the values de-
rived froms 9.7 as the main reference for the nuclear extinction,
as this quantity is measured directly from the observed spec-

galaxy. Lastly, we highlight that theA V derived from the KCWI
spectrum of region C is more than 30 times lower, demonstrat-
ing the limitation of optical lines in probing the heavy nuclear
obscuration in local LIRGs.
4.3. Detection of high IP lines
As shown in Figure 4 and 10, we detect the [Ne V] 14µm line
with IP=97 eV in both the nuclear and centralMRSspectra, as
well as [O IV] 26µm with IP=55 eV (see Table D.1). This marks
the first detections of high IP (>50 eV) lines in IRASF01364.
Previous non-detections withSpitzer/IRS(Petric et al. 2011;
Inami et al. 2013) can likely be explained by a combination of
spatial and spectral dilution, as well as limited sensitivity. As star
formation does not provide sufficient energy to quadruply ionize
Ne, the detection of [Ne V] on kpc-scales is generally considered
as a strong evidence for AGN activity (e.g. Satyapal et al. 2007;
Petric et al. 2011; Satyapal et al. 2021; Bierschenk et al. 2024).
However, the [Ne V]/[Ne II] and [O IV]/[Ne II] ratios measured
from theMRSspectra are low, at∼0.005 and∼0.2, re-
spectively, which fall in the range previously measured among
starburst-dominated local LIRGs withSpitzer/IRS(Petric et al.
2011; Inami et al. 2013; Pereira-Santaella et al. 2010). This in-
dicates that the AGN does not contribute significantly to the
ionized gas excitation at the nucleus, which is consistent with
the low AGN fraction (∼7%) derived from multi-wavelength
nuclear SED fitting by Gao et al. (2025). We further discuss
the properties of the putative AGN in Section 5.3 based on the
[Ne V] 14µm detection.
We do not detect the [Ne V] 24µm line, and estimate a
flux upper-limit of 5×10 −19 W m−2 from three times the

4.4. PAH Equivalent Width and Band Ratios
Broad emission features between 3 - 18µm from PAH molecules
mark the most common trait of MIR spectra of star-forming
galaxies (Smith et al. 2007). These molecules trace small dust
grains in the photodissociation regions (PDR; Bakes & Tielens
1994) and are sensitive probes of the radiation field and grain
properties (e.g. Draine et al. 2021). In particular, the equivalent
width (EW) of PAH features are observed to be lower in AGN
environments (e.g., EW(PAH 6.2)<0.27µm; Stierwalt et al.
2013; Inami et al. 2018), due to dilution by the strong AGN
continuum, and/or grain destruction by the harsh radiation
field (Smith et al. 2007; Diamond-Stanic & Rieke 2010). As the
3.3µm feature is not covered in ourMRSdataset, we follow
Stierwalt et al. (2013) and measure the apparent EW of the
6.2µm PAH feature from the nuclearMRSspectrum, and from
regions 5 and region C, using a spline fit of the local continuum.
This yields a consistent value∼0.29−0.33µm across these dif-
ferent apertures, which is slightly lower than previous measure-
ment withSpitzer/IRS(i.e., 0.39µm) and still puts the nucleus
among the class of composite sources (i.e., 0.27<EW(PAH 6.2)
<0.54µm), consistent with the scenario of a non-dominant AGN
(see also Section 4.3).
PAH features are also detected across region 1 - 9, suggesting
the potential presence of small dust grains outside the nucleus
and in the outflow, which have been observed in several nearby
galactic outflows (Sutter et al. 2025; Lopez et al. 2026) and pre-
dicted by simulation of multiphase outflow (Richie & Schneider
2026). In Figure 11 we show the PAH band flux ratios between
6.2 and 7.7µm, and those between 11.3 and 7.7µm, derived from
CAFEfitting of theMRSspectra extracted from region 1 - 9 (see
Appendix B). Region 3 and 7 are excluded as the 6.2 and 7.7µm
features are poorly constrained. Following Lai et al. (2022), we
overlay theoretical tracks from Draine et al. (2021), which illus-
trate the utility of the 11.3/7.7 and 6.2/7.7 ratios as tracers of
grain ionization state and size, respectively. While the 6.2/7.7 ra-
tios for the extranuclear regions (in black) are roughly consistent
within uncertainties between the observed (in triangles) and in-
trinsic (i.e., attenuation-corrected; in crosses) measurements, the
former show significantly reduced 11.3/7.7 ratios indicating a
higher fraction of ionized grains compared to the latter. This dif-
ference is even more prominent at the nucleus (i.e., region 5, in
red). This is attributed to the large optical depth near the 11.3µm
band due to the deep 9.8µm silicate absorption feature (Section
4.2). Hence, properly accounting for the effect of MIR attenua-
tion is crucial for understanding dust grain properties in heavily-
obscured centers of local LIRGs (Lai et al. 2024; Donnelly et al.
2025).

4.6.2. Warm molecular gas
We use the nine rotational transitions of H 2 detected in the
MRSspectrum to constrain the temperature and mass of the
warm (≳100 K) molecular gas within region C, by adopting
the continuous power-law temperature model described in
Togi & Smith (2016). The model is characterized by the lower
and upper temperature limits (T l,T u) and the index (n) of the
power-law distribution, and allows a smooth extrapolation
between the transitions to recover the total molecular gas mass
down to≲100 K. In Figure 14, we show the best-fit model

Tl ≃243±3Kandn=5.36±0.02. The spectral index is in
the range of values derived for nearby star-forming galaxies
by Togi & Smith (2016), but somewhat steeper than previously
derived in local LIRGs on global scales (Zakamska 2010;
Pereira-Santaella et al. 2014), suggesting a relatively higher
contribution from colder gas components in IRASF01364.
However, these values are similar to those derived by Bohn et al.
(2024) in the resolved nuclear outflows of NGC 3256. We
note that using observed values without correcting for MIR
attenuation (shown in triangles) does not affect the derived
values, as the correction only affects the H 2 0-0 S(3) line, as
shown in Figure 14.
For comparison, we also show measurements from the
nuclear conical aperture (circles) and region 5 (squares), which
respectively show excess and deficit in emission from warmer
components, particularly those traced by the S(6) to S(9) lines,
compared to region C. This indicates that the warmer molecular
gas components are mostly concentrated at the innermost area
encompassed by region 5 and the short-wavelength portion of
the nuclear conical aperture, while the colder components are
more diffuse and fill the central few kpcs covered by region
C. Accounting for the heavy element correction factor of 1.36,
the best-fit model shown in Figure 14 yields a total warm
(T≳240 K) molecular gas ofM H2,warm ≃3.4×10 8 M⊙ and
column density ofN H2,warm ≃3.5×10 21 cm−2 in region C.

5. Discussion
5.1. Identifying the kinematic components of the outflow
As mentioned in Section 4.1, the diversity and complexity of line
profiles of multi-phase gas tracers observed in region C (Fig-
ure 10) indicates that they may originate from different kine-
matic features. This is also hinted at in Figure 5 where we ob-
serve different morphologies and velocity gradients between the
ionized gas traced by the MIR neon lines and warm molecu-
lar gas traced by the H 2 emission. In this Section we attempt
to provide a coherent picture to interpret the various line pro-
files observed across the different gas phases in the central 3 kpc
of IRASF01364, in the context of the newly uncovered multi-
phase outflow (see Section 4.1) and similar biconical outflows
described in the literature.
As highlighted in Figure 10, nearly all multi-wavelength
gas tracers in the spectrum of region C feature a simi-
lar double-peaked line profile. These profiles have also been
commonly observed in biconical outflows of nearby star-
burst galaxies (e.g., M 82, NGC 253, NGC 2992; Heckman et al.
1990; Shopbell & Bland-Hawthorn 1998; Veilleux et al. 2001;
Leroy et al. 2015), and are attributed to the superposition of the
nuclear disk and/or walls of the bicone along a tilted line-of-sight
(LOS). To interpret the potential origins of the observed multiple
kinematic components, in Figure 15 we provide a modified ver-
sion of the schematic presented by Heckman et al. (1990), based

on the outflow configuration inferred previously in Section 4.1
and focusing on the LOS towards regions 8, 5 and 2 (hereafter
as R-8, R-5 and R-2) along the bicone.
5.1.1. Outflow velocities
As discussed in Heckman et al. (1990) and illustrated in Figure
15, gas flowing along the front and back walls of a biconical
outflow from a inclined disk would naturally produce two dis-
tinct narrow kinematic components along the LOS, at red-shifted
and blue-shifted projected velocities, respectively. For example,
along R-8 through the near-side NW cone, we would observe
two velocity components coming from the front and back wall,
respectively, atv NF =v 1 cos(i−ϕ) (NF - “near-side, front wall”)
andv N B =v 2 cos(i+ϕ) (NB - “near-side, back wall”), wherev 1
andv 2 are the intrinsic velocities at the observed location,iis the
inclination of the nuclear disk, andϕis the half opening angle of
the near-side cone. Similarly, we may observe two components
atv FF =v 3 cos(i+ϕ) (FF - “far-front”) andv FB =v 4 cos(i−ϕ)
(FB - “far-back”) for the far-side SE cone at R-2, in the simplis-
tic case that the bicone is perfectly symmetrical.
Now focusing on the observed MIR neon line profiles (see
Figure 6), if we assume that the two narrow components ob-
served in R-8 and R-2 originate from outflowing gas along
the walls, from Table D.2 we then have|v NF | ≃60 km s −1,
|vN B| ≃150 km s −1,|v FF | ≃10 km s −1 and|v FB | ≃180 km s −1.
We estimateϕ∼40−50 ◦ from the FWHM linewidth map
of [O III]λ5007 (Figure 9) based on the opening angle of the
area with FWHM≳500 km s −1, and adopti∼60 ◦ derived

5.1.2. Molecular vs. ionized gas
As already highlighted in previous Sections, broad wings char-
acterized by a FWHM linewidth of>500 km s −1 are seen con-
sistently in both MIR neon lines in regions 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 (see also
Table D.2) along the outflow direction, with the broadest compo-
nent measured at the nucleus, reaching FWHM∼1000 km s −1 in
both region 5 and the nuclear aperture (see Table D.1). On larger
scales, shown in Figure 10, the ionized gas emission traced by
[O III]λ5007 and Hβin regions NW and SE along the bicone
are completely dominated by a broad component with FWHM
∼300−500 km s −1. In all these regions, the broad component ex-
hibits consistent blue-shifted velocities towards the NW and red-
shifted velocities towards SE, which we interpret as the presence
of outflowing ionized gas in between the walls of the bicone.
As illustrated in Figure 15, outflowing materials traveling at
a range of velocity inside the bicone would produce a broad com-
ponent that is dominantly blue-shifted in the near-side cone seen
at R-8 and region NW and red-shifted in the far-side cone seen
at R-2 and region SE. Along a LOS closer to the nucleus (e.g.,
R-5 or region C), the superposition of materials flowing in oppo-
site directions would produce a broad component near systemic
velocity. This picture is consistent with what we observe in the
ionized gas tracers, except for the [Ne V] 14µm line, which we
discuss later in Section 5.1.3.
On the other hand, this broad component is largely absent
in the molecular gas tracers, where the broadest component with
FWHM of∼300−500 km s −1 is detected only in CO (2-1) at R-8
and marginally at R-2. As discussed in Section 5.1.1, this likely
indicates a slower outflow in the molecular gas phase, whose
line profiles are not easily distinguishable from that of the disk.

5.1.3. Kinematics of the [Ne V] 14µm line
As shown in Figure 4 and 10, unlike other ionized gas tracers,
the [Ne V] 14µm line tracing the most highly-ionized gas only
exhibits a single red-shifted narrow component at∼180 km s −1
with FWHM∼300 km s −1. Similar profiles have been observed
in optical studies of high IP lines in nearby AGN hosting radio
jet (e.g., Rodríguez-Ardila et al. 2006, 2017), where the highly-
ionized gas traces a dense energetic outflow and is driven me-
chanically by the jet. Extended coronal line emission associ-
ated with such jet-driven outflows have also been observed in
[Ne V] with MRS in local Seyferts and quasars (Davies et al.
2024; Kader et al. 2026; Bianchin et al. 2026). While VLA ob-
servations at 15 and 33 GHz do not show evidence of a radio jet
down to∼100 pc scales (Barcos-Muñoz et al. 2017; Song et al.
2022), we cannot rule out the possibility of a small-scale one-
sided jet (e.g., Mezcua & Prieto 2014) driving the observed red-
shifted emission of [Ne V] 14µm. Alternatively, this component
may trace highly-excited gas emanating from the inner NB wall
of the biconical outflow, given that it shares similar velocity as
the red-shifted narrow components seen in the other ionized gas
tracers with lower IP. The∼1.6 times higher [Ne V] 14µm line
flux measured from region C compared to that from the nu-
clear spectrum (Table D.1) potentially indicates that the [Ne V]
emission is extended. However, assuming the emission is un-
resolved and consideringMRSaperture correction factor (1.13
from Argyriou et al. (2023)) given the aperture sizes specified
in Section 3.1, the evidence for extended [Ne V] emission is
marginal. A more detailed search and characterization of other
tracers with similarly high IPs would be required to investigate
the kinematics of the highly-ionized gas, which we defer to fu-
ture works.
5.1.4. Potential contributions from the merger
Given the merger nature of IRASF01364, one may also question
whether tidal effects play a role in driving the observed gas kine-
matics. For an example, the distinctly different velocity fields
observed between the ionized and molecular may also result
from disk counter-rotation/misalignment triggered by the merger
(e.g., Lu et al. 2021). However, we highlight that the disk incli-
nation and position angle derived from the stellar velocity field
shown in Figure 10 are in agreement with those derived from
previous 10 pc-scale observations of the nuclear ionized and
molecular gas disks of IRASF01364 in Paα, rovibrational H 2,
and CO(2-1) lines by Medling et al. (2015) and Agostino et al.
(2026). Hence, there is no evidence of strong disk misalignment
among stars, ionized and molecular gas.
Nevertheless, the effect of merger is clearly present, which
manifests in the extended molecular loop and tail observed to-
wards the NE and SW direction respectively in the CO (2-1)
maps shown in Figure 7. The narrow, double-peaked profiles of
[O III]λ5007 and Hβin the NE and SW regions along the ma-
jor axis of the stellar disk shown in Figure 9 likely result from
overlapping emission between the disk and tidal features. How-
ever, given the consistent disk rotation observed in this work
and previous high-resolution studies of the nuclear disk (i.e.,
Medling et al. 2015; Agostino et al. 2026), as well as broad-
ened line profiles along the outflow direction observed across the
multi-phase tracers, we argue that the multi-phase kinematics of
the central 3 kpc of the system focused in this work are likely
dominated by disk rotation and the outflow rather than tidal fea-
tures.

5.4. Star formation rate of the nuclear starburst
Due to the relative faintness of MIR hydrogen recombi-
nation lines, many studies have explored and advocated
for the usage of other bright MIR features as extinction-
free tracers of star formation rates (SFR), such as the
[Ne II] 12.8µm and [Ne III] 15.6µm lines (e.g. Ho & Keto
2007; Inami et al. 2013; Zhuang et al. 2019; Whitcomb et al.
2020) and PAH features (e.g. Shipley et al. 2016; Xie & Ho
2019; Hernán-Caballero et al. 2020).
From the observed line flux measurements from region C
reported in Table D.1, we derive a total SFR of∼8−10 M ⊙ yr−1
from [Ne II] and [Ne III], accounting for contribution from
the AGN using the [Ne V] 14.3µm line, following calibrations
provided in (Zhuang et al. 2019). The values derived from

The general agreement between values derived from PAH

have played a role in reducing the PAH emission via destruction
of small grains (Zhang et al. 2022; García-Bernete et al. 2024).
Meanwhile, since both hydrogen recombination lines and
neon lines trace the ionizing photon rates (Ho & Keto 2007;
Murphy et al. 2011), the relatively lower values derived from the
former are likely due to their faint emission blending partially
with the underlying strong MIR continuum and PAH emission,
hence we consider the estimates from the much brighter neon
lines to be more reliable.
Previous studies of IRASF01364 based on high-resolution
(<0 ′′.1=90 pc) NIR Brγ(U et al. 2019) and radio continuum
observations (Barcos-Muñoz et al. 2017; Song et al. 2022) yield
nuclear SFR of 50 - 60 M ⊙ yr−1, which is about five times
higher than the estimates based on MIR neon lines. There are
several potential causes for the discrepancy: First, the MIR lines
may still suffer significant attenuation due to the heavy nuclear
obscuration in IRAS F01364. If we were to assume that the
wavelength-dependent attenuation derived fromCAFEapplies
equally to PAH and ionized gas, the inferred intrinsic SFR from
the neon lines would be∼30 M ⊙ yr−1, which approaches the
values reported in the literature. However, as we note in Section
3.1, it is unlikely that the PAH and ionized gas are subject to the
same attenuation.
Alternatively, as shown in a recent work by Robinson et al.
(2026) using results from radiative transfer modeling
(Efstathiou et al. 2022), SFR calibrations based on MIR
neon lines or PAH features measured in normal star-forming
galaxies consistently underestimate the SFR for local ULIRGs
by up to 1 dex, which is attributed to a distinct mode of star
formation in these more extreme systems. This is also observed
by Inami et al. (2013), who found that the neon line luminosities
strongly correlate with the total IR luminosities at low IR lumi-
nosities but show clear deviation towards high IR luminosities.
Applying the scaling relations derived by Robinson et al. (2026)
(their Eqn. 34), we obtain from the [Ne II] line flux, a SFR of
∼40−50 M ⊙ yr−1 for the nuclear starburst, which is similar
to values based on previous high-resolution NIR and radio
continuum observations. Given that the total SFR of the system
is∼130 M ⊙ (Howell et al. 2010), and the nucleus dominates
the MIR emission as shown in Figure 2, we hence adopt a
nuclear SFR∼40−60 ⊙ yr−1. A systematic, matched-resolution
comparison among multi-wavelength SFR tracers across a range
of nuclear environments in local LIRGs would further help
elucidate the origin of the discrepancy identified in this work
and the literature.
5.5. Connecting the outflow, AGN and nuclear starburst
As noted in Section 5.2, the total mass outflow rate and ki-
netic energy/power are dominated by those of the molecular
gas phase, which are highly dependent on the unconstrained
αCO factor. This hinders a direct comparison among the en-
ergetics of the outflow, starburst, and AGN to infer the ori-
gin and driving mechanisms of the outflow. Nevertheless, we
note that the outflow orientation along the galaxy minor axis
and the derived outflow velocities (Section 5.1) match what is
commonly observed in nearby starbursts (e.g. Leroy et al. 2015;
Bolatto et al. 2021; Lamperti et al. 2022). Adopting the SFR es-
timated from previous high-resolution NIR and radio continuum
observations of 40−60 M ⊙ yr−1, the kinetic power delivered by
supernovae (SNe) from the nuclear starburst would be ˙Ekin,SN =
7×10 41(SFR/M⊙ yr−1)∼4×10 43 erg s−1 (Veilleux et al. 2005),
which is about two times higher than the estimated bolomet-
ric luminosity of the obscured AGN from Section 5.3. In ad-
dition to the mechanical energy ejected by SNe, the dusty com-
pact starburst also provides ideal conditions for supplying strong
IR radiation pressure that is efficient in driving the massive
molecular outflow (e.g. Murray et al. 2005; Huang et al. 2020).
Indeed, given a starburst radius of∼140 pc estimated from
the radio continuum (Song et al. 2022), the inferred star forma-
tion rate surface density (Σ SFR) would be≳10 3 M⊙ yr−1/kpc2,
reaching the limit of radiation pressure supported maximal star-
burst (Barcos-Muñoz et al. 2017; Thompson et al. 2005). Thus,
the combined power from SNe and radiation pressure from the
optically thick starburst may be sufficient in driving the ob-
served outflow with a total kinetic energy of ˙Ekin = ˙Ekin,ion +
˙Ekin,warm H2 + ˙Ekin,cold H2 ∼2−8×10 42 erg s−1, without evok-
ing AGN contribution. The mass-loading factor, given by the ra-
tio between the total mass outflow rateM out (∼M out,warm H2 +
Mout,cold H2 ≃70−270 M ⊙ yr−1) and SFR, is about 1 - 5, which
is also similar to those previously estimated in other starbursts
(e.g., Leroy et al. 2015; Bolatto et al. 2013; Fluetsch et al. 2019).
On the other hand, as discussed by Cicone et al. (2014) and
Lutz et al. (2020), AGN may still play a significant role in driv-
ing massive molecular outflows in starburst-dominated systems
due to short-term AGN variability. Observational studies have
shown that AGN power can vary rapidly, i.e., “flicker”, over a
timescale of∼10 5 yr (e.g., Schawinski et al. 2007; Keel et al.
2012), which is much shorter than the timescale of outflows
and starbursts (>10 6 Myr; e.g., Zubovas & Nardini 2020). A
more active AGN in the past would also help explain the
high SMBH mass measured from previous 10-pc scale studies
(Medling et al. 2015; Agostino et al. 2026), given the low ac-
cretion level estimated in Section 5.3. In this scenario, AGN
winds driven by powerful AGN activity in the recent past
may have supplied significant energy to efficiently drive ma-
terials out of the disk. In addition, as predicted by simula-
tions by Richings & Faucher-Giguère (2018a,b) for AGN with
1044−47 erg s−1, some molecular gas may have also formed in-

6. Summary
We combine IFU observations fromJWST-MIRI/MRS,
Keck/KCWI and ALMA to study the multiphase ISM properties
in the late-stage merger and local LIRG IRAS F01364-1042.
This system was initially highlighted by Stierwalt et al. (2013)
due to its bright H 2 emission detected withSpitzer/IRS. The
new, spatially resolved multi-wavelength datasets allow us
to uncover and characterize, for the first time, a multi-phase
galactic biconical outflow and a dust-obscured AGN in this
starburst-dominated system. We summarize the main results as
follows:
1. The key tracers of the ionized, warm, and cold molecular gas
all show enhanced line width along the minor axis (SE-NW
direction) of an inclined rotating stellar disk. The region of
enhanced linewidths exhibits a clear biconical shape, signi-
fying the presence of a multi-phase biconical outflow extend-
ing at least 5 kpc beyond the nucleus. Dust is present in the
outflow, as visible from archivalHSTimages, and from the
derived visual extinction based on Hα/Hβratios measured
from the KCWI datasets, which also supports a picture where
the NW cone lies on the near-side, as inferred from the rela-
tively stronger gas emission towards the NW direction.
2. The multi-phase gas exhibits different line profiles, with the
ionized gas commonly showing a broad component with
FWHM>500 km s −1. We adopt a simplified kinematic
model based on similar biconical outflows studied in the lit-
erature to explain the observed line profiles, which we at-
tribute to the superposition of a gaseous nuclear disk and
outflowing gas along the bicone. The molecular gas is likely
more concentrated near the disk compared to the ionized gas,
producing the apparent perpendicular motion observed be-
tween two. Based on this model, the observed line profiles
and channel maps, we infer an outflow velocity of∼500 -
600 km s−1,∼350 km s −1, and∼200 - 300 km s −1 in the ion-
ized, warm, and cold molecular gas phase, respectively.
3. Focusing on measurements from the central∼3 kpc, we infer
∼40 % flux contribution from the outflow, and estimate the
multi-phase outflow energetics, which is heavily dominated
by the cold molecular gas phase, similar to outflows found
in other local LIRGs. We estimate a high cold molecular gas
mass outflow rate of∼38 - 240 M ⊙ yr−1, and kinetic power
of∼0.5−6.8×10 42 erg s−1, which are similar to those mea-
sured in AGN-dominated systems from the literature. How-
ever, these value are subject to high uncertainties related to
the CO-to-H2 conversion factor.
4. Detection of faint [Ne V] 14µm emission withJWST/MRSat
the nucleus confirms, for the first time, the presence of a
low-luminosity AGN in IRASF01364, which was suggested
by previous multi-wavelength studies. From the observed
[Ne V] 14µm flux we estimate an AGN bolometric luminos-
ity ofL bol =1.2−1.8×10 43 erg s−1. Comparison between the
observed and the inferred intrinsic X-ray luminosity suggests
the AGN is dust-obscured with an obscuring column density
ofN H ∼10 23 cm−2. The AGN is also accreting inefficiently
based on the high SMBH mass estimated from previous 10-
pc scale observations, indicating that the bulk of the SMBH
growth took place in the past.
5. We estimate the SFR using MIR tracers measured on kpc-
scales from theJWST/MRSdataset, and found lower values
compared to previous NIR/radio continuum observations at
higher resolution, potentially due to MIR attenuation or dis-
tinct physical conditions present in the compact nuclear star-
burst. Adopting the SFR from the latter, we estimate kinetic
power from supernovae of∼4×10 43 erg s−1. Given the addi-
tional, high expected radiation pressure from the dusty star-
burst, star formation alone is sufficient for powering the ob-
served powerful outflow. However, a more active AGN in

the recent past may also have played a role by enhancing
the molecular gas content in the outflow and producing the
bright excess H2 emission in the MIR, as predicted by sim-

withSpitzer/IRSarises from outside the nucleus, likely a re-
sult from widespread shock associated with the outflow.
The results from this work highlight the importance of spatially-
resolved multi-wavelength observations in investigating the na-
ture of dust-obscured starbursts. In particular, we show that the
ionized and molecular gas can have distinct behaviors, with ion-
ized gas exhibiting stronger signatures of turbulence associated
with the outflow, which may contribute to the differences ob-
served between ionized and molecular gas tracers in dynamical
studies of high−zstar-forming galaxies (e.g., Rizzo et al. 2024).
Additionally, we emphasize that in the case of IRASF01364, the
molecular gas distribution and kinematics observed on kpc-scale
does not solely trace disk rotation, and caution should be taken
when interpreting the cold gas dynamics when information from
other gas phases is not available. Last but not least, this work
showcases the capability ofJWST/MRSto unveil low-luminosity
AGN hidden in dusty, starburst-dominated systems, which may
represent a common phase in gas-rich galaxy mergers.

also exclude models that yield a best-fit component with SNR<5.
