Abstract.We investigate a dark energy model driven by a planar domain-wall-like structure
with a thickness comparable to, or larger than, the current Hubble radius, focusing on its
intrinsic anisotropy and observational viability. Near the centre of the domain wall (DW),
the spacetime is anisotropic, with distinct expansion rates parallel and perpendicular to the
wall. This anisotropic structure induces direction-dependent cosmic expansion and modifies
photon geodesics from cosmological sources, leaving characteristic signatures in cosmological
observables. We confront the model with recent observational data. We first compute the
anisotropic Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) temperature multipoles generated by the
DW and impose constraints from the Planck 2018 measurements. These constraints severely
limit the allowed DW abundance, requiring the DW energy density to be less thanO(10 −5)
of the current critical density in order to suppress the quadrupole contributions. We then
perform a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) analysis using Type Ia supernova (SNe Ia)
data, including the Pantheon+ SH0ES and DESY5 samples, to compare the DW scenario
with the standard ΛCDM model. We find that although the DW naturally realises anisotropic
accelerated expansion, the combined constraints from the CMB and SNe Ia favour the ΛCDM
limit, in which the DW contribution is negligible, and the universe is effectively isotropic.
Our results demonstrate that a Hubble-scale domain wall is tightly constrained by current
observations and can only play a subdominant role in the late-time cosmic acceleration.

1 Introduction
Observations have revealed that the universe is currently undergoing accelerated expansion,
which is commonly attributed to dark energy (DE). The cosmological constant is the simplest
candidate for DE, as adopted in the current standard cosmological model, the ΛCDM model.
The ΛCDM model is based on the cosmological principle, which assumes that the universe
is homogeneous and isotropic on large scales.
Recently, the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) has shown the possibility
that DE is time-dependent [1–4]. If confirmed, this would point to a deviation from the
ΛCDM model and require a modification or extension of the standard cosmological frame-
work. Various models have been proposed to explain dynamical dark energy (DE), including
quintessence models [5, 6], in which the potential energy of scalar fields drives the accelerated
expansion. Cosmic inhomogeneities have also been studied as a possible explanation of the
DESI results [7–10]. Some quintessence models for explaining dynamical DE can induce large
inhomogeneities [11], while other models [12] can produce topological defects, such as domain
walls, in certain regions of parameter space.
Recent high-precision observations have shown hints that the universe may not be
perfectly isotropic even on the largest scales. The most prominent example of large-scale
anisotropy is found in the cosmic microwave background (CMB) multipoles [13]. It is well
established that the CMB dipole, which corresponds to 10 −4–10−3 temperature fluctuations
compared to the usual 10 −5 fluctuations, can be attributed to the peculiar motion of the
observer. In an isotropic universe, the CMB dipole can therefore be treated as a purely kine-
matical effect. However, it remains possible that this anisotropy arises from other physics.
– 1 –

In fact, observations by WMAP and Planck have revealed several anisotropic anomalies that
may challenge the prevailing ΛCDM model and the cosmological principle. For example, the
alignment of the low multipole moments has attracted considerable attention [14–16]. It has
been known that the quadrupole and octopole components are unnaturally oriented relative
to the ecliptic plane [17]. It is also suggested that the recent accelerated expansion may be
anisotropic, and that the preferred direction is close to the CMB dipole direction [18]. In
addition to the expansion rate [19–23], other anisotropic features have also been reported,
such as large-scale bulk flows and quasar polarisation vectors [17].
Models based on topological defects provide a natural class of quintessence scenarios
with spatial anisotropy. Topological defects are topologically stable structures in which
the field configuration is spatially inhomogeneous. If their characteristic size is comparable
to, or larger than, the current Hubble radius, they can drive the accelerated expansion of
the universe. Topological inflation and the associated eternal inflation, which are based
on this idea in the early universe, have been extensively studied [24, 25]. Similarly, this
mechanism can be applied to dark energy: quintessence models based on topological defects
that are sufficiently stable to survive until the present epoch and behave as dark energy have
been proposed as candidates for the current accelerated expansion of the universe, and are
constrained by observations [26–33].
The purpose of this paper is to investigate whether such an anisotropic DE component,
realised by a Hubble-scale domain wall (domain wall (DW)), namely, a domain-wall structure
whose thickness is comparable to, or larger than, the current Hubble radius, can be consistent
with current cosmological observations. Hereafter, we refer to such a configuration as a
Hubble-scale DW. In particular, we emphasise that the central region of the Hubble-scale DW
is intrinsically anisotropic, leading to direction-dependent expansion rates. This is in sharp
contrast to monopole-based quintessence models, in which an observer located at the centre
can see an isotropic configuration owing to the spherical symmetry [27]. For a planar DW, by
contrast, the direction normal to the wall is distinguished from the direction parallel to it even
near the wall centre, so that anisotropic cosmic acceleration is unavoidable. We confront this
model with observational data in two complementary ways. First, we derive the anisotropic
CMB temperature multipoles induced by the DW geometry and impose constraints from
the Planck 2018 observations. Second, we perform a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)
analysis using supernovae (SNe) Ia data, including the Pantheon+ SH0ES (Supernovae and
H0 for the Equation of State of dark energy) [34, 35] compilation and the 5-year data of
Dark Energy Survey (DESY5) [36], to compare the DW scenario with the standard ΛCDM
model. By combining these probes, we demonstrate that the allowed DW energy fraction is
extremely small and that current data strongly favour the ΛCDM limit.
The rest of this paper is organised as follows. In section 2, we study the dynamics of
a cosmological model with a planar DW and clarify its anisotropic properties near the DW
centre. In section 3, we derive photon geodesics. In section 4, we present the correspond-
ing observational constraints from CMB anisotropies, and in section 5, we perform MCMC
parameter estimation using SN distance moduli. Finally, section 6 is devoted to conclusions
and discussion. Appendix A provides approximate solutions in both the near- and far-DW
regions. In appendix B, we discuss the constraints from higher CMB multipoles. Appendix C
presents the MCMC parameter estimation based on multiple observational datasets.

2 Dynamics of the planar DW universe
Throughout this paper, we consider a single planar DW-like structure with a thickness com-
parable to or greater than the present Hubble radius. Here we refer to it as a “DW-like
structure” because it extends over super-Hubble scales and does not represent either a con-
ventional domain wall in the scaling regime or the usual energy-minimising domain-wall
solution subject to asymptotic boundary conditions. For simplicity, however, we hereafter
refer to this configuration simply as a DW. We model it by a planar toy setup. Although
idealised, this approximation should be reasonable on scales smaller than the wall thickness,
since smaller-scale structure is energetically disfavoured and domain walls generally tend to
straighten.

4 Constraints from the CMB
The bending of photon paths due to the DW yields anisotropies in the CMB in addition to
the primordial perturbation. In this section, we evaluate the constraints on the model pa-
rameters from these anisotropies. To obtain a conservative bound, we neglect the primordial
perturbation and consider anisotropies induced purely by the DW. Let us suppose that the
initial timet i of our simulation is taken to be sufficiently early and the CMB anisotropies
are mainly produced after this time. Although we do not explicitly compute the effects from
the last scattering surface to the initial time, our estimate of the anisotropy is expected to
be conservative in the following sense. During this early propagation epoch, the DW energy
density is much smaller than its late-time value, and hence the DW-induced distortion of
photon geodesics and redshifts should be subdominant. We therefore expect the dominant
contribution to the anisotropy to be generated after the initial time, which is the regime
captured by our numerical calculation. The accumulated anisotropies are hence encoded

4.1 Dominant cosmological constant and subdominant DW case
Figure 6 shows the temperature perturbation for ˜ΩDW = 0.2. Temperature perturbations
remain detectable even for largex obs for a thicker DW (i.e., largerD). Figure 7 shows the

is maximal, which makes the quadrupole nonzero and dominant. Far from the DW (large
xobs), the temperature field becomes nearly isotropic, and all multipole components approach
zero. When the observer is slightly displaced from the centre, a left-right asymmetry arises,
generating odd multipoles such as the dipole and octopole.

We confronted the model with current observational data. The anisotropic expansion
inevitably induces CMB temperature anisotropies, and constraints from the Planck 2018
dipole, quadrupole, and octopole measurements place stringent upper bounds on the DW
energy density. Only a parameter region in which the DW contribution is extremely small
and the observer is located far from the wall is consistent with the observed CMB sky.
We further performed a Markov Chain Monte Carlo analysis using SNe Ia data, including
the Pantheon+ SH0ES and DESY5 samples, within the parameter region allowed by the CMB
constraints. The results favour the ΛCDM model, in which the DW abundance is negligible,
and the universe is isotropic.

C MCMC with joint data
Figure 15 presents the results of the joint MCMC parameter estimation using the “Pan-
theon+ SH0ES” and “DESY5” datasets (see section 5). The priors adopted in this analysis
are identical to those listed in table 1. The SNe Ia sample is constructed by combining the
datasets shown in figure 11, excluding 378 overlapping SNe. Figure 15 displays the resulting
posterior distributions. Notably, the parameters ˜ΩDW andσare inferred to be nonzero, which
potentially suggests an anisotropic universe favoured by the SNe observations. However, this
apparent signal could arise from the difference in the inferred values of the Hubble parameter
hbetween the two observational datasets (see figure 12), which show deviations of the an-
gular directions of the sources. We note that a robust assessment of anisotropy using a joint
analysis would, in principle, require a prior cross-calibration of the two observational datasets
before combining them, as otherwise residual systematic differences could be misinterpreted
as anisotropic signatures.
