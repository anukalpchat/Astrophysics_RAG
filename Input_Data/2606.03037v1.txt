ABSTRACT
We report JWST/NIRSpec integral field unit (IFU) observations of the quasar J1512+4422 atz∼6.2
from theAethersurvey. At∼900 Myr after the Big Bang, this object already lies on theM BH–σ∗
relation found in the local universe, with anM BH ≃8.9×10 8 M⊙ and a stellar velocity dispersion
σ∗ ≃288 km s −1. We detect an outflow with a velocity of∼478 km s −1 in the nuclear region, which
likely extends to∼3.2 kpc in projection and has a median velocity of∼352 km s −1. The outflow
dynamical time scale (∼9 Myr) is consistent with the time scale of the current quenching process
based on the star formation history as reported previously. The total mass outflow rate (92.6 +92.6
−74.1 M⊙
yr−1) is larger than the current star formation rate (0.9 +3.8
−0.8 or 4.3+5.8
−3.7 M⊙ yr−1), and the total kinetic
energy outflow rate (0.6+0.6
−0.5% of quasar luminosity) meets the threshold for negative quasar feedback as
suggested by simulations. These results suggest that the outflow is capable of suppressing/quenching
the star formation activity within the host galaxy. Furthermore, J1512+4422 exhibitsσ ∗, stellar mass
and size similar to those ofz≳3 quiescent/post-starburst galaxies, implying a link between the two.
Overall, for objects like J1512+4422, the evolution of their SMBHs and host galaxies appears to be
tightly coupled within the first billion years. The quasar feedback likely plays a critical role in both
placing them on theM BH–σ∗ relation and quenching.

1.INTRODUCTION
In the local universe, tight correlations between the
masses of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) and their
host galaxy properties, such as the stellar velocity dis-
arXiv:2606.03037v1  [astro-ph.GA]  2 Jun 2026

2
persion (σ ∗) and stellar mass (M ∗), suggest that the
evolution of SMBHs and galaxies are coupled with each
other (L. Ferrarese & D. Merritt 2000; K. Gebhardt
et al. 2000; J. Kormendy & L. C. Ho 2013). Never-
theless, when and how these correlations are established
and how SMBHs and galaxies interact with each other
across cosmic time remain open questions.
Quasars with∼10 9 −10 10 M⊙ SMBHs already ex-
ist atz >6 (e.g., X.-B. Wu et al. 2015; E. Ba˜ nados
et al. 2018; Y. Matsuoka et al. 2019a; M. Onoue et al.
2019; Y. Shen et al. 2019; J. Yang et al. 2020; J.-T.
Schindler et al. 2020; F. Wang et al. 2021; E. P. Fa-
rina et al. 2022; J. Yang et al. 2021; X. Fan et al. 2023;
C. Mazzucchelli et al. 2023), within a billion years af-
ter the Big Bang. The locations of these quasars on
theM BH–σ∗ plane offer a powerful probe of the feeding
and feedback processes of SMBHs in the early universe
and provide tight constraints on models of SMBH and
galaxy formation and evolution. Nevertheless, measur-
ingσ ∗ inz >6 quasar host galaxies is extremely chal-
lenging due to the faintness of the host galaxies and over-
whelming glare from the quasar. Gas kinematics based
on emission lines like [Cii] 158µm has been adopted
to trace the host galaxy dynamic mass (M dyn) and to
probe theM BH–Mdyn relation. Such studies generally
suggest overmassive SMBHs with respect to the local
relations (e.g., M. Neeleman et al. 2021; C. J. Willott
et al. 2017; T. Izumi et al. 2018), but at least one ob-
ject already lying on the local relation has been reported
(e.g., T. Izumi et al. 2021). Alternatively, stellar masses
have been measured forz >6 quasar host galaxies with
JWST data (e.g., X. Ding et al. 2023; M. Yue et al. 2023;
M. A. Stone et al. 2023). Again, many of these studies
indicate that these high-zquasars lie above the local re-
lations with overmassive SMBHs, while others suggest
that some of them may already lie on the localM BH–
M∗ relation (X. Ding et al. 2023; X. Ding et al. 2025;
J. D. Silverman et al. 2025; F. Ziparo et al. 2026). M.
Onoue et al. (2025), for the first time, reportσ ∗ of two
z >6 quasar host galaxies adopting JWST/NIRSpec
fixed-slit (FS) spectroscopy with moderate spectral res-
olution (R∼1000). Together with their JWST/NIRCam
imaging data, they find that one object lies on the local
MBH–M∗ relation, while the other hosts an overmassive
SMBH (see also X. Ding et al. 2025; J. D. Silverman
et al. 2025, for more discussions on the full SHELLQs
JWST Cycle 1 sample where the two objects belong).
In addition, previous observations have identified a
large number of quiescent/post-starburst galaxies at
z≳3 (e.g., I. Labb´ e et al. 2005; K. Glazebrook et al.
2017; F. Valentino et al. 2020, 2023; S. Alberts et al.
2023; A. C. Carnall et al. 2023, 2024; Z. Ji et al. 2024; T.
Nanayakkara et al. 2024; A. de Graaff et al. 2025; K. Ito
et al. 2025). Their rapid quenching challenges our cur-
rent understanding of galaxy evolution. Quasar/Active
Galactic Nucleus (AGN) feedback is one of the major
mechanisms responsible for galaxy quenching. Some
simulations indeed suggest that such feedback plays a
critical role in shutting down star formation in early
galaxies (C. C. Lovell et al. 2023; A. I. Hartley et al.
2023). As a major form of feedback, quasar-driven ion-
ized (e.g., M. Bischetti et al. 2022; M. A. Marshall et al.
2023, 2025; J. Yang et al. 2023; M. Yue et al. 2023; F.
Loiacono et al. 2024; R. Decarli et al. 2024; W. Liu et al.
2024, 2026, 2025a) and molecular (e.g., M. Bischetti
et al. 2019; D. Salak et al. 2024; J. S. Spilker et al. 2025;
Y. Zhu et al. 2026) outflows have indeed been observed
in the early universe. More intriguingly, a recent study
discovers that the frequency of fast, galaxy-scale out-
flows is significantly higher inz∼5–6 luminous quasars
than the lower-redshift ones, suggesting that quasar
feedback may easily suppress/regulate star formation
activities in early galaxies (W. Liu et al. 2026). Con-
sistently, the twoz∼6 quasar host galaxies examined
in M. Onoue et al. (2025), HSC J151248.71+442217.5
and HSC J223644.58+003256.9 (hereafter J1512+4422
and J2236+0032), are rapidly quenching, providing ex-
cellent laboratories for a close investigation of the con-
nection between quasar feedback and quenching in the
early universe.
In this paper, we report results from the
JWST/NIRSpec integral field unit (IFU) observation
of one of the two objects from M. Onoue et al. (2025),
J1512+4422 atz≃6.18, as part of the JWSTAether
survey (ID 5645; PI E. Farina). J1512+4422 was dis-
covered in Y. Matsuoka et al. (2019b). M. Onoue et al.
(2025) suggests that it is quenching rapidly with a spe-
cific star formation rate (sSFR) of≲0.2 Gyr −1 over the
last 10 Myr (see also X. Ding et al. 2025; C. L. Phillips
et al. 2025, for more JWST/NIRCam imaging and NIR-
Spec/FS spectroscopy studies on this object). We pro-
vide new measurements of itsσ ∗ and other quasar/host
galaxy properties and a first spatially-resolved view of
the outflow and feedback within this object based on
our IFU data. We describe the observations and data
reduction in Section 2 and the data analysis in Section
3. We discuss our key findings in Sections 4, 5 and 6 and
summarize them in Section 7. Throughout the paper,
we assume a ΛCDM cosmology withH 0 = 70 km s −1
Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3, and Ω Λ = 0.7. This gives∼5.6 kpc
per arcsecond at the redshift of J1512+4422.

2.OBSERVATION, DATA REDUCTION
J1512+4422 was observed on 2025 April 2 through a
JWST survey program namedAethersurvey with the
NIRSpec/IFU (T. B¨ oker et al. 2022; P. Jakobsen et al.
2022). A full description of the entireAethersurvey
will be presented in Farina et al.(in prep.) and Decarli
et al. (in prep.). The IFU observation has a field of
view (FOV) of∼3 ′′ ×3 ′′. We adopted a grating/filter
configuration of G395H/F290LP, a NRSIRS2 readout
pattern with 9 Groups and 1 Integration and 4 SMALL
CYCLING dither positions for a total exposure time
of∼45 minutes on target. The wavelength coverage is
∼2.87–5.14µm and the grating has a nominal resolv-
ing powerλ/∆λ≃2700 (velocity resolution FWHM
∼130 km s −1). The IFU data were reduced following
the same procedure as described in W. Liu et al. (2024,
2026). In brief, the data were processed primarily with
the JWST Science Calibration Pipeline (version “1.14.0”
and context file “jwst 1293.pmap”). Customized scripts
were adopted to replace or improve certain steps in the
pipeline, which includes the 1/fnoise subtraction in
count rate images, the production of the final data cube
with the flux-conserving “reproject exact” routine in
reprojectand “EMSM-weighting” 21, and the removal
of outliers in the final data cube with sigma-clipping.
The final combined data cube has a spatial pixel scale
of 0.′′05.
In addition, J1512+4422 was also observed with
JWST/NIRCam with F150W and F356W filters and
JWST/NIRSpec FS spectrograph adopting G395M
grating with a nominal resolving power ofλ/∆λ≃1000
(velocity resolution FWHM∼300 km s −1) and an on-
source integration time of∼2.19 hours through JWST
program GO #1967 (PI: M. Onoue). More details of
these observations can be found in M. Onoue et al.
(2025).
3.ANALYSIS
3.1.Nuclear Spectrum
We fit the nuclear spectrum extracted from a circular
aperture withr= 0. ′′1 from the IFU data cube with the
public software BADASS (R. O. Sexton et al. 2021). The
aperture size is chosen to maximize the S/N of the ab-
sorption lines. BADASS is an open-source spectral anal-
ysis tool designed for detailed decompositions of Type 1
AGN. It utilizes the Bayesian affine-invariant Markov-
Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler for robust param-
21 See Section “Weighting” in https://jwst-pipeline.readthedocs.
io/en/latest/jwst/cube build/main.html
eter and uncertainty estimation. Readers are referred to
their paper for more details on the design of the code.
Our fitting model consisted of a quasar power-law con-
tinuum, quasar FeIItemplates from M. P. V´ eron-Cetty
et al. (2004), stellar continuum and absorption lines, and
broad and narrow emission lines. The scaling and cen-
troid velocity of the FeIItemplates are set as free param-
eters, and the velocity dispersions of them are allowed
to vary freely between [500, 5000] km s−1. We have also
tested other popular FeIItemplates (e.g., T. A. Boro-
son & R. F. Green 1992; D. Park et al. 2022) but found
negligible difference in our final best-fits. In the fitting,
the models are further convolved with the line spread
function of the NIRSpec/IFU G395H grating.
Due to the degeneracy between the quasar continuum
and stellar continuum in the fitting, we need to first
obtain their relative strength independently. There-
fore, we used GALFIT (C. Y. Peng et al. 2010) to
decompose the pure continuum images obtained from
the IFU data (see Appendix A for details) and derived
the slope and scale of the quasar power-law continuum
which were then fixed in the spectral fitting. The stellar
continuum and absorption lines were modeled using the
widely-used pPXF (M. Cappellari 2023) integrated in
BADASS, adopting the Indo-U.S. Library of Coud´ e Feed
Stellar Spectra (F. Valdes et al. 2004) with an intrinsic
model spectral broadening of FWHM∼1 ˚A. The stellar
velocity and velocity dispersion were free to vary in the
fitting. For emission lines, the broad Hα, Hβ, and Hγ
emission lines exhibit double-peaked profiles and were fit
with two Gaussian components with velocity and veloc-
ity dispersion free to vary in the ranges of [−3000, 3000]
km s −1 and [500, 5000] km s −1, respectively. Another
two Gaussian components were adopted to fit the core
(tracing the systemic gas) and blueshifted components
(tracing the outflowing gas) of all narrow emission lines
(Hα, Hβ, [OIII]λλ4959,5007, [NII]λλ6548,6583). Here
the term “narrow emission lines” is adopted solely to
distinguish them from the double-peaked broad Balmer
lines of the quasar, and does not indicate that they
are all physically narrow. The kinematics (i.e., velocity
and velocity dispersion) of each corresponding Gaussian
component in all these lines are tied together, which are
free to vary in the ranges of [−3000, 3000] km s −1 and
[500, 5000] km s −1, respectively. The flux ratio of the
[Oiii] and [NII] doublets are fixed at their theoretical
values, 1:2.98 and 1:3, respectively (D. E. Osterbrock &
G. J. Ferland 2006). In addition, one Gaussian compo-
nent was used to fit the He I emission line. Finally, all
fluxes obtained from the best-fit models are corrected
for aperture loss determined from the curve of growth
analysis results from the NIRSpec/IFU data of the stan-

We adopt the best-fit stellar velocity to determine
the systemic redshift of our object, which givesz sys =
6.1831±0.0001. The best-fitσ ∗ is 288±13 km s −1. To
better estimate the uncertainty of it, we also tested two
other treatments for the power-law continuum in the fit-
ting: (i) one with power-law index fixed to−1.7, a typ-
ical value for low-zquasars (J. Selsing et al. 2016) and
free amplitude, and (ii) one with free power-law index
but amplitude at 5050–5150 ˚A matched to that from the
best-fit quasar continuum flux from GALFIT. Both of
these approaches gave largerσ ∗ of∼333±6 km s −1 and
341±7 km s −1, respectively. We thus use the maximum
difference between these two values and the best-fit value
above as the uncertainty for theσ ∗, which gives 288±60
km s−1. This is adopted as the fiducial value in the rest
of the paper. This measurement is independent of the
choice of aperture size as the stellar absorption lines are
spatially unresolved in our IFU data. As a caveat, the
σ∗ measured here is dominated by those of A-type and
F-type stars and may not represent the kinematics of
stellar populations with later spectral types.
3.2.Comparison with the FS Spectrum
In M. Onoue et al. (2025), they reported a lowerσ ∗
(a 2-σupper limit ofσ ∗ <190 km s −1) for J1512+4422
based on the FS spectrum extracted from a 0. ′′6×0.′′2
aperture with about∼2.7×worse spectral resolution.
The absorption lines are also spatially unresolved within
the FS data (C. L. Phillips et al. 2025). A comparison

5
of the IFU and FS spectra zoomed into the regions near
the stellar Balmer absorption lines and the correspond-
ing best-fit host galaxy models are shown in Fig. 8 of
Appendix B. In general, our IFU spectrum resembles
the FS spectrum. Nevertheless, the Balmer absorption
lines of the IFU spectrum are broader than those of the
FS spectrum. Specifically, the FWHM of the Hδabsorp-
tion, measured by fitting a Gaussian profile directly to
the IFU spectrum, is 1.7×larger than that of the FS
spectrum. This is the primary origin for the difference
inσ ∗ measured from the two spectra. In addition, the
best-fit stellar continuum of the IFU spectrum is also
slightly bluer than that of the FS spectrum (right panel
of Fig. 8).
In the fitting of the FS spectrum, they fixed the power-
law index to−1.7 in the initial fit and finalized it to
∼−2.23 where they fit the host galaxy-subtracted spec-
trum obtained from the initial fit. They then tried
to match the total quasar flux (both power-law and
emission lines) from the FS spectrum to that from the
JWST/NIRCam F356W image, but found that they had
to scale up the quasar flux from the F356W image by 0.2
dex to keep the equivalent widths of Balmer absorption
lines within the reasonable ranges of the stellar pop-
ulation models. Alternatively, they also tested flatter
power-law indices but concluded that it would lead to a
stellar continuum too blue to be reproduced by galaxy
spectral energy distribution (SED) models. Moreover,
broad Hβand Hγemission lines are modeled with a
single Gaussian component in their fitting whereas our
spectrum resolves the double-peak features of the broad
Hβand tentatively so for the broad Hγ, so these lines
are modeled with two Gaussian components in our case.
Finally, as shown in our Fig. 8 and the Extended Fig.
6 of M. Onoue et al. (2025), the best-fit model cannot
explain the red wing of Hγabsorption line in the FS
spectrum, which could again affect their best-fitσ ∗.
Ourσ ∗ is based on the higher spectral resolution IFU
data alone and the contribution of quasar emission is de-
termined from the image decomposition of the IFU data
itself, which should suffer much less from the types of un-
certainties described above. Nevertheless, our spectrum
does have a∼35% lower continuum S/N per ˚A near the
absorption lines than that of the FS spectrum, which
could make ourσ ∗ more uncertain. Moreover, while the
Balmer absorption lines are spatially unresolved in both
the IFU and FS data (C. L. Phillips et al. 2025), the
deeper FS spectrum with a larger aperture could still
have more contribution from the potentially spatially-
extended, weak absorption lines and thus lead to a dif-
ferentσ ∗. Finally, we have also fit the FS spectrum of
J1512+4422 with BADASS following the same parame-
ter settings in M. Onoue et al. (2025). It yields aσ ∗ con-
sistent with that reported in M. Onoue et al. (2025), in-
dicating that the choice of fitting software does not cause
the difference between the IFU-based and FS spectrum-
based results. In this paper, we use theσ ∗ derived from
our IFU data as the fiducial value for J1512+4422.
The other object in M. Onoue et al. (2025),
J2236+0032, does not exhibit the inconsistency in the
decompositions of the quasar and host galaxy con-
tribution between the FS spectrum and imaging for
J1512+4422 as described earlier, and the reported ab-
sorption line width of it (270±60 km s−1) is much larger
than the spectral resolution.
As mentioned earlier, clear double-peaked broad Hα
and Hβemission lines are seen in our IFU spectrum,
which confirms J1512+4422 as the highest-redshift AGN
with double-peaked broad Balmer emission lines, which
was first reported in M. Onoue et al. (2025). The
double-peaked profiles of the Hαand Hβlines in the
IFU spectrum resemble that of the Hαline as observed
in the FS spectrum. However, no double-peaked pro-
file is visible in the broad Hβof the FS spectrum,
likely due to the lower spectral resolution. Such double-
peaked broad emission lines have been observed in lower-
redshifts quasars/AGN, and many studies argue that
they represent the emission from the relativistic Kep-
lerian motion of emitting gas in a geometrically thin
and optically thick accretion disk. (e.g., M. Eracleous
& J. P. Halpern 1994; M. Eracleous et al. 2009; I. V.
Strateva et al. 2003; C. Ward et al. 2025). More details
on fitting the double-peaked broad Hαline of the FS
spectrum with accretion disk models can be found in
M. Onoue et al. (2025). A more comprehensive analysis
of the double-peaked broad Balmer emission lines of the
IFU spectrum will be presented in a future study.
3.3.Extended Line Emission
We adoptq3dfit(D. Rupke et al. 2023) to subtract
the quasar PSF in the IFU data and uncover the ex-
tended line emission within the galaxy, following the
same approach adopted by previous studies (e.g., D.
Wylezalek et al. 2022; A. Vayner et al. 2023, 2024; S.
Veilleux et al. 2023; W. Liu et al. 2024, 2025b; J. Wolf
et al. 2026). Here we summarize the key steps ofq3dfit
briefly. First, we construct a quasar template spectrum
from an aperture (r= 0. ′′05) centered on the brightest
spaxel. For each spaxel in the IFU data cube, we then
model the corresponding spectrum as a linear combina-
tion of a scaled quasar template spectrum representing
the quasar PSF contribution in that spaxel (I n
quasar), a
featureless monotonic polynomial representing the host-
galaxy continuum emission (I n
starlight,exp.model ), and a

6
set of host-galaxy emission lines modeled with up to
two components with Gaussian profiles (I n
emission). For
the emission line, a second Gaussian component is kept
only if it has peak S/N>3. In this analysis, we adopt
no stellar population models in the fitting, since we only
focus on the extended emission lines and see no evidence
of spatially-extended stellar absorption lines.
As shown in Fig. 2, the extended line emission in
each spaxel can be modeled by a single Gaussian profile
(c1 components) in general, except for several spaxels
where a secondary Gaussian profile (c2 components) is
needed (i.e., the c2 component has a peak S/N>3).
Five example spectra from representative spaxels, along
with their best-fit models of extended emission and sub-
tracted quasar PSF contribution, are shown in Fig. 3.
The locations of these spaxels are denoted in Fig. 2.
The extended line emission mainly consists of two parts
in terms of their kinematic nature: TheSystemic Gas
includes the southern and northwestern parts of the c1
components with centroid velocitiesv 50 close to the sys-
temic velocity (<50 km s −1) and narrow line widths
(σ <100 km s −1) in general. It traces the kinemati-
cally quiescent gas within the system. TheOutflowing
Gasincludes the remaining regions of the c1 components
and the c2 components, which are all blueshifted (v 50 ∼
−20 –−1040 km s −1; median:∼−174 km s −1) and have
much broader line widths (σ∼100 – 700 km s −1; me-
dian:∼156 km s −1) with respect to theSystemic Gas.

emission. Part of the broad and blueshifted line emis-

and rotating gas, although the current data are not be
deep enough to disentangle the two. Moreover, the en-
tire line-emitting nebulae may still trace the rotating
gas, although the gas kinematics observed is apparently
different from those of normal rotating disks where the
line emission would shift from blueshift to redshift more
gradually.

4.2.Locations on the SMBH–Galaxy Scaling Relations
As shown in the left panel of Fig. 4, our new data

The gas velocity dispersionσ gas (86±9 km s −1) de-

brightest, quasar photoionized regions and/or HIIre-

internal kinematics of those ionized regions rather than
the global kinematics of the galaxy. This is supported by
the lack of clear sign of rotation in the gas velocity field
of J1512+4422. Instead, the ionized gas is either highly
blueshifted, tracing a rapid outflow or close to the sys-
temic velocity determined by the stellar absorption line.
These suggest that the gas velocity dispersion is not a
good indicator of stellar velocity dispersion in high-z
quasars, at least for those like J1512+4422, and should
be used with caution when investigating correlations like
theM BH-σ∗ relation. Similar conclusions have also been
reached by studies of lower-redshift quasar/AGN (e.g.,
J. E. Greene & L. C. Ho 2005b; R. P. Remigio et al.
2025).

QUIESCENT/POST-STARBURST GALAXIES
As the host galaxies of both J1512+4422 and
J2236+0032 are quenching, we now explore how they
are connected to thez≳2 quiescent/post-starburst
galaxies in general. For the effective radius (R e) of the
two objects, we quote the measurements based on the
JWST/NIRCam images from M. Onoue et al. (2025).
As shown in Fig. 6, in the planes ofσ ∗ versusM ∗
andR e, J1512+4422 falls within the boundaries of the
regions occupied byz∼2–5 quiescent/post-starburst
galaxies (J. van de Sande et al. 2013; A. C. Carnall et al.
2024). At itsM ∗, theσ ∗ of J1512+4422 is comparable
to the highest value observed inz∼2–5 quiescent/post-
starburst galaxies and higher than the typical value
ofz <0.7 quiescent galaxies studied by (H. J. Zahid
et al. 2016). While on the lower end of both theR e
andM ∗ ranges of allz∼2–5 quiescent/post-starburst
galaxies examined, J1512+4422 exhibits aσ ∗ compara-
ble to those withM ∗ andR e even an order of magnitude
higher. Despite of its larger size, J1512+4422 has almost
the sameM ∗ andσ ∗ as GS-9209 atz∼4.7, one of the
earliest high-zquiescent galaxies observed so far with a
SFR averaged over the past 100 Myr consistent with zero
(A. C. Carnall et al. 2023). J2236+0032 is located in
regions similar to J1512+4422 with higher stellar mass

7.CONCLUSION
In this paper, we report new JWST NIRSpec/IFU
observations of the quasar J1512+4422 atz∼6.18 from
theAethersurvey. Our main results are summarized
below:
•Based on our new data, J1512+4422 has an
log(Lbol/L⊙) of∼45.1 from the 5100 ˚A contin-
uum luminosity, and anM BH of∼8.9×10 8 M⊙
(∼6.2×108 M⊙) and anλ Edd of∼0.011 (0.016)
from the broad Hα(Hβ) emission line. These are
broadly consistent with previously reported val-
ues. We measure a stellar velocity dispersionσ ∗
≃288±60 km s −1 based on the Balmer absorp-
tion lines. This value is larger than the previously
reported value (<190 km s −1), which is based on
a deeper FS spectrum with lower spectral resolu-
tion and different constraints on the stellar contin-
uum contribution in the spectrum. Meanwhile, the
emission line width of the kinematically quiescent
gas (i.e., narrow and near the systemic velocity)
measured from our data (∼86±9 km s −1) is much
smaller thanσ ∗, suggesting that the gas velocity
dispersion is not a good surrogate for the stellar
one in this object. Our new results indicate that
J1512+4422 is already on the local M–σ ∗ relation
just 900 Myr after the Big Bang, similar to the
other quasar, J2236+0032, with stellar absorption
lines detected in M. Onoue et al. (2025).
•In J1512+4422, we detect a quasar-driven outflow
with a velocity of 478±14 km s −1 in the nuclear
region. The outflow likely extends to∼3.2 kpc in
projection and has a median velocity of 352±75
km s −1, whereas the kinematically quiescent gas
within the system has velocities with absolute val-
ues≲50 km s −1. The outflow is launched about
∼9 Myr ago, consistent with the time time scale
(∼10 Myr ago) of the recent quenching process
(sSFR≲0.2 Gyr −1). The total mass outflow rate
(97.7+97.7
−78.2 M⊙ yr−1) is larger than the SFR of the
system within the last 10 Myr when assuming ei-
ther a delay-τSFH (0.9 +3.8
−0.8 M⊙ yr−1) or a non-
parametric SFH (4.3+5.8
−3.7 M⊙ yr−1). The total mo-

A.IMAGE DECOMPOSITION OF THE IFU DATA OF J1512+4422 WITH GALFIT.
We use GALFIT (C. Y. Peng et al. 2010) to decompose the continuum images integrated over 4050–4450 ˚A and
5050–5150 ˚A from the IFU data and obtain the corresponding quasar continuum flux free of emission lines. In the
decomposition, we use a point source component for the quasar, a S´ ersic component for the host galaxy, and a constant
background for the sky background unaccounted for, as shown in Fig. 7 in the Appendix. The point spread function
(PSF) model is built from the NIRSpec/IFU data of the standard star TYC 4433-1800-1 (proposal ID: 1128) over
the same wavelength ranges. The star observations were reduced with the same procedures as our object. The S´ ersic
index is fixed to 3, following M. Onoue et al. (2025). This choice is motivated by a morphology study of high-redshift
quiescent galaxies from (K. Ito et al. 2024). Nevertheless, changing S´ ersic index to other values (e.g., 1, 2 and 4) does
not affect the goodness-of-fit and final results significantly.
The continuum images, best-fit host galaxy models and final residuals from the image decompositions are shown in
Fig. 7. The host galaxy model has effective radii R e of 0.′′071±0.′′010 (0.42±0.05 kpc) at 4050–4450 ˚A and 0.′′075±0.′′009
(0.40±0.06 kpc) at 5050–5150 ˚A. The best-fit results suggest that the quasar contributes∼50.9% at 4050–4450 ˚A and
∼61.1% at 5050–5150 ˚A within ther=0. ′′1 aperture adopted to extract the nuclear spectrum, respectively. We then
fit a power-law model to the obtained quasar flux within the two spectral windows and obtain a best-fit power-law
index of∼−1.29, which is within the typical range observed in quasars (J. Selsing et al. 2016) and thus confirms that
the fit is reliable. This best-fit power-law model is used as the quasar power-law continuum component in the fitting
of the nuclear spectrum described in Section 3.1. In the analyses above, we adopt the IFU data cube resampled to 0. ′′1
spatial pixel (spaxel) size to increase the S/N of continuum in individual spaxels. However, using the original data
cube with 0.′′05 spaxel size gives consistent results despite of larger uncertainties.
Note that the best-fit host galaxy model based on the JWST/NIRCam F356W imaging from M. Onoue et al. (2025)
is more extended (with effective radius R e = 0.′′19±0.′′04 or 1.05±0.23 kpc) than our best-fit values. This is likely due
to two reasons: i) the F356W image includes both continuum and strong emission lines (i.e., [OIII]λλ4959,5007, Hβ)
whereas the IFU images only include the continuum, so the more extended part of F356W image is dominated by the
emission lines. Consistently, the size of extended line emission measured from the IFU data is indeed comparable with
the size of the host galaxy based on F356W image; ii) our IFU observation (exposure time∼45 min) is shallower than
the imaging (exposure time∼54 min) and fails to capture the more extended faint continuum emission.
