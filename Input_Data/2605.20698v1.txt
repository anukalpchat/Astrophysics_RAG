1. Introduction
The discovery of luminous quasars at redshiftsz≳6has opened
a unique observational window into the evolution of supermas-
siveblackholes(SMBHs)andtheirhostgalaxiesduringthefirst
billion years of cosmic history (Fan et al. 2000, 2003, 2006).
Up to now, more than 500 quasars have been spectroscopi-
cally confirmed atz>6(e.g., Jiang et al. 2015; Bañados et al.
2018; Wang et al. 2021; Yang et al. 2020; Fan et al. 2023), in-
cludingseveralbeyondz∼7.5(Bañados et al.2018;Wang et al.
2021; Yang et al.2020). These systemstypically harborSMBHs
with masses≳10 9 M⊙, growing at accretion rates exceeding
˙MBH ≳10M ⊙yr−1 (Schindler et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2021;
⋆ e-mail:xufx@stu.pku.edu.cn
Farina et al. 2022). Their host galaxies are sites of vigorous star
formation, with star formation rates (SFRs) typically ranging
from 100 to 1000M ⊙yr−1, as revealed by far-infrared (FIR)
andsubmillimeterobservations(Bertoldi et al.2003;Wang et al.
2013; Decarli et al. 2018; Venemans et al. 2020; Wang et al.
2024; Bouwens et al. 2025). The host galaxies themselves are
also massive systems in most cases, with stellar or dynami-
cal masses on the order of1010–1011 M⊙ (e.g., Venemans et al.
2016; Neeleman et al. 2021; Fei et al. 2025). These dual pro-
cesses place immense demand on the molecular gas reservoirs
(Mgas ∼10 10–1011 M⊙), requiring efficient replenishment via
cold gas accretion from the circumgalactic medium or frequent
mergers with gas-rich companions (Trakhtenbrot et al. 2017;
Farina et al. 2019; Decarli et al. 2017, 2024; Ding et al. 2025).
These findings indicate that both the black holes and their host

galaxies underwent rapid growth during the first billion years of

To understand the fueling of star formation and SMBH
growth, a quantitative characterization of the cold interstellar
medium (ISM)—defined as gas withT≲100K—is essential.
This cold ISM represents the immediate reservoir available for
galaxy-scale activity, and its properties (mass, temperature, den-
sity,andionizationstate)governtheefficiencyandmodeofbary-
onic conversion into stars and black hole accretion.
The Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array
(ALMA) has revolutionized studies of the cold ISM in high-
zquasars. At the time of the comprehensive review by
Carilli & Walter (2013), only two quasars atz>6(J1148+5251
and J1120+0641) had been detected in the [Cii] 158µm fine-
structureline.Today,thenumberof[Cii]-detectedquasarsatz>
6has grown to almost 200 (Decarli et al. 2018; Venemans et al.
2020; Wang et al. 2024; Bouwens et al. 2025), enabling the first
statistical studies of the ISM at the end of the reionization era.
[Cii]emissionprovidesaccurateredshifts,constraintsongasdy-
namics, and indirect SFR estimates. The observed diversity in
[Cii] luminosities, spatial extents, line profiles, and associated
dust continuum points to a wide range of evolutionary stages
and ISM conditions in these young galaxies (Decarli et al. 2018;
Venemans et al. 2019; Neeleman et al. 2021; Shao et al. 2022).
However, [Cii] alone cannot fully characterize the cold
molecular gas. [Cii] emission mostly arises from photon-
dominated regions (PDRs) in luminous quasar–starburst sys-
tems (Díaz-Santos et al. 2017; Pensabene et al. 2021), but it can
also have significant contributions from diffuse atomic and ion-
ized gas in more quiescent systems, as well as from CO-poor
low-metallicitymolecularreservoirs,whichintroducesanintrin-
sic uncertainty when using [Cii] as a tracer of cold molecular
gas. Neutral carbon fine-structure lines, [Ci] (3P1 → 3P0 and
3P2 → 3P1),provideanindependenttracerofcoldmoleculargas
(Papadopoulos et al. 2004; Jiao et al. 2017; Decarli et al. 2022;
Frias Castillo et al. 2025). Their excitation energies (E/k≈24
and63K) are readily reached in warm quasar host environ-
ments. Moreover, their moderate critical densities (ncrit ≈470
and1.2×10 3 cm−3)andtypicallyopticallythinnaturemake[Ci]
a reliable tracer of neutral gas closely associated with the low-
JCO-emitting molecular component. Nevertheless, both [Cii]
(withn crit ∼2.8×10 3 cm−3)and[Ci]arecharacterizedbymod-
erate critical densities, which limits their ability to probe denser
(nH2 ≳10 4cm−3)andwarmermoleculargasphases.Suchphases
areexpectedtobeprominentinluminousz∼6quasars,owingto
intensestarformationandpowerfulcentralactivegalacticnuclei
(AGN) activity. Additional diagnostics from mid-JCO transi-
tions are therefore particularly valuable. These lines trace highly
excitedmoleculargasthatcanbeheatednotonlybyPDRsbutalso
by X-ray–dominated regions (XDRs), cosmic rays, and shocks
(Papadopoulos et al. 2004; Meijerink et al. 2007; Vallini et al.
2018; Riechers et al. 2009, 2020; Decarli et al. 2022). Among
them, CO(7–6) lies close to the peak of the CO spectral line en-
ergy distribution in high-redshift quasars (e.g., Carilli & Walter
2013), making it a sensitive tracer of gas excitation and a useful
indicator for estimating CO(1–0)-based molecular gas masses,
albeit with uncertainties due to excitation variations.
These various tracers—[Cii], CO, [Ci], and the FIR dust
continuum—notonlyprobedifferentphasesandexcitationcondi-
tionsofthecoldISMbutalsoprovidecomplementaryapproaches
toestimatethemoleculargasmassinhigh-zgalaxies.Leveraging
thesediagnosticsenablesamorecompleteandrobustcharacteri-
zationofthecoldgasreservoirthatfuelsbothstarformationand
black hole accretion. Several techniques have been developed to
estimate the total molecular gas mass in high-zgalaxies. These
include: (i) the FIR dust continuum, calibrated via assumptions
about dust temperature and gas-to-dust ratio; (ii) low-JCO lu-
minosities,assumingaCO(1–0)conversionfactorα CO;(iii)[Ci]
line luminosities, using an abundance ratioXCI; and (iv) [Cii],
adopting a conversion factorα[CII] that linksL [CII] to molecular
gas mass, though its use is complicated by its multiphase origin
(Carilli & Walter 2013; Walter et al. 2011; Zanella et al. 2018;
Decarli et al. 2020; Dunne et al. 2021; Neeleman et al. 2021;
Sommovigo et al. 2021; Gururajan et al. 2023). Each method
carries systematic uncertainties, and cross–calibrating between
them is critical. For example, Dunne et al. (2021) employed a
joint modeling approach using CO, [Ci], and dust to constrain
gas masses in star–forming galaxies; Neeleman et al. (2021)
used spatially resolved [Cii] kinematics to derive dynamical
masses ofz≳6quasars and benchmark gas–mass estimators;
Sommovigo et al.(2021,2022)used[Cii]asadust–temperature
diagnostictorefinegas–massestimates;andDecarli et al.(2022)
combined [Cii], [Ci], CO(7–6), and dust to compare the rela-
tive strengths and assumptions of each method inz∼6quasar
hosts; Gururajan et al. (2023) cross–calibrated CO, [Ci], [Cii],
and dust tracers in a sample of lensed dusty star-forming galax-
ies (DSFGs) to quantify systematic offsets; and D’Eugenio et al.
(2023) evaluated [Cii] as a gas–mass tracer in high–zquiescent
systems, clarifying the regimes where it is most effective.
Line ratios among CO, [Ci], [Cii], and the FIR dust contin-
uum also provide constraints on the physical state of the ISM,
probing molecular gas excitation and density as well as the rel-
ative importance of different heating mechanisms. For instance,
combinations of mid- and high-JCO lines with the FIR con-
tinuum primarily trace gas excitation, density, and the inten-
sity of the heating field (Meijerink et al. 2007; Carilli & Walter
2013; Pensabene et al. 2021; Xu et al. 2024; Tadaki et al. 2025),
and ratios between the two [Ci] fine-structure lines primarily
probe the excitation temperature and level populations of neu-
tral carbon (Weiß et al. 2005a; Papadopoulos et al. 2022), while
the [Cii]-to-FIR ratio has long been used as an empirical in-
dicator of star-formation compactness and radiation-field inten-
sity, including the so-called [Cii] deficit at high infrared sur-
face brightness (Díaz-Santos et al. 2017; Herrera-Camus et al.
2018). When considered jointly, these observables can be com-
paredwiththeoreticalmodelsbyinvokingfar-ultraviolet(FUV)-
heated PDRs, XDRs, and additional heating mechanisms such
as cosmic rays and mechanical heating (Meijerink et al. 2007;
Carilli & Walter 2013; Herrera-Camus et al. 2018; Decarli et al.
2022;Kaasinen et al.2024).Butinunresolvedgalaxy-integrated
measurements, they remain intrinsically degenerate and should
beinterpretedasqualitativeconsistencychecksratherthandefini-
tive discriminants of the dominant heating mechanism.
In this work, we present a systematic ALMA survey target-
ing CO(7–6) and [Ci] emission in a sample of 18 quasars at
z∼6. These sources were selected from the broader population
of [C,ii]-detected quasars and span roughly one order of magni-
tudeininfraredluminosity,morethanfiveordersofmagnitudein
UV luminosity, and about one order of magnitude in the ratio of
[Cii]tototalinfrared(TIR)luminosity(seeTableA.1).Ourpro-
gram builds on the NOEMA pilot study by Decarli et al. (2022)
by increasing both the sample size and sensitivity, enabling sta-
tistical analysis of ISM conditions and molecular gas reservoirs.
Our main goals are to (i) constrain molecular gas masses using
multiple tracers, (ii) characterize gas excitation via line ratios,
and (iii) investigate the coupling between AGN activity and host
galaxy ISM properties in the early Universe.

2. Observations and data reduction
The observations were carried out under ALMA project
2019.1.00147.S (PI: R. Decarli) between October 2019 and
March 2020 using 43–48 ALMA 12 m antennas in the C43-4
configuration. The sources were observed using ALMA Band 3,
covering the redshifted frequencies of the [Ci] (3P1 → 3P0) and
CO(J=7→6) lines as well as the 3 mm dust continuum. This
yielded an angular resolution of∼1.0′′–2.0′′, corresponding to
physical scales of∼6–12 kpc atz∼6.
The correlator was configured with four spectral windows
(SPWs), each with a bandwidth of 1.875 GHz and a spectral
resolution of∼2 MHz (∼5 kms−1). One SPW (0) was centered
on the redshifted CO(7–6) and [Ci] lines, while the other three
(SPWs 1, 2, and 3) were placed in line-free regions to estimate
the continuum, except for J1120+0641 (z=7.0842), where the
CO(6–5)linefallswithinSPW3duetoitshighredshift,enabling
a simultaneous observation of CO(6–5), CO(7–6), and [Ci].
Each target was observed for a total on-source integration
time of∼30–60 minutes, reaching typical sensitivities of∼8–
16µJybeam −1 for the continuum and∼0.18–0.27 mJybeam−1
per50kms −1channelinthelinecubes.Theobservationaldetails
are summarized in Table A.2.
The data were calibrated using the standard ALMA pipeline
in version 5.6.1-8 of the Common Astronomy Software Appli-
cations (CASA; McMullin et al. 2007) package. The calibration
includedstandardproceduresforbandpass,fluxdensity,andam-
plitude/phase calibration.
Imaging was performed using thetcleantask in CASA,
adopting natural weighting to maximize sensitivity. The data
cubes were resampled in velocity space using a linear interpo-
lator, with a default channel width of 50 kms−1. For source
J1104+2134, a broader channel width of 80 kms−1 was adopted
to further improve sensitivity. The cell size was automatically
computed using theau.pickCellSizeutility script, with the
desired number of pixels per synthesized beam set to 5. And
we set an image size of 512×512 pixels. The imaging process
includes the following steps.
1. Continuum imaging: A pure-continuum map was created by
combining the line-free channels in SPWs 0, along with the
full available bandwidth from SPWs 1, 2, and 3. The line-
free channels in SPWs 0 were determined by computing the
expected line center frequencies of the [Ci] and CO(7–6)
transitions using the systemic redshift from [Cii], and mask-
ing out a frequency range of±0.75FWHM[CII] around the
linepeaks.Imagingwasperformedusingthetcleantaskin
CASA with theauto-multithreshparameters.
2. Line cube imaging: The pure-line visibilities were obtained
by subtracting the continuum emission from the calibrated
data usinguvcontsub, with a first-order polynomial fit
(fitorder=1) to the line-free channels to allow for a small
continuum slope across the spectral window. We then im-
aged the line data cubes for SPWs 0, 1, 2, and 3 using
the sameauto-multithreshcleaning setup, except with
negativethresholdincreased to 15.0 to ensure the mask-
ing algorithm does not exclude potential faint emission near
the noise level.
3. Momentmapcreationandspectrumextraction:Themoment-
0 (integrated intensity) maps were first generated by collaps-
ing a velocity range of±0.75FWHM [CII] centered on the
expectedfrequencyof[Ci]andCO(7–6),estimatedfromthe
[Cii] redshift. These maps were refined iteratively based on
thebest-fitcentroidvelocityandlinewidthobtainedfrom1D
spectral fitting (see below). Spectra were extracted from the
single pixel corresponding to the peak of the line emission
inthecollapsedmaps,asthesourcesarespatiallyunresolved
at our angular resolution. We fit the extracted spectra with a
single-Gaussiantoeachdetectedline,dependingonwhether
both [Ci] and CO(7–6) were detected or blended. If no sig-
nificantdetectionwasfoundinthemaps,the1Dspectrawere
instead extracted at the optical source position. The centroid
and width from the fitted spectrum were then used to update
theintegrationwindowforthemomentmaps,andtheprocess
was repeated until convergence.

While the four tracers agree on average, at the highestLTIR
we find that the [Cii]–based masses tend to fall below the CO,
[Ci], and dust estimates. In luminosity space (see Figure C.2
and C.3), the same objects exhibit (a) a decliningL[CII]/LTIR
(the [Cii] deficit; e.g., Lutz et al. 2016; Díaz-Santos et al. 2017;
Herrera-Camus et al. 2018), (b) a risingL[CII]/L[CI] withL TIR,
and (c) a roughly flatL[CII]/LCO(7−6). A plausible interpretation
is that, at high compactness and radiation–field intensity, grain
charging reduces the photoelectric heating efficiency in PDRs
(Bakes & Tielens 1994; see also Berné et al. 2022) and large
far–IR optical depths and/or [Cii] self–absorption further de-
press the [Cii] emissivity (Goldsmith et al. 2012; Sutter et al.
2021). Carbon is simultaneously driven from C0 to C+, raising
L[CII]/L[CI] (e.g., Valentino et al. 2020a). Meanwhile, CO(7–6)
emissionprimarilytraceswarmanddensemoleculargaslocated
deeper inside the clouds, and can be enhanced in compact star-
formingregionswherebothgastemperatureanddensityincrease
(Lu et al.2015;Kamenetzky et al.2016;Carilli & Walter2013),
keepingL [CII]/LCO(7−6) roughly constant. If a single, fixedα[CII]

urally translates into an underestimate ofMH2,[CII] at the bright
end (see also Narayanan & Krumholz 2017; Sommovigo et al.
2021),highlightingthatthesystematicallylower[CII]-basedgas
masses reflects changing excitation/opacity, not a contradiction
with the observed luminosity ratios.
Beyond the PDR-related effects discussed above, an impor-
tant distinction among alternative heating mechanisms lies in
their ability to heat dust relative to gas. While several non-
radiativeheatingprocessesexpectedincompactstarbursts—such
as cosmic rays, turbulence, and shocks—can efficiently heat the
molecular gas and modify carbon chemistry, they generally cou-
ple much more weakly to dust and therefore do not necessarily
produce a commensurate increase in the FIR continuum (e.g.,
Luhman et al. 2003; Papadopoulos 2010; Meijerink 2011). As a
result,thesemechanismsalonedonotnaturallypredictasystem-
aticsuppressionofmultiplegascoolingtracerswhennormalized
byL TIR.
In contrast, AGN-related heating provides a more direct
pathway to enhance the FIR continuum and dilute line-to-
continuum ratios. Hard UV and X-ray radiation from the AGN
can efficiently heat dust (Sargsyan et al. 2012; Carilli & Walter
2013), while also altering the ionization and chemical balance
of the ISM. In particular, high-energy photons can ionize C+
to C2+ and partially destroy CO, suppressing carbon-bearing
lines and redistributing cooling to other fine-structure transi-
tions such as [Oi] 63µm and [Oiii] 88µm (Luhman et al.
2003; Abel et al. 2005; Langer & Pineda 2015). This combina-
tion of enhanced dust heating and increased ionization offers a
physically motivated explanationfor the stronger decoupling be-
tweengascoolingtracersandL TIR inferredforthemostinfrared-
luminous sources, relative to starburst- and star-formation-
dominated systems (Greve et al. 2014; Herrera-Camus et al.
2018;Valentino et al.2020b;Gururajan et al.2023).Futurespec-

Overall, the results point to a diversity of ISM excitation
conditions among the high-redshift quasars. Although a subset
of sources shows ratios broadly consistent with PDR-like excita-
tion, in line with previous studies (e.g., Decarli et al. 2022), the

discussed in Section 4.2, additional non-radiative heating pro-

fullyaccount,ontheirown,forthepronounceddeficitsobserved

act in combination with radiative heating in PDRs or XDRs and
modify the excitation and chemical balance of the gas.
In particular, models including mechanical heating demon-
stratethat,athighgasdensitiesandintenseradiationfields(e.g.,
logn(cm −3)∼5.5and highlogG 0 ∼5), increasing the frac-

(Kazandjian et al.2015).Similarly,enhancedcosmic-rayioniza-

fecting the relative strengths of [Cii], [Ci], and CO emission
(e.g., Papadopoulos 2010; Meijerink 2011). In the absence of
spatiallyresolveddataoramorecompletesetofdiagnosticlines,
these processes cannot be uniquely disentangled from UV- or
X-ray–driven heating, underscoring that the interpretation of the
observed line ratios likely requires a composite excitation sce-
nario rather than a single dominant mechanism.

5. Conclusions
We have presented a systematic ALMA survey targeting the
CO(7–6)and[Ci](2–1)emissionlinesinasampleof18quasars
atz∼6, as part of project 2019.1.00147.S. This work signifi-
cantly expands on previous efforts by increasing both the sensi-
tivity and sample size relative to earlier NOEMA observations.
Our main findings are summarized below:
–CO(7–6)emissionisdetectedin15outof18quasars,[Ci](2–
1) in 6 out of 18 quasars, and the3mm dust continuum in
13outof18quasars.Withtheseobservations,thenumberof
z∼6quasars with CO(7–6) and/or [Ci] detections has sig-
nificantlyincreasedcomparedtopreviousstudies,expanding
the available sample for probing the molecular and atomic
gas properties in the early Universe.
–We measure CO(7–6) and [Ci](2–1) luminosities and derive
LTIRfromthedustcontinuum,whilethe[Cii]luminositiesare
compiled from the literature. Across our sample, line lumi-
nositiesincreasewithL TIRbutshownocleartrendwithM 1450.
In line-to-continuum space we recover the classical [Cii]
deficit, with many sources at or below the high–ΣSFR locus.
CO(7–6)/TIR and [Ci]/TIR also show analogous decreasing
trends, which is not observed in main-sequence galaxies and
starbursts. This is consistent with compact, intense star for-
mation and/or enhanced dust opacity (with a possible AGN
continuum contribution).
