Abstract
Galaxy rotation curves provide a direct test of how baryonic matter and dark
matter combine to determine the mass profiles of disk galaxies. In ultralight or
fuzzy dark matter models, numerical simulations predict a central solitonic core
surrounded by an outer halo, but the population-level relation between the core
and the host halo remains an important modelling choice. We present a hierar-
chical Bayesian pipeline for fitting soliton-plus-NFW rotation-curve models to
the SPARC database while treating the core-halo scaling exponent as a global
free parameter. The model uses a Schive-normalized soliton, a regularized NFW
envelope with a smooth transition, halo-mass priors tied toV flat, and stellar-to-
halo-mass information. We apply the pipeline to 106 SPARC galaxies, including
26 systems with bulges, and sample the resulting 346-dimensional posterior with
JAX/NumPyro NUTS. The free-scaling run has zero divergences andˆr≃1.000
for the global parameters. The posterior reaches the upper edge of the stan-
dard mass prior and the lower edge of the scaling prior, withlog 10(mϕ/eV) =

1 Introduction
Galaxy rotation curves are a direct probe of the mass distribution in disk galaxies. The
observed circular velocity at each radius depends on the total enclosed mass, includ-
ing stars, gas, and any dark component. Rotation curves therefore provide a useful
test of how baryonic matter and dark matter combine to shape galactic gravitational
potentials.
The SPARC database provides a standard sample for this type of test. It contains
rotation curves for nearby disk galaxies together with baryonic velocity components
from gas, stellar disks, and bulges where present (Lelli et al. 2016). These decomposed
contributions make SPARC useful for fitting dark-matter models because the baryonic
and non-baryonic parts of the rotation curve can be treated separately within the
same forward model.
Ultralight or fuzzy dark matter models predict structure on galactic scales that
differs from standard cold dark matter. In these models, the wave nature of the dark
matter can produce a central solitonic core surrounded by an outer halo (Hu et al.
2000; Schive et al. 2014a; Hui et al. 2017). Rotation curves can therefore test whether
a soliton-like central component is supported by galaxy data, and how that component
connects to the outer halo. For broad reviews of dark matter and ultralight/fuzzy dark
matter, including observational constraints, halo density profiles, and galaxy-scale
tests, see Cirelli et al. (2026) and Eberhardt and Ferreira (2025).
A key modelling choice is the relation between the soliton core and the host halo.
Many rotation-curve analyses fix this relation before fitting the data. This reduces the
number of free parameters, but it also fixes how the core size changes across galaxies
of different halo mass. In this work we take a different approach. We treat the core-
halo scaling exponent as a global parameter of the galaxy sample and infer it jointly
with the ultralight-particle mass scale. In the notation used below, these two global
parameters areαandm ϕ. This lets the selected SPARC sample determine whether
the hierarchy prefers an interior soliton solution or moves toward a limiting regime.
The direct comparator for our mass prior is the Bayesian SPARC analysis of Khe-
lashvili et al. (2023). They use log 10(m22)∈[−3,3], equivalent to log 10(mϕ/eV)∈
[−25,−19]. We adopt this range for the runs. The lower end includes the ultralight
masses commonly discussed in the FDM literature. The upper end reaches the high-
mass regime where the soliton becomes compact on observed rotation-curve scales and
the model approaches a CDM-like limit. Lyman-alpha forest studies give strong lower
limits on FDM masses under cosmological assumptions (Irˇ siˇ c et al. 2017; Rogers and
Peiris 2021). We cite those bounds for context. We do not use them as priors in the
SPARC fit.
We build a hierarchical Bayesian pipeline for fitting a Schive-normalized soliton
plus regularized NFW envelope to SPARC rotation curves. The model uses one global
particle mass scale and one global core-halo scaling exponent. Each galaxy has its own
halo mass, concentration, and stellar mass-to-light ratios. The outer halo is described
by an NFW profile, and the transition between the soliton and the envelope is made
smooth so that the full model can be sampled with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo.
2

We apply the pipeline to 106 SPARC galaxies selected by inclination, quality flag,
number of velocity points, and mean velocity uncertainty. The posterior has 346 sam-
pled dimensions in the free-scaling run. We sample it with JAX/NumPyro NUTS and
report convergence diagnostics, boundary diagnostics, stability checks, fixed-scaling
sensitivity runs, and a representative rotation-curve decomposition.
The main result is that the free-scaling hierarchy converges to a boundary solution.
Within the Schive-normalized model and the standard prior range log 10(mϕ/eV)∈
[−25,−19], the posterior reaches the high-mass edge of the particle-mass prior and
the low-scaling edge of the core-halo exponent prior. In this regime the soliton core is
compact on the observed rotation-curve scales, and the visible fit is carried mainly by
the baryonic terms and the regularized NFW envelope. The same boundary behaviour
is found after removing UGC06787 and after widening the high-mass stellar-to-halo-
mass prior.
The purpose of this paper is to report the hierarchical inference framework and the
diagnostic result it produces. Within the adopted model and prior range, the selected
SPARC sample does not identify an interior population-level soliton component. The
result is a statement about the posterior behaviour of this soliton-plus-NFW hierarchy
under the stated assumptions.
Principal contributions.The main contributions of this work are:
1. a hierarchical Bayesian pipeline for soliton-plus-NFW inference on SPARC rotation
curves;
2. a population-level treatment of the core-halo scaling exponent rather than fixing it
before fitting;
3. a full-sample application to 106 SPARC galaxies with explicit NUTS convergence
diagnostics;
4. boundary diagnostics showing that the free-scaling posterior moves to the high-m ϕ,
low-αedge of the adopted prior range;
5. stability checks showing that this behaviour is unchanged after removing UGC06787
and after widening the high-mass stellar-to-halo-mass prior.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 defines the soliton-plus-NFW for-

Section 5 presents the posterior results and diagnostic checks. Section 6 discusses the

3 Data and selection
We use the SPARC rotation-curve database (Lelli et al. 2016). Each rotation-curve
file gives radius, observed velocity, velocity uncertainty, gas velocity, disk velocity, and
bulge velocity when present. The table metadata give distance, inclination, quality
flag, and flat rotation velocity.
We apply four cuts. We require inclinationi≥30 ◦, quality flagQ̸= 3, at least
ten velocity points, and mean velocity uncertainty below 10 km s −1. The final sample

4 Hierarchical Bayesian model
The hierarchy separates sample-level and galaxy-level parameters. The particle mass
scale and the core-halo scaling exponent are shared by all galaxies. The halo mass,
concentration, and stellar mass-to-light ratios are inferred separately for each galaxy.
The likelihood compares the model velocity to the observed SPARC velocity at each
valid radius, while the priors set the halo-mass scale, concentration relation, and
stellar-to-halo-mass information.
The two global parameters are log 10(mϕ/eV) andα. The runs use
log10(mϕ/eV)∼U(−25,−19), α∼U(0,1.5).(14)
The log 10(mϕ/eV) prior follows the SPARC FDM range used by Khelashvili et al.
(2023). The same range is used here for one globalm ϕ because the global parameter
represents the sample-level boson mass in the same physical interval used for per-
galaxy FDM mass inference. Theαprior covers the values used in simulation-based
core-halo relations and allows the data to move toward weak halo-mass dependence.
Each galaxy has local parameters log 10 Mvir,c 200, Υdisk, and, for bulge galaxies,
Υbul. The free-αdimension count is

6 Discussion
6.1 What the model reports
Under the density normalization in Eq. 2 and the literature-standard prior
log10(mϕ/eV)∈[−25,−19], the hierarchical SPARC fit returns a boundary solution.
The free-αposterior reaches the upperm ϕ boundary and the lowerαboundary. The
high-mass edge corresponds to the regime where the soliton becomes compact on
12

observed rotation-curve scales. The low-αedge corresponds to weak dependence of
core radius on halo mass. In this regime, the regularized NFW envelope and baryonic
terms carry the rotation-curve fit.
The limitα≃0 removes the inferred halo-mass dependence of the core radius. The
hierarchy therefore does not recover a population-level core-halo scaling in the free-α
run. Combined with the high-m ϕ boundary, this places the model in a compact-core
regime across the observed SPARC radii. Inner-rotation-curve systematics can matter
in this regime. Beam smearing, non-circular motions, and inclination uncertainties
affect the same inner data points where a soliton contribution would be largest. A
different treatment of those systematics could change the posterior.
The fixed-α= 1/3 run gives an interior mass scale near log 10(mϕ/eV) =−22.87,
although it fails the strict convergence criteria. This contrast is informative. An interior
soliton solution can appear when the core-halo exponent is fixed. Whenαis sampled,
the hierarchy moves away from that conditional interior solution and reaches the
boundary solution. Theα= 1/2 bimodality shows the same transition directly: one
chain occupies an interior region and one chain occupies the high-mass boundary
region. We therefore treat the fixed-αruns as conditional probes of posterior structure.
The two robustness runs support the same conclusion. Removing UGC06787 leaves
the posterior unchanged at the quoted precision. Widening the high-mass SMHM scat-
ter leaves the posterior unchanged at the quoted precision. The boundary behaviour
therefore appears across the tested free-αconfigurations.
This result is specific to the modelling choices used here. It describes the joint
behaviour of the selected SPARC sample, the forward model, and the hierarchical
prior structure. A different selection, a different transition prescription, a different halo
prior, or a different treatment of observational systematics may change the posterior.
Several published nearby-galaxy FDM analyses report mass preferences under different
assumptions (Bar et al. 2018, 2022; Khelashvili et al. 2023; Ba˜ nares Hern´ andez et al.
2023). A direct reconciliation requires matched data cuts, matched priors, and matched
profile definitions.
6.2 Relation to earlier SPARC FDM work
Khelashvili et al. (2023) apply a Bayesian framework to SPARC galaxies with a hard
soliton-NFW transition radius and per-galaxy mass preferences. They report ten-
sion between individual galaxies and conclude that no singlem ϕ fits the full sample.
Ba˜ nares Hern´ andez et al. (2023) fit LITTLE THINGS dwarf irregulars with a fixed
core-halo relation. Bar et al. (2022) use SPARC to test the soliton-halo relation as an
exclusion criterion.
Our pipeline differs in three ways. It samplesαas a global parameter. It uses one
globalm ϕ for the selected sample. It uses a differentiable composite profile with a
smooth transition between the soliton and the regularized NFW envelope. Under these
choices, the free-αhierarchy moves to the high-mass, low-αboundary region. This is
consistent with the broad conclusion of Khelashvili et al. (2023) that a single FDM
mass is difficult to identify across the SPARC sample, although the parameterizations
and priors are not matched one-to-one.
13

6.3 What the priors are doing
TheV flat and Moster priors multiply as independent log-density terms and set the
local halo-mass scale. The per-galaxyM vir posteriors concentrate inside the expected
halo-mass range, as shown in Fig. 5. These priors keep the local halo parameters from
drifting over several dex while the global soliton parameters move to the boundary
solution.
Them ϕ prior bound at−19 has a different role. It marks the high-mass edge of the
SPARC FDM prior range used by Khelashvili et al. (2023). At this edge, the soliton
radius becomes smaller than the observed rotation-curve scale for the selected galaxies.
The free-αposterior reaches this edge. The result therefore reports the limit of this
model on the selected SPARC sample: the inference moves toward the compact-core,
weak-halo-dependence regime instead of an interior soliton solution.
6.4 Limitations and future work
The pipeline has several limitations that define the scope of the present diagnostic
study. These limitations do not change the main result reported here: within the
adopted Schive-normalized hierarchy and standard prior range, the free-αposterior
moves to the low-α, high-m ϕ boundary solution.
First, the analysis does not include simulation-based recovery tests on synthetic
SPARC-like data. Such tests would be useful for checking how the hierarchy responds
to an injected interior soliton component. Second, the paper does not present a full
model-comparison study against soliton-free halo models. The focus here is the pos-
terior behaviour of the soliton-plus-NFW hierarchy rather than a ranking of halo
prescriptions. Third, the representative NGC3198 decomposition in Fig. 6 is not a
replacement for a sample-wide posterior-predictive analysis.
Several modelling approximations should also be kept in mind. TheV flat prior uses
a fixed reference concentration,c200 = 10, while the model samplesc200 separately. The
SMHM scatter tier is assigned using the prior-mean disk mass-to-light ratio Υ disk =
0.5, rather than the sampled value. For galaxies with posterior Υdisk far from the prior
mean and stellar mass near a tier boundary, the assigned tier may differ from the tier
that would be chosen using the sampled value.
The fixed-αruns at 1/3, 1/2, and 1.0 require improved sampling before quantitative
use. The widened-prior diagnostic in Appendix C also did not converge under the
baseline sampler settings. These runs are therefore treated as diagnostics of posterior
geometry, not as posterior measurements.
Future work should address these points with synthetic-data recovery tests, soliton-
free baseline comparisons, sample-wide posterior-predictive checks, distance and
inclination marginalization, longer warmup tests, and alternative parameterizations.
7 Conclusions
This work tested whether a Schive-normalized soliton-plus-NFW hierarchy with a free
population-level core-halo exponent identifies an interior soliton solution in a selected
SPARC sample. We presented a hierarchical Bayesian pipeline for this rotation-curve

inference problem. The pipeline samples a Schive-normalized model with a free core-
halo exponentα, a global mass scalem ϕ, and galaxy-level halo and stellar parameters.
The findings are direct.
1. The free-αrun on 106 SPARC galaxies converges cleanly with zero divergences,
ˆr≃1.000, and bulk effective sample sizes above 1800 for the global parameters.
2. The posterior medians and 16/84 percentile intervals are log 10(mϕ/eV) =
−19.20+0.12
−0.11 andα= 0.014 +0.023
−0.011. The posterior reaches the high-m ϕ and low-
αprior boundaries, so boundary fractions and quantiles carry the primary
interpretation.
3. Removing UGC06787 and widening the high-mass SMHM prior both return the
same boundary solution.
4. Fixed-αsensitivity runs reveal difficult posterior geometry away from the free-α
solution. Most fixed-αruns either fail convergence checks or sit at the high-mass

The pipeline is reproducible. The diagnostics are explicit. Within the adopted
Schive-normalized model and the standard SPARC FDM prior range, the selected
SPARC sample does not identify an interior soliton component in the free-αhierarchy.
The code, run manifests, posterior samples, and diagnostic tables are provided for

Appendix C Widened-prior diagnostic
We ran one diagnostic configuration with the upperm ϕ prior bound extended from
−19 to−15. This configuration produced 2754 divergences out of 4000 post-warmup
samples, ˆr= 1.41 for log 10(mϕ/eV), ˆr= 1.89 forα, and ESS bulk(α) = 2.9. The chains
explored different regions of the high-m ϕ space and did not agree. The configuration
did not produce a reliable posterior under the baseline sampler settings.
This run is reported as a sampler diagnostic. It indicates difficult sampling geom-
etry in the high-mass region of the forward model. At masses well above the standard
upper bound, the inferred core radius can become far smaller than the observed
rotation-curve scale, which makes the forward model physically ill-conditioned for
rotation-curve inference. The run does not enter any posterior summary.
