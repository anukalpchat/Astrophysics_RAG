ABSTRACT
Context. The origin of massive black holes in the early universe remains uncertain and still unexplored. Population III (zero metallic-
ity) stars are among the ﬁrst stellar sources capable of producing such remnants, but their evolution is very sensitive to rotation.
Aims. We explore how slow initial rotation inﬂuences the evolution and black hole formation of very massive Pop III stars, and assess
their potential to become massive, fast-spinning black holes consistent with gravitational-waves events such as GW231123.
Methods. We compute a grid of non-rotating and slowly-rotating Pop III stellar models with initial masses of 80, 85, and 90 M⊙ using
the GENEC stellar evolution code. Our models includes rotationally induced mixing and angular-momentum transport by magnetic
torques (Taylor-Spruit dynamo and the magneto-rotational instability). We analyse the CO core masses and their volume-averaged
adiabatic index ⟨Γ1⟩ to assess stability against electron-positron pair creation. From the angular-momentum proﬁles of the stellar
models by the end of He-burning, we estimate the resulting black hole masses and dimensionless spins under the assumption of a
direct collapse.
Results. Our non-rotating and slowly rotating 80 and 85 M⊙ models develop carbon-oxygen core masses between 31 and 36 M⊙ and
have an adiabatic index ⟨Γ1⟩, which remains above 4 /3 (i. e. they avoid the pair-instability). Our models thus predict that Pop III stars
can keep most of their mass and collapse directly to form black holes of 80–85 M⊙ with dimensionless spins up to aBH ≲ 0.7.
Conclusions. Initially slowly rotating, massive Pop III stars can form very massive, rapidly spinning black holes just below the pair-
instability regime. This supports interpreting the lower boundary of the PISN mass gap as a smooth, structure-dependent transition
and identiﬁes single-star Pop III evolution as a possible component channel for the population of massive fast-spinning black holes
observed by gravitational-wave detectors, subject to the uncertain e ﬃciency of internal angular-momentum transport and mass-loss
prescriptions.

1. Introduction
The gravitational-wave event GW231123 has been interpreted as
the merger of two black holes (BHs) with source-frame masses
of 137 +23
−18 M⊙ and 101 +22
−50 M⊙, and with large inferred spins,
χ1 = 0.90+0.10
−0.19 and χ2 = 0.80+0.20
−0.52 (Abac et al. 2025). These
properties make GW231123 particularly challenging to interpret
within standard stellar-evolution channels. In conventional mod-
els, BH formation is expected to be strongly suppressed in the
pair-instability mass gap (PIMG), commonly placed at roughly
∼ 55–130 M⊙ (Heger et al. 2003; Giacobbo et al. 2018; Woosley
2019; Woosley & Heger 2021). The physical origin of this gap
lies in the development of su ﬃciently massive carbon–oxygen
(CO) cores. Within such cores, pair creation softens the equation
of state, triggering contraction and explosive oxygen burning,
which can either eject part of the stellar envelope in pulsational
pair-instability (PPI) episodes or disrupt the star completely in a
pair-instability supernova (PISN), leaving no compact remnant
(Heger et al. 2003; Farmer et al. 2019; Woosley & Heger 2021).

GW231123 is especially intriguing in this context. Its secondary
component lies well within the canonical PIMG, while the pri-
mary lies within or above its upper edge, depending on the true
source parameters. In addition to their large masses, both com-
ponents appear to be rapidly spinning (Abac et al. 2025). Ex-
plaining simultaneously such high masses and such large spins
is therefore a non-trivial challenge for single-star evolutionary
models.
A number of scenarios have been proposed to explain BHs
in the mass gap, including growth of lower-mass BHs through
accretion or repeated mergers (Roupas 2025; Stegmann et al.
2025; Kıro˘glu et al. 2025; Liu et al. 2025; Gottlieb et al. 2025),
premature collapse before pair-instability fully develops (Baum-
garte & Shapiro 2025), and even from primordial origin (Nojiri
& Odintsov 2025; Y uan et al. 2025). These possibilities suggest
that the PIMG is not an immutable prediction, but the outcome
of competing eﬀects involving core growth, mass loss, rotational
mixing, angular-momentum transport, and the thermodynamic
response of the stellar interior (Farmer et al. 2019; Renzo et al.
2020; Woosley & Heger 2021). The diﬃculty of forming BHs in

the mass gap through classical stellar evolution stems from the
relation between the ﬁnal stellar mass and the CO-core mass,
since the latter determines the onset of pair instability (PI). This
relation is sensitive to mass loss, chemical mixing, rotation, con-
vection, and nuclear reaction rates. Recent work suggests that the
lower edge of the PPI regime is set primarily by a critical CO-
core mass, with residual dependence on rotation and mass loss
(Winch et al. 2025). Using the same stellar-evolution code as in
the present work, Farrell et al. (2021) found that Population III
stars may form BHs of up to about 85 M⊙; this was later sup-
ported by Hirschi et al. (2025) for very low metallicity models
(Z = 10−5).
Pop III stars are particularly relevant here as their primordial
composition implies extremely weak radiatively driven winds,
allowing them to retain most of their mass and potentially much
of their angular momentum throughout their evolution. They
are therefore promising progenitors of very massive, possibly
rapidly spinning BHs, and natural candidates for probing the
lower edge of the PIMG (Heger et al. 2003; Farrell et al. 2021;
Hirschi et al. 2025).
This motivates us to re-examine the lower edge of the PIMG
at zero metallicity, with special emphasis on the role of slow ro-
tation and angular-momentum retention. Rather than asking only
what is the largest BH mass attainable below the onset of PI, we
ask a more restrictive question: what is the maximum mass of
a fast-spinning BH that can be produced by a single, very low-
metallicity star? To address this issue, we compute new stellar-
evolution models of massive Pop III stars with initial masses of
80, 85, and 90 M⊙ using the GENEC code. We follow the evo-
lution of non-rotating and slowly rotating models and analyse
their mass retention, core growth, angular-momentum budget,
and stability against PI using their CO-core masses and volume-
averaged adiabatic index. Our aim is to determine whether stars
in this mass range can avoid pair-instability disruption and col-
lapse into massive BHs, and whether the resulting remnants can
retain suﬃciently large spins to be relevant for systems such as
GW231123.
The paper is organised as follows. In Sect. 2 we describe the
stellar models and summarise their main evolutionary properties.
In Sect. 3 we discuss their predicted fate and the implications
for BH masses and spins near the pair-instability boundary. An
approximate treatment of collapse, fallback, and BH growth is
presented in Appendix A.
2. Stellar Models
We computed the evolution of massive stars for three initial
masses (80, 85, and 90 M⊙) at zero metallicity ( Z = 0), from
the zero-age main sequence (ZAMS) to at least the end of core-
helium burning and generally until oxygen burning using the
stellar evolution code GENEC (Eggenberger et al. 2008). Mod-
els were evolved with varying degrees of initial rotation, from
non-rotating up to at most 20% critical velocity. The speciﬁc ini-
tial values and nomenclature for all models computed can be
found in Table 1. The choice of masses and rotation are mo-
tivated by the predicted high mass, and high spin, of the BH
remnant inferred from the event GW231123 (and the expected
lower boundary of the PIMG). We use zero metallicity models
to minimise mass loss from stellar winds.
Indeed, in metal-free stars, line-driven winds are extremely
weak so any signiﬁcant loss of mass, and thus of total angular
momentum, can occur only through mechanical mass shedding
triggered by proximity to the critical surface rotation. The initial
rotational velocities we chose are relatively low to avoid such
mechanical mass loss. This is in contrast with previous studies
with GENEC (c.f. Murphy et al. (2021) uses ω = vini/vcrit =
0.4, where vcrit is the critical velocity). The suppression of both
mechanical and wind driven mass loss leaves our models with a
high ﬁnal mass and total angular momentum, making them good
candidates to produce heavy BHs that can be spun up by the late
accretion of angular-momentum-rich outer layers.
In this work we used the version of GENEC described in
Griﬃths et al. (2025), including the same physics as in Ekström
et al. (2012) and the recent grid of Sibony et al. (2024) except
for three key aspects. These consist of updates to the equation of
state (EoS), opacity calculations, and nuclear reaction network
(here GeValNet25 is employed). For further details on the dif-
ferences, see Sections 2, 3, and 4 of Gri ﬃths et al. (2025). These
updates, particularly that of the EoS, are critical to correctly de-
termine the evolution of the core of the massive stars under ther-
modynamic conditions where the pair-instability becomes im-
portant.
Our models evolved with rotation also include angular-
momentum transport induced by all standard shear instabilities,
meridional circulation, convection (see, Eggenberger et al. 2008,
for a complete list of mechanisms included in GENEC) and
local magnetic torques. The magnetic mechanisms of angular-
momentum transport used here are the Tayler-Spruit (TS) dy-
namo, as described in Eggenberger et al. (2022), and the
magneto-rotational instability (MRI), as described in Gri ﬃths
et al. (2022). Both of these instabilities can transport angular
momentum outwards within the stellar interior during main se-
quence and core-helium burning. The e ﬃciency of these pre-
scriptions remains uncertain, and there is no observational evi-
dence that these magnetic instabilities operate in Pop III stars.
Therefore, the models should be interpreted as exploring one
possible (perhaps extreme) angular-momentum-transport sce-
nario rather than as establishing the probability of this channel.
Angular-momentum transport shapes the ﬁnal distribution of an-
gular momentum within the stellar interior since our baseline
models e ﬀectively loose no mass (see below) and, hence, con-
serve total angular momentum.

3. Discussion and Conclusions
The PPI boundary sets the maximum stellar mass that can col-
lapse to a BH without being disrupted (below the PIMG). This
limit is not determined by a unique initial mass, but by the on-
set of dynamical instability once electron–positron pair produc-
tion softens the EoS in su ﬃciently massive CO cores. Early
stellar-evolution and hydrodynamical studies placed the onset
of pulsational pair instability at CO-core masses of roughly 35–
40 M⊙ (Heger & Woosley 2002; Farmer et al. 2019; Woosley
2019), while more recent systematic grids suggest a nearly con-
stant critical value of MCO,crit ≈ 35–36 M⊙ (Winch et al. 2025),
with remaining uncertainties linked to nuclear reaction rates and
convection modelling (Renzo et al. 2020; Farag et al. 2022;
Woosley & Heger 2021). Below this threshold, stars are ex-
pected to collapse directly to BHs; above it, they may enter
a transitional regime in which weak or moderate pulsational
pair-instability (PPI) episodes can occur before ﬁnal collapse,
whereas complete PISN disruption requires substantially larger
cores (60 M⊙ ≲ MCO ≲ 130 M⊙).
Within this framework, our Pop III models approach the PPI
boundary from below. The 80 and 85 M⊙ sequences develop CO-
core masses between ≈ 31 and 35 .9 M⊙, that is, at or below the
nominal instability threshold.
For these 80 and 85 M⊙ models, the volume-averaged adi-
abatic index satisﬁes ⟨Γ1⟩ > 4/3, and thus show no evidence
for global dynamical instability. These models are therefore ex-
pected to collapse directly, producing BHs with masses up to
∼ 80–85 M⊙. By contrast, the 90 M⊙ models reach CO-core
masses of ∼ 36.5–38 M⊙, placing them in the transitional regime
commonly associated with the onset of pulsational pair insta-
bility mass ejection episodes /supernovae (sometimes referred
to as PPISNe, which are less violent than the the full PISNe;
Farmer et al. 2019; Winch et al. 2025). Although global insta-
bility has not been explicitly conﬁrmed for all our models, their
core masses fall within the range where PPISN is expected, de-
pending on the details of the late evolutionary stages. We stress
that placing these models near the lower PI boundary does not
imply complete disruption; rather, it indicates that pulsational
pair-instability eﬀects may begin to inﬂuence the ﬁnal mass and
structure. This reinforces the view that the PPI boundary is bet-
ter interpreted as a structural transition region than as a sharp
cut-oﬀ.
The most distinctive result of this work concerns angular-
momentum retention and its impact on the ﬁnal BH spin. Be-
cause metal-free models have negligible ordinary wind-driven
mass loss, angular momentum transported toward the surface
cannot be e ﬃciently removed and instead accumulates in the
outer envelope. These layers remain bound provided the star
does not rotate rapidly enough, ω ≲ 0.15, for centrifugal ejection
to occur. As a consequence, the BH that forms is initially close
to non-rotating, because the inner stellar regions have been e ﬃ-
ciently drained of angular momentum; magnetic torques are cru-
cial for this redistribution. The high ﬁnal BH spin is therefore not
inherited from an initially rapidly rotating core, but is produced
later by the accretion of outer layers where most of the stellar
angular momentum is stored.This strongly limits the feedback
exerted by the newly formed BH until the outermost layers—
where most of the angular momentum is stored—begin to ac-
crete. The BH can therefore grow substantially in mass before
its spin increases signiﬁcantly. This behaviour contrasts with that
of rapidly rotating progenitors, for which collapse may produce
fast-spinning BHs whose feedback can strongly reduce the ﬁnal

Using the simpliﬁed one-dimensional collapse and feedback
model (App. A), we estimate the fraction of mass and angular
momentum retained during disk formation and fallback. Even
under extreme assumptions, disk-driven outﬂows remove at most
10% of the stellar mass. The ﬁnal BH mass therefore retains
most of the progenitor mass. The ﬁnal spin reﬂects the interplay
between three factors: (i) outward angular-momentum redistri-
bution during stellar evolution, (ii) weak angular-momentum
loss at zero metallicity, and (iii) late accretion of high-angular-
momentum material. In our model grid, the dimensionless spin
parameter aBH increases with the initial rotation parameter ω
up to a maximum and then decreases, reaching peak values of
aBH ≃ 0.7 for Mini ≃ 85 M⊙. This non-monotonic behaviour
shows that rapid BH rotation does not require extreme initial
stellar rotation. Rather, even moderately rotating Pop III stars
can produce heavy and rapidly spinning remnants, provided that
winds are negligible and the progenitor remains below the PPI
boundary. This conclusion is broadly consistent with rotating
stellar grids predicting BH masses up to ∼ 80–90 M⊙ just be-
low the PPI regime at very low metallicity (Hirschi et al. 2025).
These results provide a useful point of comparison with
GW231123, whose components have inferred masses and spins
of M1 = 137+23
−18 M⊙, a1 = 0.90+0.10
−0.19, and M2 = 101+22
−50 M⊙,

−0.52 (Abac et al. 2025). We stress, however, that our
calculations are single-star models and do not address the bi-
nary assembly of GW231123. In particular, we do not model
tidal interactions, mass transfer, envelope stripping, or the orbital
separations required to preserve the hydrogen envelope while
still producing a binary black hole that merges within a Hub-
ble time. Within the mass range explored here, only the prop-
erties of the secondary BH can be approached, and only from
the lower-mass side. Our models produce BHs with masses up
to ∼ 80–90 M⊙ and spins up to aBH ≃ 0.7, slightly below but
still broadly consistent, within present uncertainties, with the in-
ferred spin of the secondary. The substantially more massive pri-
mary component lies beyond the progenitor masses considered
here and likely requires either more massive progenitors above
the PIMG or a di ﬀerent formation channel. The connection be-
tween such single-star remnants and a merging binary system,
whether through isolated binary evolution or dynamical pairing,
must therefore be investigated in future work. More generally,
gravitational-wave population studies suggest a downturn in the
BH mass distribution between ∼ 45 and 60 M⊙, commonly asso-
ciated with the PI mass gap (Fishbach & Holz 2017; Farr et al.
2019), although more recent analyses do not require a sharp cut-
oﬀ below ∼ 40–50 M⊙ (Ray & Kalogera 2026). This is consistent
with interpreting the PPI boundary as a transition region rather
than as an abrupt limit.
In summary, the PPI boundary acts as a physical ceiling for
single-star BH formation. Below this ceiling, however, slowly
rotating metal-free stars can naturally produce heavy and rapidly
spinning BHs, provided that mass loss remains negligible and
angular momentum is e ﬃciently retained. The combination of
weak mass loss, internal angular-momentum redistribution, and
the retention of most of the stellar mass during collapse suggests
that, within the adopted angular-momentum-transport prescrip-
tions, heavy, fast-spinning BHs in the early Universe do not re-
