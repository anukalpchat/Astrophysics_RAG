1 Introduction
NGC 6793 open cluster (OC) resides in the first Galac-
tic quadrant and in the constellation of Vulpecula.
NGC 6793 is poorly studied in UBVRI filters but it
was extensively studied with Gaia data (See Tables 3
and 8). Thus, we present its new astrophysical parame-
ters, reddening E(B-V), distance modulus (𝜇), distance
(d) and Age from new CCD𝑈 𝐵𝑉(𝑅𝐼) 𝐾𝐶 photometry
within the Sierra San Pedro Mártir National Astronomi-
calObservatoryOpenClusterSurvey(SPMO,hereafter).
In conjunction with this, SPMO presents a rare quality
and reliable observations of the Galactic open clusters
(Schuster et al., 2007; Tapia et al., 2010). The photomet-
ric analyses towards the astrophysical parameters have
been made with the help of the(𝑈−𝐵) , (𝐵−𝑉) colour-
colour (CC) and colour-magnitude diagrams (CMD)
whicharebuiltwithnewCCD 𝑈 𝐵𝑉(𝑅𝐼) 𝐾𝐶 photometry.
Its metal abundance for the isochrone selection has been
obtained from the photomtric metallicity calibration,
[𝐹𝑒/𝐻] −𝛿 0.6 of Karataş et al. (2006).
Gaia DR3 astrometric/photometric and radial veloc-
ity data have also been utilised for the separation of the
cluster members, obtaining of the structural/dynamical
parameters, andthusestimatingkinematics, respectively.
The dynamical parameters for NGC 6793 were derived
by Hunt & Reffert (2023) (hereafter H23), Almeida et
al. (2023, 2025) and Angelo et al. (2025) (hereafter
A25) from Gaia DR3 data. We interprete its dynamical
evolution in the light of new astrophysical parameters
(distance and age) from new CCD𝑈 𝐵𝑉(𝑅𝐼) 𝐾𝐶. The
dynamical evolution of a individual OC like NGC 6793
can properly be interpreted from the trends between the

Heggie & Hut (2003) have described the internal
dynamics evolution of a star cluster in the𝑅𝑐/𝑅 ℎ versus
𝑅ℎ/𝑅𝑡 plane. As a star cluster expands to the point of
being tidally filling, it experiences violent relaxation in
its core region followed by two-body relaxation, mass
segregation, and finally core-collapse. These processes
are summarized in their Fig.2a. The most massive stars
in an open cluster transfer/impart kinetic energy to the
lower-mass stars. As a result, the centrally concentrated
massive stars should have significantly lower velocities
than fast-moving low-mass objects on the periphery of
the cluster. This is known as energy equipartition.

43Age and Distance
NGC6793possessesonlyasparsepopulationofevolved
starsneartheMainSequenceTurn-Off(MSTO)(Fig.10),
andthelow-masspre–main-sequence"turn-on"isincom-
plete in Gaia photometry. Furthermore, gyrochronol-
ogy, which derives ages from stellar rotation, cannot be
applied here as the required rotation periods for clus-
ter members are currently unavailable in the literature.
Consequently, to overcome the lack of a well-defined
turn-off or rotation-based age, we performed a Bayesian
grid-based isochrone fitting following the formalism
of Naylor & Jeffries (2006).This approach utilizes the
entire main sequence (291 Gaia-selected members) to
provide a statistically robust age and distance. The ex-
plored parameter space coveredlog(𝑡/yr)=7.0–10.0
and 𝜇=7.0–10.0 mag, both in steps of 0.02. The
posterior probability distribution, constructed from a
𝜒2-based likelihood comparison between observed and
model CMDs, was marginalized over each parame-
ter to derive the 68% credible intervals. We em-
ployed the isochrones (Bressan et al., 2012) (PARSEC
isochrones) with fixed metallicity (𝑍=0.024 ) and red-
dening (𝐸(𝐵−𝑉)=0.24).
To ensure the robustness of the solution, we per-
formed the fitting using the full membership catalog
and then repeated the analysis restricted to stars below
the nominal turn-off region; the resulting parameters
remained consistent within1𝜎, confirming that our age
is well-constrained by the MS slope despite the scarcity
of evolved stars.The maximum–a–posteriori (MAP) so-
lution yields log(𝑡/yr)=8.74±0.08 (𝑡=550±100
Myr) and𝜇=8.77±0.22 mag (𝑑=575±58 pc), con-
sistent within errors with the distances given in Tables
6 and 7. This result, represented by the red star in the
joint posterior panel of Fig. 8, lies near the peak of the
probability distribution. A mild secondary maximum
in the joint posterior reflects the usual age–distance de-
generacy, though the primary peak remains the most
probable. Therelativelybroad ageuncertaintyprimarily
arises from the small number of evolved stars and the
limited evolutionary leverage along the main sequence
(e.g., Bonatto & Bica, 2007; Bossini et al., 2019).
