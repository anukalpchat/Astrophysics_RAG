1 INTRODUCTION
The origin of the most massive globular cluster (GC) in the Milky
Way (MW) NGC 5139, or Omega Centauri, has been the topic of
muchdiscussionoverseveraldecades.Themostpopularsuggestionis
thatOmegaCentauri(hereafter𝜔Cen)isthenucleatedcoreofalong-
sinceaccreteddwarfgalaxy(e.g.,Bekki&Freeman2003;Villanova
et al. 2014), but as to which specific merger event is still unclear.
Indeed, using data fromGaiaData Release 2 (DR2) (Gaia Collabo-
ration et al. 2018a),𝜔Cen has been linked to theSequoia(Myeong
etal.2019)andtheGaia-Enceladus-Sausagemergers(Massarietal.
2019) based on its location in action-energy spaces, in both cases
as the remnant nuclear star cluster of the progenitor. More recently,
Pagnini et al. (2025) have revisited the origin of𝜔Cen, comple-
mentingGaiadatawiththeadditionofchemistryfromtheAPOGEE
survey, and argue that it stems from a potentially different merger,
which they callNephele. There are both similarities and differences
between these proposed progenitor systems, which has led (Souza
etal.2026)toexplorethepossibilitythattheyareallpartofasingle
merger event. However, the origin of𝜔Cen remains under debate.
One of the challenges in linking𝜔Cen to a particular merger is

Taken together, this suggests a long and complex star formation

its retrograde orbit (e.g., Dinescu et al. 1999; Vasiliev & Baumgardt

WiththemostrecentdatareleasesfromtheGaiaspacemission,in-
creasingly high precision astrometry and photometry meaurements
have become available for over a billion stars (Gaia Collaboration
et al. 2021, 2023). The astrometry measurements are particularly

Sollima 2020; Ibata et al. 2021). This is of particular relevance to𝜔
Cen,whichliesatlowGalacticlatitude,whereidentifyingperipheral
and tidally-stripped stars is very challenging due to the overwhelm-
ing Milky Way field populations. In the case of𝜔Cen, Kuzma et al.
(2021, hereafter K21) combined photometry and astrometry from
GaiaEarlyDataRelease3withaBayesianinferencemodeltosearch
for faint tidal structure in its outer regions. Specifically, a mixture
model was constructed that incorporated information about stellar
positions, proper motions and location on the colour-magnitude di-
agram (CMD), and this demonstrated the existence of tidal tails,
extending out to a radius of≈450 pc (≈5 deg). This agnostic ap-
proach enables a data-driven exploration of the regions surrounding
MW GCs (Kuzma et al., in prep), and in the case of𝜔Cen we
demonstrated how powerful this technique can be for uncovering
tidal extended structures. In fact, several previous studies attempted
to identify tails from𝜔Cen with varying degrees of success (e.g.,
Leon et al. 2000; Fernández-Trincado et al. 2015; Sollima 2020);
only with DR3 did the most compelling evidence for its tidal tails
emerge.
To add to the puzzle of𝜔Cen, it has also been linked to tidal
streams in the MW halo. Ibata et al. (2019, hereafter I19) usedGaia
DR2 to uncover a stellar stream that they namedFimbulthul, which
theyassociatedto𝜔Cenonthebasisofitssimilarorbit,distance,and
stellarpopulations,evenalthoughitislocatedalmost28 ◦ away.This
comparison is based on fiveFimbulthulstream stars and a matched
filter analysis, with additional support from an N-body simulation
performed by the authors. Since then, other studies have searched
for additionalFimbulthulmembers, and/or other streams that could
be attributed to𝜔Cen. For example, Ibata et al. (2024) increase the
number ofFimbulthulmember stars to 22, and show that another
stream, which they callstream #55, resides in the same location
in action-energy space. They argue both streams are trailing debris
from𝜔Cen,whichisagainbroadlyconsistentwiththepredictionsof
theirN-bodymodel.Ontheotherhand,theconnectionbetweenFim-
bulthuland𝜔Cenislessclearwhenconsideringchemistry.Simpson
etal.(2020)searchedforFimbulthulmembersintheGALAHdataset
(De Silva et al. 2015; Buder et al. 2018), and identified two stars in
the stream with similarly enhanced s-process elements to the main
body of𝜔Cen, suggesting the first chemical connection ofFim-
bulthulto the cluster. Also using GALAH, Youakim et al. (2023)
blindly searched for𝜔Cen stars in the MW halo by applying an un-
supervisedmachine-learningtechnique.Althoughtheyfoundseveral
stars with light element abundances consistent with𝜔Cen scattered
throughoutthehalo,nonewerespatiallycoincidentwithFimbulthul.
Finally,an analysisof r-processabundances inthe streamconducted
by Gull et al. (2021) led the authors to suggest that it is more likely
thatFimbulthuland𝜔Cen share a progenitor, not thatFimbulthul
is debris from𝜔Cen itself. These conflicting interpretations call for
more exploration to fully understand the connection betweenFim-
bulthuland𝜔Cen.
A recent simulation of the disruption of𝜔Cen by Zheng et al.
(2026) indicates that its tidal tails should maintain the same stellar
population mix as that seen within the central regions, with no ex-
pectedpopulationgradient.Thisisinagreementwiththefindingsof
Kuzma & Ishigaki (2025), who saw the same multiple stellar pop-
ulation signatures in the main body and outer regions of𝜔Cen,
including its tidal tails. This analysis, which was performed with
synthetic photometry from Pristine DR1 (Martin et al. 2024) and
Gaiaastrometry, underscores the importance of considering both
metallicitiesandkinematics for studying tidal debris around GCs.
However,therelativelyshallowPristinesyntheticphotometrymeant
that only the sparse red giant branch (RGB) component of the tails
could be explored.
In this study, we present the results of a spectroscopic survey
of the peripheral regions and tidal tails of𝜔Cen, following up on
the identification of probable members in these parts taken from
K21. The only previous spectroscopic study to specifically target
the outer regions of the cluster is that of Da Costa (2012, hereafter
DC12), who covered cluster-centric radii of 25 to 45 arcmin and
foundaflatline-of-sightvelocityprofile,tentativelyattributedtothe
dynamical evolution of the cluster within the Galactic gravitational
field. Our study, which probes more than four times further out,
alsonicelycomplementstherecentoMEGACatseriesofpapersthat
aims to improve understanding of the main body of𝜔Cen (e.g.,
Nitschaietal.2023;Häberleetal.2024;Nitschaietal.2024).Inthe
subsequent sections, we will describe the methods used to reduce
and analyse the spectra (Sec. 2), identify𝜔Cen stars and explore
their kinematics and metallicity as a function of position (Sec. 3),
and discuss our findings (Sec. 4).

2.2 Data Reduction
The spectra were reduced using the pipelines available from ESO,
EsoReflex2 (Freudlingetal.2013),andweusedthedefaultcalibra-
tionsetoftheassociatedGIRAFFEworkflow.Thisworkflowfollows
the standard steps of spectroscopic reduction: bias-subtraction, flat-
fielding,wavelengthcalibrationandheliocentricvelocitycorrection.
Additionally, we utilised the additional sky-subtraction and cosmic
ray subtraction usingPyCosmic(Husemann et al. 2012), which is
available as an extension package withEsoReflex.
The line-of-sight heliocentric velocities,𝑉h, and stellar metallic-
ities, [Fe/H], were calculated using the freely-available automated
spectroscopic pipelinervspecfit(Koposov et al. 2011; Koposov
2019).Thepipelinecanfitmultiplespectraofthesameobjectsimul-
taneously, allowing for precision velocities and atmospheric param-
eters to be calculated using a maximum-likelihood technique. For
this work, we supplied either both the individual (uncombined) sky-
subtractedspectraforeachstarorjustthesinglespectraifthestarwas
only observed once (e.g., due to fiber unavailability between plates)
and recorded the measurements and their associated uncertainties
provided byrvspecfit. We also compared the velocity output of
rvspecfitto the velocities of the spectral template cross-correlation
technique used in Kuzma et al. (2022), and found a near one-to-one
correspondence.
Targets where eitherrvspecfitwas unable to perform the fit ef-
fectively, or where the𝑉h uncertainties were greater than 5 km s−1
(typically due to poor signal-to-noise) were removed. This affected
23 stars, including 13 stars with𝑃mem >0.3. This corresponds to
4 per cent of the original sample, and resulted in a final sample
of 593 stars with which we search for𝜔Cen members. Of these,
166 stars have a high-probability (i.e,𝑃mem >0.3) of membership
from our Bayesian technique. As noted previously, three stars are
in common with DC12, which gave us an opportunity to search for
any systematic velocity offset in our measurements. We measure
(𝑉h −𝑉DC12)=0.7+ /−0.5km s −1, which places our velocities in
excellent agreement, and well within the measurement uncertainties
of our data. Indeed, the median radial velocity uncertainty of our
final sample is𝜎𝑉h =1.5km s −1.

4 CONCLUSION
In this paper, we present VLT/FLAMES spectroscopic observations
of candidate members in the peripheral regions and tidal tails of the
massive GC,𝜔Cen. We sampled six locations spanning 6 degrees,
ranging from a cluster-centric radii of 45 arcmin to 3 degrees (ap-
proximately 75 to 300 pc), extending more than four times further
out than any previous study of the𝜔Cen main body. Our spectro-
scopic targets were selected using the Bayesian inference model of
K21, which provided membership probabilities based onGaiapho-
tometryandastrometry.Themeasurementofline-of-sightvelocities
and[Fe/H]allowedustoassesswhichofthesecandidateswerefully
consistent with membership of𝜔Cen. We find that the majority of
our high-probability (𝑃mem >0.3) stars are indeed consistent with
belonging to𝜔Cen, nominating 157bona fidestars across the six
degreeofextentofourstudy.Fiveofthesestarslieinthetidalexten-
sions, while several more in the tails are nominally low-probability
members but with line-of-sight velocities and [Fe/H] measurements
strongly suggestive of𝜔Cen membership.
We explore the properties of the peripheral and tidal tail stars as
a function of position along the direction of the stream. Due to the
paucity of measurements at large distances along the tails, it is not
possible to draw firm conclusions about the radial behaviour but
we see no evidence of strong gradients in either Vh or [Fe/H]. We
compare our measurements to those in the literature as well as to
the predictions of an N-body simulation of the cluster’s disruption
from I19. Our Vh measurements show good consistency with those
from I19, which was specifically tailored to match the properties
of theFimbulthulstream. We also find good agreement with the
measurements presented in DC12 which cover the radial range of
10 - 40 arcmin, lying inward of and slightly overlapping with our
data. Furthermore, our [Fe/H] abundances compare well to those
of the𝜔Cen extra-tidal population presented in Kuzma & Ishigaki
(2025),basedonthePristinephotometricsurvey.Wefindadifference

cover only a small area compared to the large extent of the cluster
periphery and its tails. Even with our Bayesian inference technique
to guide us on the selection of probable members, the number of
stars detected in𝜔Cen tidal tails remains very small. Much larger
samples of stars at large radius will be required to fully understand
𝜔Cen’sorigin,disruptionandlinktoothersubstructuresintheMW
halo.Thevastspatialextentandsparsenatureofthetidalextensions
demands a specialised approach, combining modeling work with
deep wide-field multi-object spectroscopy. New and forthcoming
facilities, such as WEAVE on the WHT 4.2m (Dalton et al. 2012),
4MOST on the VISTA 4m telecope (De Jong et al. 2019; Lucatello
et al. 2023), MOONs (Cirasuolo et al. 2020) and the Prime Focus
Spectrograph(PFS;Tamuraetal.2016)areideallysuitedtothestudy
ofGCoutskirts.Inthenearfuture,wewillbeabletodissectthefaint
structures around𝜔Cen fully, and uncover the secrets of the tidal
tails belonging to the most enigmatic cluster in the Galaxy.
