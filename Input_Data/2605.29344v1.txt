ABSTRACT
We analyze the rest-frame optical (∼8000 ˚A) morphologies and star formation activity of Lyα
emitters (LAEs) at redshifts 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5, identified in the ODIN survey. To compare their physical
properties with those of other galaxies, we construct a comparison sample of typical star-forming
galaxies (SFGs) at similar redshifts from the COSMOS2025 catalog. Using theJWST/NIRCam images
from the COSMOS-Web survey, we measure the rest-frame optical sizes and S´ ersic indices. We first
examine their size-mass relations and find that LAEs at all three redshifts have smaller sizes than typical
SFGs, with the size difference decreasing at higher redshifts. We also find that LAEs tend to have larger
S´ ersic indices atz= 2.4 and 3.1 than typical SFGs, but the difference becomes weaker atz= 4.5. These
trends are qualitatively reproduced in the Horizon Run 5 cosmological hydrodynamical simulation. We
then investigate star formation activity and find that LAEs exhibit higher star formation rates than
typical SFGs at all redshifts considered. Finally, we examine the connection between Lyαemission
and galaxy structure, finding that the rest-frame equivalent width (REW) of the Lyαemission line has

1.INTRODUCTION
Studying the large-scale distribution of galaxies and
its impact on galaxy properties at high redshifts is cru-
cial for understanding the formation and evolution of
galaxies (e.g., R. Shimakawa et al. 2015; C. Laigle et al.
2018; J. M. P´ erez-Mart´ ınez et al. 2024). Since R. B. Par-
tridge & P. J. E. Peebles (1967) presented the possibility
of using the redshifted Lyαemission line to study star-
forming galaxies at high redshifts, Lyαemitters (LAEs)
have become a valuable population for studying large-
scale structures and galaxy evolution at high redshifts
(e.g., M. Ouchi et al. 2005; K.-S. Lee et al. 2014; S. H.
Im et al. 2024; V. Ramakrishnan et al. 2024). LAEs
are generally known as young, low-dust, and low-mass
star-forming galaxies, undergoing bursts of star forma-
tion (e.g., E. Gawiser et al. 2007; L. Guaita et al. 2010;
N. M. Firestone et al. 2025). They are also considered
as one of important contributors to the cosmic reioniza-
tion (e.g., C. Simmonds et al. 2023; G. C. Jones et al.
2025).
However, the exact physical mechanisms that govern
the production and escape of Lyαphotons from the in-
terstellar and circumgalactic medium remain poorly un-
derstood. This is primarily because Lyαphotons un-
dergo complex resonant scattering with neutral hydro-
gen or are absorbed by dust while escaping galaxies (e.g.,
D. A. Neufeld 1990; S.-H. Ahn et al. 2003; A. Verhamme
et al. 2006; M. Hansen & S. P. Oh 2006; A. Verhamme
et al. 2008; P. Laursen et al. 2009; M. Gronke et al. 2015;
M. Dijkstra 2017; K. Kakiichi & M. Dijkstra 2018; A.
Smith et al. 2019; H. Song et al. 2020; S. Yu et al. 2025;
E. Seo et al. 2026). Therefore, it is challenging to di-
rectly connect the observed properties of the Lyαemis-
sion line (e.g., its luminosity, spectral shape, or spatial
extent) to the intrinsic properties of the host galaxy.
A common approach to studying the physical con-
ditions for Lyαphoton escape is to examine the dis-
tinct features of LAEs compared to the entire popula-
tion of star-forming galaxies (SFGs) or those not se-
lected based on Lyαemission. For example, studies us-
ing the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) find that LAEs
have smaller sizes than star-forming galaxies (e.g., N. A.
Bond et al. 2009; K. Kim et al. 2025). However, such
studies were limited to rest-frame UV morphologies,
which are strongly affected by dust and clumpy star-
forming regions within galaxies. Only recently, there
are several studies using the James Webb Space Tele-
scope (JWST; J. P. Gardner et al. 2023), confirming
their compact nature of LAEs at rest-frame optical mor-
phologies (e.g., Y. Liu et al. 2024; Y. Ning et al. 2024;
Q. Song et al. 2026). Nevertheless, a robust comparison
with SFGs remains limited by small sample sizes, broad
redshift ranges, or the reliance on extrapolated scaling
relations.
On the other hand, there are also numerous studies
that examine the physical properties of LAEs includ-
ing stellar mass and star formation activity. These
properties are usually obtained through spectral en-
ergy distribution (SED) fittings, which requires robust
multi-wavelength photometry. Numerous studies per-
form SED fittings of LAEs and find that they are low-
mass, young, and starbursting populations (e.g., E. Ga-
wiser et al. 2007; L. Guaita et al. 2011; C.-N. Hao et al.
2018; S. Shimizu et al. 2025). However, most of these
studies rely on parametric star formation history (SFH)
models, which can impose priors on SFH reconstruction
and bias the resulting stellar mass or star formation rate
measurements (e.g., C. Pacifici et al. 2015; K. Iyer &
E. Gawiser 2017; A. C. Carnall et al. 2019; K. G. Iyer
et al. 2019; J. Leja et al. 2019).
The One-hundred-deg 2 DECam Imaging in Narrow-
band (ODIN; K.-S. Lee et al. 2024) is an ongoing sur-
vey to identify LAEs at three redshifts (z= 2.4, 3.1,
and 4.5) using three custom narrow-band filters (N419,
N501, andN673). ODIN performs deep narrow-band
imaging with 5σlimiting magnitudes of 25.5−25.9 over
seven different wide fields, totaling a survey area of
∼91 deg 2. By combining this narrow-band imaging
with archival broad-band photometric data, ODIN will
identify>100,000 LAEs in total. With these LAEs, we
plan to study large-scale structures including protoclus-
ters at high redshifts and their impact on galaxy evo-
lution (e.g., V. Ramakrishnan et al. 2024). A complete
description of the survey design is provided in K.-S. Lee
et al. (2024), and the detailed LAE selection methodol-
ogy is described in N. M. Firestone et al. (2024).

In this paper, we examine the rest-frame optical
(∼8,000 ˚A) morphologies of ODIN LAEs at redshifts
2.4, 3.1, and 4.5 in the COSMOS field (N. Scoville
et al. 2007), using theJWSTNIRCam images from the
COSMOS-Web survey (C. M. Casey et al. 2023). We
also construct samples of typical star-forming galaxies
(SFGs) at similar redshifts from the COSMOS2025 cata-
log (M. Shuntov et al. 2025) for a robust and direct com-
parison between LAEs and typical SFGs. We also inves-
tigate the star formation activities of the samples using
the SED fitting measurements with non-parametric SFH
models from COSMOS2025 catalog. This approach al-
lows us to systematically measure the rest-frame opti-
cal morphologies and physical properties of both ODIN
LAEs and typical SFGs from COSMOS2025, enabling a
robust comparison between the two populations. We
note that non-parametric SFH reconstruction is par-
ticularly relevant for our analysis, as recent ODIN re-
sults with such SFH reconstruction show that most of
the ODIN LAEs are undergoing their primary starburst
(N. M. Firestone et al. 2025).
We describe our samples of LAEs and the comparison
galaxies from the COSMOS2025 catalog in Section 2.
In Section 3, we describe theJWSTimages that we use
for morphological analysis and our methodology for size
measurements, and present our results and discussions
in Sections 4 and 5, respectively. Finally, we conclude
and summarize our results in Section 6. Throughout this
study, we adopt flat Λ Cold Dark Matter cosmological
parameters of Ω m = 0.3, Ω Λ = 0.7, Ω b = 0.047,σ 8 =
0.816, andh= 0.684 as compatible with Planck data (
Planck Collaboration et al. 2016).
2.SAMPLES
2.1.ODIN LAEs
We utilize LAEs at redshifts 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5 identified
in the∼9 deg 2 region of the extended COSMOS field
by the ODIN survey (K.-S. Lee et al. 2024; N. M. Fire-
stone et al. 2024). The 5σlimiting magnitudes are 25.5,
25.7, and 25.9 for theN419,N501, andN673 filters, re-
spectively. From these narrow-band observations, com-
bined with archival broad-band photometry, we identify
LAEs as objects with significant narrow-band excess,
which corresponds to a Lyαrest-frame equivalent width
(REW) greater than 20 ˚A. We also use the narrow-
band excesses and broad-band colors to remove possible
contaminators such as foreground [OII] and [OIII] emit-
ters. Follow-up spectroscopic observations conducted
with the DESI for thez= 2.4 and 3.1 samples indicate
that the contamination fraction for the ODINN419 and
N501 LAEs is≲10% (M. White et al. 2024). A more
detailed explanation and validation of the LAE selection
methods can be found in N. M. Firestone et al. (2024).
The total number of LAE candidates identified in the
extended COSMOS field is 6100, 5782, and 4101 forz=
2.4, 3.1, and 4.5, respectively. From this parent sample,
we focus only on those within the survey area of the
COSMOS-Web survey data release 1 (DR1; M. Franco
et al. 2025), which is used for our morphological analysis
in Section 3. The number of LAEs in this area is 338,
316, and 420 for redshifts 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5, respectively.
2.2.Star-forming Galaxies from COSMOS2025
Catalog
To compare the physical properties of our LAEs with
those of other galaxy populations, we construct compar-
ison samples of typical SFGs at redshifts similar to the
ODIN LAEs. We start from the COSMOS2025 cata-
log (M. Shuntov et al. 2025), which covers the objects
in the COSMOS-Web DR1 survey area. This catalog
provides measurements of photometric redshift and var-
ious physical parameters derived from SED fitting us-
ing theLePHAREcode (S. Arnouts et al. 2002; O. Il-
bert et al. 2006), based on photometry in 37 bands
with a wavelength range of 0.3−8µm. We first select
the sources with photometric redshifts centered on each
ODIN target redshift: i.e.,|∆z phot|<0.2. TheLePHARE
code also classifies each source based on its best-fit SED
template type. We consider only the sources classified
as “galaxy”, meaning that their best-fit template is a
galaxy template. To consider only the sources with reli-
able photometry, we also require that there are no known
problems in their photometry (i.e.,warn flag= 0).
In addition to theLePHAREresults, the COSMOS2025
catalog also provides measurements of various physi-
cal parameters, including stellar mass and SFR derived
from theCIGALESED fitting code (M. Boquien et al.
2019). This code utilizes non-parametric SFH models,
with redshifts fixed to theLePHAREresults. Specifi-
cally, the SFH is reconstructed using ten logarithmically
spaced time bins, with the first bin fixed at 10 Myr (see
R. C. Arango-Toro et al. 2025, for detailed settings). As
noted by several studies (e.g., C. Pacifici et al. 2015; K.
Iyer & E. Gawiser 2017; A. C. Carnall et al. 2019; K. G.
Iyer et al. 2019; J. Leja et al. 2019), non-parametric
models generally provide a more reliable reconstruction
of SFHs than parametric models. This is because para-
metric models can impose priors on SFH reconstruction,
which may bias the stellar mass or SFR measurements.
Therefore, in this paper, we use the SED fitting results
fromCIGALErather than those byLePHARE, which em-
ploy parametric SFH models. We refer the reader to
R. C. Arango-Toro et al. (2025) for a detailed valida-

tion of these physical parameters (stellar mass, SFR,
and SFH) derived fromCIGALE.
We select typical SFGs using a simple cut in the spe-
cific star-formation rate (sSFR) of log(sSFR/yr −1)>
−9.5. This threshold is slightly lower than the sSFR
of typical Lyman-break galaxies atz >2 (see e.g.,
N. A. Reddy et al. 2012; C. Jose et al. 2014). We note
that using other selection criteria, such as rest-frame
N U V−r−Jcolor-color selection commonly used in
literature (e.g., O. Ilbert et al. 2013; K. Ito et al. 2024;
L. Yang et al. 2025), does not change the main con-
clusions of this paper. The number of selected typical
SFGs is 36621, 20885, and 17536 forz= 2.4, 3.1, and
4.5, respectively.
2.3.Final Samples
We cross-match the ODIN LAEs with the COS-
MOS2025 catalog using a maximum sky separation of
1′′, and retrieve the stellar mass and SFR measure-
ments. To secure reliable SED fitting results, we con-
sider only the LAEs with theLePHAREphotometric red-
shifts consistent with the ODIN target redshifts (i.e.,
|∆zphot|<0.2) 25. We also reject ODIN LAEs and typi-
cal SFGs with multipleJWSTNIRCam sources within
0.′′7 from the source position. This allows us to avoid
any systematics of blended photometry or misidentifi-
cation between ground-based (i.e., ODIN narrow-bands
and others in COSMOS2025) and space-based observa-
tions (i.e.,JWSTandHST). These processes result in
final sample sizes of 111, 109, and 134 ODIN LAEs, and
23342, 12819, and 10160 typical SFGs for redshifts 2.4,
3.1, and 4.5, respectively. The F277W and F444W mag-
nitude distributions of these final samples are consistent
with those of their parent samples, suggesting that they
are representative subsets. Therefore, we assume that
this selection does not introduce significant bias in our
main results. A summary of the sample sizes after each
selection step is provided in Table 1. We note that most
of the ODIN LAEs are included in the typical SFG sam-
ples, as we do not exclude LAEs from those samples.
Removing these LAEs from the typical SFG samples
does not alter our main conclusions as their fraction is
negligible (∼1%).

3.SIZES MEASUREMENTS
3.1.COSMOS-Web Images
To study rest-frame optical (∼8000 ˚A) morphologies
of the ODIN LAEs and the typical SFGs atz= 2.4,
3.1, and 4.5, we use theJWSTNIRCam images from the
COSMOS-Web DR1 (C. M. Casey et al. 2023; M. Franco
et al. 2025). It provides mosaic images in four NIRCam
filters (F115W, F150W, F277W, and F444W) with the
angular pixel size of 30 mas for a contiguous 0.54 deg 2
area in the COSMOS field. We choose to study rest-
frame∼8000 ˚A because it corresponds to the longest
wavelength accessible with the F444W filter atz= 4.5,
thereby minimizing the effects of dust attenuation. We
specifically utilize the images in F277W and F444W fil-
ters, with central wavelengths of 2.77 and 4.44µm, re-
spectively, to study rest-frame∼8000 ˚A morphologies
of the galaxies atz= 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5.
We generate cutout images of the science and vari-
ance mosaics for the F277W and F444W filters with the
sizes of 3 ′′ ×3 ′′ centered on the position of each ODIN
LAE and each COSMOS2025 SFG. This size, which is
∼20−25 kpc atz= 2.4−4.5, is sufficient to con-
tain most of the light from the galaxies in our samples.
Using these science cutouts, we first run theSource
Extractor(SExtractor;E. Bertin & S. Arnouts 1996)
to identify sources in each cutout. As described in Sec-
tion 2.3, we exclude the galaxies with multiple NIR-
Cam sources within 0.′′7. This allows us to securely con-
sider the NIRCam source within 0.′′7 as the counterpart
of each ODIN LAE and COSMOS2025 SFG, without
concern about systematic effects from misidentifications
or blended photometry. We then create mask images
for neighboring sources (i.e., those located at>0. ′′7)
within the cutouts using the segmentation map from
SExtractor. To conservatively remove the effects from
nearby sources, we extend the masked regions belong-
ing to the neighbors by one pixel. Finally, we generate a
bad-pixel mask for each cutout to ignore the pixels with
zero variance in our morphological analyses.
We identify and remove unresolved point sources using
the half-light radius (r h) and the magnitude in F277W,
based on the well-known tight correlation between the
two quantities for point sources (see e.g., B. W. Holw-
erda et al. 2024; E. Ward et al. 2024). We examine the
relation betweenr h and magnitude for our sample galax-
ies and reject those withr h consistent with or smaller
than that of point sources (see Appendix A for more de-
tails). The numbers of removed ODIN LAEs are 12, 6,
and 12 forz= 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5, respectively. The corre-
sponding numbers for the COSMOS2025 typical SFGs
are 1285, 693, and 1086.

3.2.GALFIT Runs
We utilizeGALFIT(C. Y. Peng et al. 2002, 2010) to
study the rest-frame optical morphologies of our sam-
ple galaxies. Here, we use the cutouts of the science
and variance images, along with mask images for both
nearby sources and bad pixels. We adopt a single S´ ersic
profile (J. L. S´ ersic 1963) to model each science cutout
and measure the effective radius (R e) and S´ ersic index,
as in previous studies (e.g., J. H. Lee et al. 2024; E.
Ward et al. 2024; M. Martorano et al. 2024; L. Yang
et al. 2025). For the samples atz= 2.4 and 4.5, we use
the F277W and F444W cutouts to measure rest-frame
optical (∼8000 ˚A) sizes and S´ ersic indices, respectively.
For the samples atz= 3.1, we independently measure
these parameters using both filters and perform a sim-
ple linear interpolation to infer their values at rest-frame
∼8000 ˚A. Here, we assume that the size and the S´ ersic
index of a galaxy change smoothly as a function of wave-
length, and can be modeled by simple polynomials (see
e.g., E. Ward et al. 2024; M. Martorano et al. 2024).
WhenGALFITfinds the best-fit model, it compares the
observed image to the modeled image convolved with
a point-spread function (PSF). Recent studies of high-
redshift galaxy morphologies based onJWSTNIRCam
mosaic images (e.g., J. H. Lee et al. 2024; M. Martorano
et al. 2024) report that the PSFs directly obtained from
the mosaics are slightly broader than those modeled with
theWEBBPSF(M. D. Perrin et al. 2014). Additionally,
some studies report that PSFs can have spatial varia-
tions as well (e.g., M.-Y. Zhuang & Y. Shen 2024). To
avoid any systematic biases of using an inappropriate
PSF, we empirically select stars and construct our own
PSFs using thePSFEx(E. Bertin 2011) software, as done
in other studies (e.g., M.-Y. Zhuang & Y. Shen 2024;
J. H. Lee et al. 2024). Because the entire COSMOS-Web
DR1 data are divided into 20 different mosaics, we select
stars and generate a PSF model for each filter and mo-
saic combination. The number of selected stars in each
mosaic is∼170−300 for F277W and∼80−140 for
F444W filter. Finally, we use the PSF model generated
from each mosaic for theGALFITfitting of the sample
galaxies within the same mosaic.
During the fitting process, we allow the source center
to move by±3 pixels from the source position deter-
mined by theSExtractor. We impose the constraints
on the effective radius of [0.1,50.] pixels, and on the
S´ ersic index of [0.1,10.]. We also impose a constraint on
the total magnitude of the best-fit model to be within
±0.5 mag from the Petrosian magnitude (V. Petrosian
1976) obtained withSExtractor.

et al. 2011, 2012), which measures the amount of the
signal in the residual image that cannot be explained by
background fluctuation. As in J. H. Lee et al. (2024),
we calculate RFF using the following equation:

5.DISCUSSIONS
5.1.Comparison with the Horizon Run 5 Simulation
In Section 4.1, we find that LAEs tend to have smaller
sizes and larger S´ ersic indices than typical SFGs at
z= 2.4 and 3.1, but those atz= 4.5 only show a hint of
size difference from typical SFGs. Here, we use the data
of Horizon Run 5 (HR5; J. Lee et al. 2021) cosmologi-
cal hydrodynamical simulation to examine whether this
observed trend could be reproduced. We start from the
galaxy catalogs at three snapshots ofz= 2.4, 3.1, and
4.5 within a cuboid region of 90×90×1,000 (cMpc) 3,
which has the highest resolution (∼1 physical kpc) in
the simulation. To consider only those galaxies with
statistically reliable physical properties, we select the
galaxies with stellar masses larger than 2.137×10 8 M⊙,
which corresponds to a mass of∼100 stellar particles.
We use these galaxies to construct samples of typical
SFGs by applying a simple specific star-formation rate
(sSFR) cut of log(sSFR/yr −1)>−9.5. We note that
using different values for the criterion does not change
the main results. The resulting numbers of selected typ-
ical SFGs are 318422, 261468, and 87504, forz= 2.4,
3.1, and 4.5, respectively.
For LAEs, we adopt the samples prepared in S. H. Im
et al. (2024), and briefly introduce the selection proce-
dure as follows. We first assign a Lyαrest-frame equiv-
alent width (REW) to each galaxy based on the empir-
ical relation between the UV luminosity and the Lyα
REW from C. A. Mason et al. (2018). This approach
is motivated by empirical studies assuming that LAEs
are a subset of Lyman-break galaxies, and that their
LyαREW distribution can be described as a conditional
probability function at a given UV luminosity (see e.g.,
M. Dijkstra & J. S. B. Wyithe 2012; L. H. Weinberger
et al. 2019). While this empirical modeling cannot deter-
mine the exact physical properties of individual LAEs,
it allows us to study the statistical properties of the
LAE population. We also determine Lyαemission line
luminosity (LLyα) using the LyαREW and the UV con-
tinuum level, which is calculated from stellar population
synthesis modeling. Then we select LAEs by adopting
the detection limit of the ODIN narrow-bands as the
minimum ofL Lyα. We also require LAEs to have a Lyα
REW larger than 20 ˚A, which is the desired minimum
LyαREW of the ODIN LAE-selections (see N. M. Fire-
stone et al. 2024). A more detailed description on LAE
selection in HR5 data is given in Section 2.2 of S. H. Im
et al. (2024). The numbers of LAEs atz= 2.4, 3.1, and
4.5 are 12715, 19552, and 7116, respectively.
We measure theR e and S´ ersic index of each HR5
galaxy from the distributions of the stellar particles pro-
jected on thex−yplane of the simulation box. First, we
determine the major and minor axes using eigenvectors
and eigenvalues of the moment of inertia tensor for the
stellar particles following C. Park et al. (2022). Then,
we construct ther-band (∼6200 ˚A) surface brightness
profile as a function of the radius along the major axis.
Finally, we fit this profile with a S´ ersic model to find
best-fit parameters. We exclude galaxies whose uncer-
tainties in the size and S´ ersic index are larger than the
corresponding best-fit values. This step removes 7479,
6738, and 2880 typical SFGs atz= 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5,
respectively (corresponds to≲3% of the parent sam-
ple). For LAEs, the numbers of excluded objects are
152, 270, and 134 atz= 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5, respectively
(corresponds to≲2% of the parent sample).

4.1.2, LAEs tend to have larger S´ ersic indices than typi-
cal SFGs atz= 2.4 and 3.1. However, atz= 4.5, LAEs
and typical SFGs have much smaller differences in their
S´ ersic index, while thep-values from KS- and AD tests
still suggest that the two distributions are different. The
difference between the median S´ ersic index of the two
populations is smaller than that of observation (see Fig-
ure 4). Again, this discrepancy could be attributed to
uncertainties and potential interlopers in observations.
Another possibility is the limitations of the empirical
LAE modeling in the simulation, which may not fully
account for the complex Lyαphoton escape process.
It is possible to use the HR5 data to further explore
the formation and evolution of LAEs and the physical
conditions for Lyαphoton production and escape. How-
ever, such a detailed analysis is beyond the scope of this
study and could be a topic of future studies.
5.2.Distinct Evolution in typical Size of LAE
Population compared to SFGs
To examine whether the LAE population has a dis-
tinct size evolution or growth rate compared to the typi-
cal SFG population, we investigate the evolution of their
typical sizes as a function of redshift. For this analysis,
we perform an MCMC fitting similar to that used for
the typical SFGs. The resulting best-fit parameters are
available in Table 3. Here, we assume that LAEs follow
a size-mass relation with the same power-law slope (β)
as typical SFGs, but with a different normalization fac-
tor (A) and the intrinsic scatterσ int. We note that the
resulting best-fit parameters are highly uncertain if we
leave all three parameters free. Lower panel of Figure 11
presents the sizes of LAEs and typical SFGs at a fixed
stellar mass of 5×10 10 M⊙, which corresponds to the
normalizationAin Equation (2). We also present the
results from the literature (K. Ormerod et al. 2024; E.
Ward et al. 2024; R. G. Varadaraj et al. 2024; L. Yang
et al. 2025) in Figure 11, even though the rest-frame
wavelengths used for their size measurements are slightly
different from ours. We find that the size difference be-
tween LAEs and typical SFGs decreases at higher red-
shifts, which is also well reproduced in the HR5 simula-
tion. This result is also consistent with the studies with
rest-frame UV sizes, which show little or no size evolu-
tion for LAE populations at fixed stellar mass, compared
to the entire or other (e.g., Lyman-break galaxies) pop-
ulation of SFGs (e.g., S. Malhotra et al. 2012; K. Kim
et al. 2025). This suggests that LAE populations may
have size growth rates distinct from typical SFGs, po-
tentially due to differences in their mass accretion rates
or the underlying physical mechanisms.
5.3.Physical Conditions for LyαEscape
In Section 4.1.1, we find that LAEs tend to have
smaller rest-frame optical sizes than typical SFGs at
similar redshifts. This is consistent with previous stud-
ies on rest-frame UV sizes of LAEs usingHSTdata (e.g.,
S. Malhotra et al. 2012; K. Kim et al. 2025). There are
also several recent studies with rest-frame optical sizes
usingJWST, showing similar results (e.g., Y. Liu et al.
2024; Y. Ning et al. 2024; Q. Song et al. 2026). In this
study, we have improved such analyses by providing di-
rect and robust comparisons between LAEs and typical
SFGs using large samples with systematically measured
sizes and stellar masses in small redshift ranges.
We also find a negative correlation between the rest-
frame optical sizes of ODIN LAEs and their LyαREW
in Section 4.3. This is consistent with the studies of
rest-frame UV size measurements usingHSTdata (e.g.,
N. A. Bond et al. 2009, 2012; A. Paulino-Afonso et al.
2018; J. Kerutt et al. 2022). Because the Lyαescape
fraction (f esc
Lyα) is known to be positively correlated with
LyαREW (e.g., D. Sobral & J. Matthee 2019; G. C.
Jones et al. 2025), this suggests that more compact
galaxies tend to have a higherf esc
Lyα, increasing their
probability of being observed as LAEs. This implies
that small physical size could be a key condition for the

studies with numerical simulations showing that super-

driven by stellar mass, independently of size, and that
the observed correlation is merely a secondary effect.
This is consistent with L. H. Weiss et al. (2021), who
found thatf esc

more tightly correlated with stellar mass than with the

sample of LAEs is required to distinguish the effect of
size, independent of stellar mass.
On the other hand, in Section 4.2, we find that LAEs
tend to be located above the star-forming main sequence

Y. Liu et al. 2024; Y. Ning et al. 2024) and cosmo-
logical simulations (e.g., S. H. Im et al. 2024). This
result suggests that extreme star-formation would be
another key physical condition for a galaxy to become
an LAE. Furthermore, in Section 4.3, we find a strong
positive correlation between LyαREW and the ratio
of the instantaneous star-formation rate to that aver-
aged over the last 100 Myr (i.e., SFR inst/SFR100Myr).
This is consistent with a recent ODIN result, showing
that most of the ODIN LAEs are experiencing their
most significant starburst in their star formation his-
tory (N. M. Firestone et al. 2025). As mentioned in
Section 4.3, a strong positive correlation between Lyα
REW and SFR inst/SFR100Myr is expected. Therefore,
the observed correlation indicates that the SED fitting of
the COSMOS2025 catalog successfully reconstructs the
recent SFH, likely due to the medium-band photome-
try. Furthermore, this implies that the complex Lyα
radiative transfer process does not significantly distort
or erase the intrinsic relation between LyαREW and
SFRinst/SFR100Myr.

6.CONCLUSIONS
We analyzed rest-frame optical (∼8000 ˚A) mor-
phologies and star formation activities of Lyαemit-
ters (LAEs) atz= 2.4, 3.1, and 4.5 in comparison
with typical SFGs at similar redshifts. We utilized
theJWST/NIRCam F277W and F444W images of the
COSMOS-Web survey to examine their rest-frame opti-
cal morphologies. We also adopted the physical param-
eters of stellar mass and star formation rates derived
from theCIGALESED fitting of the COSMOS2025 cat-
alog. Our primary results are as follows.
(I) We compared the size-mass relations of LAEs and
typical SFGs, and found that LAEs tend to have
smaller sizes than typical SFGs across all three
redshifts. We found that LAEs have larger S´ ersic
indices than typical SFGs atz= 2.4 and 3.1,
whereas we could not find statistically significant
difference atz= 4.5. We also examined the star-
formation main-sequence relations and found that
LAEs are located above the main sequence. These
findings show the compact and starbursting na-
ture of LAEs compared to SFGs, which are also
reproduced in the Horizon Run 5 cosmological hy-
drodynamical simulation.
(II) We found that the rest-frame equivalent width
(REW) of the Lyαemission line has negative and
positive correlations with the rest-frame optical
size and the S´ ersic index, respectively. Given
the known positive correlation between the Lyα
escape fraction (f esc
Lyα) and the LyαREW, this
finding suggests that galaxies with smaller phys-
ical sizes and higher central concentrations favor
higherf esc
Lyα, increasing their probability of being
observed as LAEs.
(III) We found strong positive correlations between
the Lya REW and the ratio of the current star-
formation rate to that averaged over the last
100 Myr (i.e., SFR inst/SFR100Myr), which is ex-
pected. This indicates that the SED fitting of the
COSMOS2025 catalog successfully reconstructs
recent SFH, and that the Lyαradiative transfer
process does not significantly distort the intrinsic
relationship.
(IV) We found that the size difference between LAEs
and typical SFGs decreases with increasing red-
shift. This trend results in a different slope for the
size evolution of the LAE population compared
to typical SFGs. This finding indicates that the
LAE population has a distinct growth rate of their
typical sizes compared to that of SFGs, implying
differences in their mass accretion mechanisms or
rates.
Overall, most of our findings are consistent with the
previous observational results on rest-frame UV size
measurements usingHSTdata. However, thanks to
JWSTand large survey programs such as ODIN and
COSMOS-Web, we were able to extend these analyses
into the rest-frame optical and make a more robust com-
parison between LAEs and typical SFGs. We also em-
phasize that these findings are qualitatively consistent
with the predictions from the HR5 cosmological hydro-
dynamical simulation. The HR5 simulation data can
offer a valuable opportunity to investigate the physical
origins of these distinct LAE features in more detail.
Furthermore, once the ODIN survey is completed, we
anticipate having a much larger sample of LAEs. When
combined with future large or deepJWSTimaging sur-
veys, this will substantially improve the robustness of
these types of analyses, providing a more detailed pic-
ture of LAE morphologies and the physical mechanisms
responsible for the Lyαphoton escape.
