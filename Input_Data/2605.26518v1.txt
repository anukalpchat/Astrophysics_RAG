ABSTRACT
Dark matter constitutes roughly one-fourth of the Universe, yet its physical nature remains unknown.
Warm dark matter (WDM), a class of dark matter candidates, has non-negligible velocity dispersion
that suppresses the formation of small-scale cosmic structures. Current constraints therefore rely
mainly on small-scale probes such as the Lyman-alpha (Lyα) forest and Milky Way observations
of satellite galaxies and stellar streams. We propose a novel large-scale probe based on long-lived
“reionization relics”: because the thermal and dynamical evolution of the intergalactic medium depends
on thelocalreionization redshift, patchy reionization imprints additional large-scale fluctuations in
Lyαforest opacity and post-reionization H I traced by 21 cm intensity mapping. The strength of these
imprints depends on WDM through both small-scale gas evolution and WDM-driven changes in the
reionization history. For example, the Lyα(21 cm) power spectrum in 3 keV WDM differs from cold
dark matter by≃19% (≃19%) atk= 0.05 Mpc −1 atz= 4 (z= 5.5) when reionization relics are
included. Using Lyαforest with a covariance model designed to mimic the capabilities of the Dark
Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), we forecast a constraint ofm WDM >5.0 keV (95%), which
improves tom WDM >7.1 keV when combined with 21 cm intensity-mapping observations from the
Square Kilometre Array (SKA). The next-generation surveys can further strengthen the current best
lower bounds from 9.7 to 39 keV.

1.INTRODUCTION
The Λ-Cold Dark Matter (ΛCDM) framework success-
fully explains large-scale phenomena like the cosmic mi-
crowave background (e.g., Planck Collaboration et al.
2020), but has historically faced tensions on small scales
(see J. S. Bullock & M. Boylan-Kolchin (2017) for a re-
view). Warm dark matter (WDM) suppresses structures
below the free-streaming scale through its residual ther-
mal velocities and could alleviate these tensions. Alter-

Nevertheless, if dark matter has particle origins, investi-

as CDM represents the zero-thermal-velocity limit of
WDM. Many efforts have been devoted to constraining
the mass of WDM particles (mWDM). The Lyman-alpha
(Lyα) forest – absorption features in quasar spectra from
neutral hydrogen (H I) along the line of sight (LoS) – is
highly sensitive to small-scale structure. Milky way ob-
servations of satellite galaxies and stellar streams (e.g.,
E. O. Nadler et al. 2021; N. Banik et al. 2021) and 21 cm

The strongest current limit,m WDM >9.7 keV (E. O.

While current constraints on WDM mass mainly lever-
age small-scale observations (k≳1 Mpc −1), this work
proposes a novel large-scale constraint mechanism via
the “memory of reionization” effect. This effect links
the timing oflocalreionization (z re) to the changes in
the transparency to Lyαphotons of the intergalactic
medium (IGM) and H I density.
Reionization raises gas temperatures to∼2×10 4 K,
typically followed by cooling, with which the IGM ulti-
mately relaxes to a temperature-density (T−∆) power-
law relation on cosmological timescales (L. Hui & N. Y.
Gnedin 1997). The gas in mini-halos and mini-filaments
is driven by the increased pressure to expand and “evap-
orate” from shallow potential wells. Meanwhile, the
low-density gas surrounding the small structures is com-
pressed to mean density, some heated to>3×10 4 K
(C. M. Hirata 2018). For late reionization (z re ≲8), a
significant amount of the compressed and heated “high-
entropy mean-density” (HEMD) gas remains above the
median (T−∆) relation long after reionization (e.g.,
z∼2.5) (C. M. Hirata 2018). In the post-reionization
era, the IGM is highly ionized, while H I gas mainly
resides in halos due to self-shielding. Earlierlocalreion-
ization allows the IGM to cool for longer, leading to a
higher recombination rate and increased opacity to Lyα
photons. Furthermore, earlier local reionization allows
more time for pressure to dissipate small-scale baryonic
structures and suppress subsequent gas accretion, reduc-
ing the baryonic fraction in low-mass halos, therefore
lowering H I density (H. Long et al. 2022).
On large scales, reionization is inhomogeneous. Gas
around star-forming galaxies is reionized first; then the
ionized bubbles surrounding the ionizing sources gradu-
ally expand until they overlap. The spatial variation in
zre results in fluctuations in the IGM transparency and
H I density on ionized-bubble scales, since both quanti-
ties are sensitive toz re. Previous works have shown that
the imprint of inhomogeneous reionization can suppress
21 cm IM (H. Long et al. 2023) and enhance Lyαforest
(e.g., P. Montero-Camacho et al. 2019b; J. O˜ norbe et al.
2019; X. Wu et al. 2019; M. Molaro et al. 2022) power
spectra on large scales (k≲0.4 Mpc −1).
In this work, we identify different levels of suppression
(enhancement) on 21 cm IM (Lyαforest) power spectra
across various WDM models. These models predict dis-
tinguishable signals on large scales, which can be used
to constrainm WDM. The thermal and dynamical evolu-
tion of post-reionization gas differs between WDM mod-
els, affecting the sensitivities of IGM transparency and
H I density toz re. Because the WDM suppression of
small-scale power reduces the abundance of small-scale
structures and the density contrast on small scales, gas
expansion in low-mass objects is less prevalent and is
less violent for lighter WDM. In addition, the gas has
less deviation from the (T−∆) power-law relation rela-
tive to CDM. Moreover, suppressed growth of low-mass
halos delays reionization by limiting ionization sources.
Toward the end, reionization accelerates in WDM mod-
els as larger halos that are unaffected by WDM suppres-
sion form and host star formation, and rapidly ionize the
IGM.
The rest of this paper is structured as follows. Sec-
tion 2 describes our models of reionization imprints on
the Lyαforeset and 21 cm IM power spectra, as well as
the hybrid simulations. Section 3 presents the WDM-
dependent enhancement of the Lyαpower spectrum and
suppression of the 21 cm IM power spectrum, along with
their physical origins. Section 4 presents MCMC fore-
casts of WDM constraints from current and near-future
surveys, and the impact of reionization astrophysics on
the constraints. Section 5 compares our methodology
with previous works, discusses modeling challenges, and
explores observational prospects using Lyαforest and
21 cm IM. Section 6 summarizes our main conclusions.

2.4.Hybrid simulations
Accurately modeling the imprints of inhomogeneous
reionization in the post-reionization era requires a huge
dynamical range. First, the simulation must feature
sufficient mass resolution to capture masses below the
pre-reionization Jeans mass, allowing us to track the re-
sponse of small-scale structures to the passage of ion-
ization fronts. Inadequate resolution could lead to in-
accuracies in modeling the post-reionization IGM and
the behavior of minihalos. This limitation arises from
the inability to resolve the bi-modality of theT−∆ re-
lation (C. M. Hirata 2018), i.e., to resolve the HEMD
gas, and to probe the smoothing of baryonic structures
(H. Long et al. 2022, 2023). Second, the patchy nature
of reionization must be modeled with enough statistical
power to capture its large-scale effects, requiring simula-
tion box lengths of at least∼300 Mpc (H. D. Kaur et al.
2020). Hence, we adopt a hybrid strategy (P. Montero-
Camacho et al. 2019b; H. Long et al. 2023), combin-
ing high-mass-resolution hydrodynamical simulations to
track small-scale structures and semi-numerical simula-
tions to handle the patchy nature of reionization, as il-
lustrated in Figure 2.
We use a modified version ofGadget-2(V. Springel
et al. 2001; V. Springel 2005; C. M. Hirata 2018) for
the small-scale high-mass resolution simulations. These
simulations track the gas evolution after local reion-
ization, and the goals include extracting the trans-
parency of small-scale IGMψ(z re, zobs), determining the
sound speedc s for computing the H I density fluctuation
Ξ(zre, zobs), and investigating the variations ofψand Ξ
withlocalreionization redshiftz re. The box length is
1275 kpc, and the number of particles is 2×192 3. The
particle masses of dark matter and gas are 9.72×103M⊙
and 1.81×10 3M⊙, respectively, thus providing suffi-
cient mass resolution to resolve structures below pre-
reionization Jeans mass. The convergence tests per-
formed in C. M. Hirata (2018) demonstrate that for
zre = 7 and 9 and 2.5≤z obs ≤4.0, increasing the
mass resolution by a factor of 8 changesψby<7%.
Similarly, increasing the simulation volume by 8 yields
ψvariation at 1σlevel, except forz obs = 4.0, where de-
viations are within 10%. Our configuration was selected
to enable sufficient realizations.

3.REIONIZATION IMPRINTS ON POWER
SPECTRUM
3.1.WDM-dependent enhancement of Lyαpower
Figure 3 shows the 3D Lyαand 21 cm IM power
spectra for dark-matter models. Reionization enhances
large-scale Lyαforest power, particularly at higher red-
shifts and lowerm WDM. Atz Lyα
high = 4, enhancements at
k= 0.05 Mpc −1 (0.2 Mpc−1) reach 75% (48%) for 3 keV
WDM and 47% (31%) for CDM. Including reionization
relics, fractional differences between WDM (m WDM =
3,4,6,9 keV) and CDM are 19% (13%), 10% (7%), 4%
(2%) and 2% (0.4%) atk= 0.05 Mpc −1 (0.2 Mpc−1),
respectively. Without reionization relics, large-scale dif-
ferences between dark matter models are negligible. At
zLyα
low = 2, heavier WDM marginally boosts the enhance-
ment (∼4-5% atk= 0.05 Mpc −1), with a fractional dif-
ference within 1.5% relative to CDM.
The impact of reionization on the Lyαforest is an
enhancement because without considering reionization,
high-density regions are more opaque than low-density
regions (note that the flux bias is negative). After con-
sidering reionization, high-density regions are even more
opaque since they usually reionize earlier and the IGM
has more time to cool. The inhomogeneity of reioniza-
tion enhances the contrast in opacity on scales where
localz re changes, i.e. the ionized bubble scales, which
are several to tens of comoving Mpc (cMpc). The de-
gree of large-scale enhancement differs between WDM
models due to two factors.
First, a stronger enhancement occurs when IGM
transparency significantly varies withlocalz re, causing
larger fluctuations on large scales. At∼z Lyα
high, this varia-
tion is more pronounced for lighter WDM (see Figure 4),
as the scarcity of small-scale structures in the voids lim-
its their evaporation and the compression and heating of
surrounding low-density gas, leading to less HEMD gas
(see Figure 5). The cooling of low-density IGM is less
affected by the dynamical response of the small-scale
structures, leading to a more substantial temperature
and transparency decrease with increasingz re. How-
ever, at∼z Lyα
low , heavier WDM shows more variation.
Long after reionization, transparency sensitivity toz re
is mainly due to cooling of HEMD gas, which is more
abundant and reaches a higher temperature in heavier
WDM models (see Figure 6), because of a more violent
post-reionization dynamical response with more small-
scale structures and larger density contrast. These be-
haviors are captured by the small-box simulations.
Second, the enhancement is greater when the whole
reionization process is delayed, which is more pro-
nounced for lighter WDM and captured by the large-box
reionization simulations, as shown in Figure 7. Com-
pared to an early reionization scenario, late reionization
results in more regions with lowerlocalz re, i.e. more
regions have less time to relax and their transparency is
more sensitive toz re.
3.2.WDM-dependent suppression of 21 cm IM power
For the 21 cm IM, reionization imprints cause large-
scale suppression, with stronger effects for heavier
WDM. At high redshift, e.g.,z 21 cm
high = 5.5, the sup-
pression atk= 0.05 Mpc −1 (0.2 Mpc−1) is 21% (16%)
for CDM and 6% (5%) for 3 keV WDM. The fractional
difference between WDM and CDM power spectra at
k= 0.05 Mpc −1 (0.2 Mpc−1) is 19% (12%), 15% (10%),
10% (6%) and 5% (3%) form WDM = 3,4,6,9 keV,
respectively. At low redshift, e.g.z 21 cm
low = 3.5, the
suppression weakens: only approximately 2% to 4% at
k= 0.05 Mpc −1 for DM models, with fractional dif-
ference between WDM and CDM from sub-percent to
1.5%, depending on WDM mass (9 to 3 keV).
In the post-reionization era, the 21 cm signal mainly
comes from neutral hydrogen in self-shielded halos. The
21 cm brightness temperature is proportional to the H I
density. Without considering reionization, dense regions
tend to have high H I density. However, reionization
reduces H I density by lowering the baryonic fraction
in low-mass halos. When smoothed on ionized bubble
scales, the H I density in dense regions is reduced more
significantly, leading to smaller H I density fluctuations
and suppressed 21 cm emission fluctuations. In the post-

4.3.Impact of reionization astrophysics
Our results are robust even if we include the astro-
physical parameters ionization efficiencyζand turnover
mass of halos unable to host star-forming galaxies
Mturn, which introduce astrophysics uncertainties (see
Appendix B for details of the method).ζgoverns ioniz-
ing photon escape and reionization timing, whileM turn
primarily sets the halo population contributing photons,
shaping reionization onset, pace, and morphology. As
WDM suppresses small-scale structure, it changes the
timeline of reionization; however, this change can be
mimicked by reionization astrophysics, as shown in Fig-
ure 7. Besides, WDM can also accelerate the late stages
of reionization (M. Sitwell et al. 2014), therefore we re-
quire at least bothζandM turn to mimic these trends.
Modest variations inζandM turn relax them WDM con-
straints from DESI, DESI + SKA1-Low, Stage V Lyα
surveys, and Stage V + Stage II IM by 12%, 12%, 40%
and 36%, respectively (see Table 1 for details). Even
with these astrophysics uncertainties, the constraints
from next-generation surveys remain capable of push-
ing well beyond current bounds.
More interestingly, Figure 10 shows that the degener-
acy betweenζandM turn withm WDM, which is present
inx HI(z), is gone in our constraints by post-reionization
observables. Figure 7 and 8 of P. Montero-Camacho
& Y. Mao (2021), demonstrate that an increase inζ
or a reduction inM turn both yield a decrease in the
resulting 3D flux power spectrum, since both changes
make reionization occur earlier, which diminishes the
large scale enhancement cause by reionization relics. As
shown in Figure 3, increasingmWDM also reduces the 3D
flux power spectrum at high redshifts. However, at low
redshifts, increasingm WDM leads to anincreasein the
flux power spectrum. This redshift-denpendent effect of
WDM breaks the degeneracy between reionization as-
trophysics andm WDM in our signal. This interesting
feature means that – ignoring the instrument systemat-
ics and signal-to-noise – even if higher redshifts translate
to better sensitivity to the impact of warm dark matter
in the Lyαforest, incorporating lower redshift obser-
vations would be crucial to disentangle its effects from
those of reionization astrophysics.
5.DISCUSSION
It is well established that the IGM’s large-scale fluc-
tuations of temperature and ionization following patchy
reionization boost the large-scale Lyαforest power spec-
trum. Our methodology gives 10-20% 1D power boost

6.CONCLUSION
We introduce a new large-scale probe of WDM based
on the long-lived “memory of reionization” in post-
reionization observables. Although WDM primarily

15
suppresses structure formation below the free-streaming
scale, reionization couples this small-scale physics to
large scales: thelocalreionization redshift,z re, is spa-
tially inhomogeneous on ionized-bubble scales (several
to tens of comoving Mpc), and post-reionization gas
properties retain a measurable and WDM-dependent
memory ofz re down toz∼2–5. Consequently, the
spatial variations inz re modulate both the Lyαforest
transparency and the halo H I content traced by 21 cm
IM, introducing large-scale sensitivity to WDM physics.
For the Lyαforest, patchy reionization enhances large-
scale power because overdense regions typically reionize
earlier and have more time to cool, increasing their re-
combination rate and opacity. This amplifies large-scale
transparency contrast on ionized-bubble scales. The
level of this enhancement is WDM-dependent for two
reasons: (1) WDM alters the post-reionization thermal
and dynamical response of small-scale structures (in-
cluding the production and evolution of HEMD gas),
which changes how strongly IGM transparency depends
onz re; and (2) WDM tends to delay reionization by
suppressing low-mass sources, increasing the fraction of
regions that reionize late and therefore retain stronger
reionization memory at a fixedz obs. Quantitatively, in-
cluding reionization relics, the fractional difference be-
tween WDM and CDM Lyαpower atz= 4 reaches
∼19% form WDM = 3 keV and∼2% form WDM = 9 keV
atk= 0.05 Mpc −1. Without reionization relics, these
large-scale differences largely vanish.
For 21 cm IM, reionization relics suppress large-scale
power because photoheating reduces the baryonic con-
tent (and hence the H I mass) of low-mass halos more
strongly in regions that reionize earlier. When smoothed
on ionized-bubble scales, this stronger reduction in over-
dense regions decreases H I fluctuations and therefore
suppresses 21 cm brightness-temperature fluctuations.
The suppression is weaker for lighter WDM because
fewer low-mass halos exist to be affected and the sen-
sitivity of H I density toz re is reduced. The WDM-
CDM fractional differences atz= 5.5 are∼19% for
mWDM = 3 keV and and∼5% form WDM = 9 keV at
k= 0.05 Mpc −1.
Using these WDM-dependent large-scale signatures,
we performe MCMC forecasts for constraints onm WDM
jointly withσ 8. For a Lyαsurvey with a covariance
matrix designed to mimic DESI-like performance, we
forecast a lower bound ofm WDM >5.0 keV (95%).
Adding 21 cm IM from a 5,000 h SKA1-Low-like survey
breaks degeneracies and strengthens the constraint to
mWDM >7.1 keV. Looking further ahead, we find that
next-generation experiments can be substantially more
powerful: a Stage-V Lyαsurvey yieldsm WDM >30 keV,
a Stage-II IM survey yieldsm WDM >10 keV, and
their combination yieldsm WDM >39 keV. Our fore-
casts demonstrate that large-scale measurements can
give competitive (and potentially leading) constraints
on WDM when considering reionization relics.
We also assess robustness to uncertainties in reion-
ization astrophysics by allowing the ionizing efficiency
ζand the turnover massM turn to vary. These parame-
ters can partially mimic WDM in the global reionization
history; however, the power spectra retain additional
discriminatory power because the WDM imprint is me-
diated by small-scale gas dynamics and exhibits a char-
acteristic redshift dependence that differs from simple
shifts in reionization timing.
More broadly, our results highlight reionization relics
as a generic pathway to connect small-scale dark-matter
physics to large-scale post-reionization observables. Our
method can be applied to other dark-matter candidates,
likely with similarly tight constraints, such as fuzzy
dark matter (e.g., W. Hu et al. 2000), primordial black
holes (e.g., P. Montero-Camacho et al. 2019a), and self-
interacting dark matter (e.g., D. N. Spergel & P. J.
Steinhardt 2000), provided that inhomogeneous reion-
ization and the post-reionization evolution of gas on
small scales can be modeled accurately. Reionization
relics therefore offer a complementary and potentially
powerful probe of the nature of dark matter.

The 2-parameter MCMC forecast in Figure 9 averages four realizations of Lyαforest and 21 cm IM power spectra.
In contrast, the 4-parameter MCMC uses a single realization, making direct comparison inappropriate. For a valid
comparison, we also conduct a 2-parameter MCMC on the same realization (see Figure 10 and Table 1).
Figure 10 shows Stage II IM constraints (grey) are bounded byζ= 20 and log 10(Mturn/M⊙) = 9, suggesting a
5% tighterm WDM limit than the 2-parameter MCMC. Nevertheless, Figure 7 reveals some models withζ <20 and

reionization,ζlower andM turn upper limits should depend onm WDM andσ 8, creating a “wedge-like” prior. Extended
parameter space and prior impact exploration are deferred to future work.
