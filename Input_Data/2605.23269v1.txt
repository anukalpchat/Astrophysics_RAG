We measured the Concentration ( /u1D436 ) index of H /u1D6FC and CO ( J = 2–1) in 17 nearby star-forming galaxies from the PHANGS
survey. We have found four barred spiral galaxies with a /u1D436 (H /u1D6FC )//u1D436 (CO) ratio of ∼ 0.3–0.4, while the other 13 galaxies exhibit
ratios of ∼ 1 (range from 0.7 to 1.3). All four barred galaxies with low ra tios host active galactic nuclei (AGNs), consistent
with a scenario in which star formation is suppressed by mech anical and radiative feedback from the AGN. Therefore, nega tive
feedback is eﬀective in these four barred galaxies with a low molecular gas mass fraction ( ≲ 0. 1), even when AGN activity is
relatively weak. The formation of bar structures causes molecular gas to collect in the central region, leading to starb urst activity.
However, after the starburst, the remaining gas becomes ine ﬃcient for star formation rapidly due to AGN feedback. It can mean
the quenching process occurs more rapidly in AGN-barred gal axies. Furthermore, since gas remains in the central region , AGN
activity is likely to continue. These quenching processes are a unique mechanism found in barred spiral galaxies and are essential
to understanding galaxy evolution.

1 INTRODUCTION
Despite the vigorous study of nearby galaxies in recent year s, the
evolutionary process of galaxies is still unclear. The most common
scenario is that multiple mergers of disc galaxies form massive ellipti-
cal galaxies (e.g.,
Cox et al. 2006 ; Hopkins et al. 2008 ). The merger
of gas-rich galaxies trigger starbursts (e.g., Jog & Solomon 1992 ;
Gao et al. 2001 ). However, we must not overlook that the forma-
tion of galactic bar structures eﬃciently transports molec ular gas
toward the centre, thereby inducing central starbursts. Fo r example,
according to a recent investigation, Baba et al. (2022) utilized N-
body/hydrodynamical simulations to illustrate the gas con centration
at the centre due to the galactic bar formation, and the subse quent
central starburst and formation of the galactic bulge. On th e other
hand, Maeda et al. (2023) reported that the star formation in the bar
region except for galactic centre is systematically suppressed, caused
by dynamical eﬀects, such as strong shocks, shears, and fast cloud-
cloud collisions.
Furthermore, recent studies have revealed a growing number of

2.1 PHANGS-ALMA archival data
We retrieved The Physics at High Angular Resolution in

ant molecular clouds are often found to have radii of ∼ 30 – 60
pc (e.g., Leroy et al. 2021b ). That is, the resolution achieved by
PHANGS–ALMA is suﬃcient to resolve the typical size of indi-
vidual giant molecular clouds.
In PHANGS-ALMA, there are two types of data products: the strict
mask products and the broad mask products. The broad mask pro d-
ucts have more completeness and cover a larger fraction of th e total
ﬂux than the strict mask products (
Leroy et al. 2021a ). Leroy et al.
(2021a) recommend using the strict mask for calculations sensitiv e
to noise, e.g., many types of kinematic analysis, and the bro ad mask
for any analysis aimed at a complete characterization of theemission.
Completeness is crucial to this paper’s analysis, which mea sures the
concentration of CO and H /u1D6FC . Therefore, we use the broad mask
products for the analysis in this study.

context, the total ﬂux is the ﬂux measured within the ﬁeld of v iew
for each galaxy’s dataset. Concentration ( /u1D436 ) is calculated using the
equation (1) below.
/u1D436 = 5 × log( /u1D45F90%/ /u1D45F10%) , (1)
This equation is slightly diﬀerent from the deﬁnition adopt ed by

uses case B approximation. However, since not all nebulae necessar-

by the attenuation. The most eﬀective method is to create a sc atter
plot of the correlation between H /u1D6FC and H /u1D6FD for each index of CAS.

excluding A-type galaxies, indicate that star formation and molecular

enhanced central gas concentration, which can further trig ger co-

the presence of an AGN can suppress central star formation. W e
elaborate on this in the following subsection.

4 CONCLUSIONS
We analysed 17 of 19 PHANGS survey (PHANGS-ALMA and
PHANGS-MUSE) galaxies, excluding two with signiﬁcant dust at-
tenuation. We ﬁnd that 13 galaxies are aligned near the one-t o-one
line in the /u1D436 (H /u1D6FC )-/u1D436 (CO) plane. These galaxies exhibit 0 . 9 ± 0. 0
(range: 0 . 8 − 1. 3) in /u1D436 of H /u1D6FC to /u1D436 of CO ratio (Ro /u1D436 ). In these
galaxies, there are seven B-type barred galaxies, with high /u1D436 (H /u1D6FC )
and /u1D436 (CO). The respective mean /u1D436 values are 6. 2 ± 0. 3 and 6. 3 ± 0. 3
(see Table
1 and Figure 2).
We have found that four A-type barred galaxies exhibit star-
formation suppression in their centres, with a Ro/u1D436 of 0. 4± 0. 0 (range:

0. 3 − 0. 4). These four barred galaxies are all AGN host galaxies. The
A-type galaxies have a high mean/u1D436 in molecular gas (/u1D436 = 5. 7± 0. 5),
but they do not have a high mean /u1D436 in H /u1D6FC (/u1D436 = 2. 2 ± 0. 1), indi-
cating suppressed star formation in the central region. Thi s suggests
evidence that negative feedback from AGN suppresses star formation
at the galactic centre. To facilitate a more quantitative di scussion of
galaxy evolution, we measured the oﬀset from the main sequen ce of
star-forming galaxies ( Δ MS). In general, galaxies may evolve from
the right to left (from starbursts to GV) in Figure 4 if galaxies do
not undergo gas-rich mergers. Our sample galaxies show no si gns of
recent mergers. The four AGN-barred galaxies are likely on a path
of ‘inside-out quenching’. However, given the limited samp le size,
further research is needed to clarify the quenching pathway . In any
case, our essential ﬁnding is that AGN-barred spiral galaxi es exhibit
a distinct speciﬁc distribution in Ro /u1D436 versus Δ MS relationship.
We note that the activity of AGNs in these nearby galaxies is
not strong enough to blow away all the molecular gas. The phot o-
dissociation of a part of molecular gas by intense radiation from
AGN may be one of the reasons for preventing star formation. A lter-
natively, due to the AGN outﬂow, molecular gas clouds are lik ely to
be in a high-velocity, turbulent state, which may prevent th e forma-
tion of molecular cloud cores. The velocity dispersion of mo lecular
clouds on the parsec scale remains unknown due to insuﬃcient spatial
resolution. Further investigation is needed to verify this point.

In present-day galaxies, GV occupy an intermediate region b e-

galaxies (the Red Sequence) (e.g.,

suggest that the GV galaxies constitute a ‘quasi-static’ po pulation
undergoing a slow-quenching process. As part of the ALMaQUE ST
survey (ALMA-MaNGA Quenching and Star Formation Survey;
Lin et al. 2020 ), Pan et al. (2024) categorized their sample into three
distinct classes based on sSFR. They are; star-forming mainsequence
(log sSFR > − 10. 5), ‘moderate sSFR’ ( − 11. 0 < log sSFR < − 10. 5,
GV in our deﬁnition), and ‘low sSFR’ (log sSFR < − 11. 0). The lat-

represented by Δ MS values of -0.2 to +0.4, -0.7 to -0.2, and -1.7 to
-0.7, respectively.
We discuss below with the perspective of quenching along the
Δ MS axis. Present-day galaxies’ star formation is thought tohave pro-
gressed from the SFMS phase, through the GV phase, to the quenched
phase (red sequence) over a long time-scale (e.g.,Walters et al. 2022;
Pan et al. 2024). Starburst galaxies have elevated SFRs for their stellar
mass (Ellison et al. 020a ), which means starbursts lie above the main
sequence of star-forming galaxies, indicating high Δ MS positive val-
ues. In our sample, NGC 1365 (log sSFR = − 9. 76, Δ MS = 0. 72) is
a starburst galaxy whose elevated star formation may be rela ted to
central gas inﬂow through the bar structure. After the starb urst and
consumption of the available gas, galaxies are thought to un dergo a
quenching process (e.g., Baba et al. 2022 ), observed in the decrease
of the Δ MS value.
We adopt the sSFR classiﬁcation of Pan et al. (2024). However,
we do not adopt their classiﬁcation of ‘central starbursts’ ; instead,
we deﬁne galaxies with SFR larger than four times the value in
the main sequence (e.g.,
Rodighiero et al. 2011 ; Wang et al. 2019 ),

starburst activity is central or not. The ALMaQUEST data is s imilar

(2019) at /u1D467 = 0, /u1D440 ∗ ≳ 3 × 1010. Thus, our sample is distinguished
