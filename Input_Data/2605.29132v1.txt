Abstract.In this work we generalize the thermal decoupling, or freeze-out, of weakly interacting massive
particle dark matter within the Tsallis nonextensive formalism. The generalization is implemented through
q-deformed distribution functions obtained from the maximum entropy principle with Curado-Tsallis con-
straints. The Tsallis parameterq, which measures deviations from extensivity with respect to the limit
q= 1, is promoted to a dynamical quantity depending on the dimensionless variablex=m χ/T, where
mχ is the dark matter mass. This dynamical evolution is characterized by a relaxation toward extensivity,
while requiring that the nonextensive deformation is not completely erased before freeze-out. We solve the
Boltzmann equation assuming a sectorial deformation, where only the dark matter equilibrium abundance
is generalized and the radiation background remains extensive. The relic abundance is computed for dif-
ferent dark matter masses and initial values of the Tsallis parameter. From this evolution, we extract the
residual valueq fo
χ at freeze-out, which is then used as the initial input for a phenomenological memory
channel. This channel propagates the residual nonextensivity into the radiation sector, specifically into the
electron-positron plasma and neutrinos, while photons are kept extensive in order to avoid direct tensions
with CMB physics. The resulting deformation modifies the neutrino energy density and the photon reheat-
ing contribution, producing a correction toNeff. We compare the predicted values with the compressed
CMB+BAO constraint onNeff and find that the residual-memory scenario can remain phenomenologically
compatible with current bounds.
1 Introduction
The standard cosmological model provides a remarkably
successful description of the Universe. With a relatively
small number of parameters, it gives a precise and co-
herent account of a wide range of observations, from the
anisotropies of the cosmic microwave background to the
formation of large scale structure and the late time expan-
sion history [1,2,3]. However, despite this success, some
fundamental aspects of the model remain unresolved. In
particular, the physical nature of the dark sector is still
unknown, including both dark matter and dark energy [4].
This motivates the study of modified scenarios and exten-
sions of the standard framework, in order to explore how
new physical ingredients may affect thermal decoupling
processes and cosmological observables.
After the thermal decoupling of dark matter, other
radiation components remain thermally coupled, such as
photons, electrons, positrons, and neutrinos. Around the
MeV scale, neutrinos decouple from the electromagnetic
plasma, while the electron-positron pairs subsequently an-
nihilateandtransfertheirentropymainlytophotons.This
process reheats the photon bath relative to the already
decoupled neutrino sector, generating the standard tem-
perature difference between photons and neutrinos [5,6,7,
8].
Both scenarios can be generalized by using Tsallis
statistics. Tsallis statistics provides a generalization of
Boltzmann-Gibbs statistics, aimed at capturing phenom-
ena associated with nonextensivity, such as long range
interactions and non negligible correlations among the
components of a system. Its starting point is a modified
entropic functional,S q, which, through the maximum
entropy principle and appropriate constraints, can be
connected with the thermodynamics of the early Universe
by means of equilibrium distribution functions. In the
present context, these distributions are replaced by their
q-deformed counterparts [9,10,11,12].
Several works have already explored the possible role
of Tsallis statistics in early Universe cosmology. In the
context of dark matter, Tsallis generalized freeze-out dy-
namics has been studied as a way to modify the equilib-
rium abundance and the relic density calculation [13,14].
Other analyses have used nonextensive effects to constrain
deviations from the extensive limit through Big Bang nu-
cleosynthesis, relic abundance, observables from radiation
sector [22,17,18]. These works indicate that any nonexten-
siveresidualsurvivingaroundtheMeVepochmustremain
close to the standard limitq= 1, but they also motivate
the question of whether a residual deformation generated
at dark matter freeze-out stage could be transferred to the
radiation sector in a controlled way.

2 Tsallis nonextensive formalism
Nonextensive statistical mechanics provides a generaliza-
tion of Boltzmann-Gibbs statistics designed to capture
phenomenology associated with strong correlations, mem-
ory effects, long range interactions, and related departures
from ordinary extensivity [9,10]. These deviations are en-
coded in the Tsallis parameterq, which quantifies how
far the system departs from the Boltzmann-Gibbs, or ex-
tensive, regime. The formalism is built from a modified
entropic functional which, through the maximum entropy
principle, establishes the connection between microscopic
statistical weights and macroscopic thermodynamic ob-
servables [11,12]. As discussed below, this provides the
statistical basis for introducing controlled nonextensive
corrections into the dark matter freeze-out process.

Freeze-out, or thermal decoupling, occurs when the
expansion of the Universe becomes faster than the mi-
croscopic processes that keep the dark matter particles
in chemical equilibrium with the thermal bath. At early
times, annihilations and inverse annihilations are efficient,
so the abundance closely follows its equilibrium value. As
the Universe expands and cools, the equilibrium abun-
dance becomes suppressed and the interaction rate de-
creases. When the annihilation rate can no longer compete
with the Hubble expansion rate, the dark matter abun-
dance departs from equilibrium and approaches an ap-
proximately constant comoving value. Schematically, this
transition is controlled byΓann(T)∼H(T), whereΓann is
the annihilation rate andHis the Hubble expansion rate
[5,1,19].

of the Universe.

7 Conclusions
In this work we generalized the thermal decoupling of
WIMP dark matter within the Tsallis nonextensive frame-
work. The deformation was implemented through gener-
alized distribution functions, modifying the equilibrium
abundance of the dark matter species while keeping the
radiation background and the Standard Model plasma ex-
tensive. In this sectorial setup, the Tsallis parameter was
promoted to a dynamical function of the dimensionless
variablex=m χ/T, evolving uniformly inlnxtoward the
extensive limit.
We then solved the Boltzmann equation for different
dark matter masses using a partial wave approximation,
focusing on thes-wave dominated scenario as a first ap-
proach. The resulting behavior agrees with previous re-
sultsintheliterature:forq χ>1,freeze-outisdelayedwith
respect to the extensive case, while forqχ<1, freeze-out
occurs earlier. This is understood from the effect of power-
law tails forq χ>1and compact support forq χ<1,
respectively.
Next, we extracted the residual nonextensive value at
freeze-out,q fo
χ, which becomes the input parameter for the
Tsallis memory channel. This residual quantity allows the
radiationsectortoinheritpartofthenonextensiveimprint
generated during the dark matter freeze-out stage.
The memory channel was defined in terms of the
freeze-out residual inputq fo
χ, an efficiency parameterλi,
and a thermal profile that describes how this memory
evolves with the temperature of the primordial plasma.
We considered the case where only neutrinos and the
electron-positron plasma can inherit the residual nonex-
tensivity, while photons are kept extensive in order to
avoid direct tension with CMB physics. From this setup,
two scenarios for the efficiency parameters were analyzed.
In the first one, the residual memory is transferred with
equal efficiency to neutrinos and to the electron-positron
sector, namelyλe± =λν= 0.5. In the second one,λe±
is treated as a free parameter, whileλνis fixed by the
conditionλe± +λν= 1.
This construction connects the memory channel with
Neff through two effects. The neutrino sector contributes
directly by modifying the neutrino energy density through
the residual valueqdec
ν, while the electron-positron sector
contributes indirectly by modifying the entropy transfer
to photons and therefore the temperature ratio(Tν/Tγ)q.
Combining both effects, the residual nonextensivity in-
herited from freeze-out produces a phenomenological
correction toN (q)
eff . The comparison with the compressed
CMB+BAO valueN eff = 2.99±0.17shows that the
memory channel scenario can remain compatible with
current cosmological bounds, although the result should
be interpreted as a consistency test rather than as a
strong statistical constraint [3].
As a final remark, this work can be improved in sev-
eraldirections.First,insteadofusingonlyarepresentative
list of dark matter masses, a more exhaustive scan over a
continuous mass grid would allow a more complete ex-
ploration of the parameter space and of the relic density
surface. Second, a future extension should include a more
realistic treatment of neutrino decoupling by solving the
neutrino Boltzmann equations with modified distribution
functions. This would make it possible to go beyond the
instantaneous decoupling approximation and to test the
memory channel scenario in a more robust way.
