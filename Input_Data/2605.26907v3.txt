ABSTRACT
Context.Hydrodynamical cosmological simulations based on theΛ-Cold Dark Matter (ΛCDM) model predict that∼40% of the
baryons in the local Universe are missing. These missing baryons are predicted to lie in low-density filamentary structures that
trace connections between galaxies. This so-called warm-hot intergalactic medium (WHIM) is predicted to be observable in the
far-ultraviolet (FUV) and soft X-ray regimes, and detectable in ion species with transitions in these bands.
Aims.We investigate the capability of the X-ray Integral Field Unit (X-IFU) on NewAthena to detect this WHIM through Ovii
absorption lines imprinted on bright power-law sources in soft X-rays (0.3-1.0 keV) by examining the parameter space of different
observing conditions.
Methods.Through advanced simulations of the X-IFU detector, a multidimensional approach is taken to investigate the relationship
between source parameters and their impacts on the significance of detection of a single absorption feature. By first studying the effect
of line placement (redshift) for an observation of fixed length, followed by varying the observation length, the required counts for 3σ
and 5σdetections are calculated for various photon indices and local Galactic absorptions. The rest-frame equivalent widths of the
imprinted absorption features are then varied, and the same requirements are calculated for each line strength.
Results.We discuss the detection and recovery of the imprinted WHIM features. Another method is used to derive count requirements
for detecting these features at different levels of significance, which is then compared to the previously obtained requirements. Using
publicly available data from the Swift archives, various non-transient sources are analysed for their suitability to detect WHIM features
in absorption.

1. Introduction
Predictions from a combination of high-redshift cosmic mi-
crowave background (CMB) measurements (Aghanim et al.
2020) and big bang nucleosynthesis (Fields et al. 2020) demon-
strate that the baryonic content accounts for∼4-5% of the en-
ergy budget in the Universe. However, in the low-redshift Uni-
verse, approximately 30-50% of this baryonic matter is missing
(e.g. Tepper-García et al. 2012). Through cosmological simula-
tions based on theΛCDM model, it is evident that these miss-
ing baryons lie in a filamentary web that traces connections
between galaxies and galactic clusters (Cen & Ostriker 2006;
Cui et al. 2019; Tuominen et al. 2021). These baryons, known as
the warm-hot intergalactic medium (WHIM), are shock heated
through gravitational infall to high temperatures;T≈10 5–107 K,
and exist in a highly tenuous state;n b ≈10 −6–10−5 cm−3 (e.g.
Cen & Ostriker 1999; Dave et al. 2001; Nicastro et al. 2005a).
The amount of baryons residing in the WHIM increases with
decreasing redshift, which is in agreement with the high-redshift
observations, and explains why a high number of baryons are
missing in the local Universe (Cen & Ostriker 1999). The high
temperatures of the WHIM cause its atomic constituents to be
present in highly ionised states, such as H - or He -like ionisa-
tions, causing it to both absorb and emit in the far-ultraviolet
(FUV) and soft X-ray bands. The best atomic transitions to study
this matter are Cvin the FUV band, and Oviand Oviiin the
soft X-ray band, with the Ovii-Heα(0.574 keV , 21.6 Å) to be
the strongest transition in this band (e.g. Perna & Loeb 1998;
Cen & Ostriker 2006; Branchini et al. 2009; Wijers et al. 2019).
Many previous attempts have been made to observe the
WHIM through these absorption lines. While Fang et al. (2024)
give a detailed overview, some of the milestones are described
here. One of the first major efforts to detect WHIM was car-
ried along the line of sight to H1821+642 (Mathur et al. 2003).
The Low Energy Transmission Grating Spectrometer (LEGTS)
instrument on Chandra was used to detect Ovii-Heα, Oviii-Hα
and Neix-Heαlines at redshifts of known Ovilines. While these
lines were not detected at a high significance, this method of
using lines whose positions are known ‘a priori’ provided ro-
bust methods for future work. Nicastro et al. (2005a,b) report on
blind-line observations along the line of sight to Mrk421. Us-
ing the Reflection Grating Spectrometer (RGS) on XMM New-
ton, two intervening systems at redshiftsz=0.011 andz=0.026
were detected through Ovii-Heαlines with different associ-
ated ions at each redshift. However, it is unclear if these ab-
sorption lines arise from filamentary WHIM, or from circum-
galactic medium (CGM) of galaxies present in the line of sight

Nicastro et al. (2018) report on observations along the line of

limits of the column densities, temperatures and metallicity of
the absorbers were determined. However, the former system
likely arises from the environment local to the host galaxy, and
should be excluded from the baryon census (Johnson et al. 2019;
Jones et al. 2021). Kovács et al. (2019) expanded on the afore-
mentioned work of Mathur et al. (2003) and analysed the line of
sight to source H1821+643 at redshifts corresponding to known
Lyαabsorbers (Tripp et al. 1998). To probe much lower column
densities than before, the spectra from four LEGTS observa-
tions were stacked at the expected locations of Oviifeatures at
the previously obtained redshifts, resulting in a spectrum with
an effective observation length of 8 Ms. From this, the aver-
age column density of the stacked filaments was found to be
∼10 15 cm−2, under the assumption that all possible filaments
contribute equally to the column density. It was independently
verified through 10 4 Monte Carlo simulations, which stacked
17 randomly selected redshifts in the spectrum, that this detec-
tion was statistically significant. The locations of Neix, Nex,
Nvi, Nviiand Oviiiwere also analysed, but no significant fea-
tures were detected. By adopting ionisation fractions from non-
equilibrium simulations, and with the assumption that all fil-
aments contribute equally to the detected feature, it was esti-
mated that Oviiabsorbers contribute (37.5±10.5)% of the total
baryonic content. Ahoranta et al. (2020) used a combination of
XMM Newton’s RGS, and Chandra’s High Energy Transmis-
sion Grating Spectrometer (HEGTS) and LEGTS to study ab-
sorption features along the line of sight to 3C273 at different
epochs. To determine the most likely redshifts at which WHIM
absorbers could be found, they examined FUV absorber data
from Tilton et al. (2012) and determined redshifts where (1) at
least two significant metal absorption lines were identified, in-
dicating a high concentration of metals in the absorber, or (2)
a broad Ly-αfeature withb>30 km/s was found, indicating
a high enough temperature (∼3×10 5 K) to produce significant
soft X-ray features. Using these criteria, they find two redshifts,
z≈0.09 andz≈0.12, at which WHIM features were to be ex-
pected. Through simultaneous fitting of the co-added spectra of
each instrument, Neixand Oviii-Hαabsorption features were
found at the expected wavelengths for thez≈0.09 system. These
two ions are likely indicators of the hot WHIM phase, with tem-
peraturesT≈10 6 – 6.5K. This temperature was determined using
a model that assumed that the ions were in CIE with solar abun-
dances. Through analysis of the equivalent width (EW) of the
corresponding Oviabsorption feature using this model, it was
determined that the column density of Oviwas underestimated,
hence requiring a multi-phase structure for this to be plausible.
The location at which the Ovii-Heαline was predicted to fall
in this system coincides with the local Galactic Oiline, and
hence cannot be accurately studied. This highlights a key ob-
stacle when aiming to study these features. Furthermore, no hot
WHIM features were identified for thez≈0.12 system, indicat-
ing that only a warm component exists. This lack of detection is
in agreement with the line widths of the measured Oviand Hi-
Lyα, yieldingT<10 4.7 K determined by Tripp et al. (2008), and
hence confirms these findings.
While these older instruments have provided tentative re-
sults, it is clear that newer detectors are needed to bridge this
gap. NewAthena will provide a better opportunity to detect and
study the WHIM through these absorption lines. The Athena
mission, from which the NewAthena mission is derived, was
selected in 2014 to address and study the scientific theme of
‘The Hot and Energetic Universe’ (Nandra et al. 2013). To do
this, the reformulated NewAthena will utilise two on-board in-
struments: the X-ray Integral Field Unit (X-IFU) (Barret et al.
2023; Peille et al. 2025) and the Wide Field Imager (WFI), the
former being the instrument of use in this investigation. The X-
IFU will be able to provide observations with high sensitivity
to line detections, allowing detections of WHIM features in ab-
sorption (e.g. Wijers et al. 2020).
Previously, for the Athena mission, there was a requirement
of a four-hour target of opportunity (ToO) time for 50% of ob-
servations. Hence, both gamma-ray burst (GRB) afterglows and
bright active galactic nuclei (AGN) were selected to be used as
background beacons to provide capability for absorption studies
of the WHIM. Previous studies (see below) have been carried
out to investigate the ability of NewAthena to detect the missing
baryons in the local Universe using GRBs as the primary back-
ground source. However, with the current NewAthena rescope,
the ToO time goal has increased to 12 hours, making detections
of WHIM through GRB afterglows unlikely due to the rapidly
decaying flux (Cruise et al. 2024). However, the findings pre-
sented in this work can still apply to the spectra of X-ray bright
GRBs, provided they produce sufficient counts during the obser-
vation. This is discussed further in Section 4.8.
Walsh et al. (2020) investigated the capability to observe
WHIM with older response files that assumed a larger effective
area than the present setup, and utilised GRBs as the background
beacons. In each simulation, a constant photon indexα X =2.0
was assumed for the whole population, and each observation
lasted 50 ks. The fluxes of the GRBs were modelled using a de-
caying power-law model, whereF∝t −α, withα=1.2. It was
found that an excess of 10 6 counts in the 0.3 – 10.0 keV range
was required to yield a 75% chance of detection of an Ovii-
Oviiipair with equivalent widthsEW Ovii =0.13 – 0.39 eV and
EWOviii =2/3∗EW Ovii =0.09 – 0.26 eV . Additionally, a limit for
the local Galactic absorption ofN H <8×10 20 cm−2 was found
to be required to obtain a combined significance of 4.2σfor
an Ovii-Oviiipair, withEW Ovii =0.28 eV andEW Oviii =2/3∗
EWOvii ≈0.19 eV for a GRB starting flux ofF 0.3−10.0 =5×
10−11 erg s-1 cm-2.

ing; (1) The observed fluxes of GRBs have a decaying nature,
as previously stated, whereas those of AGN can be modelled
as approximately constant over the length of an observation of
∼50 ks; (2) The limit was derived based on a fixed photon in-
dexα X,GRB =2.0. However, AGN attain a range of photon in-
dices, 1.0≤α X,AGN ≤3.0 (Joffre et al. 2022). This range of pho-
ton indices will affect the number of photons detected in the
0.3 –1.0 keV band, making an updatedN H limit and hence the
number of counts required, that are dependent onα X, necessary;
(3) The mirror array has since been updated, and the new re-
duced effective area must be taken into account when accurately
simulating observations. We aim to determine the source and ob-
serving parameters that are required to detect WHIM absorption
lines. Throughout this investigation, the background sources are
not studied in detail. For simplicity, we refer to all non-transient

2. Simulating WHIM absorption spectra
SImulation of X-ray TElescopes (SIXTE), the official Monte
Carlo simulation toolkit for the X-IFU, was used to realisti-
cally simulate the observations in this investigation (Dauser et al.
2019). This work adopted the current baseline response files1 as
described by Peille et al. (2025), that assumes an effective area
of∼0.62 m 2 at 1.0 keV with no filters. The geometry of the in-
struments, as well as the capability of the mirror to provide a
35 mm defocusing configuration, were modelled throughout the
simulations to allow for maximum photon collection and to pre-
vent saturation of pixels.
The required inputs for the pipeline are the XML file that de-
scribes the X-IFU calorimeter geometry and the grading scheme
for the generated photons, and the SIMulation inPUT (SIM-
PUT) file (Schmid et al. 2013) that describes the spectrum, light
curve and position of the observed source.XSPEC v12.14.1,
interfaced usingpyxspec v2.1.4, was implemented to create
models of the source spectra. Other inputs for the pipeline in-
cluded the telescope pointing, the exposure time of the observa-

In all cases, the pointing was set to the source position, such
that it was centred and defocused on the detector. Crosstalk was
simulated between pixels throughout, and a physically accurate
non X-ray background (NXB) was included to reflect the base-
line configuration of the detector. Due to the high effective area
of NewAthena, a cosmic X-ray background was not considered
here as its effect is negligible in comparison to the count rates
from sources utilised here (see Lotti et al. (2021) for predicted
contributions). Only photons with the highest energy resolution
(∆E≤4 eV) were used for spectrum generation, and all spectra
remained unbinned for analysis.
All spectral parameters were set to the values seen in Ta-
ble 1 unless otherwise stated. The observed continuum spectra
of the sources, absent of any WHIM absorption features, con-
sist of two components: cold local Galactic interstellar medium
(ISM) absorption in the Milky Way, represented byTBabs
(Wilms et al. 2000), and a simple power-law representing the
emission of the source,pegpwrlw. To model a WHIM ab-
sorption line, thezgaussmodel with a negative normalisation
parameter was added to the continuum, which represented an
Ovii-Heαabsorption feature with rest-frame energy 0.574 keV
(21.6 Å). Throughout this investigation, only single-line absorp-
tion spectra were studied, with the rest-frame EWs of the lines
fixed to 0.28 eV (∼10.5 mÅ), representing a mid-strength line;
Wijers et al. (2019) predict a possible maximum EW of 0.42 eV ,
see also Walsh et al. (2020). The rest-frame line width was set
toσ=0.1 eV throughout (Brand et al. 2016). Holding these pa-
rameters fixed for the initial investigation allowed a study of the
effects of line placement in the spectrum. It also provided an
opportunity to investigate the effect of power-law spectral pa-
rameters, which is important when aiming to detect these ab-
sorption features in the spectra of bright transient background
sources, such as GRBs, as they may not be quantified. The line
EW was varied in the later stages of the work. The source fluxes
were modelled as constant over the length of each simulation,
and were set toF 0.3−10.0 keV =5×10 −11 erg s-1 cm-2 throughout,
unless otherwise stated. Keeping the flux constant allowed the
observation length to serve as the primary variable governing
the detected counts, rather than a combination of flux and ob-
servation length. All sources were simulated and fit over the full
0.3 – 10.0 keV energy range using Cash statistics Cash (1979).
Figure 1 displays an example WHIM absorption spectrum sim-
ulated using these observing and source parameters, with a line
redshift ofz=0.25. The Oi-Kαtransition contained inTBabs
can be seen atE∼527.5 eV (∼23.5 Å). Here, we do not con-
sider the warm or hot phases of the local Galactic ISM. This is
because the non-cold intergalatic medium (IGM) is very line-
of-sight dependent, and is not yet well-quantified. This makes it
harder to accurately represent without adding two more dimen-
sions for analysis for the column densities of the warm and hot
phases. Moreover, while the inclusion of such absorbers would
affect the early results of this study, the final results are indepen-
dent of these quantities, discussed further in Section 3.

3.2. Derived count limits
The most direct proxy for determining the expected line signif-
icance is the counts detected in the energy band of interest. To
accurately determine the relation between the significance of de-
tection of a WHIM absorption feature and the counts detected for
lines of a rest-frame EW of 0.28 eV , the simulated observation
length was varied between 6.25 ks and 150.0 ks. The same pro-

3.3. Analysing real sources
Many sources, both of transient and non-transient nature, are
predicted to provide adequate brightness for detecting WHIM
features in absorption. Here we focus our efforts on studying
non-transient sources, simply referred to here as AGN, and pro-
vide a grading scheme for their suitability. To do this, their fluxes
in the energy band of interest and their photon indices must be
examined for different observing lengths given the knownN H
along the line of sight to the source to ensure that they can detect
enough counts (∼800 k for a 3σdetection, and∼1.5 M for 5σ)
in a reasonable observation length.
The AGN that were investigated in this work were selected
due to their inclusion in previous works; Fang et al. (2005) anal-
ysed six different sources for WHIM absorption features in the
X-ray regime, but did not find any lines present across obser-
vations from various instruments; Danforth et al. (2010) studied
thermally broadened Lyαabsorbers in the lines of sight to seven
background sources using HST; Prochaska et al. (2011) investi-
gated the connection between IGM and galaxies by investigating
the fields of 20 UV-bright quasars; Tilton et al. (2012) studied
absorption lines from ions in the UV regime, including Oviand
Cvito account for missing baryonic matter in the local Universe;
Savage et al. (2014), later updated by Stocke et al. (2014), inves-
tigated Oviand Hiabsorption features along the line of sight to
14 quasars; Bregman et al. (2015) compiled a list of background
sources for detecting WHIM features in absorption, and pro-
vided a method to quantify the merit of each source; Dalton et al.
(2021) studied the properties of intervening IGM along the lines
of sight to 40 randomly selected blazars; Jones et al. (2021) de-
veloped a method for constraining the redshifts of blazars, partic-
ularly those which will provide an opportunity to detect WHIM
absorption features; Spence et al. (2024) performed a systematic
search for X-ray absorption features in the spectra of sources
in which UV absorption features have previously been detected.
AGN from these previous works withz>0.1 were selected for
analysis in this work. The full selection of sources can be seen
in Section B.
Archival data of the selected AGN were analysed from the
Swiftdatabase (Evans et al. 2009). For each source, the time-
averaged spectra across all PC-mode observations were cre-
ated. Using these, the flux between 0.3 – 10.0 keV andα X were
extracted by fitting the spectrum with an absorbed power-law
model, allowing theα X and flux to vary until a fit was reached.
TheTBabsN H value was frozen at its known value along the line
of sight to the source. Usingsteppar, the two-dimensional re-
lation between theα X and flux was found, with theN H frozen
at its best-fit value. The limiting ellipse that corresponds to
∆C∼∆χ 2 =9.21 was extracted in each case, providing the
source state at a 99% confidence level, averaged across all ob-
servations. Along this ellipse boundary, the observed Swift count
rates were calculated, andα X and flux values corresponding to
the maximum and minimum count rates were extracted. Using
these values, the expected X-IFU count rate was calculated by
simulating ten 25 ks observations with theα X and flux set to
those corresponding to the best fit, the minimum and the maxi-
mum expected count rates.
To allow for comparison between the AGN and to determine
which are better suited to study the WHIM features in absorp-
tion, we adapted the methods of Bregman et al. (2015), where
the merit of a source can be calculated using

lines of sight and thus have a higher chance of probing filaments.
The full results are discussed in Section 4.3.
This investigation did not account for different states of
the AGN (i.e. flaring), and only used their time-averaged
states as observed by Swift. Flaring causes an increase in
observed flux, but is often correlated to a decrease inα X
(i.e. an increase in hardening). This so-called harder-when-
brighter behaviour has been seen in sources included in Ta-
ble B.2, for example PKS2155-304 (Bhagwan et al. 2016); and
other sources not studied here; Mrk501 (Krawczynski et al.
2000); Mrk421 (Abeysekara et al. 2016; Abe et al. 2025). (e.g.
Zhang et al. 2002) However, other sources have displayed a
weakly correlated softer-when-brighter behaviour during flaring;
PKS0716+714 and 3C66A (Wierzcholska & Wagner 2016); and
others (e.g. Majumdar et al. 2025). In the case of 3C273, the cor-
relation betweenα X and flux has been observed to be positive,
negative and absent over the course of several years (Soldi et al.
2008). For these reasons, flaring states are not considered, as
their complex natures are beyond the scope of this work.

4. Results and discussion
4.1. Line placement effects
Mid-strength WHIM absorption features, in the form of Ovii
lines of rest-frame EW 0.28 eV , have been accurately represented
in the continuum of bright X-ray power-law sources using the
NewAthena X-IFU instrument with the 35 mm mirror defocus-
ing capability, as detailed in Section 2. To test the effects of
line redshift on the measured line significance, advanced sim-
ulations using SIXTE toolkit were carried out. By assuming a
fixed observation length of 50 ks and unabsorbed source flux of
F0.3−10.0 =5×10 −11 erg s-1 cm-2, X-ray background sources were
simulated with photon indices 1.0–3.0 and local Galactic absorp-
tion 1020 – 1022 cm−2 modelled usingTBabs. The line redshifts
were varied in equal steps of sizedz=0.005 forz=0 – 0.5. For
each redshift, theN H value that corresponds to a 3σdetection,
NH,3σ, was calculated, as seen in Figure 3. NoN H values yielded
a significant detection forαX =1.0. It can be seen that at redshifts
near the instrumental feature and localTBabsGalactic absorp-
tion, the measured line significances, and henceN H,3σ was re-
duced. Due to warm and hot IGM lines not modelled in this work
(see Figure 3), a detection of Ovii-Heαabsorption features aris-
ing from the WHIM withz<0.1 is unlikely. At redshiftsz>0.1,
an approximately linear decrease ofN H,3σ with increasingzwas
observed.
4.2. Minimum required counts
Following the initial simulations of varied redshift regions, a re-
lation between counts and measured line significance was found
to determine the minimum counts required for a significant de-
tection. To do this, the redshifts of lines were set to values be-
tweenz=0.110–0.125, whereN H,3σ, as seen in Figure 3, re-
mains approximately constant and is not affected by local ab-
sorption or instrumental features. Figure 4 displays the rela-
tion between observed counts in the 0.3–10.0 keV range and the
measured line significance, which is well described by Equa-
tion (2). Using this relation and its associated 1σuncertainty,
for a WHIM absorption feature with rest-frame EW of 0.28 eV
and redshiftsz=0.110 – 0.125, it was found that∼1.5×10 6
counts in the 0.3–1.0 keV range are required for a 5σdetection,
and∼8×10 5 counts are required for a 3σdetection. While the
range in which the counts detected could have been decreased to
better match the possible locations of where the Oviiabsorption
features falls (that is,∼0.38 – 0.58 keV corresponding toz=0.0 –
0.5), the larger band was chosen to account for other transitions
not included in this work, such as Oviii, Neixand Fexvii.
This process was repeated for lines of rest-frame
EWs=0.14 – 0.42 eV to quantify the count requirements
for a detection at the 3σand 5σlevel. Figure 5 displays the
results from this analysis. The variation related to the count
requirement for a 0.28 eV line arises from the fact that more
simulations were carried out compared to the other EWs. As
expected, fewer counts are required for more prominent lines,
whereas more counts are required for weaker lines, provided that
they are detected in thez=0.100 – 0.125 region. Thus, to probe
weaker lines, NewAthena should target: (1) bright non-transient
sources that can provide enough counts in a feasible exposure
time, or dimmer sources provided a longer exposure is possible;
or (2) transient sources that can provide a high flux over a short
period, given that the ToO response time is sufficient.

4.7. Calibration effects
Due to the ongoing development of the X-IFU instrument, the re-
sults presented in this investigation should be taken as a best-case
scenario. As is the case with XMM Newton’s European Photon
Imaging Camera (EPIC), the spectrum becomes very poorly de-
fined below∼0.5 keV due to detector noise. The variation in
count rate of each energy band in the 0.5−12.0 keV band remains
approximately constant at∼8%, while below 0.4 keV, it reaches
∼14% (Katayama et al. 2004). While the current requirement
for the knowledge of the energy scale is 0.5 eV (see Peille et al.
2025), due to contamination below 0.5 keV it is possible that the
uncertainties in the absolute calibration may be as high as 10-
20%. Moreover, it is a possibility that more features from edges
of materials not previously accounted for will be present below
0.5 keV. A combination of these effects will cause difficulties
when detecting WHIM features in absorption within the key en-
ergy range of<0.5 keV.
4.8. Applicability for transient sources
While this work does not directly analyse transient sources, in
particular GRBs, as a background X-ray beacon for detecting
WHIM features in absorption, it does provide groundwork for
the updated NewAthena configuration for doing so. The main
proxy for detecting these features, regardless of temporal nature,
remains the same: the counts detected in the band of interest. In
this work, we consider the soft X-ray band of energy range 0.3 –
1.0 keV due to the number of X-ray transitions from ions present
in the WHIM falling in this band. As mentioned in Section 1,
the new ToO response time of NewAthena has increased to 12
hours. While this increase will prove problematic for detecting
absorption features in the spectra of GRBs, it may still be pos-
sible to do so with sufficiently bright sources. The count limits
derived in this work, combined with early flux measurements of
GRBs, can be used for determining if a transient event is bright
enough to provide enough counts for a significant detection.
5. Conclusions
This work has investigated the capability of NewAthena to ac-
curately detect missing baryons residing in the WHIM, observed
through single-line Ovii-Heαabsorption features in the contin-
uum spectra of bright background power-law sources. The fea-
ture significances were calculated for these absorption features
across a wide range of varied line redshift, local Galactic ab-
sorption, source photon index and observing length, with the as-
sumption that the line positions were known a priori. The results
of this work are summarised as follows:
1. Using a multidimensional approach, for a fixed unabsorbed
source flux ofF 0.3−10.0 keV =5×10 −11 erg s-1 cm-2 and ob-
servation length of 50 ks, the redshift region corresponding
to the highest usable cold GalacticN H value for a 3σa pri-
ori single-line detection,N H,3σ, was found to bez=0.110 –
0.125. This was done by imprinting a line of fixed rest-frame
EW of 0.28 eV , representing a mid-strength WHIM absorp-
tion feature, at different redshifts between 0.0 and 0.5, and
varying source parameters, including cold Galactic absorp-
tion and photon index. For each set of parameters, 25 lines
were simulated and their significances recorded. For each
redshift region of sizedz=0.005 and photon index, the mea-
sured significance was fit as a decaying exponential func-
tion of absorption, and hence theN H corresponding to a 3σ
detection was found, as seen in Figure 3. This source flux
and observation length allowed significant detections to be
made forα X =2.0 and 3.0, but no significant detections were
found forα X =1.0. Warmer phases of the IGM have not been
included in this work, but known lines that can affect the de-
tection of WHIM absorption features have been included in
the upper portion of Figure 3. No such lines fall within the
redshift region of interest, and hence do not affect any further
calculations in this work.
2. For absorption features within thez=0.110 – 0.125 region,
the number of counts required to obtain a 3σand a 5σde-
tection were found by varying the sourceα X between 1.0 –
3.0, theN H along the line of sight to the source between
1020 – 1022 cm−2, and the observation length between 6.25 –
150.0 ks. It was found that∼7.41 – 8.92×10 5 and∼1.40 –
1.66×10 6 counts are required for a 3σand 5σdetection,
as seen in Figure 4. This process was repeated for lines in
the same redshift region with varied rest-frame EWs be-
tween 0.14 – 0.42 eV . The corresponding count requirements
for these lines can be seen in Figure 5.
3. A sample of known non-transient X-ray background sources
was analysed here to assess their suitability for use in detect-
ing and studying WHIM features in absorption. We utilised
all publicly available data from the Swiftarchive to obtain
the time-averaged spectrum for each source. Each spectrum
was fit, and the two-dimensional relation represented by the
3σellipse between flux and photon index was found using
steppar. Along this ellipse, the source states correspond-
ing to the minimum and maximum Swiftcount rates were
extracted. Ten 25 ks observations were simulated for each
of the best fit, minimum and maximum count parameters to
obtain the expected X-IFU count rates for each source. The
merit for each source considered was then calculated, which
can be seen in Figure 6. The full list of sources considered
can be seen in Table B.2.
4. The measured single-line a priori significance averaged
across all redshifts between 0.0 and 0.5 for an observation
length of 50 ks and unabsorbed source flux ofF 0.3−10.0 keV =
5×10 −11 erg s-1 cm-2 was found to decrease with increas-
ing Galactic absorption, and increase with increasing source
photon index, as seen in the top plot of Figure 7. For the case
ofα X =1.0, no significant lines were consistently detected,
while a clear decreasing trend can be seen forα X =2.0 and
3.0. This reinforces that a higher photon index and lower
Galactic absorption should be favoured when selecting a
source to use as a background beacon to detect WHIM ab-
sorption features.
5. The recovery of the measured rest-frame EWs was inves-
tigated, as displayed in Figure 7. A higherα X and lower
NH are required to allow better measurements with mini-
mal spread. This is clearly demonstrated for aα X =3.0 for
lower absorption values. With decreasingα X, the spread of
the measurements increases. For increasing values ofNH, the
spread also increases, and forN H ≳3×10 21 cm−2, the line
width recovery becomes extremely poor. No sources above
thisN H should be considered as a background X-ray source
for studying WHIM features in absorption.
6. Across fixed values ofα X and flux in the 0.3 – 10.0 keV
range, an increase in GalacticN H causes a decrease in counts
detected in the 0.3-1.0 keV band, and hence lowers the mea-
sured line significances. Figure 8 displays the proportions of
lines that fall below 5σ, 3σand 2σthresholds, as well as
lines which are deemed undetected. Using these proportions,
GalacticN H limits for eachα X, and hence count limits, were
