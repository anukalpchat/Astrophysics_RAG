ABSTRACT
We report the identification of the likely X-ray and optical counterpart to the unassociatedFermisource 4FGL J2249.4+6229.
To clarify its nature, we investigate the X-ray data fromSwift/XRT and SRG/eROSITAas well as photometric data from optical
catalogues and archival spectroscopic data from theGemini-North telescope. Using Zwicky Transient Facility data spanning
over6.6yr,weconfirmedaperiodof≈5.6hlikelyassociatedwiththeorbitalmotioninabinarysystem.Thefoldedlightcurves
have a smooth sinusoidal shape with two peaks per period and the amplitude of≈0.2 mag. The X-ray spectra of the source are
well fitted by an absorbed power law with the photon index of≈2.0 and unabsorbed flux of≈1.4×10−13 erg s−1 cm−2. All these
together with the X-ray to optical flux ratio of∼0.2 implies that 4FGL J2249.4+6229 is a promising redback candidate. Fitting
the optical light curves with the direct heating model, we obtained the companion mass of≈0.5 M⊙ and temperature of≈3600
K implying an M-type star. This places it among the coldest and most massive companions known in redback systems. Optical
spectra confirms the M-type star and shows the broad asymmetric H𝛼emission line. For the distance of 500–550 pc derived
from the optical data, the source can be the redback with the lowest X-ray and𝛾-ray luminosities.

1 INTRODUCTION
Thanks to theFermiGamma-ray Space Telescope, a large num-
ber of binary millisecond pulsars (MSPs) have been discovered in
recent years. Among them, there are members of the ‘spider’ fam-
ily: redbacks (RBs) and black widows (BWs) (Roberts 2013). They
are characterised by short orbital periods,𝑃𝑏 <1d, and low-mass
companionstars.CompanionsofBWsaresemi-degenerateandhave
masses𝑀 c <0.05M ⊙,whileRBspossessnon-degenerateandmore
massive (𝑀c ≈0.1–1 M⊙) secondaries. Three RBs – the so-called
transitional millisecond pulsars (tMSPs) – show transitions between
rotation-powered pulsar and active X-ray states (Papitto & de Mar-
tino2022)confirmingtheevolutionarylinkbetweenlow-massX-ray
binaries (LMXBs) and MSPs. The pulsar mass in spider systems
can exceed 2 M⊙ which makes their studies particularly important
for constraining the equation of state of the superdense matter in
neutron stars (NSs) interiors (e.g. Kumar et al. 2023).
Spidersshoworbitalmodulationintheopticalduetotheheatingof
thecompanionbythepulsarand/oritsellipsoidalshape(e.g.Callanan
etal.1995;Draghisetal.2019;Clarketal.2021;MataSánchezetal.
2023;Kandel&Romani2023;Dodgeetal.2024),andinX-raysdue
to the intrabinary shock (IBS) produced by the interacting winds
from the pulsar and its companion (e.g. Romani & Sanchez 2016;
Sullivan & Romani 2024). The optical spectra are dominated by the
cool secondary star (e.g. van Kerkwijk et al. 2011; Romani & Shaw

To date, about 80 confirmed spider pulsars have been detected in
the Galactic field (Koljonen & Linares 2025). However, a signifi-

the periodic obscuration of the pulsar’s radio emission by ablated
material from the companion star, which prevents the detection of
pulsations. Nevertheless, these systems can be identified through
multi-wavelengthinvestigations,especiallyintheopticalandX-rays,
of likely counterparts to unassociatedFermisources (e.g. Salvetti
et al. 2017; Braglia et al. 2020). Currently, such studies have re-

2021, 2022; Halpern 2022; Karpova et al. 2023, 2025; Zyuzin et al.

5 DISCUSSION
We have found a likely X-ray and optical counterpart to the unas-
sociatedFermisource J2249. ItsGaiamagnitude𝐺=18.01and
colour𝐵𝑃−𝑅𝑃=2.33combined with the observed X-ray flux in
the 0.2–12 keV band of≈1.2×10 −13 erg s−1 cm−2 provides the
X-ray to optical flux ratio of∼0.2. According to fig. 1 in Rodriguez
(2024), this implies that the source can belong to the spider pulsar
family.
6 https://www.swift.ac.uk/user_objects/
The optical light curves of the source are typical for RB systems
where ellipsoidal modulations strongly dominate over the effect of
thecompanionheatingbythepulsar.Moreover,theirradiationeffect
here is small or even absent as observed for some RBs, e.g., PSR
J1622−0315orPSRJ1816+4510(Turchettaetal.2023;Koljonen&
Linares 2023).
The effective temperature of the J2249 counterpart candidate de-
rivedfromthelightcurvesfittingis3560(50)Kwhichisclosetothe
M2V-type star. This is confirmed by the optical spectroscopy. The
mentioned in Sec. 3.3 flux excess in the∼5000–5600 Å range may
beattributedtoahotspotonthecompanion’ssurfaceasobservedin
somesystems(e.g.Swihartetal.2019;Kandeletal.2020).However,
additionaltime-resolvedspectroscopicandphotometricobservations
areneededtoconfirmitspresence.Basedontheseresults,J2249can
have one of the coldest companions among known RB systems.
Indeed, typical temperatures are higher, about 4000–6000 K (e.g.
Turchetta et al. 2023). The even lower base (‘night-side’) tempera-
ture of≈3300 K was obtained only for PSR J2339−0533 (Kandel
et al. 2020). In addition, PSR J1628−3205 can have a rather cold
companion though its temperature is quite uncertain, 3560–4670 K
(Li, Halpern & Thorstensen 2014).
With a mass of≈0.5 M⊙ the J2249 likely counterpart resides in
the high-mass tail of the RB companion population (Strader et al.
2019), possibly making it one of the most massive known in this
class. Other examples of massive sources are, e.g., companions of
PSR J1306−4035 (𝑀c =0.51 +0.02
−0.01 M⊙; Swihart et al. 2019), PSR
J1803−6707 (𝑀c =0.44 +0.05
−0.04 M⊙; Phosrisom et al. 2026), tMSP
candidate 3FGL J0427.9−6704 (𝑀c =0.65(8); Strader et al. 2016)
andRBcandidate1FGLJ0523.5−2529(𝑀 c ≳0.8M ⊙;Straderetal.
2014).
The most interesting feature in the optical spectrum of the J2249
likely counterpart is the Balmer emission lines. Such emission lines
(sometimes clearly double-peaked) were observed for some spider
pulsars. For example, PSR J2339−0533 mentioned above shows
strong H𝛼and weaker H𝛽emission lines in some spectra (Kan-
del et al. 2020). Other examples are RBs PSR J1048+2339 (Miraval
Zanon et al. 2021), PSR J1306−4035 Swihart et al. (2019), PSR
J1628−3205 (Strader et al. 2019) and PSR J0838−2827 (Halpern,

6 CONCLUSIONS
To sum up, J2249 is likely a rare member of the RB family – one
with a cool companion and unusually low X-ray and𝛾-ray lumi-
nosities. Such systems are challenging to detect due to the limited
depth of current optical and X-ray surveys. At a distance just three
times greater (≳1.5 kpc), J2249 would have remained undetected
byeROSITAandSwift, and its optical periodicity would have been
missed in ZTF data.
Searching for pulsations in the radio from an NS are needed to
confirm the RB nature of the J2249 likely optical/X-ray counterpart.
The present analysis is limited by the relatively noisy ZTF data
and sparse spectroscopic coverage. Deeper, phase-resolved optical
photometryandspectroscopyarethereforenecessarytoconfirmand
robustly constrain the system parameters, including the mass ratio
andpulsarmassderivedfromradialvelocitycurves,andtoinvestigate
the IBS geometry.
