ABSTRACT
We test whether compact objects in the so called mass gap (2.5< M max/M⊙ <5.0) can be hybrid
stars. Using a generic hybrid equation of state with a first-order deconfinement transition, we map the
allowed parameter space in a Seidov-type diagram and confront it with modern mass–radius constraints.
We find that mass-gap hybrid stars require an extremely early onset of deconfinement and very stiff
quark matter. The Bayesian analysis, however, favors equations of state with deconfinement at typical
neutron-star masses around 1.4M ⊙ with mass-twin stars that, if confirmed, would rule out hybrid
stars as candidates for observed mass-gap compact objects.
Keywords:neutron stars — mass-gap objects — hybrid stars — mass-twin compact stars
1.INTRODUCTION
Multi-messenger astrophysics by combining gravita-
tional waves, electromagnetic signals, and other cosmic
messengers provides unprecedented insights into the in-
ternal composition of neutron stars (NSs) and proper-
ties of strongly interacting matter at extreme condi-
tions (Koehn et al. 2025; Ascenzi et al. 2024). Thus,
the mass and radius of NSs are measured by X-ray and
radio observations, gravitational wave detections of bi-
nary NS and NS-black hole mergers constrain the star’s
tidal deformability related to the equation of state (EoS)
of NS matter (Abbott et al. 2018; Lattimer 2021). Grav-
itational wave signals from merger events of compact ob-
jects have led to the detection of objects in the mass gap
2.5≤M/M ⊙ ≤5, recent examples being: GW170817
Abbott et al. (2017) with a total mass of 2.74 +0.04
−0.01 M⊙,
GW190814 Abbott et al. (2020) where the lighter com-

nature of these objects is unclear: are they necessarily

With this setup of the EoS we can answer our question
whether mass gap objects like the ones we mentioned
in the Introduction could be not just neutron stars or
hybrid stars, but even members of the third family of
hybrid stars, thus forming a separate pulsar population.
In addition, we want to give an argument why the
why the existence of a third family and thus of mass-
twins of compact stars could be important for pulsar
phenomenology. In a recent investigation, Chanlaridis
et al. (2025) considered the puzzle of the appearance of
millisecond pulsars (MSPs) in eccentric orbits with ec-
centricities of the order ofe≈0.1 in some low-mass X-
ray binary (LMXB) systems (see Grunthal et al. (2024)
for a recent compilation of data for those objects) while
generally the orbits of those systems are extremely well
circular withe <10 −5. They demonstrated that an
accretion-induced phase transition from a hadronic to
a hybrid mass twin star can explain the eccentric or-
bits when this transition is accompanied with a gravita-
tional mass defect ∆M/M⊙ ≈0.01 which induces a kick
to the pulsar. For this scenario to work it is necessary
that the transition between hadronic and hybrid star
configurations can be completed in a sufficiently small
time, much smaller than the typical orbital period of
≈20−30 days for these objects, see Table 1 of Grun-
thal et al. (2024). As it was argued in Chanlaridis et al.
(2025), because of angular momentum conservation in
this transition, the fast conversion between two configu-
rations is possible only when the final state is on a third
family branch. Conversion to a connected hybrid star
configuration would require the change of angular mo-
mentum which takes about 10 4 years, see Glendenning
et al. (1997).

From these diagrams, one can read off, that mass
gap objects (M max ≥2.5M ⊙) can be hybrid stars as
they populate the lower left corner of all three Seidov
diagrams. The highest maximum masses are obtained
for crossover type transitions (∆n= 0), lowest possi-
ble transition densities and maximum stiffness of quark
matter. As it has been recently reported in a revisiting
the Rhoades-Ruffini bound (Rhoades & Ruffini 1974) by
Blaschke & Wojcik (2026), maximum masses of hybrid
stars may exceed 4M ⊙, see the lower-left corner of the
bottom panel in Fig. 2, if the onset of deconfinement
could be around the nuclear saturation density and the
speed of sound could reach the speed of light. However,
in order to find cases of third-family objects among the
high-mass hybrid stars, the density jump at the decon-
finement transition has to exceed the value determined
by the modified Seidov criterion, which is shown by the
black line in Fig. 2.
For the typical value of the squared sound speed in
color superconducting quark matter models (Contrera
et al. 2022),c 2
s = 0.5, one can find mass-gap objects
for all onset densities below 2.5n 0. But third family
members among them are just a marginal case at the
left border of the Seidov diagram, forn onset =n 0 and
∆n= 0.6n 0.
Following the arguments of Hippert et al. (2025) on
the basis of relativistic hydrodynamic transport, the
maximum possible squared sound speed in dense nuclear
matter isc 2
s ≤0.781, well represented by the case of the
middle panel in Fig. 2. There, third-family members are
found among the mass-gap objects for all onset densities
fulfillingn onset ≤1.5n 0. However, the transition from
hadronic star configurations to their third-family mass
twins involves mass defects of only∼10 −4 M⊙ which are
too small to generate a sufficient kick that would explain
the observed eccentricities of some MSPs in LMXBs. In
order to be relevant for explaining the eccentric MSP
puzzle, the onset mass for the deconfinement transition
should exceed∼1.2M ⊙, which concerns onset densities
nonset ≳1.7n 0. The interesting case ofM onset ∼1.4M ⊙
(see Fig. 1) would implyn onset ∼1.9n 0. For these onset
masses, mass gap objects are not members of the third
family.
So far we were focused on discussing the theoretical
possibility that mass gap objects could be members of
the third family of hybrid stars and have identified the
conditions under which this is the case. We should, how-
ever, not ignore the existing observational constraints
on masses and radii of neutron stars which need to be
simultaneously fulfilled. In order to take into account
theseM−Rconstraints depicted in Fig. 1, we ap-
ply the Bayesian analysis scheme described in Ayriyan
et al. (2025a), but with the quark matter EoS param-
eters (n onset,∆n) instead of the Lagrangian model pa-
rameters for the coupling strengths in the vector me-
son and diquark interaction channels (η V , ηD) of that
model approach. The results are shown as a grayscale
heatmap in Fig. 2. We want to focus our discussion
on the top and middle panels since the case of the bot-
tom panel (c 2
s = 1.00) cannot be considered a realistic
one. There are solutions with an early onset of decon-
finement, 1.0≤n onset/n0 ≤1.5, corresponding to on-
set masses below 0.5M ⊙, which have maximum masses
above 2M ⊙, but can reach also into the mass gap re-
gion forc 2
s = 0.75. See Fig. 1 for the corresponding
M(R) line. Such solutions have recently been identified
as favorable ones within a Bayesian analysis performed
in Ayriyan et al. (2025b).
The other solutions with high posterior probabil-
ity correspond to onset masses with twin star solu-
tions around 1.4M ⊙, but for these sequences maximum
masses just reach beyond 2M ⊙ but cannot reach the
mass gap region. This latter solution class could sup-
port the speculation that PSR J0614-3329 and PSR
J0437-4715 (possibly including PSR J0030+0451) could
be mass twin stars, see Fig. 1. This EoS would then
support the scenario of an accretion induced deconfine-
ment transition as a solution of the puzzle of eccentric
MSP orbits (Chanlaridis et al. 2025).
4.CONCLUSIONS
In this letter we have shown that for a generic two-
parametric hybrid neutron star EoS in dependence of
the onset density and density jump at the deconfinement
transition there exist parameter sets for which the max-
imum mass is in the mass gap region,M max >2.5M ⊙.
These sequences belong to an early onset of deconfine-
ment atM onset ≲0.7M ⊙ with onset densities 1.0≤
nonset/n0 ≤1.2. In order to justify that such low on-
set densities may be plausible, we refer to arguments
from the occupation of fermionic phase space in cold,
degenerate (quarkyonic) matter, where due to quark ex-
change effects among nucleons (quark Pauli blocking, see
Blaschke et al. (2020)) just above the nuclear saturation
density forn T = 0.17 fm−3 = 1.13n 0 a transformation
of nuclear matter to quarkyonic matter sets in (Koch
et al. 2024; McLerran & Miller 2024).

Should, however, the conjecture be confirmed that the
pulsars PSR J0614-3329 and PSR J0437-4715 are mass
twin stars at 1.4M ⊙, then the maximum mass for such
a hybrid EoS shall be limited toM max <2.2M ⊙ so that
the detected mass gap objects cannot be hybrid neutron
stars but rather must be black holes.
