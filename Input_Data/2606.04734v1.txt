ABSTRACT
In this Letter, we report the detection of an absorption-like feature at ∼ 1.89 keV in Chandra/ACIS spectra of ultraluminous X-
ray source NGC 4861 X–2, based on the deepest observation (ObsID 20992; ∼ 58 ks). The feature is consistently recovered across
independent continuum models and signiﬁcantly improves the ﬁt statistics. Monte Carlo simulations yield a detection signiﬁcance
of ∼ 3.5–4.1σ, depending on the adopted continuum, and a blind line scan reveals a single, localized peak at the same energy. The
observed properties are consistent with a proton cyclotron resonant scattering feature (CRSF), implying a magnetic ﬁeld strength of
B ∼ (3–4) × 1014 G. The spectrum is well described by a multicolor disk blackbody ( diskbb) with kTin ∼ 0.8 keV or a strongly curved
continuum with a low cuto ﬀ (cutoﬀpl) energy ( Ecut ∼ 1.3 keV). The source shows variability conﬁned to the soft X-ray band in the
two Chandra observations where the absorption-like feature is detected. In these observations, a candidate periodic signal at P ≈ 7.4
s is also detected, with a global signiﬁcance of ∼ 2.5σ.
Key words. X-rays: binaries – accretion, accretion disks – stars: neutron – stars: magnetic ﬁeld – X-rays: individual: NGC 4861 X–2
1. Introduction
Ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs) are o ﬀ-nuclear point
sources with apparent X-ray luminosities exceeding 2 × 1039 erg
s−1, above the Eddington limit for typical stellar-mass compact
objects (Kaaret et al. 2017; Fabrika et al. 2021; King et al. 2023;
Pinto & Walton 2023). The currently favored interpretation is
that most ULXs are powered by super-Eddington accretion onto
stellar-mass black holes or neutron stars (NSs), where geomet-
ric beaming and radiation-driven outﬂows can signiﬁcantly en-
hance the apparent luminosity ( Poutanen et al. 2007; King 2009;
Pinto et al. 2016; Kosec et al. 2018). The discovery of coherent
X-ray pulsations in several ULXs has provided unambiguous ev-
idence that a subset of the population hosts accreting NSs ( Ba-
chetti et al. 2014; Motch et al. 2014; Israel et al. 2017; Carpano
et al. 2018; Wilson-Hodge et al. 2018; Rodríguez Castillo et al.
2020; Ducci et al. 2025). These sources provide important labo-
ratories for studying accretion physics in the presence of strong
magnetic ﬁelds. Recent studies suggest that some ULX pulsars
may host strong dipolar magnetic ﬁelds of order ∼ 1013–1014 G,
potentially enabling super-Eddington accretion via magnetically
channeled inﬂow (e.g., Erkut et al. 2020).
In this context, cyclotron resonant scattering features
(CRSFs) provide the only direct observational probe of the mag-
netic ﬁeld strength near the surface of accreting NSs ( Meszaros
1992; Caballero & Wilms 2012; Staubert et al. 2019). These
absorption-like features arise from resonant scattering of X-
ray photons by charged particles quantized in Landau levels
in strong magnetic ﬁelds ( Schwarm et al. 2017). These fea-
tures frequently exhibit harmonic structures and strong pulse-
phase dependence, reﬂecting the complex geometry of the ac-
cretion column and magnetic ﬁeld conﬁguration ( Maitra et al.
2012; Staubert et al. 2019). Candidate CRSFs have also been
reported in extragalactic ULXs, including M51 ULX-8 ( Bright-
man et al. 2018) and NGC 4656 ULX-1 ( Cruz-Sanchez et al.
2026), extending CRSF phenomenology into the ultraluminous
regime. CRSFs can arise from either electrons or protons and
therefore probe di ﬀerent magnetic ﬁeld regimes. Electron cy-
clotron lines are typically observed at tens of keV , implying mag-
netic ﬁeld strengths of ∼ 1012 G (Truemper et al. 1978; Coburn
et al. 2002). In contrast, proton cyclotron features are expected at
much lower energies (typically ∼ 0.1–a few keV) for magnetar-
strength ﬁelds of B ∼ 1014–1015 G (Harding & Lai 2006; Tiengo
et al. 2013). The detection of an isolated absorption feature at
soft X-ray energies without harmonics is therefore commonly
interpreted as indicative of a proton CRSF ( Potekhin 2010).
NGC 4861 (Mrk 59) is a Magellanic-type irregular galaxy at
a distance of 9.95 Mpc ( Tully et al. 2013). It hosts two ULXs,
X–1 and X–2, identiﬁed with ROSAT (Colbert & Ptak 2002; Liu
& Bregman 2005). Follow-up XMM–Newton and Chandra ob-
servations associate these sources with H II regions and massive
stars, and in the Chandra observation, a thermal disk model is
preferred with a characteristic temperature of kT ∼ 0.80 keV,
and its luminosity lies in the range (1 −3) × 1039 erg s−1 (Thuan
et al. 2014; Ozdogan Ela et al. 2021). In this Letter, we report
the detection of an absorption feature at ∼ 1.89 keV in the Chan-
dra/ACIS-S spectrum of NGC 4861 X–2. The feature is consis-
tently recovered across di ﬀerent continuum models and is sup-
ported by Monte Carlo simulations and a blind line scan. Its

2. Observation, data reduction and analysis
2.1. Observation
NGC 4861 was observed multiple times with Chandra and
XMM–Newton. The available Chandra dataset consists of one
ACIS-I observation and four ACIS-S observations obtained be-
tween 2012 and 2018, while three XMM–Newton observations
were carried out in 2003. A summary of the observations used in
this work is provided in Table A.1.
2.2. Energy spectra
The spectral analysis was performed using XSPEC ( Arnaud
1996) over the 0.3–10 keV band for the deeper Chandra/ACIS-
S observation (ObsID 20992; ∼58 ks), employing a range of
spectral models, including single-component continua such as
diskbb, a power law (pl), and a cuto ﬀ power law ( cutoﬀpl), as
well as combinations of these components, together with pho-
toelectric absorption modeled using tbabs. The Galactic col-
umn density was ﬁxed at NH,Gal = 1.3 × 1020 cm−2 (Dickey &
Lockman 1990), while any additional absorption was allowed to
vary unless otherwise noted. Given the limited number of source
counts, all spectral ﬁtting was performed using C-statistics. The
spectra were grouped using both 10 and 15 counts per bin in
order to assess the robustness of the detected feature against
spectral grouping. Unless otherwise stated, all parameter un-
certainties are quoted at the 90% conﬁdence level. Among the
models considered, the diskbb and cutoﬀpl models provided the
best phenomenological descriptions of the spectrum and were
therefore adopted for the subsequent analysis. The diskbb model
yields an inner-disk temperature of kTin = 0.79+0.06
−0.05 keV . In this
case, the intrinsic column density is not constrained by the data
and was therefore ﬁxed at NH = 1.3 × 1020 cm−2. The cutoﬀpl
model gives a hard photon index of Γ = 0.76+0.51
−0.54, together with
a low cuto ﬀ energy of Ecut = 1.37+0.74
−0.37 keV , indicating strong
spectral curvature already within the Chandra band.
Although both continua provide statistically acceptable de-
scriptions of the overall spectrum, they leave a localized nega-
tive residual at ∼ 1.89 keV (Fig. 1, middle panel). The resid-
ual appears at the same energy in both models, supporting its
model-independent nature. We therefore added a multiplicative
Gaussian absorption component, gabs, to each continuum. The
best-ﬁt line parameters are consistent across di ﬀerent continuum
models, with a centroid energy of Eline ∼ 1.89 keV , a width of
σ ∼ 0.08 keV , and a line strength ofS line ∼ 0.2 keV . The addition
of the absorption component leads to a signiﬁcant improvement
in the ﬁt, yielding ∆C = 20.96 and ∆C = 17.23 for the spec-
tra grouped to 10 counts per bin using the diskbb and cutoﬀpl
models, respectively. Consistent improvements are also obtained
for the spectra grouped to 15 counts per bin using C-statistics,
with ∆C = 20.04 and ∆C = 17.35, respectively. As shown in
the lower panel of Fig. 1, including the absorption component
removes the residual structure around 1.89 keV . This indicates
that the feature is not driven by the choice of ﬁtting statistic.
We derived the statistical signiﬁcance of the absorption feature
using Monte Carlo simulations with the XSPEC simftest routine
(see Appendix A.1). The feature is detected with signiﬁcances of
∼ 4.1σ and ∼ 3.5σ for the diskbb and cutoﬀpl continua, respec-
tively. The blind line scan also reveals a single localized peak at
∼ 1.89 keV (see Appendix A.1), supporting the robustness of the

3. Discussion and conclusions
We report the detection of an absorption feature at ∼ 1.89 keV
in the deepest 59 ks Chandra observation of NGC 4861 X–2.
The feature is consistently recovered across the adopted con-
tinuum models ( diskbb and cutoﬀpl), with signiﬁcant improve-
ments in the ﬁt statistic ( ∆C ≈ 17–21 across the adopted contin-
uum models and spectral grouping schemes). Monte Carlo sim-
ulations yield signiﬁcances of ∼ 3.5σ and 4 .1σ for the cutoﬀpl
and diskbb continua, respectively. To search for localized spec-
tral features without prior assumptions on their energy, we per-
formed a blind line scan over the 0.3–10 keV band (Fig. A.4; see
Appendix A.1). These results indicate that the feature is local-
ized and statistically robust.
The inferred line width ( σ ∼ 0.08 keV; FWHM ∼ 0.19 keV)
exceeds the ACIS-S energy resolution at this energy ( Plucinsky
et al. 2003, ∼100–150 eV; FWHM), suggesting that the mea-
sured width is broader than expected from the instrumental re-
sponse alone. Although an atomic origin cannot be ruled out, as
ULXs are known to host highly ionized outﬂows capable of pro-
ducing narrow absorption features in their X-ray spectra (e.g.,
Pinto et al. 2016; Kosec et al. 2021), a simple photoionized ab-
sorber scenario is disfavored since no accompanying transitions
(e.g. Si XIV at ∼2.006 keV) are detected. A possible identiﬁca-
tion is the Si XIII He α complex at ∼1.86 keV , but the absence of
expected additional lines weakens a straightforward atomic in-
terpretation. Given the limited data quality, alternative scenarios
such as absorption in outﬂowing material cannot be excluded.
To further test whether the feature could be a ﬀected by oversam-
pling near the instrumental Si K-edge, we regrouped the spec-
trum using the optimal binning method of Kaastra & Bleeker
(2016) (see Appendix A). The absorption-like feature near 1.89
keV remains visible with consistent best-ﬁt parameters and a sig-
niﬁcance of ∼ 3.6σ based on simulations. Finally, no signiﬁcant
gain shift is required, ruling out an instrumental origin for the
feature.
An additional Chandra observation (ObsID 19497) shows a
weak ( ∼ 2.2σ) feature at 1 .97 ± 0.07 keV , consistent in energy
with that detected in the deepest observation. The line centroid
is consistent, within uncertainties, with that of the deepest Chan-
dra observation, indicating no signiﬁcant energy shift between
the two epochs. No statistically signiﬁcant feature is detected
in the other Chandra or available XMM-Newton observations,
likely due to lower photon statistics, although intrinsic variability
(e.g., in viewing geometry, accretion rate, or accretion-column
structure; Staubert et al. 2019; Becker et al. 2012) cannot be ex-
cluded. Moreover, we computed 90% upper limits on the line
strength for the remaining Chandra observations without a sig-
niﬁcant feature by ﬁxing the line energy and width to the val-
ues measured in ObsID 20992. The resulting upper limits are
S line < 0.18 keV ,< 0.06 keV , and< 0.06 keV for ObsIDs 12473,
20993, and 21036, respectively. These limits are consistent with
the line strength measured in the deepest observation, indicat-
ing that the non-detections can be explained by the lower data
quality. The recurrence of the feature at the same energy in an
independent observation disfavors a statistical ﬂuctuation or a
continuum modeling artifact. This behavior supports a common
physical origin, most naturally interpreted as a CRSF, and im-
plies a persistent and highly magnetized accretion environment.
The centroid energy of the feature at ∼ 1.89 keV allows
an estimate of the magnetic ﬁeld strength under cyclotron in-
terpretations. For a proton cyclotron origin, using Ecyc,p ≃
0.63 B14/(1 + z) keV ( Harding & Lai 2006), the observed en-
ergy implies B ≃ (3.6–4.2) × 1014 G for gravitational redshifts
(1 + z) = 1.2–1.4, implying magnetic ﬁeld strengths in the mag-
netar ﬁeld-strength range, while the source itself remains con-
sistent with an accreting NS. In contrast, an electron cyclotron
interpretation, based on Ecyc,e ≃ 11.6 B12/(1 + z) keV ( Staubert
et al. 2019), yields B ≃ (1.9–2.3) × 1011 G for the same redshift
range. While such values are typical of accreting NSs, several

models of ULX pulsars invoke strong magnetic ﬁelds to sustain
super-Eddington accretion through magnetically channeled in-
ﬂow onto the NS poles ( Mushtukov et al. 2015; Kaaret et al.
2017). The inferred ﬁeld likely reﬂects the local conditions in
the line-forming region rather than the global dipole compo-
nent, consistent with the presence of complex, possibly multi-
polar magnetic ﬁeld geometries proposed for some ULX pulsars
(e.g. Israel et al. 2017). Proton cyclotron features have also been
reported in other ULXs. In M51 ULX-8, an absorption feature
at ∼ 4.5 keV has been interpreted as a proton CRSF ( Bright-
man et al. 2018), while a feature at ∼ 3.3 keV has been reported
in NGC 4656 ULX-1 ( Cruz-Sanchez et al. 2026). Compared
to these sources, the lower energy of the feature in NGC 4861
X–2 implies a somewhat lower magnetic ﬁeld strength, but still
within the magnetar regime.
Assuming the absorption feature represents a proton cy-
clotron line, the observed width ( σ ≈ 0.08 keV) likely requires
additional broadening mechanisms beyond the instrumental re-
sponse. In CRSFs, the line proﬁle can be a ﬀected by pulse-phase
averaging due to angle-dependent scattering and changing view-
ing geometry during the neutron-star rotation cycle. Therefore,
part of the observed broadening may arise from phase-dependent
variations of the line centroid and depth ( Maitra & Paul 2013).
However, if the measured width were interpreted purely as ther-
mal Doppler broadening of a proton CRSF, the required plasma
temperature would be unrealistically high (kT ∼ 1.5 MeV). Sim-
ilarly, rotational Doppler broadening alone would require spin
frequencies much higher than the candidate ∼ 7.4 s periodicity
inferred from the timing analysis. This suggests that magnetic-
ﬁeld gradients, geometric averaging, or scattering and reprocess-
ing in optically thick funnel material may also contribute to the
observed line width. One possible scenario is that an intrinsi-
cally narrower proton cyclotron feature becomes broadened dur-
ing multiple scatterings in the funnel walls. In this case, the re-
quired electron temperature ( ∼ 0.9 keV) is comparable to that
inferred from the diskbb continuum model.
The diskbb model yields kTin ∼ 0.8 keV and an apparent
inner disk radius of Rin ∼ 130 km (assuming a distance of 10
Mpc and a face-on geometry), corresponding to ∼ 150 km after
standard corrections. Although larger than a typical NS radius,
this estimate should not be interpreted literally, as the emission
in ULXs is likely dominated by the accretion column and repro-
cessing in an optically thick wind or funnel. The cutoﬀpl model
instead indicates strong spectral curvature within the Chandra
band, with a low cuto ﬀ energy ( Ecut ∼ 1.3 keV) implying that
the rollover occurs within the observed range. This suggests that
the model primarily traces the intrinsic spectral curvature.
The candidate periodic signals detected in the two Chan-
dra observations show similar properties, with modulation con-
ﬁned to the soft X-ray band and no signiﬁcant variability in the
hard band. The centroid frequencies are consistent within un-
certainties, with f ≈ 0.135 Hz (corresponding to P ≈ 7.4 s)
in both observations, although the limited statistical signiﬁcance
prevents a ﬁrm conclusion. Given the limited statistical signif-
icance and short exposure of ObsID 19497, we cannot distin-
guish between intrinsic spin evolution and orbital e ﬀects. The
non-detection in the remaining datasets is consistent with lim-
ited photon statistics and possible frequency drift. The derived
pulse fraction ( PF ∼ 41 − −51%) is consistent with values typ-
ically observed in ULX pulsars, supporting the interpretation of
an NS accretor.
In summary, we identify a localized absorption feature at
∼ 1.89 keV that is robust against continuum modeling and con-
sistent with a possible proton cyclotron origin. The observed line
width may result from a combination of phase-dependent CRSF
variability, magnetic-ﬁeld gradients, and scattering or reprocess-
ing in optically thick funnel material. A feature at the same en-
ergy is also tentatively detected in an additional Chandra obser-
vation, providing further support for a physical origin. We also
ﬁnd candidate soft-band periodic signals at P ≈ 7.4 s in two
Chandra observations, with properties consistent with a possi-
ble NS spin modulation. The fact that both the candidate peri-
odic modulation and the absorption feature are detected in the
same two observations may indicate a common physical origin,
possibly related to the accretion column of a highly magnetized
NS.

Appendix A: Energy spectra
The Chandra ACIS-S (Advanced CCD Imaging Spectrome-
ter Spectroscopic array) observations were reduced using the
Chandra Interactive Analysis of Observations (CIAO; Fruscione
et al. (2006)) together with the most recent calibration database
CALDB. Level 2 event ﬁles were generated using the standard
chandra_repro pipeline. Spectra and light curves were extracted
with the specextract and dmextract tools, respectively. For the
Chandra analysis, the source spectrum was extracted from a cir-
cular region with a radius of 4 ′′ centered on the source position.
This radius encloses the majority of the Chandra/ACIS point-
spread function at the source position. Larger extraction regions
were avoided due to the presence of nearby sources, which could
lead to contamination. Background spectra were extracted from
nearby source-free circular regions with radii of 8 ′′ on the same
CCD. In addition, ﬁve independent background regions were
tested (see Fig. A.3) to assess whether the spectral results depend
on the choice of background. Background spectra extracted from
these regions are consistent with each other and do not show any
feature at ∼ 1.9 keV . In addition, the pile-up fraction was es-
timated using the CIAO task pileup_map, yielding a maximum
value of < 3% within the source extraction region. This level
is well below that at which pile-up is expected to signiﬁcantly
distort the spectral shape in ACIS observations, and therefore,
pile-up does not a ﬀect our spectral results.
The XMM-Newton EPIC data were reduced using the Sci-
ence Analysis System ( SAS) v22.0 with the most recent calibra-
tion ﬁles. Calibrated event lists were produced using epproc for
EPIC-pn and emproc for EPIC-MOS. Periods of high particle
background were ﬁltered out using full-ﬁeld high-energy light
curves to deﬁne good-time intervals. V alid X-ray events were
selected by requiring PA TTERN ≤4 for pn and PA TTERN ≤12
for MOS, together with FLAG==0. However, the XMM–Newton
analysis is aﬀected by contamination from nearby variable X-ray
sources. We adopted a relatively small extraction region to min-
imize this eﬀect; however, two nearby variable X-ray sources lie
within ∼ 8′′ of X–2, and their contribution cannot be fully ex-
cluded given the EPIC PSF. We also tested smaller extraction
regions, but no robust result was obtained. We therefore do not
use the XMM–Newton data to constrain the presence of the ∼ 1.9
keV feature.
Chandra spectra were grouped to ensure a minimum of 10
and 15 counts per bin, providing a balance between spectral res-
olution and statistical reliability, and all spectral ﬁts were per-
formed using C-statistics (cstat). The resulting best-ﬁt parame-
ters obtained with the di ﬀerent grouping schemes are consistent
within uncertainties. Parameter errors were estimated at the 90%
conﬁdence level using the XSPEC steppar command. To assess
the robustness of the detected feature against spectral grouping,
spectra grouped with minimum bin sizes of 10 and 15 counts per
bin were independently examined using C-statistics, and both
grouping schemes consistently reveal the absorption feature at
∼1.89 keV (Fig. A.1). For the Chandra spectra, the intrinsic ab-
sorption is generally low and not well constrained. We there-
fore ﬁxed NH to the Galactic value, NH = 1.3 × 1020 cm−2,
for continuum models in which no additional absorption was re-
quired by the data. To exclude any instrumental or background-
related origin, background spectra were extracted from mul-
tiple source-free regions on the same CCD and analyzed in-
dependently, yielding no absorption features near ∼ 1.9 keV .
We focus on the deepest Chandra/ACIS-S observation (ObsID
20992; ∼58 ks), which provides the highest-quality spectrum.

Appendix A.1: Blind line scan and Monte Carlo simulations
For each continuum model, 10 5 spectra were simulated under
the null hypothesis, that is, without the gabs component, and the
likelihood-ratio improvement was compared with that obtained
from the real data. In each simulation, the line centroid energy
was allowed to vary over the searched energy range, following
the same ﬁtting procedure applied to the real spectrum. For the
diskbb continuum, only 2 out of 10 5 simulations exceeded the
observed likelihood ratio, corresponding to a p-value of 2 .0 ×
10−5 (4.1σ). For the cutoﬀpl continuum, we obtain p = 4.3×10−4
(3.5σ).
To search for localized spectral features without prior as-
sumptions on their energy, we performed a blind line scan over
the 0.3–10 keV range using XSPEC. Starting from the best-
ﬁt continuum-only model, a multiplicative Gaussian line com-
ponent ( gabs) was added, and its centroid energy was stepped
across the spectrum on a uniform grid with a step size of 0.02
keV . At each trial energy, the line centroid was ﬁxed, while
the line strength was allowed to vary, and the line width was
ﬁxed to σ = 0.08 keV , consistent with the best-ﬁt value ob-
tained from the spectral analysis. The ﬁt was then re-optimized,
and the improvement in ﬁt statistic relative to the continuum-
only model was computed as ∆C. The spectra were optimally
grouped following the Kaastra & Bleeker (2016) prescription
implemented in ftgrouppha, and ﬁtted using C-statistics. All
continuum parameters were allowed to vary freely at each step.
The scan was performed for diﬀerent continuum models, includ-
ing tbabs*diskbb and tbabs*cutoﬀpl, to test the robustness of the
results against continuum uncertainties.
In both cases, a single localized peak is detected at ∼ 1.88–
1.90 keV . The maximum improvement reaches ∆C ≈ 19 for
the diskbb continuum and ∆C ≈ 15 for the cutoﬀpl model. No
other features with comparable ∆C values are found across the
scanned energy range. The adopted energy range and step size
adequately sample the spectral resolution of Chandra/ACIS, and
the consistency of the detected feature across di ﬀerent contin-
uum models indicates that it is robust against continuum uncer-
tainties. In addition, a weak excess is present near ∼ 0.95 keV
in the cutoﬀpl blind scan, approximately half of the main feature
energy. However, its ∆C is very small compared to the dominant
peak at ∼ 1.9 keV , and we therefore do not consider it statisti-
cally signiﬁcant.

Appendix A.3: Instrumental and background checks
We performed several tests to assess a possible instrumental or
background-related origin of the feature. First, background spec-
tra extracted from multiple independent source-free regions on
the same CCD do not show any feature near ∼ 1.9 keV . Sec-
ond, the absorption feature persists under di ﬀerent background
selections and spectral groupings, indicating that it is not sensi-
tive to data reduction choices. Third, allowing for a linear gain
shift in the spectral ﬁts does not improve the ﬁt and does not re-
move the feature, ruling out a calibration-related energy o ﬀset.
Finally, the feature is independently recovered across di ﬀerent
continuum models and is also detected at a consistent energy in
an additional Chandra observation. Taken together, these tests
strongly disfavor an instrumental or background-related origin
and support a physical interpretation of the feature.
