Abstract
LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6 is a bright star with an apparent magnitude of 10.764
in the g band. We have obtained the LAMOST Low Resolution Spectrum, the Gaia XP
spectrum, the apparent magnitudes from SDSS, 2MASS, and WISE, as well as the light
curve from TESS. In the spectroscopic study, the detected He I lines trace high temperature
features and the detected Ca I, Na I lines trace low temperature features. The multi-band
apparent magnitudes and color indices also exhibit characteristics that do not match those
of a normal single F8 type star. Most interestingly, we find that the light curve’s vari-
ability period gradually decreases from 1.7216 d to 0.8444 d. We conduct binary fitting
investigations on this source using Binary Tools, MESA, and PHOEBE programs. LAM-
OST J010103.13+275449.6 should be a binary system consisting of a∼3000 K,∼0.12M ⊙
star and a∼6000 K,∼1.2M ⊙ star, with an orbital period of 1.7216 d and a separation of
6.63R ⊙. The tidal locking of the cool star, along with the magnetic activity on its hemi-
sphere facing the hot star and the opposite hemisphere, can explain the halving of the pho-
tometric variability period. For a fully convective M dwarf with a rotation period of about
1.7 d, strong magnetic activity is frequently observed. This is only a semi-quantitative pre-
liminary fit. More in depth studies can be carried out in the future based on more spectra
that may be released by LAMOST.

1 Introduction
Stars constitute the majority of celestial objects, and binary stars make up a sig-
nificant proportion of them. Winters et al. (2019) studied 1120 M dwarf primaries
within 25 pc via trigonometric parallaxes and reported that the stellar multiplicity
rate is 26.8±1.4% and the stellar companions rate is 32.4±1.4%. Raghavan et
al. (2010) conducted an in depth study of 454 F6-K3 type stars within 25 pc from
the Hipparcos catalog and found that the proportion of binaries is at least 33±2%.
About 90% of all stars fall on the main sequence (MS) and about 98% (Winget &
Kepler 2008) of all stars will evolve to be white dwarfs (WDs). In 2018, El-badry &
Rix constructed wide binaries within 200 pc from Gaia DR2 data, including more
than 50,000 MS/MS binaries, 3,000 MS/WD binaries, and 400 WD/WD binaries.
Studying binaries is of universal significance.
Binaries contain a wealth of physical laws. The previously described categories
such as double MS stars, MS/WD binaries, and double WDs are all classifications
based on the evolutionary status of the component stars. According to different ob-
servational methods, binaries can be classified into visual binaries, spectroscopic
binaries, eclipsing binaries, and astrometric binaries. According to the different
shapes of their light curves, eclipsing binaries can be classified into EA Algol
type, EBβLyrae type, and EW W Ursae Maijoris type (Heintz 1978). Combined
orbital solution of eclipsing binaries (photometric plus spectroscopic) can yield
complete physical parameters of the two component stars, including their masses,
radii, orbital inclination, temperature ratio, distance, and other related quantities.
According to the extent to which the component stars fill their Roche lobes, bi-
naries can be classified into detached binaries, semi-detached binaries, contact bi-
naries, and overcontact binaries. Cataclysmic variables (CVs) are semi-detached
binary systems consisting of a WD and a MS (or a giant), in which mass transfer
occurs, making them important laboratories for studying mass transfer. Based on
data from Gaia DR3, the Sloan Digital Sky Survey V (SDSS-V), and the Large Sky
Area Multi-Object Fiber Spectroscopic Telescope (LAMOST) DR6, Canbay et al.
(2023), Inight et al. (2025), and Sun et al. (2021) studied 1587, 504, and 323 CVs
or CV candidates, respectively. After a WD accretes material from a companion
star and reaches the Chandrasekhar mass limit, it triggers a runaway thermonu-
clear reaction, resulting in a type Ia supernova explosion. This is an interesting
and important process in astrophysics. Ultra-short orbital period double WDs are
important sources for gravitational wave detection (Burdge et al. 2019).
Research on stellar magnetic activity in binary systems is also one of the hot topics
in binary star physics. Messina (2008) analyzed about 38,000 high-precision pho-
toelectric nightly observations in the U, B, and V filters, conducting a thorough in-
vestigation of 14 famous magnetically active close binary stars. K˝ov´ari (2025) pro-
vided a brief review of the dynamo mechanisms operating in RS CVn and BY Dra
type binaries, focusing on spot cycles, active longitudes, flare activity, and differen-

2 LAMOST LRS spectrum and TESS light curve of LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6
The Chinese national scientific research facility LAMOST has a 20 square degree
field of view, a large effective aperture ranging from 3.6 to 4.9 meters in diameter,
and can obtain 4,000 spectra in a single exposure (Zhao et al. 2012). From October
24, 2011, to June 30, 2025, the LAMOST DR13 dataset has officially released
over 30.82 million spectra, providing an extremely rich spectral data resource for
various astrophysical research. Cui et al. (2012) reported that the LAMOST LRS
spectra cover a wavelength range of 3,700-9,000 ˚A, with a blue channel optimized
for 3,700-5,900 ˚A and a red channel for 5,700-9,000 ˚A. In accordance with the
characterizations of the SDSS spectral resolution in Stoughton et al. (2002) and
Abazajian et al. (2003), the LAMOST LRS spectra have a spectra resolution of
1,800 at 5,500 ˚A.
While studying the stellar spectra from the LAMOST LRS DR13, we serendip-
itously noticed LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6. It was observed only once by
LAMOST, on December 3, 2011, as shown in Fig. 1. The signal to noise ratios
(SNRs) and atmospheric parameters are shown in Table 2. Despite having been
observed only once by LAMOST, this source exhibits relatively high SNRs. The
SNR is greater than 50 in the u band and exceeds 150 in the remaining bands. The
atmospheric parameters are derived from the LAMOST Stellar Parameter Pipeline
(LASP, Wu et al. 2014; Luo et al. 2015) directly. It is an F8 type MS star labeled
in the LAMOST data release. Judging from the errors, the values of effective tem-
perature (Teff) and surface gravity (logg) are reliable results from the single star

We search the data from the VizieR online data catalog and display the apparent
magnitude and color index for LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6 in Table 3. The
apparent magnitude data are sourced from Ahn et al. (2012) for SDSS, Cutri et

Fukugita et al. 1996, Skrutskie et al. 2006, and Wright et al. 2010 respectively. This
is a very bright target source, with an apparent magnitude of 10.764 in the g band.
However, it is strange that the apparent magnitudes in the u band and z band differ
by about 2 magnitudes in the two SDSS observations from 2008 and 2009, respec-
tively. No obvious infrared excess phenomenon is detected. By comparing with the
color index table of V luminosity class F8 spectral type stars from the synthetic
photometry data of Covey et al. (2007), we find that the i-z values correspond to
significantly hotter stars, while the z-Jvalues correspond to significantly cooler
stars. Additionally, the SDSS pipeline classifies this source as a galaxy, which also
suggests that its single star nature is suspect. In addition, the SDSS has not mea-
sured a spectrum for LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6. The trigonometric parallax
measured by Gaia DR3 is 6.2765±0.0158 mas, corresponding to a distance of
159.32 pc. The Renormalized Unit Weight Error metric (RUWE, Lindegren et al.
2021) value is 0.925, corresponding to a single star. Due to its excessive brightness
and spatial extension, LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6 cannot be easily classified
as a single star, a binary, or a galaxy.

The observation of the gradual halving of the light variation period and the weak-
ening of the light variation amplitude is very clear. It is interesting. For a MS star
characterized byT eff= 6235.71 K and logg= 4.424, the observed phenomena are
difficult to explain. In an F8 MS star, the probability of simultaneously observing
rapid rotation (1.7216 d) and a 1% brightness variation arising from magnetic ac-
tivity is low. The occurrence of a 0.8444 d period, approximately half the rotation
period, which would normally be interpreted as two active longitudes separated by
∼180◦, is even less probable for this spectral type, owing to its shallow convection
zone and nearly rigid rotation when fast. These combined anomalies suggest that a
single star magnetic activity model may be insufficient.

5 A Discussion and Conclusions
When studying the released LRS spectra from LAMOST, we accidentally noticed
LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6. In the open source spectral data, we only found
one LAMOST spectrum and one Gaia XP spectrum. The SDSS has two observa-
tions but no spectral records. The relative flux of the LAMOST spectrum is consis-
tent with the calibrated flux of the Gaia spectrum, up to a multiplicative coefficient.
In the LAMOST LRS spectrum, the identified He I lines versus the Ca I and Na
I lines correspond to higher and lower temperatures, respectively, which may hide
spectroscopic signatures of a binary star. It is bright, with an apparent magnitude
of 10.764 in the g band. The nearly two magnitude discrepancies in the u band and
z band magnitudes across the two SDSS observations, along with the color index
values, are also incompatible with a single star, as presented in Table 3. What is
most interesting is that we have obtained the TESS light curve data, which cap-
tures the physical process of the photometric variability period gradually evolving
from 1.7216 d to 0.8444 d, as shown in Fig. 2. In their study of the large sample of
TESS light curves, Zhang et al. (2023) recognized the long photometric variability
period for LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6 but overlooked the short photometric
variability period that is nearly half of it.
It is difficult for an F8 type MS star to explain the gradual period change ob-
served in the light curve. We conclude that LAMOST J010103.13+275449.6 is a
close binary system. We conducted fitting studies on it using Binary Tools, MESA,
and PHOEBE, respectively. We prefer the binary configuration consisting of a
∼3000 K,∼0.12M ⊙ star and a∼6000 K,∼1.2M ⊙ star. This mass ratio exists in
binaries (Moe & Stefano 2017, Ula ¸s et al. 2012), and the system with stellar sepa-
ration of 6.63R ⊙ is stable. Most importantly, the fully convective structure of the
M type red dwarf possesses strong magnetic activity. Under tidal locking, magnetic
activity of a starspot on the hemisphere facing the hot star generates the light curve
in the lower panel of Fig. 2, while magnetic activity of a starspot at the antipodal
point produces the observed period halved (0.8444 d) light curve. The orbital incli-
nation is not sufficient for eclipses to occur. Instead, the light curve variation arises
from rotation and stellar magnetic activity of the M type MS star.
Binary systems contain rich physical processes. Our fitting process is only prelim-
inary, semi-quantitative, and exploratory. The He I lines could also suggest that
the effective temperature of the hot star is supposed to be higher. In the future, if
LAMOST releases more low resolution and medium resolution spectra of LAM-
OST J010103.13+275449.6, we will perform more in depth studies.
