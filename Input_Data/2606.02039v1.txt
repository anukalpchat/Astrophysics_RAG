Abstract:Oscillating spacetime curvature can drive particle production during reheat-
ing, whose accurate modeling requires the use of non-perturbative out-of-equilibrium meth-
ods. Tachyonic instabilities have previously been studied using 2-Particle Irreducible (2PI)
formalism in the Hartree approximation, which however misses important momentum ex-
changing interactions. We present a self-consistent approximation scheme for reducing
the non-local next-to-leading order 2PI equations of motion to local quantum kinetic equa-
tions, which can be solved with standard methods. We pay special attention to interactions
involving unstable modes during tachyonic instabilities.
Keywords:Non-equilibrium Field Theory, Non-perturbative Effects, Early Universe Par-
ticle Physics, Models for Dark Matter

1 Introduction
Many important processes in the early universe involve out-of-equilibrium quantum dynam-
ics of coherent scalar fields in the presence of decohering interactions. Some key examples
include resonant phenomena [1–5] and tachyonic instabilities [6–12] in reheating, various
non-thermal mechanisms for dark matter production [13–20], the electroweak baryogene-
sis [21–27] and the leptogenesis mechanism [28–37]. Non-perturbative methods, such as the
Schwinger-Keldysh 2PI approach [38, 39], are often needed for comprehensive modelling of
non-linear quantum dynamics in such setups [40–47].
In [48, 49] the 2PI methods in the lowest order Hartree truncation were applied for the
dark matter scenario of [18, 19], where a scalar singlet with a non-minimal couplingξRχ 2
undergoes tachyonic instabilities during reheating, when the curvature scalarRoscillates
between positive and negative values. The excitations produced via this gravitational
coupling can constitute the observed dark matter relic abundance even if the singlet is
completely decoupled from the visible matter [18, 19]. The 2PI Hartee results of [48] showed
that scalar self-interactionsλχ 4 can trigger strong resonant instabilities at the end of the
– 1 –

tachyonic stage, and qualitatively similar effects were seen in the classical lattice simulations
performed in [49]. The Hartree approximation however does not account for momentum
exchanging processes in the quantum transport equations which describe the evolution of
the system. Such processes were found to be dynamically relevant in the classical lattice
evolution in [49], but it is not a priori clear how well the classical approximation describes
the phase space evolution in this dark matter setup, which features non-linear out-of-
equilibrium evolution of coherent quantum states.
In this work we develop the application of quantum 2PI methods for self-interacting
scalar fields beyond the Hartree approximation, accounting for dynamical effects of to
the collision terms. We use the dark matter scenario of [18, 19] as a concrete template
for setting up the formalism but our methods are general and can be straightforwardly
applied to any scalar model. Our approach is based on the moment expansion of the exact
2PI equations formulated for scalar fields in [50, 51]. The coupled non-linear moment
equations contain non-local collision integrals which in practice cannot be solved without
further approximations. To render the problem into a computationally feasible form we
approximate the two-point functions within the collision integrals by an Ansatz derived
from leading order gradient expansion, similar to methods applied in [51]. We formulate our
Ansatz separately for stable modes with positive effective mass squared and for tachyonic
modes with negative mass squared, corresponding to the tachyonic regimes in the dark
matter scenario of [18, 19]. With this approach we derive explicit expressions for the
collision integrals, and present renormalized quantum transport equations which account
for momentum exchanging collision processes and are formulated in terms of local moments
the two-point function. Our equations can be solved numerically using methods similar to
momentum-dependent Boltzmann equations.
An alternative strategy would be to solve the 2PI equations directly on lattice [43, 47],
with no other approximations than truncating the infinite set of equations in some coupling
constant expansion. While this approach is in principle more fundamental than ours,
the lattice simulations are computationally expensive and their resolution is limited by
numerically accessible simulation volumes. The lattice approach may therefore not be
practical for setups like [19], where evolution of the system has to be followed over a
long time, a wide range of momentum scales affects the dynamics, and details of the final
phase space distribution are phenomenologically relevant. Indeed, even the classical lattice
simulations in [49] were not able to fully track the final stages of the evolution in this setup
and the computational challenges would be even more pronounced in quantum simulations.
In contrast, our 2PI methods are substantially simpler to implement numerically and should
be ideally suited for setups like [19]. Concrete numerical work is beyond the scope of this
work however, and we will return to it elsewhere [52].
This paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we briefly specify the setup. In sec-
tion 3 we write down the general Kadanoff-Baym equations and specify the approximations
under which they reduce to a local aquantum kinetic equation in the form of a coupled set
of moment equations. In section 4 we present the lowest order gradient expansion Ansatz,
which renders the non-local collision integrals into a computationally feasible form. In
section 5 we evaluate the collision integrals for stable modes and in section 6 for stable

tachyonic modes with real valued frequencies. In section 7 we evaluate the collision inte-
grals for unstable tachyonic modes with imaginary valued frequencies. Finally, in section 8
we present our conclusions.

Key approximations.Any practical solution of the coupled Kadanoff-Baym equations,
(and the equation for the background field (3.2)), requires a set of approximations, which
still should not reduce their ability to describe the relevant physics. Our scheme proceeds
in the following steps:
1. We decouple the (pole) equations for ∆ r,a from the (statistical) equation for ∆ <.
2. We use a different approximations for different self-energy functions. The Hermitian
self-energy ΠH is computed at one loop (Hartree) level, while the width Γ is neglected
and the self-energies Π <,> are computed beyond Hartree to two loops.
3. We use a moment expansion in Wigner space on the evolution equations.
4. We employ the lowest order gradient expansion within the collision integrals.
Decoupling the pole and statistical equations is essential to scheme to reduce the Kadanoff-
Baym equations to local limit. We state only the main conceptual issue here and refer the
interested reader to ref. [54] for more details. The key observation is that the pole equations
are inhomogeneous even in the collisionless limit and depend on the statistical function only
indirectly through the self-energies Π r,a. They can then be solved formally independently
of the non-equilibrium statistical function, which only affects the values of Πr,a, but not the
form of the pole solutions. Physically the pole functions describe the phase space structure
of the theory rather than its kinematics.
Next observe that while the decay rate Γ and the self-energies Π <,> appear on same
footing in KB-equations, their roles are completely different in decoupled equations. While
Π<,> create the collision integral in the statistical equation, giving rise tocollisionaland
coherence damping, the decay rate Γ gives a finite width to pole functions, representing
Landau damping. Due to their physically different roles one can consistently employ dif-
ferent approximations for these functions. In particular, one can adopt the spectral limit
Γ→0 in pole equations while retaining Π <,> in the collision integral. This is of course
exactly what one does to derive the standard Boltzmann limit.
The Hermitian self-energy function Π H gives rise to modified dispersion relations due
to background corrections, and keeping Π H while neglecting Γ corresponds to the usual
quasiparticle approximation. This is consistent with the coupling constant expansion in
our setup, where Π H appears at the one-loop level and Γ arises first at two loops. It is
similarly consistent to use one-loop result for ΠH while computing Π<,> at two loops. This
is a great simplification, because with the momentum independent one-loop self-energy the
2PI-renormalization procedure leads to a simple gap-equation for the effective mass [46, 48],
but this would generalize to an integral equation for a momentum dependent Π H beyond
one loop. The small increase in accuracy this in most cases would bring, would come with
a significant increase in computational load.

finding the phase space structure of the converging unstable modes. In the end we use

8 Conclusions and discussion
In this work, we used 2PI effective action methods to derive non-perturbative, renormalized
quantum kinetic equations applicable for interacting scalar fields in a homogeneous and
isotropic spacetime. The methods developed here are general, but as a concrete template for
the scalar Lagrangian we have used the dark matter setup of [19], where scalar excitations
are produced by tachyonic instabilities during reheating.
We started from the exact non-local Kadanoff-Baym equations and introduced four key
approximations to reduce them to local kinetic equations, which still contain the relevant
quantum effects. First, we showed that the equations describing the dynamics of the two-
point functions can, to a good approximation, be decoupled from those governing the phase
space structure of the system. Second, we performed next-to-leading order loop expansion
in the self-energy components that enter collision terms, and leading order loop expansion
in the Hermitian self-energy component, which defines the dispersive properties of the
system. Third, following [51], we moved to the Wigner space and derived equations of
motion fork 0-moments of the two-point function. These moment functions are local in
time, but the collision terms in their equations of motion still contain memory over the
entire time evolution of the system. The fourth element in our approximation scheme was
to reduce these memory-integrals to local limit by replacing the two-point functionswithin
themby an Ansatz corresponding to lowest order expansion in gradients. We separately
formulated the gradient expansion Ansatz for cases with positive and negative effective
mass squared, and quantified its limitations in capturing deep infrared contributions of the
collision integrals in the tachyonic regime.
With this approach, we arrived at local equations of motion for the moments of the
two-point function, renormalized at leading order in the 2PI-expansion, and with next-to-
leading order collision terms. These equations describe the evolution of scalar quantum
systems with coherent structures in the presence of decohering, momentum exchanging
interactions, and they can be solved numerically using methods developed for momentum-
dependent Boltzmann equations. The computational resources required for the numerical
implementation of our formalism are substantially less extensive than those required for
direct lattice simulations of the 2PI equations. Moreover, the renormalization process
– 25 –

in our approach is straightforward and, in contrast with the lattice methods, does not
involve any additional numerical cost. Our methods therefore provide a powerful tool for
cosmological setups where out-of-equilibrium evolution of quantum fields has to be followed
over a long period of time with a high phase-space resolution. The dark matter scenario
of [19] is a concrete example of such a setup, and we will return to the numerical application
of our methods to this specific case in near future [52]. Finally, our approach can also be
straightforwardly generalized to more complicated systems, not only with more scalar fields
but also including gauge fields and fermions [59, 60].
A substantial part of this paper went to analyzing collision integrals involving unstable
tachyonic modes, for which the the lowest order Ansatz for the 2-point-function is not
always a consistent approximation. While the unstable tachyon contribution is not expected
to be numerically large, their self-consistent treatment is important for numerical stability,
e.g.to ensure the continuity of collision terms, as infrared modes can cross from normal to
tachyonic and back dynamically during the evolution of the system. After a careful analysis
of converging unstable tachyonic modes and a general treatment using a temporal box-
regularization, we were able to construct a simple prescription for computing collision terms
with all tachyonic modes. As a prelude to this problem, we also presented a concrete method
for accurately evaluating collision integrals for excitations with a finite decay width. The
main finding here was, that in the presence of a finite width the exact energy conservation
at the interaction vertices is lost. In addition to direct numerical application of our methods
and their extension to higher spin fields, it will be interesting to study the collisions with
growing unstable tachyonic modes further, for example using some exactly solvable time
dependent mass models.
