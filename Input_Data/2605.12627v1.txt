ABSTRACT
We revisit the ultraviolet absorption spectroscopy of the edge-on debris disk surrounding the A0V
starηTelescopii. Previous work found absorption components at four velocities (∼-23, -18, -10, -1 km
s−1), with the most blueshifted component (-23 km s −1) interpreted as a likely disk wind. However,
optical spectroscopy ofηTel and other nearby stars in projection demonstrate that the -23 km s −1
component is likely interstellar in origin. We find that there are three interstellar components toward
this sight line (-23, -18, -10 km s −1), but that the fourth component near -1 km s −1, which was only
detected in OI, is inconsistent with an interstellar origin and could be circumstellar. We place a 3-σ
upper limit on the C/O ratio of the -1 km s −1 gas (log C/O<-2.1), finding that it is consistent with
Earth and solar system comet abundances. However, the abundance is inconsistent with the carbon-
rich disks ofβPic (A5V) and 49 Cet (A1V), probably becauseηTel (A0V) is a warmer star imposing
greater levels of radiation pressure on carbon atoms in the disk. A low C/O ratio is also inconsistent
with Herschel’s [CII] detection towardηTel and may indicate that carbon gas is misaligned from the
line of sight or variable in time.

1.INTRODUCTION
Compared to protoplanetary disks, debris disks are
generally gas poor in terms of total gas mass, with any
primordial gas remaining from the protoplanetary stage
cleared out by the star or depleted during planet forma-
tion. Gas in these disks is continually sourced through
collisions between planetesimals (H. Beust et al. 1990; A.
Czechowski & I. Mann 2007; A. Grigorieva et al. 2007;
B. Zuckerman & I. Song 2012), although a primordial
origin has been proposed for some CO-rich debris disks
(´A. K´ osp´ al et al. 2013; A. Mo´ or et al. 2017). Assuming
a secondary origin, characterizing the gas of debris disks
reveals the chemical composition of planetesimals.
ηTel A is a young A0V star at 48.5 pc ( Gaia Col-
laboration et al. 2023) in the∼23 Myr oldβPic mov-
ing group (B. Zuckerman et al. 2001; E. E. Mamajek &
C. P. M. Bell 2014). It has a brown dwarf companion,
ηTel B, approximately 208 au away and a third co-
moving star, HD 181327 (F6V), which is approximately
20,000 au away (P. H. Nogueira et al. 2024) and has a
face-on debris disk (i≈30 ◦; G. Schneider et al. 2006;
S. Marino et al. 2016). Hereafter, we refer toηTel A
simply asηTel.
Infrared (IR) data ofηTel reveal an edge-on debris
disk (i≈0 ◦) with two dust temperature components
of∼350 K and∼115 K (D. E. Backman & F. Paresce
1993; V. Mannings & M. J. Barlow 1998; C. H. Chen
et al. 2006; R. Smith et al. 2009; Y. Chai et al. 2024),
indicating the presence of two belts of planetesimals near
22 and 26 au (R. Smith et al. 2009). The disk appears to
be roughly coplanar within∼10 ◦ with the brown dwarf
companion, and the brown dwarf may gravitationally

There have been few detections of gas from theηTel
disk in the literature. Herschel detected CIIgas but no
OIor H 2O gas (W. R. F. Dent et al. 2013; P. Riviere-
Marichalar et al. 2014). CO was not detected by APEX
or ALMA (W. R. F. Dent et al. 2013; A. Youngblood
et al. 2021).
A. Youngblood et al. (2021, hereafter Y21) reported a
radiatively driven outflow fromηTel’s debris disk based
on high-resolution ultraviolet (UV) spectroscopy reveal-
ing absorption lines of CII, OI, MgII, AlII, SiII, MnII,
and FeIInear -23 km s −1 (i.e., blueshifted∼20 km s −1
in the stellar rest frame). The presence of OI* and
OI** absorption near this velocity was a major factor
in the classification of this component as circumstellar,
because those transitions cannot be excited in the local
interstellar medium.
However, D. P. Iglesias et al. (2023) presented com-
pelling evidence that the absorption lines interpreted
by Y21 as circumstellar in origin are likely actually in-
terstellar. They presented high-resolution CaIIspec-
troscopy ofηTel and three other stars within<3.5 ′′ on
the plane of the sky, including the comoving F6V star
HD 181327, showing that all four sightlines exhibit CaII
absorption near -23 km s−1. With this evidence in mind,
we revisit the interpretation of the HST/STIS spectra
ofηTel.
2.REVISITING THE EVIDENCE FOR A DISK
WIND
2.1.Summary of the UV spectra and absorption line
fitting
We briefly describe the UV spectra ofηTel here
and refer the reader to Y21 for a detailed description.
ηTel was observed with the Hubble Space Telescope’s
STIS spectrograph in two visits separated by three days
(2016-Oct-03 and 2016-Oct-06) with the E140H and
E230H echelle gratings. Multiple grating settings were
used to provide broad wavelength coverage from 1164–
1951 ˚A and 2587–2851 ˚A at high spectral resolving
power (∆λ=λ/228 000 ˚A pixel−1). No significant vari-
ability was observed between visits, with the exception
of an unidentified “mystery” absorption feature at 1594
˚A. The spectra were combined via a weighted average
into a master spectrum.
The UV spectrum shows many narrow absorption
lines arising from gas along the line of sight to the cen-
tral star, including CI, CII, NI, OI, MgII, AlII, SiII,
SII, MnII, and FeII. Using a Markov Chain Monte
Carlo (MCMC) method, Y21 fit each species’ absorption
line to recover the radial velocity (heliocentric frame),
column density, and Doppler broadening parameter for
each species (see their Table 1). Many species have mul-
tiple transitions present in the spectra (e.g., OItran-
sitions at 1302.2, 1304.9, 1306.0, and 1355.6 ˚A). These
were fit simultaneously assuming a single radial velocity
and Doppler broadening parameter for all energy levels
and a single column density for each energy level.
2.2.Summary of the multiple velocity components
detected
Multiple velocity components were recovered for sev-
eral species. Three velocity components were identified
for OI, at approximately -25 km s −1, -18 km s−1, and -1
km s −1. Two components were detected each for CII,
SiII, SII, MnII, and FeII, at approximately -23 km
s−1 and -18 km s −1. For MgII, two components were
detected near -23 km s −1 and -10 km s −1. Upper limits
were placed on the column densities for NIand SI, and
one absorption component was very tentatively detected
for CI(-24 km s −1).
Y21 summarized the above absorption line detections
as a∼-23 km s −1 component and a∼-18 km s −1 com-
ponent. Some scatter was observed between the differ-
ent species’ radial velocities, and this was attributed to
uncertainties in the STIS wavelength calibration. MgII
and OIdiverged from this framework in that MgII
showed absorption near -23 km s−1 and -10 km s−1, and
OIshowed absorption near -25 km s −1, -18 km s−1, and
-1 km s −1. The -1 km s −1 component was not seen in
any other species.
2.3.Reclassifying the -23 km s −1 component as
interstellar
Y21 interpreted the -23 km s−1 component as circum-
stellar and the -18 km s −1 and -10 km s −1 components
as interstellar. The OIcomponent at -1 km s −1 was
labeled as unknown in origin. Although the -23 km s −1
exhibited absorption from ground energy level transi-
tions of many species present in the interstellar medium
(ISM), it was labeled as circumstellar rather than inter-
stellar because of the presence of excited transitions of
CIIand OI(CII*, OI*, and OI**) near -23 km s −1.
Populating O I* and O I** relies on relatively weak
collisions with neutral hydrogen, whose density in the
local ISM (n(H)∼0.1 cm −3; J. Linsky et al. 2022) is
well below the critical densities of the two transitions
(ncrit = 4.7×10 5; A. G. G. M. Tielens & D. Hollenbach
1985). As a result, O I* and O I** absorption is rarely
if ever observed in the local ISM. On the other hand, C
II* is populated with stronger collisions with electrons,
whose density (n(e −)∼0.1 cm −3; S. Redfield & R. E.
Falcon 2008) is closer to the critical density of the tran-
sition (n crit = 44; P. F. Goldsmith et al. 2012). Thus,

3
populating C II* in a warm, partially ionized medium
is marginally efficient. Variation in the detectability of
C II* in the local ISM is attributed to spatial variations
in thermal pressure and ionization (S. Redfield & R. E.
Falcon 2008).
The ground and excited levels of OItriplet are occa-
sionally seen from the Earth’s atmosphere (S. Redfield
& J. L. Linsky 2004). We find that the barycentric ve-
locity towards theηTel sightline on the dates of the
STIS observations was -25.2 km s −1. This value is in
excellent agreement with the OI-25.3 km s −1 compo-
nent reported as circumstellar in Y21. In that work, the
slight deviation of this value from the -23 km s−1 frame-
work was attributed to wavelength calibration uncer-
tainty. There is probably blended terrestrial (-25 km/s)
and interstellar (-23 km/s) absorption in the O I ground
state transition. Thus, we conclude that the -23 km s −1
component is interstellar and does not trace a disk wind.
Note that we have calculated the barycentric velocity
during the 49 Cet debris disk observation that yielded
OI* and OI** detections (A. Roberge et al. 2014) and
confirm that those features are not terrestrial in origin.
3.IS THERE GAS IN THEηTel DEBRIS DISK?
With the -23 km s −1 velocity component reclassified
as interstellar, we revisit the other three velocity com-
ponents (-18, -10, and -1 km s −1) detected towardηTel
to determine whether they could be interstellar or cir-
cumstellar.
3.1.The -18 and -10 km s −1 components: interstellar
in origin
The Ca II spectroscopy presented by D. P. Iglesias
et al. (2023) revealed a∼-23 km s −1 absorption com-
ponent towardηTel and three other stars nearby in
projection on the plane of the sky.ηTel also showed a
very weak -18 km s−1 component, but its comoving star
HD 181327 and the other two stars did not exhibit this
component. None of the stars, includingηTel, had a
-10 or -1 km s −1 component in their Ca II spectra.
In the UV spectrum ofηTel, the -18 km s −1 com-
ponent is detected in ground-state transitions of C II,
O I, Al II, Si II, and Fe II that are commonly observed
in the ISM. It was not detected in the Mg II and Mn
II transitions and was weakly detected in S II. The col-
umn densities of the detected species are all at least 0.5
dex lower than the those of the -23 km s −1 component
(Y21), offering an explanation for its absence in Mn II
and S II. S II is inherently less sensitive to lower column
densities due to its lower oscillator strengths compared
to the other transitions analyzed in this work. Mn II has
strong transitions in the UV but suffers from inherently
lower cosmic abundance. The Ca II H&K lines are also
strong transitions, but Ca + is not the preferred ioniza-
tion state of Ca in the ISM, implying lower abundance,
and possibly explaining why it was possibly not detected
toward HD 181327. Considering the above points, we
tentatively confirm the interstellar classification of the
-18 km s −1 component. High-resolution UV spectra of
the stars analyzed by D. P. Iglesias et al. (2023) could re-
solve lingering questions about the interstellar medium
in this area of the sky.
The -10 km s−1 velocity component was only reported
in MgIIby Y21. It was deemed interstellar given its
close match with the local Aql cloud’s predicted radial
velocity of -10±1 km s −1(S. Redfield & J. L. Linsky
2008). A closer visual inspection of the UV spectrum at
common ISM transitions shows evidence for an absorp-
tion component near -10 km s −1 distinct from the -18
km s −1 component for OI, SiII, and FeII(Figure 1).
CIIis heavily saturated, and there could be a -10 km
s−1 velocity component blended with the -18 km s −1
component. Generally, the Doppler broadening param-
eters of the -18 km s −1 component reported by Y21 are
greater than for the -23 km s −1 component. It is likely
that the larger Doppler widths compensated for a weak
-10 km s −1 velocity component not included in the fits.
Thus, we conclude that the -10 km s −1 component is
interstellar.

We estimate the significance of this detection as 10-σ

degeneracy between the Doppler broadening parameter
and the number of components, meaning there could be
multiple narrower components. Y21 assumed a single
component for simplicity and labeled the origin of this
absorption as “unknown”.
We assess the likelihood that this component origi-
nates from the ISM by comparing the column density
to other values reported for the local ISM, assessing the
presence or absence of other common ISM tracers near
-1 km s−1, and considering whether -1 km s −1 is consis-
tent with a kinematic model of the local ISM.
The column density for the -1 km s −1 component is
on the lower end of, but still fully consistent with, the
OIcolumns reported by S. Redfield & J. L. Linsky
(2004), such as the third components ofκ 1 Cet, HR

3.3.Local ISM kinematic model predictions toward the
ηTel sightline
We compare theηTel absorption components’ radial
velocities to the predictions of a kinematic model 10 of
the local ISM (S. Redfield & J. L. Linsky 2008). The
ηTel sight line does not traverse any of the clouds in-
cluded in the model, but passes near (<20◦) the LIC, G,
Aql, and Vel clouds, which have radial velocities of∼-
17, -19, -10, and -28 km s −1, respectively. The reported
radial velocity uncertainties are 1-1.5 km s −1.
The kinematic model is constructed from sparse sight
line data, so it appears these clouds have a greater spa-
tial extent than previously thought. The LIC and G
clouds could be blended into the -18 km/s component
and the Aql cloud could be the -10 km/s component.
The Vel cloud could be the -23 km/s component, al-
though the 5 km s−1 discrepancy is significant. There is
no known ISM cloud near theηTel sight line that has
a radial velocity near -1 km s −1. The Blue and Hyades
cloud have predicted velocities of -6 km s −1 and -5 km
s−1 but are>20 ◦ from theηTel sight line.
3.4.Could the -1 km s −1 component be circumstellar?
Stable, bound circumstellar gas in Keplerian orbit
would have a radial velocity equal to the stellar radial
velocity. Infalling gas (e.g., exocomets) would be red-
shifted, and outflowing gas would be blueshifted. Y21
reported a stellar radial velocity of -5.6±2.8 km s −1,
which is in fairly good agreement with values reported
by (I. Rebollido et al. 2018, 2020), although D. P. Iglesias
et al. (2023) found -0.6±1.6 km s −1. Measuring the ra-
dial velocity of a young, rotationally-broadened A-type
star likeηTel is notoriously challenging, but it seems
likely thatηTel’s radial velocity is between -5 and 0 km
s−1.
The -1 km s −1 component is roughly consistent with
the stellar radial velocity and could be circumstellar gas
on a Keplerian orbit or infalling star-grazing comets. In-
stead of a single broad component, there could be mul-
tiple narrow absorption components out to∼+10 km
s−1. Assuming exocomets would be in free-fall toward

To assess the likelihood that the -1 km s−1 component
traces gas from colliding or evaporating planetesimals,
we derive an upper limit on the C/O ratio of the gas.
Using the same MCMC method as Y21, we determine an
upper limit on the C II ground state column density for
the -1 km s −1 component. We fit only the ground-state
line (1334.5323 ˚A), permitting the free parameters to
vary as follows: -5 and +5 km s−1 for the radial velocity,
100-1016 cm−2 for the column density, and 1–7 km s −1
for the Doppler b value. We included absorption from
the -23 km s −1 and -18 km s −1 interstellar components
in the model fit, but those parameters were kept fixed
to the best values reported in Y21. We find log N(C II)
<9.1 (1-σ) or<11.4 (3-σ).
To infer the total carbon and oxygen columns from
C II and O I, we rely on ionization balance estimates.
R. Fernandez et al. (2006) presented ionization balance
calculations of debris disk midplanes near 100 au. We
adopt their C +/C = 0.99 value for an A0V star, al-
though the ratio could be an order of magnitude lower
in more distant orbits and/or at higher densities. We
assume O0/O = 1 based on oxygen’s high ionization po-
tential energy. We derive log C/O<-4.4 (1-σ) or<-2.1
(3-σ).
The log C/O upper limit is consistent Earth abun-
dance (∼-2; C. All` egre et al. 2001) and solar system
comets (-3 to -1.5; D. Z. Seligman et al. 2022 and refer-
ences therein), but is inconsistent with solar abundance
(-0.3±0.06 dex; K. Lodders 2003), polluted white dwarfs
(<0.8; D. J. Wilson et al. 2016; -0.5±0.23; S. Sahu
et al. 2025), and the circumstellar gas ofβPic (0.95;
A. Roberge et al. 2006) and 49 Cet (0.65; A. Roberge
et al. 2014).
If the -1 km s −1 component is circumstellar, the low
C/O ratio compared toβPic and 49 Cet could arise
via preferential carbon loss from the system. If CO is
the parent molecule of carbon and oxygen gas in the
system, we would expect log C/O = 0. Y21 showed
thatβ, the ratio of radiation pressure to gravitational
force, is unity for OIatoms in this system, whereas
the values for CIIand CIare 19 and 52, respectively.
A. Lehtmets et al. (2026) reportβ=1.8±0.1 for OI,
12.0±0.7 for CII, and 53.6±3.9 for CI, for aT=10 000
K star (A0V). Thus, carbon atoms experience sufficient
stellar radiation pressure to overcome the inward pull of

It is possible that the -1 km s −1 component traces
carbon-poor circumstellar gas or water-rich star-grazing
comets.
3.5.Reconciling UV absorption and far-IR emission
fromηTel
Complicating the picture of a carbon-poor disk is the
Herschel PACS detection of [CII] 158µm emission
and the absence of [OI] 63µm emission. P. Riviere-
Marichalar et al. (2014) report an upper limit on [OI]
emission (Fint <6.2×10 −18 W m−2) and a∼3-σdetec-
tion of [CII] emission (F int=2.3×10−18 W m−2), which
appears blueshifted∼100 km s −1 from the stellar rest
frame. The significant Doppler shift of the [CII] emis-
sion could suggest a background object, but a visual
examination of the available Herschel images ofηTel
shows no obvious background sources. BothηTel and
its brown dwarf companion lie within a single PACS
9.4′′×9.4′′ spaxel, but brown dwarfs are not likely to be
sources of detectable [CII] emission and the expected
radial velocity ofηTel B is far less than 100 km s −1
(P. H. Nogueira et al. 2024). In addition to the brown
dwarf companion, Y. Chai et al. (2024) detected two
other objects within 3 ′′ ofηTel with JWST MIRI at
11.55µm; both are assumed to be background galaxies
although only one of them is previously known (2CXO
J192251.5-542530).
The inconsistency between the UV detection of OI
and the far-IR non-detection of [OI] could be attributed
to gas volume densities below the critical density nec-
essary to populate the [OI] 63µm line (>10 5 cm−3;
D. Hollenbach & C. F. McKee 1989) leading to weak,
sub-thermal emission. Indeed, Herschel detected only
two debris disks in [OI] line emission (W. R. F. Dent
et al. 2013; A. Brandeker et al. 2016). In contrast, the
critical density threshold for [CII] 158µm (<10 cm −3;
D. Hollenbach & C. F. McKee 1989) is much more likely
to be met in debris disks. The UV non-detection of CII
and the far-IR detection of [CII] may indicate that the
carbon gas is misaligned from the line of sight or vari-
able in time. We confirmed that no CIIabsorption is
present in the STIS spectrum near -100 km s −1.

4.SUMMARY
We have reviewed the evidence for and against a disk
wind from the edge-on debris disk surroundingηTel
based on high-resolution spectroscopy (Y21, D. P. Igle-
sias et al. (2023)). We confirm the interpretation of
D. P. Iglesias et al. (2023) that the -23 km s −1 absorp-
tion component in the system is likely interstellar.
With this in mind, we reconsider the -1 km s−1 absorp-
tion component detected only in OIand approximately
at rest within the stellar rest frame for which Y21 could
not explain. This absorption is inconsistent with an in-
terstellar origin and could be bound or infalling circum-
stellar OIgas. The uncertainty surrounding the exact
stellar radial velocity (∼-5 to 0 km s −1) and the broad
OIabsorption centered on -1 km s −1 prevent an exact
kinematic determination.
We derive an upper limit on the C/O ratio of the -
1 km s −1 component, finding that it is consistent with
Earth and solar system comets but inconsistent with the
carbon-rich debris disks surroundingβPic and 49 Cet.
