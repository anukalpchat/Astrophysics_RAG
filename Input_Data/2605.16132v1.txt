ABSTRACT
Neutron star mergers are today considered a major production site for rapid neutron capture elements. While the bulk of the
matter escapes at fast, but non-relativistic velocities (∼0.2𝑐), a small amount of the dynamically ejected mass reaches mildly
relativistic velocities (≳0.6𝑐). It has been suggested earlier, that in such ejecta parts neutrons may avoid being captured and
that their decay could power an early blue precursor to the main kilonova event. Here we study in detail the nucleosynthesis in
such fast ejecta with nuclear network calculations along both parametrized and numerical relativity trajectories. We find that
the nucleosynthesis can be divided into three channels, in one of which a substantial amount of free neutrons survives when
the main r-process has frozen out. We provide a (semi-)analytical model for surviving free neutrons which agrees very well
with the network calculations. If the mass fraction of the free neutrons exceeds∼0.05, their𝛽−-decay dominates the nuclear
heatingratebetween∼100and∼10 4 seconds.Thisdominanceleadstoapronouncedkilonovaprecursorthatshouldforplausible
ejecta parameters be visible forULTRASATout to∼200 Mpc. Since at low electron fractions free neutrons can survive even for
moderatevelocities,mergerswithlargetidalejecta,suchasasymmetricneutronstarmergersorfavorableneutronstarblackhole
mergers, may produce particularly bright blue precursors to their subsequent kilonovae.
Key words:nuclear reactions, nucleosynthesis, abundances – stars: neutron – hydrodynamics – opacity – gravitational waves
1 INTRODUCTION
Thedynamicsofaneutronstarmerger(NSM)isgovernedbyphysics
at the extremes (Shibata 2016; Baiotti & Rezzolla 2017; Sarin &

get close to these extreme conditions, the only way to probe the in-

– ideally for several messengers – with potential observations.

2 THE NUCLEAR REACTION NETWORK
For our nucleosynthesis calculations, we use the open-source nu-
clearreactionnetworkWinNet(Winteler2012;Reichertetal.2023),
including approximately 6700 nuclei. At temperatures above9 GK,
we assume nuclear statistical equilibrium (NSE), using a combina-
tion of experimental and FRDM nuclear masses (Möller et al. 1995)
from the JINA Reaclib database (Cyburt et al. 2010). We motivate
our choice of the NSE temperatures in Appendix A. Below8.5 GK,
we solve the full nuclear reaction network equations, incorporating
around 70000 reactions from the JINA Reaclib. In between8.5and
9 GK, we assume a transition phase in which the network can either
beinNSEorinnetworkmode.Inbothtemperatureregimes,weapply
screeningcorrectionsfollowingKravchuk&Yakovlev(2014).Since
the weak reaction rates in the JINA Reaclib are not measured under
stellar conditions, they are replaced above temperatures of0.01 GK
with theoretically calculated weak rates (Fuller & Meyer 1995; Oda
etal.1994;Langanke&Martinez-Pinedo2001;Pruet&Fuller2003;
Suzukietal.2016).Theseincludeelectronandpositroncaptures,as
well as𝛽-decays on nucleons and nuclei. As the JINA Reaclib only
provides experimental𝛼-decay rates, we supplement them with the-
oretical rates from Dong & Ren (2005) and Reichert et al. (2023).
We also include spontaneous fission (Khuyagbaatar 2020), neutron-
induced fission (Panov et al. 2010), and𝛽-delayed fission including
𝛽-delayed neutron emission (Mumpower et al. 2022), with fission
fragment distributions taken from Mumpower et al. (2020). Tem-
peratures are self-consistently calculated by updating the entropy
withtheenergyreleasedinnuclearreactions,seeFreiburghausetal.
(1999b) and Reichert et al. (2023) for more details. Neutrino energy
losses from weak interactions are included, either as provided in
thetheoreticalweakratetablesor,forReaclibrates,basedonexperi-
mentalmeasurementsfromtheENSDFdatabase(Brownetal.2018).
For𝛽-decays without available experimental or theoretical neutrino
energy data, we assume that 35% of the nuclear energy is lost via
neutrino emission (Reichert et al. 2023; Wollaeger et al. 2018).

7 SUMMARY AND DISCUSSION
Inthispaperwehaveexploredthenucleosynthesisinfastdynamical
ejecta of neutron star mergers. We investigated in particular the
suggestion (Metzger et al. 2015) that in fast parts of these outflows
free neutrons may avoid being captured and may lead to a precursor
event before the main kilonova emission. We find indeed that in
a large fraction of the ejecta parameter space, favored by large
velocities, low electron fractions and high entropies, free neutrons
avoid being captured by nuclei and will lead to a ”kilonova
precursor” about one hour after merger. This indicates that such
precursors are a common feature of neutron star mergers.
More specifically, our main results can be summarized as follows.
Nucleosynthesis
•Ejected neutrons either remain largely unbound or are almost
entirely captured during the nucleosynthesis. The parameter space
splits into a neutron-rich and a neutron-poor regime.
•We find that the trajectories can be grouped in three channels,
see Fig. 3 and Tab. 2:
–Channel I: neutrons run out before charged particle freeze-
out, so there are no neutrons left to be captured when seed forma-
tion is complete.
–Channel II: there are still abundant amounts of neutrons left
afterseedformationandtheyaresubsequentlycapturedbytheseed
nucleiinclassicalr-process.Thischannelyieldsthecharacteristic
power-lawheatingrate∝𝑡 −1.3,seethesecondpanelinthesecond
row of Fig. 3, that is considered a tell-tale for r-process (Metzger
et al. 2010; Korobkin et al. 2012; Lippuner & Roberts 2015)
and has been observed in the bolometric light-curve decay of
AT2017gfo (Rosswog et al. 2018).
–Channel III: a substantial amount free neutrons survive after
ther-processhasfrozenout.Thedecayoftheseneutronsdominates
theheatingratebetween∼10 2 and∼10 4 secondsafterthemerger,
provided that the free neutron mass fraction is𝑋𝑛 ≳0.05.
•We provide (semi-)analytical criteria for the presence of a sub-
stantial amount of free neutrons in Sec. 6.1.1, which are inspired by
the pioneering work of Hoffman et al. (1997). In its semi-analytic
form, see Eq. (25) in Sec. 6.1.1, the criterion correctly predicts the
presence of free neutrons in≈98% of the cases, and slightly less
accurate in its analytic form, see Eq. (29). Analytical (green) and
semi-analytical criterion (red) are shown together with the full nu-

•For our numerical relativity simulations we find an accurate

consider these electron fraction values as too large for the fast ejecta

Different compact binaries
As we have shown, large free-neutron fractions arise particularly at
low𝑌 𝑒,0, i.e. in ejecta that are still close to their cold𝛽-equilibrium
values from the original neutron stars. This naturally points to cold
tidal dynamical ejecta as a promising candidate for strong precursor
emission, a conclusion further supported by our finding that free
neutrons can survive even at moderate ejection velocities. Recent
workoncompactbinarieswithsubstantialmassasymmetries,either
double neutron stars or neutron star black hole systems, has also
foundfastejectathatseemtobelaunchedbytidaltorquesduringthe
merger (Matur et al. 2024; Bernuzzi et al. 2025). Such binaries with
their large tidal ejecta masses, low electron fractions and potentially
fast velocities should result in particularly bright kilonova precursor
transients.
Neutron star binaries that are dynamically formed in locations with
large number densities such as globular clusters have a fair proba-
bility to contain a single, spun-up millisecond pulsar together with
a slowly/non-spinning neutron star (Rosswog et al. 2024). Such sys-
tems with large one-sided tidal tails are morphologically similar to
neutronstarmergerswithalargemassasymmetry.Withtidaltailsof
∼10−2 M⊙ and typical bulk velocities of∼0.3𝑐they may be accom-
panied by bright free neutron decay-powered kilonova precursors.
At the same time, high specific entropies also favor free-neutron
survival by suppressing seed nucleus formation. Reaching such en-
tropies, however, requires the ejecta to be strongly shocked, as is the
case for the fast ejecta produced during the collision itself (Radice
etal.2018;Combi&Siegel2023;Rosswogetal.2025).Theelectron
fractionevolutioninsuchejectadependsonthecompetitionbetween
theexpansionandtheweak-interactiontimescales.Toaccuratelyde-
termine the physical conditions in these small amounts of ejecta is a
serious challenge that is left for future work.
