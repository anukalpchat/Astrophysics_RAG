I. INTRODUCTION
Cosmological First-order Phase Transitions (FOPT) have well-understood motivations involving baryogenesis
which explain the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry of the Universe [1–4], (asymmetric) dark matter candi-
dates [5–13], Standard Model (SM) extended Higgs sector physics [14–17], and from scenarios involving spontaneously
broken conformal symmetry which alleviates the hierarchy problem in the SM [18–28], to name a few. Besides this,
FOPTs also generate gravitational waves (GW) [29–36] which can be detected in the near future with upcoming GW
detectors like LISA, ET, etc. Besides this, very recent observations of the Stochastic GW background from several
Pulsar Timing Array (PTA) collaborations [37–42] have motivated the existence of FOPT. Lastly, ‘H0 tension’ or the
Hubble tension from early (CMB) and late time measurements (SNe) of the Hubble constant of the Universe [43, 44]
(see [45] for a review on this topic) can have possible resolution from FOPT happening at late times [46].
Already, there exist several constraints on FOPTs from Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) and the CMB, either in
the visible or in the dark sector [47]. from the measurements of post-BBN dark sector effective relativistic degrees of
freedom or simply dark radiation (DR) ∆N eff <0.29 at 95% CL [44, 48]. This leads to constraints on the total GW
energy density in turn constrain the FOPT parameters, namely the latent heat factorα, temperature of FOPT,T ⋆,
speed of the FOPT,β/H, or the rate of nucleation of bubbles, and the bubble wall velocityv w.
Apart from a cosmological phase transition being strong first-order, it can be of second-order and simply be a
cross-over scenario in early Universe. In this scenario it may still lead to large Gravitational Wave signals from Domain
Walls (DW). Particularly, during the presence of nearly degenerate vacua may lead to the formation of DW networks
which are but are topological defects. Very shortly after its formation, the DW network evolves to become what is
know as scaling solution, that is, a state where the correlation length of the network more or less becomes equal to the
Hubble horizon sizeL∼t[49]. During this time fraction of the total Universe energy density stored in DWs tend to
increase linearly with timeρ DW/ρtot ∝t[49] which lead to the fact that the Universe becomes dominated by the DWs
which is dangerous, and may lead to formation of Primordial black hole (PBH) or an eternal inflation scenario [50].
However with a simple asymmetry term in the potential or an energy biasV bias between the different vacua, the DWs
can be annihilated away due to the vacuum energy differenceV bias being now compensated with the pressure due

In this article, we propose a novelgauge-invariantformulation of a mechanism which leads to the formation of
PBHs in the early Universe during FOPTs or from DWs arising simply due to their GW emission or the tensor
spectrum at first-order in perturbation theory. As known, several mechanisms have been investigated which in the
early Universe may have led to the formation of black holes (BHs), particularly in the asteroid mass range is of
significant astrophysical and cosmological importance as it is able to explain all dark matter (DM) [56]. Even if PBHs
were of lighter mass, such that they evaporated away quickly after their formation before the onset of BBN leaving no
signatures [57, 58], but could possibly affect DM formation phenomenology [59–66] or baryon asymmetry generation
[59, 67–69] or simple PBH reheating of the visible and the dark sector Universe. Lastly, recent observations of LIGO-
Virgo gravitational wave (GW) events may arise due to stellar mass PBHs [70–76] or provide seeds for cosmic structure
formation [77–79], and be also be one of the candidates responsible for explaining the observed PTA Gravitational
Wave background signal [39–42] in the nano-Hertz frequency regime. [39, 80, 81]. There have been recent claims of
directly observing PBH events in the lensing experiments [82].
PBH formation in typical scenarios mostly involves large peaks in the curvature power spectrum during cosmic
inflation, or from spectator fields in the post-inflationary regimes, see Ref. [83, 84], for a review, leading to collapse into
the highly overdense regions which re-enter the Hubble horizon after inflation [85]. This PBH abundance so formed
as well-known is exponentially sensitive to the amplitude of the perturbations and quite some fine-tuning becomes
necessarily in order to achieve PBH as the entire DM relic density, of the Universe see detail discussion in Ref.[86] 1.
Other scenarios, such as PBH formation from collapsing false vacuum bubble scenarios [88–91], or from collapsing
domain walls during the DW annihilation era, when closed DWs undergo significant shrinking within some criteria of
collapse etc.. As long as they could enter within their Schwarzschild radius they may form PBHs [88, 89, 92, 93], also
sometimes called dubbed as “catastrogenesis” for better names, see in Ref. [94, 95]. ForT dom ≳T ann, the Universe
may become completely filled with PBHs and also wormholes which maybe connected to some possible baby-Universes
[50]. Such scenarios however may involve less amount of fine-tuning.
In this paper we are going to explore a different but quite natural consequential mechanism for PBH formation
from both FOPT and DW, involving second-order cosmological perturbation theory. Here the first-order tensor
perturbations or Gravitational Waves generated from FOPT bubbles & sound waves and DWs will lead to formation
of second-order scalar perturbations. As the FOPTs proceed via the nucleation of bubbles of the broken phase in an
initial background of the symmetric phase [96–98] they collide and convert the entire Universe into the new vacuum
of the symmetry broken phase. During the growth of such bubbles the energy of the false vacuum is transferred into
the kinetic and gradient energy of the bubble walls. During this time of phase transition due to bubble collisions there
exists large stochastic primordial GW background arising due to bubble collisions. We will show that these tensor
perturbations will eventually also lead to, at second-order in perturbation, large density fluctuations. If the generated
overdensity is large enough, these regions may collapse into PBHs. Similarly, during the DW annihilation, large GW
radiation is emitted (see [99–104]). We will provide an analytical understanding of this picture, involving second-order
induced curvature,P δ(2), as well as fractional abundancef PBH and mass of PBHM PBH formed, which will complement
our numerical analysis, to provide an important step forward in the estimate of the PBH abundance in this set-up.
We will also compare the generation of PBH from other existing mechanism, for instance from delayed false vacuum
transition, see Ref.[105], and from DW nucleation [104] and understand where the novel mechanism proposed in this
paper becomes important. Our formulation is naturally gauge-invariant, and utilizing the constraints ofP ζ, and of
PBHs arising due to PBH evaporation, lensing and merger signals, we are able to derive model-independent constraints
on FOPT parameters: (α, β/H, T ⋆)2 and on DW parameters: (α ann, Vbias, σ). We will also compare the generation
of first-order and second-order induced density perturbations and show where the later can be large as compared to
their respective first order density perturbations. Furthermore, we will also derive model-independent constraints on
phase transition parameters from such PBH formation and also identify the parameter space where PBH can be the
entire DM candidate of the Universe.

second-order density perturbations generated from the GW generated during phase transition and domain walls. In
Section IV we estimate the fractional abundance of PBH and it mass, followed by numerical results and derivation

A. First-order Phase transitions
Cosmological fist-order phase transitions (FOPT) involve growing bubbles of true vacuum within the meta-stable
phase. It is common to adopt the thin-wall (of the bubbles) approximation to track the growth of a single true
vacuum bubble. In this approximation the bubble wall is just a boundary of negligible width between the true and
false vacua but carries a certain amount of surface energy density. The interactions between the bubble wall and the
surrounding particles in the plasma are also neglected for our present purpose. This is usually true for those kinds of
phase transitions involving occurs in scenarios if the phase transitions are strongly supercooled or when the couplings
between the scalar field (which drives the phase transition) and the particles, are very small [107–114].
GWs arise during such strong FOPT due bubble collisions and by motions of inhomogeneities in the fluid [115, 116].

VI. DISCUSSION & CONCLUSION
We proposed a novel mechanism for PBH formation which isgauge-invariant. This mechanism is based upon
first-order tensor sourcing second-order density fluctuations. In the first part of this study (in Section III), we
investigated the formation of PBHs originating due to large curvature perturbations induced at second-order in
perturbation theory from first-order tensor perturbations originating during FOPTs from bubble wall collision and
sound waves. Our analysis builds on work recently studied in Ref.[140] investigating primordial tensor modes from
inflation inducing such large curvature. Extending the previous work, we implemented a comprehensive analysis of the
mechanism, including detailed computations of the PBH formation involving Peaks theory. Our results suggest that
the PBH formation is strongly dependent on the tunneling probability growth rateβ/H(the inverse of which denotes
the duration of FOPT), and on the strength of FOPTα(see Fig. 7). We find that the the parameter space overlaps
with the regions which are either currently probed by current or within the reach of future GW observatories, see
Fig. 11. We also compared with PBH formation from first-order curvature perturbation and found the second-order
induced ones provide larger effects in our parameter space.
We also show that the PBH formation implies model-independent constraints on FOPT parameters (β/H, α, T ⋆
) which implyβ/H≲6 over a wide range of phase transition temperaturesT ⋆, are the strongest to datein gauge-
invariant formulation. We find that asteroid mass PBHs can become the entire DM of the Universe, forT ⋆ ∈
(4×10 2,10 4) GeV,β/H≃6 involvingα >O(1) given . The corresponding FOPT GWs amplitude will have its
characteristic peak at Ωp
GWh2 ∼ O(10 −8) between frequenciesf p ∈(10 −5,10 −2) Hz which is within the reach in LISA
and SKA (see Table III). The scenario is consistent, that is PBH is not overproduced, for those GWs from bubble
collisions during FOPT which may explain the GW signals seen by various PTA collaboration [38, 40–42, 215–219] 11.

also found that the first-order curvature spectrum scales ask 3 andk −8 respectively, for FOPT and DW, compared to
the IR scaling of our derived tensor induced density power spectrumP δ(2) (see Eqs.(24)and(25)) such that the latter
is dominant at small scales.
A detection of Primordial GW (PGW) from our analysis will lead to expected PBH prediction while a non-
detection of GW as for instance, already from LVK will suggested regions of PBH parameter space to be constrained
assuming FOPT or DW pathway formation ofPGW-induced PBH. We envisage that the study done in this paper
will pave the way for explaining DM candidate as PBH from FOPTs and DW originating in several BSM scenarios
without requiring a separate particle DM candidate and be testable via the associated GW predictions in current
and upcoming GW missions. As a future outlook, a careful consideration of primordial non-Gaussianity [287], the
impact of equation of state of the Universe (w) and the threshold of collapse (δ c) make the estimations for PBH
fraction abundance more robust, however it is beyond the scope of the present study and will be taken up in future
publication. The upcoming precision era of gravitational wave cosmology made possible via several GW missions
planned worldwide holds significant potential for meaningfully probing fundamental physics, particularly we showed
that the generation & measurements of GW may open doors to understand the formation of PBH as dark matter
candidate.
