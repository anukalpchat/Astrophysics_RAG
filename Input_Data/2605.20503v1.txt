ABSTRACT
Context.The origin ofωCentauri remains one of the key open problems in stellar dynamics and chemical evolution. Its large
abundance spreads and multiple populations suggest a formation history more complex than that of a typical globular cluster.
Aims.We investigate whether the chemical sub-populations identified in APOGEE DR17 also exhibit distinct spatial and kinematic
signatures, and what constraints these provide on the formation pathways ofωCen.
Methods.We analyse a sample of APOGEE DR17 red-giant stars using a Gaussian Mixture Model in an eight-dimensional chemical-
abundance space. The resulting chemical components are combined with Gaia proper motions and APOGEE line-of-sight velocities
to derive intrinsic mean velocities and velocity dispersions in all three observable directions. We measure both global kinematic
quantities and radial profiles for each chemically defined group, extending from the inner regions to∼4 half-light radii.
Results.The Gaussian Mixture Model identifies five chemical components, which, when examined through their radial cumulative
distributions, naturally group into two broader families characterised by lower and higher aluminium enrichment. The two families
differ significantly in their spatial and kinematic properties: the Al-rich stars are more centrally concentrated and exhibit stronger radial
anisotropy than the Al-poor stars, which remain closer to isotropy over the radial range probed. Despite these significant differences (>
2σ), the two populations share a common rotation pattern, with comparable intrinsic rotation amplitudes and rotation-axis inclinations
within the uncertainties (∼0.5 km s −1). Both families span a wide range in metallicity, indicating that they do not simply correspond
to a first- and second-generation dichotomy, but rather trace chemically and dynamically complex substructures withinωCen.
Conclusions.This work represents the first chemo-dynamical study ofωCen linking detailed chemical tagging to internal kinematics
from the inner regions to the cluster outskirts, and provides a key benchmark for models of its formation. A formation path involving
both hierarchical assembly within a dwarf-galaxy potential and centrally concentrated, chemically enriched star formation offers a
natural explanation for the observed chemo-dynamical complexity. Extending this approach to larger fields of view and to more
numerous, fainter stars will be essential to robustly uncover the origin of this uniquely complex system.

1. Introduction
Omega Centauri (ωCen, NGC 5139) is the most massive—and
arguably the most puzzling—globular cluster (GC) in the Milky
Way, with a present-day mass ofM≃3.55×10 6 M⊙ (Baumgardt
& Hilker 2018). Long before the modern view of the ubiquitous
phenomena of multiple stellar populations (MPs) in GCs,ωCen
was known for star-to-star abundance variations (e.g., Norris
& Bessell 1975; Butler et al. 1978; Persson et al. 1980) and
for photometric splits along the red giant branch (RGB), main
sequence (MS), and subgiant branch (SGB e.g., Lloyd Evans
1977; Bedin et al. 2004; Lee et al. 1999). Spectroscopy later
revealed a wide internal metallicity range—from [Fe/H]≈ −2
to [Fe/H]≳−0.7—together with increasing s-process elements
and increasing total C+N+O at higher [Fe/H] (e.g., Norris & Da
Costa 1995; Johnson & Pilachowski 2010; Marino et al. 2011,
2012; Nitschai et al. 2023). At fixed metallicity,ωCen shows
the familiar GC light-element (anti)correlations (C–N, Na–O,
Mg–Al) and helium variations; the strongest effects often occur
at intermediate [Fe/H] (Johnson & Pilachowski 2010; Marino
et al. 2011, 2012; Clontz et al. 2025). Reported age spreads range
from∼2 Gyr to≲0.5 Gyr, with C+N+O variations complicating
SGB-based estimates (Hilker et al. 2004; Villanova et al. 2007,
2014; Tailo et al. 2016; Clontz et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2026).
All of these chemical and age peculiarities, point to an origin
in a substantially more massive parent system, withωCen most
naturally interpreted as the bound nuclear remnant of a disrupted
dwarf galaxy rather than a typical Galactic GC (Lee et al. 1999;
Majewski et al. 2000; Carraro & Lia 2000; Bekki & Freeman
2003; Tsuchiya et al. 2003, 2004; Souza et al. 2026).
Color–magnitude diagrams ofωCen reveal a remarkably
rich set of sequences at all evolutionary stages (e.g. Pancino
et al. 2000; Bedin et al. 2004; Ferraro et al. 2004; Bellini et al.
2010, 2017; Milone et al. 2017a; Clontz et al. 2025). To dis-
entangle these features at fixed luminosity, Milone et al. (2015)
introduced thechromosome map(ChM), a pseudo two-colour

While the ChM is sensitive to light–element and He vari-
ations, it is not a direct abundance measurement; spectroscopy
is therefore essential to anchor the photometric tags to element-
by-element trends. High-resolution spectroscopic surveys have
mapped element-by-element trends acrossωCen’s [Fe/H]
range, refining the light- and heavy-element patterns and high-
lighting sub-populations (e.g., Johnson & Pilachowski 2010;
Marino et al. 2011, 2012). Large spectroscopic surveys such as
APOGEE have further expanded this view by providing homo-
geneous abundance measurements for large stellar samples. For
example, Mészáros et al. (2021) analysed APOGEE DR16 spec-
tra ofωCen using the BACCHUS pipeline and confirmed the
presence of multiple chemically distinct stellar populations span-
ning a wide metallicity range. More recently, Mason et al. (2026)
combined APOGEE DR17 abundances withGaia, MUSE, and
HST data to chemically tagωCen stars. They identify three
chemically distinct groups—(i) a population resembling metal-
poor halo stars, (ii) an intermediate group with narrow [Fe/H]
and classic GC light-element anti-correlations, and (iii) an ex-
treme second-generation population—and show that these alone
reproduce the observed ChM distribution. This result links dis-
crete ChM features to chemically defined groups, showing that
a small set of abundance patterns can reproduce the observed
photometric substructure.
Using high-resolution optical spectroscopy, Alvarez Garay
et al. (2024) further investigated the Mg–Al abundance patterns
ofωCen giants and showed that Al-enhanced populations are
preferentially concentrated toward the cluster centre. Similarly,
by combining complementary photometric and spectroscopic
datasets, Dondoglio et al. (2026) identified chemically distinct
stellar groups acrossωCen from the centre out to∼30 ′ (≈5r h),
showing that these populations are not fully spatially mixed but
instead display clear radial gradients. In particular, stars enriched
in light elements are found to be more centrally concentrated
than their chemical counterparts. If these chemical and spatial
differences are the results ofωCen’s complex formation history,
one might also expect differences in the stellar kinematics, and
more generally in chemo-dynamical space.
The kinematic picture ofωCen has progressed from small
bright-giant samples to panoramic phase-space maps. Early
work used line-of-sight velocities for a few hundred giants
(Suntzeff& Kraft 1996; Mayor et al. 1997; Reijns et al. 2006)
and ground-based proper motions for bright stars (van Leeuwen
et al. 2000). Multi-epoch HST data then provided proper motions
for hundreds of thousands of stars down to faint MS levels (An-
derson & van der Marel 2010; Bellini et al. 2014, 2018; Häberle
et al. 2025), and MUSE added spectra for comparably large sam-
ples (Bacon et al. 2010; Kamann et al. 2018; Nitschai et al. 2023;
Pechetti et al. 2024). Beyond the half-light radius, US stars show
more radial anisotropy than LS stars (Cordoni et al. 2020); at
∼3.5r h, 2P stars are more radially anisotropic than 1P (Bellini
et al. 2018). These contrasts weaken when stars are sorted only
by [Fe/H] (Cordoni et al. 2020), and inside the half-light radius
stars with different iron abundance can share similar kinematics
(Vernekar et al. 2025; Häberle et al. 2025). Collectively, these
results indicate that kinematic differences are more tightly cou-
pled to light–element population identity than to iron abundance
alone, at least over the radii probed so far.
In the context of nuclear star cluster (NSC) formation (e.g.
Neumayer et al. 2020), the main mechanisms commonly in-
voked include hierarchical assembly through the inspiral and
merging of stellar systems, centrally concentrated star formation
sustained by gas retention and chemical enrichment, and hybrid
“wet merger” scenarios in which cluster merging is accompanied
by gas inflow and subsequent star formation (e.g. Guillard et al.
2016).
Recent advances in large spectroscopic and astrometric sur-
veys now offer a new opportunity to investigate the formation
ofωCen through a fully multi-dimensional approach. High-
resolution chemical abundances from APOGEE, combined with
preciseGaiaproper motions and line-of-sight velocities from
APOGEE, make it possible to jointly characterise stellar pop-
ulations in chemical-abundance space and in three-dimensional
kinematics over a wide field of view. This combination provides
the completeness and precision required to explore how chemi-
cal enrichment, spatial structure, and orbital properties are cou-
pled within the cluster, from the inner regions to the outskirts.
In this work, we exploit these new data to perform a chemo-
dynamical analysis ofωCen, with the aim of constraining its
formation path and discriminating between scenarios dominated
by mergers of globular clusters, centrally concentrated in-situ
chemical enrichment, or a combination of both.
This paper is structured as follows: in Sect. 2, we discuss
the observational sample analysed in this work, in Sect. 3 we
explain the chemo-dynamical analysis, and in Sect. 4 we present
the results. In Sect. 5, we discuss the novelties of our results, also
in the context of other recent studies on the subject, and finally,
in Sect. 6 we derive our conclusions.
2. Observational data
For this study, we make use of data from the APOGEE Value
Added Catalogue (V AC) of Galactic globular cluster stars (Schi-
avon et al. 2024). From this catalogue, we select stars associated
withωCentauri by applying the following criteria:
1. a high membership probability according to Vasiliev &
Baumgardt (2021) (VB_PROB≥0.9);
2. a signal-to-noise ratioSNREV>70;
3. effective temperatures in the range 3500 K<T eff <5500 K
and surface gravities logg<3.6;
4. APOGEE quality cuts requiringAPOGEE_STARFLAG=0 and
APOGEE_STARBAD=0.
This defines a parent sample of 1201 stars. For each of them,
the APOGEE V AC provides detailed chemical abundances, line-
of-sight velocities, andGaiaEDR3 proper motions (Gaia Col-
laboration et al. 2021). We then retain only stars with valid
abundance measurements for all eight elements simultaneously,
namely Fe, Mg, Si, Ca, C, Al, K, and Mn, which are used in
the Gaussian mixture analysis below. The final sample used in
this work therefore consists of 588 red giant stars, spanning pro-
jected clustercentric distances out to∼40 arcmin, thus covering
a large fraction of the cluster field of view and extending well up
to∼8r h.

3. Analysis
3.1. From chemical to spatial differences
This work builds on the analysis of Pagnini et al. (2025), where
we applied a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) approach 1 to an
eight-dimensional chemical-abundance space defined by [Fe/H],
theα-elements [Mg/Fe], [Si/Fe], and [Ca/Fe], the light and odd-
Zelements [C/Fe], [Al/Fe], and [K/Fe], and the iron-peak ele-
ment [Mn/Fe] (see Sect. 3 of Pagnini et al. 2025). The choice of
eight elements reflects a compromise between the ability to cap-
ture chemical differences between populations and retaining a
sufficiently large sample with reliable abundance measurements.
The distribution ofωCen in this eight-dimensional abundance
space was fitted by using an increasing number of Gaussian com-
ponents and the optimal number of components for our dataset
was then determined by minimising the Bayesian information
criterion (BIC). With this procedure, we found that the number
of components that best reproduces the eight-dimensional distri-
bution ofωCen is five (see Sect. 3 in Pagnini et al. 2025, for
discussion on how this number can vary). For simplicity, we la-
bel these five GMM components as populations 0–4. Figure 1
(left panel) shows these components in the [Al/Fe] vs. [Mg/Fe]
space (see Fig. A.2 for the other chemical abundance spaces
analysed in the GMM). The five GMM components occupy dis-
tinct regions of chemical-abundance space and are most clearly
separated in the [Al/Fe]–[Fe/H] plane, where aluminium defines
the primary axis of separation. Two components (Populations 2
and 4) are Al-poor across a broad metallicity range, consistent
with first-generation–like chemistry. The remaining three com-
ponents (Populations 0, 1, and 3) are Al-enhanced relative to
these Al-poor groups. Population 0 traces the most chemically
extreme, Al-rich sequence, while Population 1 occupies an in-
termediate Al-enhanced regime. Population 3 is characterised by
higher metallicity combined with enhanced aluminium, forming
a chemically distinct metal-rich, Al-rich component.
To better understand the physical meaning behind this data
driven sub-population division, we started by focusing on the
spatial distribution of these five different components withinω
Cen. In this regard, Figure 1 features the spatial cumulative dis-
tribution function (sCDF) of the five sub-populations compared
to the global sCDF across the entire radial range. From this plot,
we can see that these chemically identified populations converge
into two distinct ones: components 0, 1, and 3 appear more spa-
tially concentrated with respect to components 2 and 4. Interest-
ingly, the former two also differ from the latter from a chemical
point of view, as they are richer in aluminium (see left panel of
Fig. 1).
Motivated by these chemical and spatial differences, we de-
cided to combine the five sub-populations into two main groups:
the spatially concentrated Al-rich one with a total of 363 stars
(i.e. 0+1+3 components), and the more spatially extended Al-
poor one with a total of 225 stars (i.e. 2+4 components). Al-
though we refer to these two families simply as Al-poor and
Al-rich, this division can be viewed, to first order, as analo-
gous to the distinction between less chemically processed, first-
population-like stars and more enriched, second-population-like
stars commonly discussed in the multiple-population literature.
We stress, however, that inωCen this correspondence is only
approximate, since both families span a wide metallicity range
and do not map uniquely onto the standard 1P/2P classification.

3.2.2. Global kinematics
In addition to the radial profiles, we also computed global
(radius-integrated) mean velocities and velocity dispersions for
each subpopulation using the same modelling framework, by
considering all stars within a single radial bin. These global val-
ues provide an integrated characterisation of the internal kine-
matics of each population and enable a direct comparison be-
tween the Al-poor and Al-rich groups.
To assess differences in orbital structure, we quantified the
velocity anisotropy of each subpopulation by computing ratios of
the velocity dispersions in the tangential, radial, and line-of-sight
directions. Specifically, we evaluated the ratiosσ PM,tan/σPM,rad
andσ PM,tan/σlos, which serve as proxies for the intrinsic orbital
anisotropy in the available kinematic data. These ratios were
measured both globally, using all stars in a single bin (see the
bottom-left and central panels of Fig. 2), and as a function of
radius by combining the dispersion profiles in multiple bins to
construct anisotropy profiles (see Fig. 3). While the global values
provide a summary measurement of the internal kinematic struc-
ture, the radial profiles allow us to trace how orbital anisotropy
varies with clustercentric distance.

4. Results
The results presented in this work reveal a tight connection
between chemical tagging and internal kinematics inωCen.
Our APOGEE-based GMM identifies five components in multi-
dimensional abundance space. When we examine their [Al/Fe]
and [Mg/Fe] distributions together with their radial cumulative
profiles, these five components naturally group into two broader
families, here labelled Al-poor and Al-rich (see Section 3.1 and
Fig. 1). The top-left panel of Fig. 2 shows that the two groups
occupy distinct regions of the [Al/Fe]-[Mg/Fe] plane, while the
top-right panel illustrates that the Al-rich stars are more centrally

5. Discussion
The spatial segregation between the two chemically defined fam-
ilies identified in this work is qualitatively consistent with previ-
ous studies based on HST chromosome maps and ground-based
photometry, which show that chemically enriched or “upper-
stream” populations are more centrally concentrated, while less
enriched components become increasingly prominent at larger
radii (e.g. Bellini et al. 2018; Cordoni et al. 2020; Dondoglio
et al. 2026). Our APOGEE-based analysis extends this picture
by demonstrating that a similar radial segregation is recovered
using spectroscopically defined populations and traced over a
much wider field of view. We note that the APOGEE DR16 sam-
ple analysed by Mészáros et al. (2021), and discussed by Al-
varez Garay et al. (2024), shows limited spectroscopic coverage
in the innermost region ofωCen. In particular, the comparison
presented in their Fig. 10 reveals a deficit of APOGEE targets
within the central arcminutes of the cluster. The APOGEE DR17
catalogue used in this work provides improved spatial sampling
and includes stars down to projected radii of∼0.9 arcmin from
the cluster centre. Therefore, the enhanced central concentration
of the Al-rich population observed in our analysis cannot be at-
tributed to the observational bias discussed by Alvarez Garay
et al. (2024), but instead reflects an intrinsic spatial segregation
between the chemically defined populations.
Beyond this structural segregation, the anisotropy measure-
ments provide a direct dynamical counterpart to the spatial dif-
ferences. The comparison with the HST-based analysis of Bellini
et al. (2018) is particularly instructive. Although their study is
based on chromosome-map selected main-sequence stars and
our analysis uses APOGEE red giants, the anisotropy levels are
consistent within the uncertainties over the radial range where
the datasets overlap (i.e. atr∼3.5r h). In particular, in the left
and central panels of Fig. 3, we show that the anisotropy of
our Al-poor population closely matches that of their MS-I com-
ponent, which is identified as the less enriched, 1P-like popu-
lation and is less centrally concentrated within the HST field.

Conversely, the Al-rich population exhibits anisotropy values
similar to those of their MS-II component, corresponding to a
more chemically enriched (2P-like) population that is both more
centrally concentrated and more radially biased. This trend is
also consistent with Cordoni et al. (2020), who find that light-
element–enriched (N-rich) stars are more radially anisotropic
than the less enriched (N-poor) population. The recovery of the
same population-dependent anisotropy pattern across different
stellar evolutionary stages and independent datasets strengthens
the connection between the Al-poor and Al-rich division adopted
here and the multiple populations traced by the chromosome
map inωCen.
The right panel of Fig. 3 provides an additional consistency
check by comparing our results with the oMEGACat kinemat-
ics of Häberle et al. (2025). Their MUSE+HST measurements
show thatωCen is approximately isotropic in the core and be-
comes increasingly radially anisotropic toward the half-light ra-
dius. Our combined APOGEE+Gaia profile follows the same be-
haviour where the datasets overlap, but the population-resolved
view reveals that this global trend is not uniform: the Al-rich
group carries most of the increasing radial anisotropy, while the
Al-poor stars remain close to isotropic. Because the oMEGACat
data primarily probe the inner few central arcminutes, whereas
our APOGEE+Gaia sample extends from the crowding-limited
core to∼4r h, we obtain a nearly continuous view of the radial
behaviour of the two populations.
One of the most striking chemical properties ofωCen is
its large internal iron spread, spanning more than one dex in
[Fe/H]. Previous studies have investigated the relation between
rotation and metallicity inωCen, finding no strong systematic
dependence of the rotation amplitude on [Fe/H] (Pancino et al.
2007). Consistent with this picture, we do not detect significant
differences in the rotation properties when subdividing the sam-
ple either by iron abundance or by aluminium enrichment. In-
stead, the population-dependent kinematic differences identified
in this work are primarily seen in the velocity anisotropy. When
dividing the sample by metallicity alone, we do not detect sta-
tistically significant differences in anisotropy. In contrast, a clear
anisotropy difference emerges when the populations are sepa-
rated according to aluminium enrichment, with the Al-rich stars
exhibiting more radially biased orbits than the Al-poor popula-
tion (see also Cordoni et al. 2020; Häberle et al. 2025). This
behaviour contrasts with that observed in systems such as M54,
where kinematic differences are more closely linked to metal-
licity (e.g. Alfaro-Cuello et al. 2019), suggesting that similar
present-day nuclear clusters may arise from different chemo-
dynamical formation pathways.
Furthermore, the fact that population-dependent kinematic
signatures are still observed today suggests that these structures
are long-lived. In particular, the systematic difference in veloc-
ity anisotropy between Al-poor and Al-rich populations, recov-
ered over a wide radial range, indicates that the internal dynam-
ical evolution ofωCen has not completely erased the imprint
of population-specific orbital properties. This behaviour is also
qualitatively consistent with theoretical models of the long-term
dynamical evolution of multiple stellar populations in globular
clusters. Numerical simulations (see e.g. Mastrobuono-Battisti
& Perets 2013; Tiongco et al. 2019; Mastrobuono-Battisti &
Perets 2021; Vesperini et al. 2021) show that when one pop-
ulation is initially more centrally concentrated than another,
the two components naturally evolve toward different velocity-
anisotropy profiles: the concentrated population tends to develop
a radially anisotropic velocity distribution, while the more ex-
tended population remains closer to isotropic or mildly tangen-
tially biased. Importantly, these anisotropy differences are pre-
dicted to be among the most long-lived kinematic signatures of
incomplete dynamical mixing and can persist over several relax-
ation times (Aros et al. 2025). The spatial and anisotropy order-
ing observed here between the Al-rich and Al-poor populations
therefore follows the qualitative expectations of such dynamical
evolution models.
The coupled chemical, spatial, and kinematic properties re-
vealed by our analysis provide important constraints on the for-
mation history ofωCen. In particular, the APOGEE-defined Al-
poor and Al-rich populations differ in their spatial distributions
and orbital anisotropies, while simultaneously sharing a com-
mon large-scale rotation pattern. This combination of similari-
ties and differences offers insight into the physical processes that
shaped the system.
In the broader context of NSC formation (e.g. Neumayer
et al. 2020, Guillard et al. 2016), the growth of nuclear star
clusters is generally thought to involve a combination of cluster
assembly, gas inflow, and subsequent star formation, with their
relative importance depending on the host galaxy environment.
In gas-rich spiral galaxies, NSCs are thought to grow primarily
through repeated episodes of in-situ star formation fueled by gas
inflow from the surrounding disk. In contrast, in dwarf and ellip-
tical galaxies the cluster-inspiral channel, in which globular clus-
ters sink to the centre through dynamical friction and merge, is
expected to play a more prominent role. Nevertheless, gas reten-
tion and subsequent star formation may still occur in sufficiently
deep potentials, so that both processes can operate simultane-
ously and leave partially distinct chemo-dynamical signatures.
(i) Hierarchical assembly within a dwarf-galaxy environment.
One possibility is thatωCen formed through the hierarchi-
cal assembly of multiple stellar systems — for example glob-
ular clusters or massive star-forming clumps — inside a larger
host galaxy (e.g. Bekki & Freeman 2003; Bekki & Tsujimoto
2016; Calamida et al. 2020). In this framework, dynamical fric-
tion causes massive clusters to spiral toward the centre of the
dwarf-galaxy potential, where they merge and contribute to the
growth of a nuclear star cluster. Numerical simulations show
that such mergers can naturally produce multiple chemical pop-
ulations, typically characterised by differences in metallicity, as
well as spatial segregation between components and incomplete
dynamical mixing, leaving residual differences in orbital struc-
ture or anisotropy.
Our results show that the Al-rich population is more centrally
concentrated and exhibits stronger radial anisotropy than the Al-
poor population. This behaviour could be consistent with a sce-
nario in which chemically processed stellar systems—enriched
in aluminium but spanning a similar metallicity range—were
preferentially deposited deeper in the cluster potential well.
However, a merger of independent systems would typically be
expected to produce chemo-dynamical differences that correlate
with metallicity, rather than primarily with light-element abun-
dances such as aluminium. The absence of clear metallicity-
dependent signatures suggests that, if such an event occurred,
it must have been followed by substantial phase-space mixing.
At the same time, mergers alone do not straightforwardly ac-
count for the extreme light-element enrichment and large metal-
licity spread observed inωCen, which instead point to effi-
cient gas retention and prolonged chemical evolution. In partic-
ular, the large Al spread is closely linked to the formation of
multiple stellar populations in globular clusters and is generally
interpreted as the result of internal self-enrichment processes.

We note that other massive globular clusters not suspected to
be the nuclei of stripped dwarf galaxies exhibit comparable Al
variations (e.g. NGC 2808, NGC 6752, NGC 6205). A detailed
chemo-dynamical characterisation of such systems may there-
fore help place the properties ofωCen in a broader context.
(ii) Prolonged internal chemical evolution and multi-epoch star
formation.An alternative, but not mutually exclusive, inter-
pretation is thatωCen represents the nuclear star cluster of a
low-mass galaxy that retained gas over an extended period, en-
abling multiple episodes of star formation and progressive self-
enrichment (e.g. Romano et al. 2007; Marcolini et al. 2007; Grat-
ton et al. 2012). In this view, the Al-poor stars correspond to an
earlier generation with weaker light-element anomalies, while
the Al-rich stars formed later from centrally concentrated gas
enriched by previous stellar populations.
The chemical-evolution models of Dondoglio et al. (2026)
provide a quantitative framework consistent with this interpre-
tation, showing that the distinct chemical populations observed
inωCen can arise from successive enrichment phases involving
both supernova ejecta and proton-capture processed material, di-
luted with more pristine gas. Such centrally concentrated star
formation would naturally produce enriched populations that are
more spatially concentrated, as observed in Fig. 2.
From a dynamical perspective, stars formed from centrally
concentrated gas are expected to inherit the angular momentum
of the inflowing material and may initially form in a rotating
configuration. In this case, newly formed populations could start
with somewhat different spatial and orbital properties from pre-
existing stars. Subsequent dynamical evolution within the same
global gravitational potential, however, tends to mix these com-
ponents and align their large-scale rotation patterns. This is con-
sistent with our finding that the Al-poor and Al-rich populations
follow the same global rotation field. The residual differences in
orbital anisotropy discussed above can therefore be interpreted
as the surviving imprint of different initial concentrations of the
populations.
Implications for the formation ofωCen.Taken together, the
observational constraints presented here could be consistent with
a hybrid formation pathway in which both hierarchical assembly
and prolonged internal star formation contributed to the build-up
ofωCen. The shared rotation pattern strongly suggests that the
chemically distinct populations evolved within a common grav-
itational potential, while their differing spatial distributions and
orbital anisotropies indicate that their dynamical histories were
not identical. Such behaviour is naturally expected ifωCen orig-
inated as the nuclear star cluster of an accreted dwarf galaxy
whose evolution combined cluster infall, gas retention, and mul-
tiple star-formation episodes.
The correspondence between our APOGEE-defined pop-
ulations and the chemical groups identified by Mason et al.
(2026) provides additional insight into the formation sequence
ofωCen. In their framework, the P1 population represents
the least chemically processed component, while the IM and
P2 populations correspond to progressively more enriched stel-
lar generations. Our Al-poor stars broadly map onto their P1
population, whereas our Al-rich sample appears to encompass
both their IM and P2 components. Within this interpretation,
the observed chemo-dynamical structure may be naturally ex-
plained by a multi-phase formation history. A first major episode
of star formation could have produced the P1/Al-poor popula-
tion, which today appears more spatially extended and closer to
isotropic. Subsequent evolution may then have involved either
(i) the accretion of relatively pristine gas that triggered further
centrally concentrated star formation, producing chemically en-
riched stars with higher aluminium abundances, or (ii) the in-
ward migration and merging of additional stellar clusters with
IM-like chemistry, which could both deepen the central poten-
tial and contribute gas reservoirs that fuel the formation of the
most chemically extreme P2 population.
In either case, the IM and P2 components — corresponding
to our Al-rich population — would be expected to form or settle
preferentially in the central regions and to develop partially dis-
tinct orbital properties with respect to the P1/Al-poor population.
The persistence of their stronger radial anisotropy may therefore
reflect either their later formation within a deeper central poten-
tial or their dynamical deposition through inspiral processes.
The origin of the most metal-rich and Al-enhanced popula-
tions remains an open question. A merger origin for these com-
ponents would require the accreted system to share a very sim-
ilar angular-momentum structure with the pre-existing cluster,
in order to reproduce the observed common rotation axis and
inclination. Such a configuration is not impossible, but it would
imply a degree of dynamical alignment that may require fine tun-
ing. Conversely, a scenario in which metal-rich and Al-enhanced
stars formed in situ from centrally retained and progressively en-
riched gas provides a more straightforward explanation for their
shared rotation geometry and enhanced central concentration.
6. Conclusions
This work provides the first wide-field, fully spectroscopic view
of population-dependent 3D internal kinematics ofωCen. We
combined APOGEE DR17 abundances with Gaia kinematics to
study the chemo-dynamical structure ofωCen. A Gaussian Mix-
ture Model applied to 8-dimensional abundance space identifies
five components, which, after examining their radial cumulative
distributions, we group into two broader families that we refer to
as Al-poor and Al-rich. These chemically defined families also
differ in their spatial and kinematic properties.
Our main findings are as follows:
1. The Al-rich stars are significantly more centrally concen-
trated than the Al-poor stars, which dominate at larger radii.
2. The Al-rich population exhibits stronger radial anisotropy
and largely drives the global anisotropy trend ofωCen,
whereas the Al-poor stars remain closer to isotropic across
the radial range probed.
3. The chemo-dynamical differences correlate primarily with
light-element enrichment rather than with iron abundance.
Despite the well-known internal [Fe/H] spread inωCen, the
spatial segregation and anisotropy differences identified here
are more associated with aluminium enrichment than with
metallicity itself, indicating that light-element chemistry is
the dominant tracer of the dynamical substructure.
4. Despite their different anisotropies, the two populations
share a common rotation pattern, suggesting that they are
embedded in the same global angular-momentum structure.
Their total rotation amplitudes are comparable within the un-
certainties, and we find no evidence for kinematically de-
coupled or counter-rotating components in our large field of
view.
Taken together, these chemo-dynamical constraints are diffi-
cult to reconcile with a simple, monolithic globular-cluster ori-
gin. Instead, they support a formation pathway in whichωCen

A&A proofs:manuscript no. aa
evolved within a deep potential well capable of sustaining pro-
longed chemical enrichment and internal dynamical differen-
tiation. In this context, our results are compatible with self-
enrichment models proposed forωCen (Dondoglio et al. 2026),
in which extended star formation within a dwarf-galaxy envi-
ronment gives rise to multiple chemically distinct but dynam-
ically coupled populations. A sufficiently deep progenitor po-
tential could retain a fraction of supernova ejecta, enabling pro-
gressive enrichment and naturally accounting for the observed
internal [Fe/H] spread. Within such a framework, the Al-poor
stars likely trace an earlier, more spatially extended component,
whereas the Al-rich population formed later from centrally con-
centrated, chemically processed gas. The shared rotation geom-
etry and consistent angular-momentum orientation indicate that
both populations evolved within the same global gravitational
potential, while their distinct orbital anisotropies reflect incom-
plete dynamical mixing rather than independent dynamical ori-
gins.
Future work should combine extended spectroscopic sam-
ples, especially in the outermost regions of the cluster, with
orbit-based orN-body models that explicitly treat multiple tracer
populations. Such chemo-dynamical modelling will be essential
to quantify the relative roles of mergers and internal chemical
evolution in assemblingωCen, and to place this system in the
broader context of nuclear star cluster formation in dwarf galax-
ies.
Finally, we note that our work provides a fully spectroscopic
way to characterize the chemo-dynamical properties of GCs, in-
dependent on the widely used photometric detections of multi-
ple stellar populations. An application of our method to a wider
samples of MW GCs could further shed light onto the puzzling
formation of these dense stellar systems.

Appendix B: Statistical significance of
population-dependent spatial and kinematic
differences
To assess the statistical significance of the difference in veloc-
ity anisotropy between the Al-poor and Al-rich populations, we
adopt a permutation-based approach.
We define a proxy anisotropy parameter as
β′ ≡σ PM,tan/σPM,rad,
whereσ tan andσ rad represent the tangential and radial PM com-
ponent, respectively. We quantify the anisotropy contrast be-
tween the two populations as
∆β′ =  β′
Al-poor −  β′
Al-rich .
To construct a null distribution for∆β ′, stars are randomly
reassigned between two populations 100 times while keeping
the population sizes fixed. For each random realization, we re-
compute∆β ′, thereby building the distribution of anisotropy con-
trasts expected under the null hypothesis that population mem-
bership is unrelated to orbital anisotropy.
Figure B.1 shows the resulting null distribution of∆β ′. The
observed anisotropy contrast,∆β ′ ≃0.19, is indicated by a ver-
tical dashed line and lies well outside the bulk of the distribution
obtained from random reshuffling. The correspondingp-value is
defined as the fraction of random realizations that yield a value
of|∆β ′|at least as large as the observed one.
This result indicates that the measured difference in velocity
anisotropy between the Al-poor and Al-rich populations is un-
likely to arise from a random partitioning of the data, supporting
the interpretation that the two populations occupy systematically
different regions of orbital phase space.
