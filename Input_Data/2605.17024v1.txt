ABSTRACT
We analyze the line ratio of the 13CO (2-1) to 12CO (2-1) rotational transitions observed from new ALMA
observations of 100 Giant Molecular Clouds (GMCs) that span the Large Magellanic Cloud. We measure a
median line ratio of13CO(2−1)/12CO(2−1) = 0.078with68%of the sample falling between 0.058 and 0.107.
A regression analysis confirms a nearly linear relationship across two orders of magnitude in line luminosity.
Moreover, we find that the inclusion of(LFIR)from Young Stellar Objects as a predictor variable of the line ratio
significantly improves the quality of the fit, with clouds hosting IR-bright YSOs having relatively brighter13CO
emission. This analysis indicates that active star forming molecular clouds have different internal conditions
than more quiescent clouds.
Keywords:Interstellar medium (847), Molecular Clouds (1072), Star Formation (1569)
1.INTRODUCTION
Because of the invisibility of H 2 in cold molecular clouds, CO is the brightest tracer species used to map out the distribution
of star-forming molecular gas (A. D. Bolatto et al. 2013). Several rotational transitions from different isotopologues of CO are
detected in the Milky Way and extragalactically. A single line ratio between a pair of lines does not yield a full solution for
the conditions of the gas (density, temperature, relative abundance of the species), but single line ratios are proxies for changing
conditions of the gas. The ratio of individual spectral lines has been used to indicate the general excitation conditions of the
molecular gas (e.g., 12CO(2−1)/12CO(1−0); A. K. Leroy et al. 2022) or a proxy for the typical density of the gas as inferred
through its opacity (e.g., 13CO(1−0)/12CO(1−0); K. S. Polk et al. 1988).
Here, we measure theR 13 ≡ 13CO(2−1)/12CO(2−1)line ratio for 100 molecular clouds in the Large Magellanic Cloud. This
line ratio, like its(1−0)counterpart, traces the relative opacity of the two spectral lines. The two lines should have comparable
excitation temperatures (J. G. Mangum & Y . L. Shirley 2015), and would haveR13 set by the column density ratio (13C/12C∼
1/50in previous LMC studies; e.g., L. E. B. Johansson et al. 1994). Because of the opacity,R 13 is higher with the ratio varying
based on the relative opacities of the two spectral lines.
We reportR 13 ratio primarily to measure its range of variation in an extragalactic environment and sensitivity to different
molecular cloud properties. Notably, the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) is capable of simultaneous
observations of these two species in Band 6, so this line ratio will inform planned observations in that commonly used observing
mode.

The GMCs in the sample have a median⟨13CO/12CO⟩= 0.078with68%of the clouds falling within the range 0.058 - 0.107.
The upper tail of this distribution remains well below the line ratio of0.171±0.007derived by R. Indebetouw et al. (2013). This

(2013) measureR 13 within 13CO-defined clump boundaries, whereas we apply a 12CO-defined mask.

linear fit implies a line ratio of 13CO(2−1)/12CO(2−1) = 0.082±0.002.
We compare the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) to compare the two models finding that includingLFIR reduces the BIC
from−94to−122. Thus, including the effects ofL FIR produces a significantly better prediction of the 13COluminosity. The
best-fit relationship is:
logL( 13CO) = (−1.31±0.08) + (0.92±0.03) logL( 12CO) + (0.12±0.02) logL FIR,(1)
where line luminosities are in units of K km/s pc 2 andL FIR is in units ofL ⊙. This model captures91.4%of the observed
variance. The near unity slope shows that clouds have an approximately constant line ratio but the coefficient for luminosity
indicates that, for a fixed 12CO intensity, clouds with higherL FIR have higher 13CO emission. This cause of this increased
line ratio could reflect that clouds with higher densities have more active star formation or it could indicate that IR heating is
increasing the excitation of both species.
