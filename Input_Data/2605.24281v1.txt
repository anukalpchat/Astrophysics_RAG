ABSTRACT
While the origin of merging black-hole binaries observed in gravitational waves remain uncertain,
different formation channels are expected to leave distinct imprints on their observed mass and spin
distributions. In this work, we focus on the mass dependence of the spin magnitudes (χ 1, χ2), allowing
for multiple spin subpopulations whose relative fraction varies with mass. Using the binaries from the
fourth LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA gravitational wave transient catalog (GWTC-4), we find strong evidence
for two subpopulations, with a log Bayes factor of log 10 B= 4 +6
−2. The data support a picture in
which low-mass systems are composed almost entirely of slowly spinning black holes (χ≲0.2), while
high-mass systems are dominated by a broader component with moderate to rapid spins, peaking at
(χ1, χ2)≈(0.7,0.7) and retaining a non-negligible tail toward asymmetric spins (χ 1, χ2)≈(0.7,0). The
transition between these regimes spans a broad range fromm 1 ≈35 +20
−20 M⊙ tom 1 ≈70 +90
−25 M⊙. For
the low-mass, slowly-spinning regime, a flexible model of spin orientations does not allow us to exclude
identically vanishing spins,χ 1 =χ 2 = 0. Meanwhile, the high-mass, rapidly-spinning population does
not neatly display the spin-magnitude asymmetry expected from hierarchical mergers, although the
data do not exclude that. This kind of localized structure is generally difficult to access with standard
Monte-Carlo likelihood estimators, which we replace with a regularized representation in terms of
truncated Gaussian mixtures. Taken together, our results provide a new target for formation models,
which should reproduce not only the mass and effective spin distributions, but also the mass-dependent
structure of the individual component-spin magnitudes.
1.INTRODUCTION
Over the past decade, gravitational wave (GW) de-
tections by the Advanced LIGO (Aasi et al. 2015),
Virgo (Acernese et al. 2015) and KAGRA (Aso et al.
2013) detectors have provided a powerful probe of as-
trophysics (Abac et al. 2025a). The origin of the bi-
naries that produce these signals, composed of black
holes (BHs) and neutron stars (NSs), remains an open
question. Answering it addresses the complex and still
uncertain physics underlying the lives and deaths of
massive stars, especially in binary systems (e.g., Man-
del & Farmer 2022). With more than 200 signals de-
tected (Abac et al. 2025b), primarily from binary black
holes (BBHs), population-level inference has revealed
multiple features in the mass distribution of BHs (Ab-
bott et al. 2021a; Tiwari & Fairhurst 2021; Edelman
et al. 2022), indications that multiple channels produce
merging binaries (Abbott et al. 2021a), possible sig-
natures of pair-instability processes (Tong et al. 2026;
Ray & Kalogera 2026), and correlations among binary
parameters that promise further insights (Abac et al.
2025a).
Remarkably, the spins of compact objects are directly
measured from the GW signals. The orientation of these
spins relative to the binary orbit, encoded in their tilt
anglesθ i can reveal the imprint of interactions with
other bodies (Stevenson et al. 2017; Gerosa et al. 2018).
The (dimensionless) magnitudes of the spinsχ i hold in-
formation about angular momentum and its transport
in the cores of massive stars (Fuller & Ma 2019; Ma &
Fuller 2019), as well as angular momentum transfer from
a stellar companion (Qin et al. 2018; Ma & Fuller 2023).
It can also reveal a population of second-generation bi-
naries (e.g., Gerosa & Fishbach 2021), one or both of
whose members was formed in a previous BBH merger
and so would haveχ∼0.7, as determined by the or-
bital angular momentum of the binary (Pretorius 2005;
Scheel et al. 2009). Hints of multiple populations in
the BBH spin distribution are already present in the

Inferring the distribution of BH spins can be chal-
lenging technically, especially for models which support
multiple populations and where the distributions are al-
lowed to be narrow, as when allowing for a concentration
at negligible spins. Standard GW population methods
use samples derived from Bayesian parameter estima-
tion (PE) produced with a fiducial prior to estimate the
population-level likelihood via Monte Carlo (MC) aver-
aging. These approaches are often limited by the vari-
ance of such estimators (Talbot & Golomb 2023; Heinzel
& Vitale 2025; Hussain et al. 2025), which becomes es-
pecially severe for narrow populations. Addressing this
limitation with specialized approaches is key to further
improving our understanding of the population of GW
sources, particularly as our catalogs grow (e.g. Essick
& Farr 2022; Talbot & Golomb 2023; Heinzel & Vitale
2025). As we showed in Hussain et al. (2026, 2025), these
issues can be mitigated by using (truncated) Gaussian
mixture models to regularize the likelihood.
In Hussain et al. (2026), we studied the distribution of
BH spin magnitudes in GWTC-3 by allowing for correla-
tions in the space of component spin magnitudes, with
up to two independent subpopulations in that space.
The result was tentative evidence for two distinct spin
distributions: a dominant distribution displayed pri-
mary spinsχ 1 ∼0.2, smaller secondary spins, and some
potential anti-correlation between the two; a subdomi-
nant distribution, containing∼15% of the BBHs, pre-
ferred high spinsχ i ∼0.8 for both BHs, with indica-
tions of preferentially containing more massive binaries.
Some of these results were driven by GW190517, a bi-
nary with large component spins well-measured away
from zero (Abbott et al. 2021c, 2024). In this work we
update Hussain et al. (2026) using GWTC-4 and intro-
duce an explicit mass dependence to the mixing fraction
between the two subpopulations.

2.METHODS AND MODELS
We use hierarchical Bayesian inference to infer the
population properties of BBHs (Mandel et al. 2019;
Thrane & Talbot 2019; Vitale et al. 2022; Abac et al.
2025a). The goal is to compute posteriors over the
hyper-parametersΛof our chosen population model.
We take as our baseline the fiducial strongly modeled
BBH population model applied in Abac et al. (2025a):
the primary massm 1 drawn from a broken power law
plus two normal distributions (“peaks”), the mass ratio
q=m 2/m1 drawn from a power law conditioned onm 1,
and a merger rate that evolves as a power law in 1 +z,
withzthe redshift.

Evaluating the population likelihoodL(Λ|{d}) re-
quires integrating the population model over the likeli-
hood for each event. Similarly, accounting for selection
effects using the detection fractionξ(Λ) requires inte-
grating the population model over the detection prob-
ability for binaries with given parameters. These inte-
grals are typically carried out by summing over samples
from reference distributions using MC methods, and are
limited by the variance of the MC integrals (Thrane &
Talbot 2019; Mandel et al. 2019), particularly for narrow
population distributions which may otherwise be consis-
tent with the data (Talbot & Golomb 2023; Heinzel &
Vitale 2025). To address this we use the methods de-
scribed in Hussain et al. (2026, 2025), fitting truncated
Gaussian mixture models (TGMMs) to the underlying
(weighted) event posteriors or recovered injections. We
carry out the needed integrals efficiently using a combi-
nation of semi-analytic integration (in the spin sector of
parameter space) and MC integration (in the mass and
redshift sector) using the TGMMs, controlling the vari-
ance even forΛvalues where the spin subpopulations are
narrow or concentrated in corners of parameter space.
We briefly review the method and updates made for this
work in Appendix A.
We carry out the TGMM fitting using thetruncat-
edgaussianmixturespackage (Hussain 2024a) and
population inference using thegravpoppackage (Hus-
sain 2024b). We sample our hyper-posteriors using the
no-U-turn sampler (Hoffman & Gelman 2011) Hamilto-
nian MC algorithm (Neal 2011; Betancourt 2017) imple-
mented innumpyro(Phan et al. 2019; Bingham et al.
2019).

4.ASTROPHYSICAL IMPLICATIONS
The mass-dependent spin mixing we infer provides a
new empirical target for models of BBH formation. Our
results point toward a mixture of channels whose relative
contribution shifts across the BBH mass spectrum, but
the current data do not uniquely identify which channels
are responsible. Successful formation models should re-
produce not only the marginal mass distribution and the
distribution of effective inspiral spin, but the full condi-
tional distributionp(χ 1, χ2 |m 1), including the transi-
tion we observe nearm 1 ∼35M ⊙. Here we discuss how
our results compare to astrophysical expectations and
to recent independent population analyses of GWTC-4.
At low primary masses, the BBH population is con-
centrated near small spin magnitudes,χ i ≲0.1, with
posterior support extending toward zero spin. This is
broadly consistent with isolated binary-star evolution,
in which efficient angular-momentum transport from the
stellar core to the envelope leaves the resulting black
holes slowly rotating (Fuller et al. 2019; Belczynski et al.
2020). Several independent analyses of GWTC-4 find
a low-spin or small-χ eff subpopulation at lower masses
(e.g., Banagiri et al. 2025b; Sridhar et al. 2025; Ray
et al. 2026), all broadly consistent with an isolated-
binary origin. The degree to which this population is
truly negligible-spin, versus merely slowly spinning, re-
mains uncertain. Adamcewicz et al. (2025) find no ev-
idence for a non-spinning subpopulation in GWTC-4,
while we find that the data is unable to exclude this hy-
pothesis; this difference may reflect the mass-dependent
mixing our model allows, as well as differences in the
choice of spin-tilt model and in how MC variance is con-
trolled (see e.g. Hussain et al. 2025).
Above the transition, the rapidly spinning component
contributes an increasing fraction of BBHs. Its distri-
bution is centered nearχ i ∼0.7 on both BHs, the char-
acteristic remnant spin from a previous BBH merger
(e.g., Fishbach et al. 2017; Gerosa & Berti 2017), al-
though the distribution is broad and includes support
for configurations where the primary spins rapidly while
the secondary spins more slowly. The former configu-
ration (χ 1 ∼χ 2 ∼0.7) would be suggestive of second-
generation plus second-generation (2G+2G) hierarchi-
cal pairings; the latter (χ 1 ∼0.7, χ 2 ∼0) resembles
a 2G+1G event, a hallmark signature of hierarchical
growth (Gerosa & Fishbach 2021). Environments ca-
pable of retaining post-kick merger remnants—globular

6
clusters, nuclear star clusters, or active galactic nucleus
(AGN) disks—are natural candidates for hosting these
BBHs (Yang et al. 2019; Tagawa et al. 2021; Gerosa
& Fishbach 2021). On the other hand, chemically ho-
mogeneous evolution is also consistent with the prefer-
ence for both spins in the binary having high spin and
high mass (Mandel & de Mink 2016; de Mink & Man-
del 2016), although it does not naturally account for the
asymmetric-spin tail that our inference allows. Multiple
analyses of GWTC-4 independently find evidence for a
high-spin, high-mass subpopulation and variously asso-
ciate it with hierarchical mergers in star clusters (An-
tonini et al. 2025; Banagiri et al. 2025b; Ray et al. 2026),
AGN accretion (Bartos & Haiman 2026; Li et al. 2025),
or a combination thereof; related structure in reduced
spin observables has also been discussed in Vijaykumar
et al. (2026), and data-driven analyses similarly support
multiple spin subpopulations (Guttman et al. 2026); our
results are consistent with all of these interpretations
and do not presently discriminate between them.
The transition massm s = 36+19
−21 M⊙ sits in the same
broad mass range where several independent studies
have identified a change in character of the BBH pop-
ulation. Tong et al. (2026) find evidence for a pair-
instability gap in thesecondarymass distribution of
GWTC-4 with a lower boundary near 44M ⊙, and note
that binaries with primary masses in this range tend to
spin more rapidly—a direct parallel to our findings. The
absence of this gap in the primary mass distribution is
naturally explained if a hierarchically formed subpop-
ulation begins to contribute above the pair-instability
scale, filling the depleted region and carrying the high-
spin signature we observe (Antonini et al. 2025; Tong
et al. 2026); Ray & Kalogera (2026) offer a complemen-
tary view, arguing that the apparent secondary-mass
gap is better described as a smooth falloff, consistent
with a gradual onset of hierarchical contributions rather
than a sharp threshold. This picture may also con-
nect naturally to the second-generation peak near 70M⊙
predicted for cluster hierarchies plus a pair-instability
cutoff in Ginat et al. (2026). Similarly, Mould et al.
(2026) show that an inferred gradual secondary-mass
falloff can remain consistent with an underlying sharp
pair-instability cutoff when sufficiently flexible popula-
tion models are used. Banagiri et al. (2025b) and Srid-
har et al. (2025) independently find that the 35M ⊙ fea-
ture in the primary mass distribution is associated with
a subpopulation having isotropic spins and near-equal
mass ratios—properties consistent with dynamical as-
sembly in globular clusters—while the higher-mass sub-
population they identify has a distinctly different spin
character, broadly aligned with our rapidly spinning
component.
Our results underscore the importance of working in
component-spin space rather than solely withχ eff. Dif-
ferent configurations of (χ1, χ2)—both modest, one fast
and one slow, or both large but misaligned—can project
onto overlappingχ eff distributions after marginalizing
over tilts and mass ratios (Banagiri et al. 2025a; Payne
et al. 2024). The mass-dependentp(χ 1, χ2 |m 1) we
infer provides a sharp, more direct diagnostic for distin-
guishing formation channels, and is complementary to
theχ eff-based analyses of Banagiri et al. (2025a) and
Adamcewicz et al. (2024), who respectively find non-
Gaussian structure in theχ eff distribution and that the
primary BH tends to drive the spin signal.
Taken together, these independent lines of evidence
from GWTC-4 consistently point to the operation of at
least two distinct formation channels across the BBH
mass spectrum. The challenge for population synthe-
sis is to reproduce the full conditional structure: a
narrow low-spin component at low masses, a rapidly
spinning component whose contribution grows above
m1 ∼35M ⊙, and either saturates or coexists with the
other at high mass. The increase in the number of de-
tected BBH mergers promises to substantially sharpen
these constraints as the size of our catalog increases.
5.CONCLUSIONS
We have investigated the distribution of spin magni-
tudes using the 153 BBHs candidates in GWTC-4 with
FAR<1 yr −1. We find strong support for at least two
distinct populations of spinning BBHs, a slowly spin-
ning subpopulation that dominates at low masses, and
a rapidly spinning subpopulation that makes up a sub-
stantial fractionη e = 0.17 +0.29
−0.17 at high masses. Our
results point to multiple formation channels for LIGO–
Virgo–KAGRA BBHs and, in particular, prefer that
both black holes have large spinsχ i ∼0.7 in a sig-
nificant number of high-mass BBHs. Such systems,
like GW231123, and the asymmetric, high-spin events
GW241011 and GW241110 (Abac et al. 2025h), pose a
challenge for current models of binary formation, and
do not neatly fit expectations for hierarchical mergers.
Meanwhile, the population of slowly spinning black holes
can potentially be explained if a large fraction of BHs,
in isolated or dynamical environments, form with small
natal spins. Relative to our GWTC-3 analysis (Hussain
et al. 2026), we find restored support for a subpopula-
tion of BBHs with vanishing spins (χ i ∼0). This could
be due to efficient angular momentum transfer from the
core to envelope during stellar evolution Fuller & Ma
(2019); Ma & Fuller (2019).

There are a number of natural extensions to our work
that could provide further insights into BBH formation
channels and the underlying astrophysical processes.
Our population model factorizes into independent mass,
redshift, spin-magnitude, and spin-tilt sectors. However,
the known features across the BH mass spectrum, such
as the overabundance of BHs at∼10M ⊙, and the feature
at∼35M ⊙ (Abac et al. 2025a), hint at distinct BBH
formation processes in action at these masses. There
are also additional hints of dynamical formation (Ab-
bott et al. 2021a, 2023; Abac et al. 2025a) and systems
that may be the merger of second and first generation
BHs (Abac et al. 2025h). Each formation scenario is ac-
companied by particular expected spin magnitude and
tilt distributions, and a natural next step is to couple
distinct mass and tilt models to our flexible spin mag-
nitude distributions. Moreover, the sensitivity of our
inferred slowly-spinning population to the chosen form
of our spin-tilt model indicates that further structure
may be present in the BH spin distribution, requiring
additional components in our model. Our novel TGMM
methods can help mitigate the impact of variance in like-
lihood estimation for such complex models, and help to
reveal the imprint of specific astrophysical scenarios on
BBH populations.
Finally, our analysis was carried out using GW detec-
tions through only the first part of the fourth LIGO-
Virgo-KAGRA observing campaign. An additional 186
GW candidate detections have been announced as open
alerts over the remainder of the campaign (LIGO–
Virgo–KAGRA Collaboration 2025). This large ad-
dition of binary mergers to our data set promises to
sharpen our ability to infer multiple subpopulations
and ultimately tease out features to help reveal the un-
certain origin of these explosive events.

B.FURTHER RESULTS
B.1.Further results for mass-dependent mixing fraction
Our fiducial model allows for mass-dependent mixing fractionη(m 1) andfreetilt model. We plot the hyperparam-
eters controllingη(m 1) in Figure 5.
In addition to the mean and width parameters illustrated in Figure 5, our spin magnitude model allows for correlation
between primary and secondary spins in each subpopulation. The controlling parametersρ a andρ b cannot be naively
interpreted as the degree of correlation for our truncated normal distributions since theirµparameters can lie outside
the truncation domain, and then the truncation can reshape the distribution. For this reason we directly compute
the Pearson correlation coefficient ˆρfor each subpopulation. This is shown in Figure 6, along with the induced
prior inherited from our priors on the hyperparameters of our model. We see that the correlation of the rapidly
spinning subpopulation is essentially unchanged from the prior. On the other hand, we see that for the slowly spinning
population, the posterior for ˆρa peaks away from the prior, favoring mild anti-correlation between the spins. However,
it also develops a more extended tail to positive values of ˆρ a relative to the prior. Additional detections are required
to better characterize these correlations.
B.2.Comparison to GWTC-3 results
Relative to our previous analysis of Hussain et al. (2026), the results presented here utilize more events, a modified
mass model, and thefreerather thanfixedtruncated normal component for the cosine tilts. To make contact
with the previous results following GWTC-3, we fixηto be constant across masses but otherwise leave our fiducial
model unchanged. In Hussain et al. (2026) we preferred two spin populations to one, but the evidence was modest;
with constantηwe find here strong evidence for two populations. Figure 7 shows the posterior overηas compared
to Hussain et al. (2026), withη= 0.83 +0.11

increasingly localized nearχ i ≈0, while the more rapidly spinning subpopulation becomes tighter but is still diffuse.
The rapidly spinning subpopulation in GWTC-4 takes up markedly different territory onχ 1, χ2 compared to the
slowly spinning subpopulation, allowing more confident identification based on spin-magnitudes alone. In comparison,
GWTC-3 allowed some label switching behavior due to non-negligible posterior support atη= 0.5.

B.4.Leave one out results
Our previous analysis following GWTC-3 was sensitive to excluding the most extremely spinning BBH of that
dataset, GW190517 (Hussain et al. 2026). In the GWTC-4 dataset, GW231123, a signal from a massive and highly
spinning BBH, although how extreme and tightly measured its spin magnitudes are depends on the model applied
in inference (Abac et al. 2025g). Here we perform inference with our fiducial model while first excluding GW231123.
Figure 9 compares this leave-one-out analysis to our full result. While the leave-one-out PPD forη(m 1) is visibly
different than our full analysis, we see that our main conclusions are unchanged: the slowly-spinning population
strongly dominates at low masses, with a definite turnover starting at masses∼35M ⊙. At higher masses there is a
wider range of mixing fractions, with the rapidly spinning population preferred but with a substantial portion of BBHs
from the slowly spinning population. The spin distributions themselves are inferred to be very similar with GW231123
left out, although the rapidly spinning component broadens and has somewhat more support for smaller values ofχ 2.
The same is conclusions hold if we leave out both GW231123 and GW190517, as seen in Figure 9.
