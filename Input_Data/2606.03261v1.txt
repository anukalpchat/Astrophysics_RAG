ABSTRACT
We present the serendipitous discovery of a nine-member system comprised of protostellar and candidate prestellar sources in
∼350 au-resolution images from Complex Chemistry in hot Cores with ALMA (CoCCoA). The system is bound in a stability
analysis,hasameanseparationbetweenpairsof7930au,andappearstohaveformedviathefragmentationofasinglelarge-scale
filamentarystructuretracedby1.20mmcontinuumandH 13CO+J=3-2emission.Twomultipleswithinthenine-membersystem,
a triple and a binary, have properties consistent with formation by core fragmentation on∼1500-1700 au scales. The hot core
NGC 6334-43 is resolved into two components (ALMA2a/ALMA2b) separated by 618 au and driving a bipolar outflow traced
by 12CO J = 2-1 and SiO J = 5-4 in∼1250 au-resolution archival Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)
data. Only one other source in the nine-member system is clearly protostellar: ALMA6a, which drives an outflow traced by
12CO.TheoutflowpropertiesofALMA2a/ALMA2bandALMA6aareconsistentwithhigh-massandlow-massClass0sources
respectively. By fitting the CH3CN J = 13-12 emission towards ALMA2a, ALMA2b and ALMA6a, we derive Mvir = 4.5, 5.4
and 2.6 M⊙ respectively. The other six sources in the nine-member multiple have Mgas = 0.50-1.87 M⊙ and appear young,
as indicated by their sparse mm-wavelength line emission and non-detection in published cm continuum observations. Our
results highlight the potential of serendipitous discoveries in ALMA surveys to add to the small observational sample of young
high-mass protomultiple systems.

1 INTRODUCTION
Whilst on the main sequence, massive stars (MZAMS > 8 M⊙, Zero-
Age Main-Sequence) exhibit binarity or higher order multiplicity
almost ubiquitously (e.g Sana et al. 2014; Moe & Di Stefano 2017;
Pauwelsetal.2023).However,therelativeprevalenceofthedistinct
formation mechanisms that can lead to this multiplicity is unknown
(see recent review by Offner et al. 2023). Fragmentation of discs
(e.g. Adams et al. 1989; Bonnell 1994; Kratter et al. 2010; Oliva &
Kuiper 2020), cores (e.g. Larson 1972; Boss & Bodenheimer 1979;
Offneretal.2010;Guszejnovetal.2017)andfilaments(asobserved
by Pineda et al. 2015) as well as gas mediated capture (e.g. Ostriker
1994; Bate et al. 2003; Cournoyer-Cloutier et al. 2020) and N-body
interactions (e.g. Bate et al. 2002; Moeckel & Bate 2010; Parker
& Meyer 2014) have been suggested as mechanisms that may form
high-mass multiple systems. Together, these mechanisms may act
across a large range of scales and times.
Overthepast12years,awealthofsurveysthatsuccessfullyresolve
the discs of individual protostars have been carried out in low-mass
starformingclouds(e.g.Chenetal.2013;Tobinetal.2016;Kounkel

et al. 2016; Encalada et al. 2021; Tobin et al. 2020, 2022). In such
work,itisoftensuggestedthatbothcoreanddiscfragmentationcon-
tribute to the overall observed multiplicity of low-mass protostars.
For example, the bimodal distribution of separations, with peaks at
∼100 au and∼103 au, found in Orion by the VLA/ALMA Nascent
Disk and Multiplicity (VANDAM) survey is primarily driven by the
youngest, Class 0, sources (Tobin et al. 2022). As such, Tobin et al.
(2022) suggest that both disc and core fragmentation, as well as in-
ward migration acting on short timescales (see e.g. Bate & Bonnell
1997; Stahler 2010; Lee et al. 2019), are required to reproduce their
observed separation distribution. However, understanding of how
each formation mechanism acts and their relative importance is de-
bated.Forexample,theseparationdistribution(withpeaksat∼75au
and∼3000 au) of the VANDAM Perseus sample (Tobin et al. 2016)
is reproduced in simulations by Kuruwita & Haugbølle (2023) with
onlyturbulentfragmentationanddynamicalcapture,i.e.withoutdisc
fragmentation.

ple systems are much more challenging to identify and study. This
is because larger distances to denser clouds that are highly extincted
at infrared (IR) and optical wavelengths, as well as high-mass stars’
rapid evolution whilst remaining deeply embedded in their natal en-

mass multiple formation has to date been derived from simulations.
Many works adopting initial conditions of isolated non-turbulent
massive prestellar cores have found that multiples form primarily
via disc fragmentation. For example, Krumholz et al. (2009) and
Oliva & Kuiper (2020) both found that fragmentation takes place
within prominent spiral arm structures in the disc. Binaries with
initial separations of 1000 - 2000 au are produced by both works,
and Krumholz et al. (2009) also show that migration and gravita-
tional interactions between fragments can close separations to <30
au.Mignon-Risseetal.(2023)haveshownthatmagneticfieldshave
a strong influence on primordial system parameters; their non-ideal
magneto-hydrodynamical(MHD)simulationsresultininitialbinary
separations 2×smaller than their non-magnetised runs. And whilst
most studies produce high-mass ratio multiples, Mignon-Risse et al.
(2021) produce binaries with a more even mass ratio (q = 1.1−1.6)
viadiscfragmentationintheirnon-idealMHDsuper-Alfvéniccases.
There, fragmentation of spiral arms results in binaries with separa-
tions of 300−700 au, which in turn host their own 100−200 au radii
discs.
Factors including magnetic fields may also resist disc fragmenta-
tion.Forinstance,intheidealMHDregime,Commerçonetal.(2022)
find that the inclusion of magnetic fields prevents the production of
multiplesentirely.Thesub-AlfvénicturbulenceadoptedbyMignon-
Risse et al. (2021) in their non-ideal MHD simulations also results
in single stellar systems. Additionally, Meyer & Vorobyov (2024)
show that ‘stellar wobbling’ (star-disc interactions) acts against the
development of gravitational instabilities in the disc.
Introducing additional physics into simulations may promote the
production of multiples via core fragmentation. The work of Rosen
& Krumholz (2020), which assumes an isolated prestellar core and
includesoutflows,radiationfeedbackandturbulence,findsthatbina-
ries are produced via core fragmentation at early times and via disc
fragmentation at late times.
Certainly, however, the commonly assumed initial conditions of
isolated massive prestellar cores are observationally very rare (see
Valeille-Manet et al. 2025, and references therein, for the complete
sample). A notable exception in this regard is He & Ricotti (2023),
whoconductzoom-insimulationsofagiantmolecularcloud(GMC),
and find that multiples residing in shared circumstellar discs (radii
of200−6000au)wereformedvia‘quasi-spherical’orfilamentfrag-
mentation at early times, before their accretion onto a dominant
circumstellar disc. Notably, this result suggests that the observation
ofamultiplewithinashareddiscisnotinitselfsufficienttoconclude
that the system has formed via disc fragmentation.
Close comparison between the multiples produced in simulations
and system parameters obtained from observations may be highly
complex as observed system properties are highly dependent on the
evolutionary stage of the protostar(s). Parameters such as the mass
ratio, separation and disc properties of protomultiples of all masses
may dramatically change on short timescales due to a variety of
physical processes, including gas-dynamical friction (Bate & Bon-
nell1997),discmigration(e.g.Tokovinin&Moe2019;Muñozetal.
2019;Siweketal.2023),massaccretion(e.g.Lund&Bonnell2018),
magnetic braking (e.g. Harada et al. 2021) and interaction with the
disc or nearby protostars (e.g. Ramírez-Tannus et al. 2021). Simula-
tions of whole clouds populated by low-mass multiple systems find
that the transition between a widely and closely separated system
can occur on timescales of just 104−105 years due to mass accretion
and gas-dynamical friction (Offner et al. 2010; Offner et al. 2016;
Lee et al. 2019). Moreover, Cournoyer-Cloutier et al. (2024) simu-
late three massive clusters (initial Mgas of 2×10 4, 8×10 4 and 3.2
×10 5 M⊙) and find that the timescales such processes act on are
environment-dependent:intheirmostmassivecloud,theseparations
are reduced on the shortest timescale. Thus, using observed system
propertiestodistinguishbetweenformationmechanisms(e.g.Offner
et al. 2023) requires identifying and studying the youngest multiple
systems.
To date, only a few very young high-mass multiple systems have
been identified (Cyganowski et al. 2022; Kong et al. 2023; Barnes
et al. 2023), with most previous studies characterising systems that
are already IR-bright, host UC/HC HIIregions or display rich hot
core line emission (e.g. Kraus et al. 2017; Beuther et al. 2017; Ilee
etal.2018;Pomohacietal.2019;Koumpiaetal.2021;Ahmadietal.
2023).Recently,Lietal.(2024,2025)showedthatboundhigh-order
protomultiple systems can form via distinct formation mechanisms.
Lietal.(2024)findthatatriple,quadrupleandquintupleprotostellar
system have each formed via core fragmentation on scales of∼731
au in G333.23−0.06. Li et al. (2025) show that a septuple system
with a mean deprojected separation of 332 au has formed via disc
fragmentation in NGC 6334I(N).
The limited sample of young protobinaries and protomultiples in
high-mass star forming environments known in the literature moti-
vatessearchingforadditionalexamples.Notably,oneoftheyoungest
massive protobinaries discovered to date, the G11.92−0.61 MM2
system, was identified serendipitously in Atacama Large Millime-
ter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) observations targeting the nearby
line-rich disc G11.92−0.61 MM1 (Cyganowski et al. 2022). In this
work, we use the Complex Chemistry in hot Cores with ALMA
(CoCCoA) survey (Chen et al. 2023, 2025) observations of the field
containingtheNGC6334-43hotcoretocharacterisetheprotostellar
multiplicity and report that a bound nine-member system appears to
haveformedviathefragmentationofasinglelarge-scalefilamentary
structure.

5.3 Formation mechanisms
Here,wediscussthelikelyformationmechanismsfortheboundnine-

Threerecognisedformationmechanismsofprotostellarmultiplesys-
tems are disk, core, and filament fragmentation (see Offner et al.
2023). The detection of multiple bound sources within a disc-like
structure would support the first scenario, while the other scenar-
ios would be favoured by the detection of fragmentation on larger
scales,whereanaspectratiocriterion,𝑎,of>3istypicallyadoptedto
distinguishbetweencoreandfilamentstructures(Offneretal.2023).
5.3.1 The nine-member system
The estimated width of the large-scale filamentary structure in the
fieldis1500−3000au(seeSection3.1)andthenine-membersystem
has a maximum separation between components of∼15,000 au. Its
aspect ratio of𝑎 >3 is consistent with formation via filament frag-
mentationanditsmaximumseparationof∼15,000auiscomparable
to that of an observed quadruple system shown to be formed via fil-
ament fragmentation (maximum separation 11,400 au; Pineda et al.
2015).
Sourcesformedviafilamentfragmentationareexpectedtobeofa
similar age,Δt∼0.5 Myr (Offner et al. 2023). The nine sources con-
sidered here display a range of evolutionary signatures. ALMA2a/b
and ALMA6a are clearly protostellar, based on their outflows (Sec-
tion 3.2.2) and the high physical temperatures inferred from the
CH3CN modelling (Table 6), which indicate significant heating by
embedded protostars. As noted above (Section 3.3.2), ALMA2b
is also coincident with cm-𝜆free-free emission, consistent with a
more evolved evolutionary state. Conversely, the relative paucity of
molecular line emission observed towards ALMA2c, ALMA3a/b,
ALMA4a/b and ALMA6b is suggestive of early evolutionary states
(e.g. Nony et al. 2018; Barnes et al. 2023, see also Section 3.2.1).
However, age differences ofΔt∼0.5 Myr are roughly in line with
the total timescale of massive star formation (∼300 kyr: Motte et al.
2018), so a filament-fragmentation origin for the nine-member sys-
tem remains feasible.
The nine-member system presented here is notable for its higher
order multiplicity and the presence of bound sub-systems. Interest-
ingly, the more evolved, main-sequence nine-member multiple HD
93206(QZCar)alsocontainsboundsub-systems(Mayeretal.2022;
Brož et al. 2022). Of protostellar multiple systems that have been
shown to be bound, Li et al. (2024) find remarkable evidence of
high-order multiplicity (up to a quintuple system) in the high-mass
star forming region G333.23–0.06, where a population of multiples
with a mean separation of 731 au is found to be formed via core
fragmentation. Their mean separation is rather lower than the mean
separation found here (7930 au), although this is likely impacted
by their higher angular resolution (∼260 au compared to∼350 au
here). Recently, Li et al. (2025) reported a close separation (332 au
mean deprojected separation) septuple system formed via disc frag-
mentation.Ourresultsindicatethatfilamentfragmentationmayalso
produce very high-order multiple systems.

5.3.2 The ALMA2 triple system
The two hot core components ALMA2a/b, separated by 618AU
(Table 4), appear to be amongst the most evolved mm sources in
the NGC 6334-43 region (see Section 5.3.1). While there is some
indication of possible disc-like signatures towards ALMA2a (Ap-
pendix A and Figure A1), there is no evidence for a shared disc
structure encompassing both ALMA2a and ALMA2b in the CoC-
CoA data. The maximum separation between the components of
the ALMA2 system is 2330 au, which is similar to the surrounding
structure’s width (see Figure 3c). This is generally consistent with
expectations for formation by core fragmentation, particularly if the
hot core components have migrated inward (e.g. Tokovinin & Moe
2019); simulations in Kuruwita & Haugbølle (2023), for example,
findatypicalcorefragmentationseparationthatisindependentofgas
density,andcorrespondstoaprojected2Dseparationof∼700−2000
au.
5.3.3 The ALMA6 binary system
The ALMA6 protobinary stands out for its youth and for the pres-
ence of a connective bridge and spiral-arm-like feature(s) in the mm
continuum (Section 3.3.6). The projected separation between com-
ponents of the ALMA6 protobinary is 1530 au, similar to the width
ofitscommonstructure.AswiththeALMA2triple,ALMA6’ssepa-
rationisconsistentwiththetypicalscaleofcorefragmentationfound
in Kuruwita & Haugbølle (2023).
Bridge-like features are found in simulations of multiple forma-
tion via both disc (e.g. Mignon-Risse et al. 2021) and turbulent
(e.g. Kuffmeier et al. 2019) fragmentation. A distinguishing feature
of ALMA6 is the prominent spiral-arm-like structure to its south,
whichhasapotentialcounterparttothenorth(seeFigure3g).Spiral
armsoftenplayanimportantroleintheformationofbinariesinsim-
ulations of disc fragmentation (e.g. Krumholz et al. 2009; Oliva &
Kuiper 2020; Mignon-Risse et al. 2021), although here the structure
(which is∼4400 au long) extends well beyond the expected scale of
a disc.
Comparing ALMA6 to the small existing sample of young high-
mass multiple systems (Section 1), the presence of a linking bridge-
like structure is notably similar to the G11.92−0.61 MM2 proto-
binary (formerly a candidate high-mass starless core, Cyganowski
et al. 2014) in the G11.92−0.61 protocluster (Cyganowski et al.
2022). This relatively equal-mass, 505 au-separation protobinary
system (Cyganowski et al. 2022; Sanhueza et al. 2025) has prop-
erties comparable to the super-Alfvénic disk fragmentation models
ofMignon-Risseetal.(2021).G11.92−0.61MM2andALMA6also
both show evidence of ongoing accretion, with MM2 fed significant
materialfromalarge-scale(∼35,000au)filamentarystructure(∼1.8
×10 −4-1.2×10 −3 M⊙ yr−1, Zhang et al. 2024; see also Sanhueza
et al. 2025).
Kong et al. (2023) find that the C1-Sa system, a∼1400 au binary
residinginthedragoncloud(G028.37+00.07),haslikelyformedvia
turbulent fragmentation. The authors note that only one component
of C1-Sa exhibits any mm line emission and that N2D+ and ortho-
H2D+ in the vicinity of the binary indicates an early stage of star
formation. The separation of C1-Sa is very similar to the 1530 au
ALMA6 system and both systems show evidence of differences in
evolutionarystateamongsttheirmembers.Basedonthedetectionof
outflows, Kong et al. (2023) find that C1-Sa1 is protostellar and that
C1-Sa2 is likely starless, an evolutionary discrepancy also seen in
ALMA6 (see Section 5.3.1).
6 CONCLUSIONS
We have used high resolution (∼350 au) ALMA data from the CoC-
CoA survey to characterise the protostellar multiplicity within a
∼0.05pc 2 FOVnearthehotcoreNGC6334-43.Thehigh-resolution
CoCCoA observations reveal 12 compact mm sources, three of
which, ALMA1, ALMA5 and ALMA7 appear to be isolated. Our
main results are summarised below.
(i) We find that a nine-member protostellar system appears to
have formed via the fragmentation of a single large-scale filamen-
tary structure traced in 1.20mm continuum and H13CO+ emission.
A simple stability analysis, comparing gravitational and kinetic en-
ergies, shows that the system, which has a mean separation between
pairs of 7930 au, is bound.
(ii) The sources in the nine-member system display a variety of

APPENDIX A: SEARCH FOR DISC SIGNATURES
Sources ALMA1, ALMA2a/b, ALMA5 and ALMA6a launch out-
flows (see Section 3.2.2), motivating investigation into the presence
of a disc. We look for signatures of Keplerian rotation using strong
unblendedlinesamongALMA2a/b’scopioushotcorelineemission
and in the CH3CN emission towards ALMA6a in both the uncon-
volved and convolved line cubes (Section 2.1). As no compact line
emission is observed towards ALMA1 or ALMA5 we do not search
for disc signatures. We find that moment 1 maps made from the
unconvolved and convolved cubes show similar velocity gradients,
andpresentmapsmadefromtheconvolvedcubesinFiguresA1and
A2.TowardsALMA2a,thereistentativeevidenceofdisc-likeveloc-
ity structure in CH3CN emission, with a velocity gradient roughly
perpendicular to the low-velocity blueshifted SW component of the
outflow(seeFiguresA1and 7andSection3.3.2).Wealsofindsome
evidence of an E-W velocity gradient across ALMA6a in CH3CN𝑘
= 3 (see Figure A2d). However, the direction of the gradient is not
obviously perpendicular to the outflow lobe (see Figure 7). In each
case higher resolution line data are required to confirm the possible
presence of a disc.
