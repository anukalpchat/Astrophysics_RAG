ABSTRACT
Cosmic ray (CR) propagation is controlled by scattering in turbulent magnetic fields in space. In
general, diffusive propagation is governed by pitch-angle diffusion in phase space. In this study, pitch-
angle diffusion in the local interstellar medium (LISM) deduced from the analysis of the CR small scale
anisotropy data from the Tibet ASγexperiment is compared with theoretical predictions. While it is
difficult to reconcile the inferred LISM pitch angle diffusion coefficient with conventional theoretical
results of particle scattering by Alfv´ enic turbulence, we find very good agreement with the prediction
from particle scattering in quasi-slab fast modes shaped by the damping in the warm ionized medium.
These findings offer direct evidence that CR scattering is predominantly governed by fast-mode tur-
bulence. Furthermore, the comparison between experimental and theoretical results imposes strong
constraints on plasma and magnetic field parameters within the local bubble, indicating that the LISM
is in a lowβ≃0.1 condition. The turbulence in the LISM should be compressible with a fast mode
component of amplitude approximatelyδB/B 0 ≈0.5.

1.INTRODUCTION
It is challenging to directly trace the behavior of cos-
mic ray (CR) pitch-angle scattering in the interstellar
medium, although numerous studies using theoretical
calculations and test-particle simulations have been con-
ducted. The difficulty arises because it requires deter-
mining the pitch-angle distribution of CRs in the pris-
tine local interstellar medium (LISM), whose anisotropy
is extremely small, on the order of≲10 −3 (e.g., Lon-
gair 1997; Abbasi et al. 2010; Amenomori et al. 2010;
L´ opez-Barquero et al. 2017; Abeysekara et al. 2019;
Zhang et al. 2020; Maalal & Zhang 2025; Qiao et al.
2026). While the source distribution influences large-
scale anisotropy (e.g., Blasi & Amato 2012; Liu et al.
2017), the small-scale features are primarily determined
by the pitch-angle scattering rateD µµ (Maalal & Zhang
2025).D µµ is governed by magnetic fluctuations instead
of the source distribution, as demonstrated in the litera-

The CR anisotropy is also subject to strong distortion

Over the past decade, our understanding of the he-

of the heliospheric structure, making it possible to map
particle distributions observed at Earth back to the

strated that the pitch-angle distribution in the LISM
can be reconstructed by backtracking particle trajecto-

(Zhang et al. 2020; Maalal & Zhang 2025).
The determination of the pitch-angle scattering rate in
the LISM from the analysis of the small scale anisotropy
by Maalal & Zhang (2025) has allowed a direct compar-

2.PITCH-ANGLE SCATTERING FROM THEORY
AND OBSERVATIONS
The pitch-angle scattering of CRs is governed by
their interactions with MHD fluctuations (Jokipii 1966;
Schlickeiser 2002; Yan 2022). Depending on the en-
ergy of CRs, two main sources of MHD-scale pertur-
bations contribute. For average CR flux in the inter-
stellar medium, CRs with energies≲100 GeV are pre-
dominantly scattered by self-generated MHD waves aris-
ing from streaming and pressure-anisotropy instabilities
(Farmer & Goldreich 2004; Yan & Lazarian 2004, 2011;
Bykov et al. 2013; Lebiga et al. 2018). For CRs with
energies≳100 GeV, scattering by preexisting turbu-
lence is dominant. The physical reasons for the energy
dependence are two-fold. First, the small-scale instabili-
ties operate only when there is sufficient particle flux for
the wave growth to overcome various damping processes,
including damping by background turbulence (Farmer
& Goldreich 2004; Yan & Lazarian 2004, 2011; Lazarian
2016) and nonlinear Landau damping (Lee & V¨ olk 1973;
Kulsrud 2005). Second, preexisting large-scale turbu-
lence – particularly fast modes, which are most effec-
tive for scattering those≳100GeV CRs—are subject to
damping and become subdominant for CRs with ener-
gies≲100 GeV (YL08).
For the TeV CRs considered in this paper, only the
scattering in large-scale MHD turbulence matters. The
key finding from the analysis of the measurements of
TeV CR anisotropy is the weak dependence of the scat-
tering rate as a function of the pitch angle of the par-
ticles (Maalal & Zhang 2025). This result is in conflict
with the scattering in isotropic or Alfv´ enic turbulence.
Instead, the pitch-angle scattering rate obtained from
Maalal & Zhang (2025) appears to be consistent with
earlier theoretical results obtained for the Warm Ion-
ized Medium (WIM) (YL08) with a temperature close
to that of the LISM (see Fig. 1). This striking agree-
ment motivates this detailed investigation of pitch-angle
scattering that explicitly incorporates recent advances
in compressible MHD turbulence studies and charged-
particle transport.
Compressible modes have been demonstrated to play
a pivotal role in particle scattering (Yan & Lazarian
2002, 2004, 2008; Beresnyak et al. 2011; Maiti et al.
2022). Alfv´ enic turbulence, which has traditionally
been regarded as the dominant contribution, is highly
anisotropic (Goldreich & Sridhar 1995; Chandran 2000;

Yan & Lazarian 2002), leading to ineffective scattering

3.PROPERTIES OF COMPRESSIBLE
TURBULENCE
Before revisiting the detailed calculation of CR scat-
tering, we first summarize the relevant properties of
compressible turbulence. Compressible turbulence is
governed by both cascade and various damping mecha-
nisms. Although substantial progress has been made in
understanding compressible turbulence (Cho & Lazarian
2002, 2003; Lazarian et al. 2009; Makwana & Yan 2020),
its fundamental characteristics have long remained a
subject of debate. A central issue concerns the na-
ture of the cascade of fast magnetosonic modes: in
particular, whether it actually conforms to the weak
Iroshnikov–Kraichnan (IK, Iroshnikov 1963; Kraichnan
1965)–type acoustic turbulence model.
Recently, significant progress has been made in under-
standing compressible turbulence across multiple scales
through theoretical developments, MHD simulations,
and analyses of in situ measurements in the heliosphere
(e.g., Chandran 2005; Hadid et al. 2018; Zhao et al.
2024). Theoretical studies indicate that fast modes un-
dergo a weak cascade (Galtier 2023). This is supported
by the weak nonlinearity revealed by spatiotemporal
analysis (Yuen et al. 2025; Zhao et al. 2026). Kinetic
simulations also reveal a 1D power law spectrum with
a slope less than 2 and a scaling of -3/2 for the struc-
ture functions (Hou et al. 2025). Thek −2 power law
spectrum, reported in some MHD simulations, is not
contradictory because of the highly intermittent nature

Damping plays a central role in shaping the spectrum
and anisotropy of fast-mode turbulence in the inertial
range. Early theoretical work suggested that the average
propagation angle of fast modes decreases along the cas-
cade, as oblique and quasi-perpendicular modes are pref-
erentially damped through transit-time damping (TTD)
with thermal particles (Yan & Lazarian 2004, 2008; Pet-
rosian et al. 2006) (Fig. 2). However, direct evidence
for this picture was elusive until recently. Particle-in-
cell (PIC) simulations provide the first clear evidence of
the highly pitch-angle-dependent damping profile (Hou
et al. 2025). Moreover, analyses of turbulence in solar
wind and Earth’s magnetosheath demonstrate that com-
pressible fluctuations become increasingly concentrated
at largerk ∥, suggesting that fast modes evolve toward a
quasi-parallel configuration at smaller scales (Zhao et al.
2024, 2025b).
There would be no gyroresonance interaction to bring
particles into the normal diffusion regime if the turbu-
lence cascade is truncated on large scales, i.e.,k crL ≪1,
wherek c is the cutoff wavenumber, andr L is the par-
ticle Larmor radius. The quasi-parallel structure of
fast modes, which naturally forms on small scales in
lowβplasmas, is essential since the gyroresonance only
requires the existence of turbulence perturbations at
k∥,res = Ω/|v∥ ±v A|, where Ω is the Larmor frequency,
v∥, vA are the particle speed along the magnetic field
and Alfv´ en speed, respectively. In other words, quasi-
2D (k ⊥ ≫k ∥,k ∥, k⊥ are the projections of the wave
vector parallel and perpendicular to the magnetic field)
structure contributes marginally to the gyroresonance,
whereas quasi-slab modes dominate the interaction (see
Yan & Lazarian 2002). Here, plasmaβis the ratio of
the plasma pressure to magnetic pressure.
In what follows, we will demonstrate that the pitch-
angle dependence of the CR scattering rate is di-
rectly correlated with the quasi-parallel structure of fast
modes.
4.PITCH-ANGLE SCATTERING BY FAST-MODE
TURBULENCE
Pitch-angle diffusion can be driven by gyroresonance
and mirror/TTD interaction. The diffusion coefficient
depends on the pitch angle of the particle. For small
pitch angles, gyroresonance dominates. For large pitch
angles, particularly near 90 ◦, the mirror interaction is
the key scattering mechanism by lifting particles out of

the mirror potential well (Felice & Kulsrud 2001; Yan &
Lazarian 2008). The TTD treatment is equivalent to the
mirror interaction in the case of quasi-parallel modes.
These modes are characterized by a three-dimensional
(3D) turbulence spectrum with power concentrated on
k⊥ ≪k ∥, for which the compression/mirror force is re-
duced due to the smaller obliquity of the waves. As a re-
sult, Landau/Cerenkov resonance becomes necessary for
effective interactions. Such quasi-parallel fast modes are
expected in low-βplasmas (β≲1), where the damping
rate increases with the wave pitch angle, in general (Yan
& Lazarian 2004, 2008; Petrosian et al. 2006; Suzuki
et al. 2006; Zhao et al. 2024; Hou et al. 2025; Zhao et al.
2025b) (see Fig. 2).

5.DISCUSSION
The 3D structure of turbulence is crucial for under-
standing the pattern of pitch angle scattering, particu-
larly the dependence onµ/pitch angle. The dependence
of the TeV CR pitch-angle distribution is incompati-
ble with traditional isotropic turbulence or slab waves.
We find that compressible turbulence with a quasi-slab
structure, which naturally arises from fast modes in low

6
βenvironments, provides the best explanation for the
observed pitch-angle diffusion behavior. Firstly, the cur-
rent understanding of the physical parameters in LISM
is consistent with the lowβcondition. Secondly, both
mirror and gyroresonance should operate to reach the
normal diffusion regime with a finite mean free path,
which cannot be realized in either isotropic turbulence
or slab waves. The latter can only give rise to gyrores-
onance, but not to the mirror/TTD interaction. The
former can, in principle, give rise to mirror/TTD scat-
tering if it is compressible fast modes. The assumption
of isotropy is not valid, though, due to anisotropic damp-
ing. Furthermore, the observed weak dependence on the
pitch angle means that the mirroring effect must be re-
duced, disfavoring MHD modes with high obliquity. In
other words, the turbulence must be quasi-parallel.
Some recent work has advocated for potentially large
angle scattering by intermittent structure in MHD tur-
bulence (Lemoine 2023). We do not consider it here
for two reasons. First, there is no indication of super-
Alfv´ enic turbulence in LISM. The analysis of Voyager
1 data indicates the turbulence in the local bubble is
trans-Alfv´ enic (Zhao et al. 2025a). Secondly, there is
no indication of large-angle scattering from either test-
particle simulations in trans- or sub-Alfv´ enic turbulence
or CR anisotropy observations (Yan et al. 2026).
One may argue that mirror interaction and gyroreso-
nance can arise from different modes/structures in tur-
bulence rather than from a single type of quasi-slab fast-
mode turbulence. In this scenario, extensive fine-tuning
would then be inevitable in order to reproduce the ob-
served smoothµdependence of the pitch-angle scatter-
ing.
We also want to emphasize the difference between
global CR transport and that occurring in the LISM.
The pitch-angle diffusion coefficient derived from TeV
CR anisotropies reflects mainly local propagation prop-
erties since CRs quickly lose their memories of initial
directions due to frequent scattering (see Kuhlen et al.
2022). There have been attempts to test the scatter-
ing coefficient obtained through microscopic physical
processes directly against global CR observables, e.g.,
(Kempski & Quataert 2022). We argue that global and
local transport can be completely different. For exam-
ple, CR diffusion perpendicular to the local magnetic
field is found to be small compared to parallel diffusion
(Maalal & Zhang 2025), whereas global CR transport
indicates nearly isotropic diffusion. Similarly, reduced
diffusion in local environments has been identified in
other observations (Abeysekara et al. 2017; Liu et al.
2019). These are not necessarily contradictory, as the
magnetic field structure at global scales differs substan-
tially from that at local scales. On the global scale, the
random walk of magnetic field lines can also contribute
to CR diffusion.
We recognize that the experimental result adopted
from Maalal & Zhang (2025) obtained by back-tracing,
is not exclusive and is contingent upon the heliospheric
magnetic structure selected for the modeling. Neverthe-
less, the concordance between the two entirely indepen-
dent studies originating from the cosmic ray nonlinear
scattering theory (Yan & Lazarian 2008) and the back-
tracing analysis in Maalal & Zhang (2025), spanning
several decades, elucidates certain fundamental charac-
teristics of the local interstellar turbulence and its inter-
action with cosmic rays.
6.CONCLUSION
We have revisited the calculation of CR scattering in
LISM turbulence based on the earlier NLT model from
YL08 for compressible MHD turbulence, motivated by
recent advances in both the understanding of interstel-
lar turbulence and observations of cosmic-ray properties.
Our main findings are as follows:
•Gyroresonance and mirror/TTD interactions with
fast-mode turbulence naturally provide pitch-
angle scattering rates consistent with those in-
ferred from TeV CR anisotropy measurements.
•The quasi-slab structure of fast modes resulting
from damping in a low-βmedium is found to be
responsible for a weak pitch-angle dependence of
the scattering rate.
•The physical parameters in local interstellar tur-
bulence are constrained to beβ∼0.1, and an
amplitude ofM A ∼0.5 for the compressible fast
modes, consistent with the known plasma condi-
tion in the LISM.
The agreement between the theoretical and observa-
tional results confirms the NLT theory on CR scattering
in turbulence, an essential piece of CR transport, and
has broad implications for all related processes in high
energy astrophysics and the interstellar medium.
