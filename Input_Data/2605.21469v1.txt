Title: The giant pulse population of PSR B0355+54

Published: 2026-05-20
Categories: astro-ph.HE

The giant pulse population of PSR B0355+54

Abstract
Giant pulses are rare bright radio bursts that occur in restricted ranges of pulsar
rotational phase. Here we report giant pulses from PSR B0355+54, a pulsar with
spin periodP≃0.156s. Using 7.97 hours of observations centred at 1.46 GHz,
with 128 MHz bandwidth in each circular polarizations, we identify 432 pulse
periods containing bright pulses. The giant pulses recur in two compact longitude
regions inside the radio emission window. They are narrow compared with the
mean profile, with medianW 50 = 290.3µs, and reach relative peak flux density
ratios up toS pk/⟨Spk⟩= 149.7. The early longitude group has a timing scatter
of139.7µs, or8.9×10 −4 of a rotation. The first longitude group favours right,
while the second favours left circular polarization.
Keywords:pulsars: individual: PSR B0355+54; pulsars: general; giant pulses;
methods: data analysis
Giant radio pulses (GPs) are rare individual radio pulses that stand out from ordi-
nary pulse to pulse modulation by a combination of high intensity, short duration and
restricted rotational longitude. In the Crab pulsar, GPs have broad intensity distri-
butions and can show extremely short time structure [1–4]. In PSR B1937+21, GPs
occur in narrow phase windows tied to the regular main pulse and interpulse, and can
contain nanosecond structure [5–8]. These two classical sources set the observational
template for the phenomenon: GPs are defined by both high intensity and recurrence
at restricted rotational longitudes.
The reported census of GP sources remains small and heterogeneous. Beyond the
two classical sources, GPs have been reported from young or millisecond pulsars such

J1752+2359, PSR B0950+08 and PSR J1047–6709 [15–20].
The non millisecond part of the census is especially diverse. Several reported cases
were detected at low radio frequencies, where propagation, profile evolution and dif-
ferent intensity measures complicate comparison with the classical high frequency GP
phenomenology [16–19]. Among previously reported non millisecond GP sources, PSR
J1047–6709 is the only L band case [20].
The data analysed here were obtained in a broader programme of high time resolu-
tion single pulse observations with the Quasar VLBI network [21]. The present paper
concerns PSR B0355+54, a pulsar withP≃0.156 s period [22, 23]. Its radio emission
is broad, structured and variable. Scintillation measurements show time dependent
propagation effects, and recent L band observations show profile variability between
epochs and across frequency [24, 25].
PSR B0355+54 is also detected in X-rays.XMM-NewtonandChandraobserva-
tions reveal emission from the pulsar and extended emission interpreted as a pulsar
wind nebula or bow shock structure [26, 27].
Here we report that PSR B0355+54 emits a phase confined GP population at L
band. The selected bright pulses recur in two compact longitude regions within the
radio emission window.
1 Results
A search over the full pulse period identifies repeatable giant pulses in PSR B0355+54.
Across the 7.97 h primary data set, 432 stellar rotations contain selected bright pulses,
giving one GP rotation per≃424 rotations. Counting the right and left circular
polarizations (RCP and LCP) separately, the primary sample contains 551 events. The
events appear in both observing sessions and in both recorded polarizations.
The full period search also provides an internal background control (Fig. 1a).
Outside the two repeatable longitude groups described below, the selection leaves 63
candidates spread over the remaining pulse phase. In contrast to the two longitude
groups, no phase bin outside the groups contains more than one selected event, and
none of the outside candidates has a corresponding selected event in the other polar-
ization in the same rotation. The number of outside candidates is the same order as the
≃43 outliers expected from the detected powerχ 2 tail of the blind full phase search.
The selected pulses are distinguished first by their longitude distribution. They
concentrate into two longitude groups superposed on the normal profile (Fig. 1). The
two groups are separated by 1.91 ms in pulse longitude. The late group contains most
of the events, while the early group is rarer.
The polarization information adds structure to this longitude picture. Of the 432
bright pulse rotations, 119 contain selected events in both circular polarizations, while
313 are selected in only one. These RCP only and LCP only detections are a large
part of the sample and carry information about the polarization pattern. Their occur-
rence depends strongly on longitude. In the early group, 82 rotations are RCP only, 5
are LCP only, and 26 are detected in both circular polarizations. In the late group, 64

The selected pulses are also narrow compared with the ordinary emission. Their
median width isW 50 = 290.3µs, much narrower than the mean radio profile, and their
relative peak flux densities extend toS pk/⟨Spk⟩= 149.7. Figure 2 shows a representa-

polarizations with broadband dynamic spectrum structure.
Both circular polarizations show extended high intensity tails (Fig. 3). Formal
descriptive fits to the upper quartile of the complementary cumulative distributions
quantify the terminal tails with slopes of 4.93±0.06 for RCP and 5.94±0.12 for LCP.
The early longitude group is the most compact in phase. Times of arrival formed
from the selected pulses give a post fit scatter of 139.7µs for the early group, about
10−3 of a rotation, compared with 474.5µs for the broader late group (Fig. 4, Table 1).
Thus the selected events show three linked signatures of a phase confined population:
they recur in two compact longitude groups, detections in RCP and LCP remain
within the same group when both are present, and the control region outside the
groups contains only sparse background candidates with no repeated phase bin and
no comparable polarization coincidence.

2 Discussion
PSR B0355+54 shows a giant pulse population embedded in the mean radio profile.
The GPs recur at two preferred longitudes inside the normal pulse window. This points
to a rare state of selected parts of the radio emission beam.
The closest comparison is PSR B1937+21, although the analogy is geometric,
scaled to the pulsar period. In PSR B1937+21, giant pulses occur in narrow phase
regions associated with the trailing edges of the regular main pulse and interpulse com-
ponents [6, 8]. For B1937+21, the mean widths of the folded main pulse and interpulse
GP distributions are about 4µs, corresponding to≃2.6×10 −3 of the rotation [8]. In
PSR B0355+54, the early group has a timing scatter of 139.7µs, or 8.9×10 −4 of the
period, while the broader late group has a scatter of 3.0×10 −3 of the period. Thus
the B0355+54 groups are more compact in the phase scale. In both objects, rare high
peak pulses select longitudes tied to the mean radio profile. In PSR B0355+54 the
early group lies near the trailing side of the first profile component, while the broader
late group is associated with the second component.
The polarization behaviour gives a second parallel with PSR B1937+21. Strong
giant pulses from PSR B1937+21 have been detected above threshold in either RCP or
LCP without simultaneous threshold crossing in both channels [28]. Later full polar-
ization studies showed that individual B1937+21 giant pulses can have high linear or
circular polarization, unlike the average profile [8, 29]. In PSR B0355+54, the circu-
lar polarization preference changes with longitude: RCP only detections dominate the
early group, whereas LCP only detections dominate the late group.
The Crab pulsar gives a useful comparison in absolute arrival phase. In an L band
Crab GP sample, Majid et al. measured TOA residual widths of about 90µs for main
pulse GPs and 140µs for interpulse GPs [30]. In PSR B0355+54, the early longitude

Previous radio studies already suggested that PSR B0355+54 is not well described
by a stationary two component beam. Single pulse polarimetry found rare flaring of
weak profile regions and used such behaviour to argue against a simple inactive cone
sector picture [31]. High frequency polarimetry identified B0355+54 as the prototype
of a class in which one profile component remains highly polarized and has a flatter
spectrum than the rest of the profile [32]. Recent L band FAST observations show
profile changes between epochs and across frequency, consistent with a nonuniform
and time dependent distribution of emitting regions [25]. The bright pulses reported
here give a direct view of the high intensity end of this nonuniform emission pattern.
The two longitude groups have different rates, phase widths and circular polariza-
tion preferences. The early group is less frequent but more compact in phase, while
the late group is more populated and spans a wider longitude range. The RCP to LCP
detections ratio changes from 82/5 in the early group to 64/162 in the late group.
A single gain imbalance, leakage term or scintillation state would not naturally pro-
duce such a reversal between two nearby profile components. The contrast therefore
points to different circular mode content, or different propagation conditions, in the
two bright pulse regions.

A simple interpretation is that the line of sight crosses two active regions within
the open field line beam. The early region may correspond to a narrower range of field
lines or emission heights, giving a stable pulse longitude and a preferred circular mode.
The late region may sample a broader range of longitude, height or mode conversion
in the magnetospheric plasma. In this picture the bright pulses are rare coherent or
amplified states of selected parts of the radio beam, not a separate beam component.
Scintillation remains relevant for the observed rate and apparent intensity. PSR
B0355+54 shows variable scintillation behaviour, so scan to scan changes in the num-
ber of events cannot be interpreted as intrinsic without care [24]. Scintillation, however,
does not naturally produce two fixed pulse longitudes with opposite circular mode
preference. The phase and polarization structure therefore points to the pulsar mag-
netosphere, while scintillation mainly modulates how many events are detected in a
given observing interval.
If the similarity to PSR B1937+21 is mainly morphological, the rarest bright events
may be governed by rotation count rather than by observing time. Because PSR
B0355+54 has a period about two orders of magnitude longer, the same rotational
sample would require much longer observations. The present data show a recurrent
GP population confined in phase, but do not yet probe the rarest part of its amplitude
distribution.

3 Methods
3.1 Observations and instrumental setup
PSR B0355+54 was observed with the Badary 32 m radio telescope of the Quasar
VLBI network [33] during the RUP112 and RUP117 sessions on 2026 March 25 and
April 30. The observations were centred at 1464.49 MHz. A VLBI backend recorded 2-
bit raw voltage data in sixteen 16 MHz channels, split equally between RCP and LCP,
giving 128 MHz of bandwidth per polarization. A hydrogen maser provided the station
time and frequency reference. The primary sample contains 17 scans and 28676 s of
data on source. The full instrumental setup is given in Supplementary Table 1.
3.2 Folding and single pulse pipeline
The first bright pulse candidates were found with our coherent single pulse search
pipeline developed for the Quasar VLBI stations [21]. This pipeline is optimized for
event discovery in raw voltage data at high time resolution, whereas the population
study required stable profiles for individual rotations and uniform measurements of
pulse longitude, width and relative strength. The discovery pipeline found several GPs
at time resolutions between 1 and 16µs. Trial dispersion measure and time resolution
tests of the strongest event showed that its significance increased after smoothing to
coarser resolution on microsecond scales. This check is described in Supplementary
Note 4.

The folded products were written as PSRCHIVE internal format archives [35].
We inspected persistent radio frequency interference and instrumental artefacts with
psrzap, and then applied the same fixed mask for contaminated channels to every
scan and polarization in the Python processing pipeline.
A dedicated Python pipeline produced the final event table. It read the folded
archives scan by scan, applied the fixed channel mask, preserved the subintegrations
for individual rotations, estimated the baseline outside the pulse window and noise
level, and measured the candidate pulse properties. For each candidate it records
the scan identifier, polarization, subintegration number, peak phase bin, peak phase,
folded profile peak SNR, pulse width and profile normalized peak flux density ratio.
The two circular polarizations are measured independently. Polarization coincidences
are identified only after the single polarization event table has been constructed.
For each scan and polarization the pipeline also constructs a scan local clean mean
profile. Rotations containing candidate bright pulses and rotations marked as RFI
contaminated are excluded from the average, and the remaining rotations define the
clean reference profile. This local normalization reduces sensitivity to changes between
scans in intensity, scintillation and profile variation between polarizations.
The same automatic selection was also applied to one Crab pulsar scan as a control
case. The result is described in Supplementary Note 3.
3.3 Automatic event selection and phase grouping
The candidate search was deliberately blind in pulse longitude. After application of
the fixed frequency channel mask, each single rotation profile was searched across all
2048 phase bins in each circular polarization. A candidate entered the clean event
table if it passed the RFI veto and its folded profile peak signal-to-noise ratio satisfied
SNR≥5.4. No cut on pulse width or relative peak flux density was applied.
The threshold was chosen to generate candidates permissively across the full pulse
phase. The expected number of natural detected power outliers is small but nonzero
for a search over all pulse phases and both polarizations. The detailed statistic and the
comparison with the observed background outside the two longitude groups are given
in Supplementary Note 1. The selected events were then grouped by pulse phase for
morphological analysis. The early and late groups are not search gates: they describe
the two repeatable longitude concentrations that appear after the full phase selection.

3.5 Local timing refinement
The first folding pass used the catalogue ephemeris for PSR J0358+5413 / B0355+54.
The astrometric and spin parameters were taken from the ATNF Pulsar Catalogue
[23] entry based on the timing solution of Li et al. [36]. This ephemeris is adequate for
detecting single bright pulses within individual scans, but the full data set spans two
observing sessions separated by several tens of days, so a small error in folding spin
frequency produces a measurable drift of bright pulse longitude between scans.
To place all scans on a common pulse longitude scale, we constructed a local timing
refinement from the early bright pulse component. For each GP in the early group
we formed a diagnostic time of arrival (TOA) using a local template estimator and
fitted the resulting TOAs withtempo2[37, 38]. The local template was formed from
GP profiles after alignment to their measured peaks. Each TOA was then measured
by cross correlating the single rotation profile with this template over a fixed window
around the component. The timing scatter quoted in Table 1 was computed after
applying the same residual clipping used in the localtempo2fit.
Only the spin frequencyF 0 was adjusted in the pulsar timing model. The fitted
constant phase offset was used only to align the local timing reference. The local
fit used 139 TOAs from the early group. The prefit residual RMS was 8514.5µs,
while the postfit residual RMS was 140.9µs. The fitted spin frequency changed from
F0 = 6.39440176623564 s−1 toF 0 = 6.39440180469109 s−1, with a formal uncertainty
of 5.38×10 −11 s−1 at PEPOCH = 61142. The refined spin frequency was then used
to refold the data and define the corrected pulse longitude scale used throughout the
final analysis. This correction removes the phase drift between scans common to the
data set.

Supplementary Note 1: Full phase pulse selection and
background control
The automatic selection was applied to the candidate table produced from the original
folded archives, before any phase gate was imposed. After the fixed frequency channel
mask had been applied, each single rotation profile was searched over all 2048 phase
bins in each circular polarization. A pulse was retained if it was not marked by the
RFI veto and if its folded profile peak SNR satisfiedSN R≥5.4.

Supplementary Note 2: Flux density and fluence scale
Pulse strength is reported primarily asS pk/⟨Spk⟩, measured relative to the clean mean
profile for the same scan and circular polarization. To estimate approximate peak flux
densities for the strongest events, we used single polarization SEFD values measured
at the Badary station: SEFD RCP = 365 Jy and SEFD LCP = 340 Jy.
With ∆νeff ≃102 MHz and ∆t bin = 76.37µs, the single bin flux density noise scale
is
σS,bin,pol = SEFDpol√∆νeff∆tbin
,(6)
which givesσ S,bin,RCP ≃4.14 Jy andσ S,bin,LCP ≃3.85 Jy. A single pulse peak flux
density is then estimated as
Spk,GP,pol ≃(SN R) pk,polσS,bin,pol.(7)
For rup117 scan no0005, the RCP and LCP peak signal-to-noise ratios of 14.63 and
9.28 correspond to peak flux densities of 60.5 Jy and 35.7 Jy, respectively. Summing
the two circular polarization streams gives an approximate peak scale of≃96 Jy. The
maximum SNR event, rup112 scan no0037, reaches≃120 Jy summed over the two
circular polarizations.

Supplementary Note 3: The Crab pulsar processed with the
same pipeline
The Crab pulsar was used as a reference processed with the same pipeline. Applying
the same automatic selection to one Crab scan recovers the expected dominant main
pulse concentration, wrapping through phase zero, together with a smaller interpulse
concentration. In the 2048-bin folded profiles, the main pulse events occupy bins 2034–
2047 and 0–12, while the interpulse events occupy bins 821–850. Of the 162 clean Crab
events, 142 are in the main pulse region, 9 in the interpulse region and 11 outside
these two regions.
15

The Crab sample is more extreme than PSR B0355+54 in recurrence rate, polar-
ization coincidence fraction, peak flux density ratio and pulse width (Supplementary
Table 6). This is the expected behaviour for a classical GP source. The control shows
that the analysis chain recovers a known and more extreme GP population.
Supplementary Note 4: Discovery stage trial DM and time
resolution diagnostic
The first bright pulse candidates were identified with the coherent single pulse search
pipeline previously developed for the Quasar VLBI pulsar programme [21]. The
strongest event in scan no0037 was reprocessed over a grid of trial dispersion measures
at effective time resolutions of 1, 4, 8, 16 and 32µs (Supplementary Fig. 4). For each
time resolution and circular polarization, the pulse signal-to-noise ratio was measured
as a function of coherent trial DM.
The event is recovered in both circular polarizations near the expected dispersion
measure of PSR B0355+54. The RCP gives a stable maximum near DM≃57.09–
57.13 pc cm−3. The LCP is noisier at the finest resolution, but from 4 to 32µs
its maximum lies near DM≃57.13–57.21 pc cm −3. The peak signal-to-noise ratio
increases systematically toward coarser effective time resolution, from 10.5 to 19.4 in
RCP and from 8.3 to 15.3 in LCP. The strongest response therefore does not occur at
the finest tested resolution, arguing against an instrumental artefact confined to one
time bin and motivating the folded archive analysis used for the population study.
